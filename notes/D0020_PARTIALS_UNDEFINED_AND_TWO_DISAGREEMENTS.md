## 1. The four PARTIAL rows discharged

### 3.6 — folding as an argmin of γ

**CLASSICAL-SOURCED.** C. B. Anfinsen, *Principles that govern the folding of protein chains*,
**Science** (New Series) **181** (4096), 20 July 1973, **223–230**; DOI
`10.1126/science.181.4096.223`. The paper is the printed form of the 1972 Nobel lecture.
Bibliographic record confirmed from multiple independent indices.

The thermodynamic hypothesis, in the wording carried by the secondary literature quoting the
paper (**not** decoded from the primary; science.org 403, mirror 503): the native
three-dimensional structure "*in its normal physiological milieu … is the one in which the Gibbs
free energy of the whole system is lowest; that is, that the native conformation is determined by
the totality of interatomic interactions and hence by the amino acid sequence, in a given
environment*."

Two things follow for the row, and both **strengthen** the ledger's split rather than soften it:

1. The hypothesis as stated is **explicitly milieu-relative** ("in a given environment"). The
   transmission's display carries no environment parameter, so even the half the ledger calls
   CLASSICAL is classical only after a parameter the display omits is restored. In
   `ABHAVA.md`'s vocabulary this is a universal statement quoted without its **avacchedaka**.
2. The row's "false in general" half is not a modern quibble against Anfinsen: Levinthal's
   paradox (1969) is contemporaneous, and the kinetic-vs-thermodynamic control literature
   (Baker–Agard, *Biochemistry* **33** (1994) 7505–7509, "Kinetics versus thermodynamics in
   protein folding", bibliographically verified) is the standing statement of the exception
   class. **Earliest source for the exception is not established here** and is a live question.

### 5.5 — the seven padārthas, with ∅ for abhāva

**CLASSICAL-SOURCED, and the search produced a correction the ledger does not have.**

The seven-fold scheme **is not Kaṇāda's**. The *Vaiśeṣikasūtra* enumerates **six** padārthas
(dravya, guṇa, karma, sāmānya, viśeṣa, samavāya), and Praśastapāda's
*Padārthadharmasaṅgraha* keeps six. **Abhāva is a later addition**, argued for by
**Śrīdhara**, *Nyāyakandalī* (commentary on Praśastapāda, self-dated **991 CE**) — who states
that Kaṇāda omitted abhāva separately because abhāva depends on bhāva — and made a **numbered
seventh category** in **Śivāditya Miśra**, *Saptapadārthī*, c. **1150 CE**, whose title is the
claim; Udayana is in the same later line. Bibliographically verified via reference works
(wisdomlib's Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika category surveys; Shodhganga chapter VII "Abhāva as a separate
category"); **no Sanskrit edition read**.

So the row's defect is **doubled**, and the second half is new:

- as the ledger says, the **∅ slot is the wrong type** — see §3.2 below;
- and **the attribution is anachronistic**: a "seven padārthas" labelled वैशेषिक is the
  Śrīdhara–Śivāditya recension (10th–12th c.), not the sūtra's. The transmission presents a
  1150 CE syncretic count as the school's own.

### 5.6 — Sāṃkhya, and the guṇas "summing to 1"

**CLASSICAL-ATTRIBUTED** (priority not establishable in the strict sense used by
`D0020_CLASSICAL_SOURCES.md`: the doctrine is older than any surviving text).

Earliest **surviving** text: **Īśvarakṛṣṇa**, *Sāṃkhyakārikā*, 72 kārikās in āryā metre.
Dating: terminus ad quem **569 CE** via Paramārtha's Chinese translation; conventional
attribution *fl.* c. 350 CE; a 2025 re-examination (Acta Orientalia Academiae Scientiarum
Hungaricae **78** (3) (2025) 447–, "Revisiting the Date of Īśvarakṛṣṇa and His Sāṃkhyakārikā")
argues **447–490 CE**. The boxed द्रष्टा ≠ दृश्यप्रवाहः is **SK 19** (puruṣa as *sākṣitva*,
*kaivalya*, *mādhyasthya*, *draṣṭṛtva*, *akartṛbhāva*) — verse number from secondary
translations, **not from a Sanskrit edition**.

**The ledger's finding stands and is now sourced negatively:** no textual warrant for
"summing to 1" was found. The guṇas are given qualitatively and by mutual suppression/support
(SK 12–13), not as a normalised triple; nothing in the kārikā or in the standard commentaries
surveyed assigns them numerical weights at all. **This is an absence of evidence from
secondary surveys, not a proof of absence from the primary text**, and is reported as such.
It is enough to sustain the ledger's split: the normalisation is the transmission's addition.

### 5.9 — the three Vedāntas distinguished by a relation

**CLASSICAL-ATTRIBUTED for each school's own text; CLASSICAL-SOURCED for the tri-partition as a
doxographic act.**

- Advaita ($\alpha=\Omega$): **Śaṅkara**, *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya*, c. 700–750 CE.
- Viśiṣṭādvaita ($\alpha,\beta\subset\Omega$): **Rāmānuja**, *Śrībhāṣya*, c. 1100–1130 CE.
- Dvaita ($\alpha\ne\Omega$, $\alpha\to\Omega$): **Madhva**, *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* /
  *Anuvyākhyāna*, 13th c.

The **comparison itself** — the three read as three answers to one question — is doxographic,
and its earliest form available here is **Mādhava, *Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha*, 14th c.**, which
carries separate chapters for the *Rāmānuja-darśana* and the *Pūrṇaprajña- (Dvaita) darśana*
inside an Advaita-terminal ordering. Bibliographically verified (Project Gutenberg #34125,
Cowell–Gough translation; a 2024 Brill retranslation, *Compendium of All Philosophies*).
**None of these read.**

This matters for the row's grade: the ledger files the *attribution* of relations to schools as
"CLASSICAL, not read", and it is right to; but the further claim implicit in the display — that
the three schools are individuated **by that relation** — is a **doxographic** proposition with
a datable first form and an Advaita-partisan ordering, not a neutral fact about the schools.
Row 5.9's strength is unaffected: what is proved-trivially there is a distinction of *forms*.

## 3. The two disagreements adjudicated

The ledger records two places where a corpus note contradicts the transmission's own triage.
Both are adjudicated here **in the same direction: the note is right and the transmission is
wrong** — but the two are wrong in different ways, and the difference is the useful part.

### 3.1 — apoha and the type of alignment (rows 5.16, J3): **the note is right**

**The claim at issue.** J3 (archive l. 517–526) asserts that §5's $\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\perp\perp}$
and §7's two-sided evaluation are "the SAME polarity closure", the Birkhoff polarity the fleet
has theorems about. `notes/APOHA_CHANGES_THE_TYPE_OF_ALIGNMENT.md` §2 concludes, from Dignāga's
text, that "apoha is therefore not an untyped Boolean complement of a pre-given set."

**Formal adjudication (independent of doctrine).** A Birkhoff polarity *is* a relation: given
$R\subseteq A\times B$, $S^\perp:=\{b\mid\forall a\in S,\ aRb\}$, and $(-)^{\perp\perp}$ is then
a closure operator — that is a theorem **about $R$**, and it has no content without one. The
transmission (l. 283) writes $\alpha^\perp:=\{\tau\mid\tau\ \text{अल्फेतरविभेदकः}\}$: a
**one-place predicate on $\tau$**, with $\alpha$ occurring only inside an untranslated gloss.
No $R$, hence no Galois connection, hence $\alpha^{\perp\perp}$ is not shown to be a closure and
the identification with §7 is unavailable **as written**. J3's own hedge — "a 'purely formal'
answer is fine and likely" — concedes the interesting half but not the word *SAME*, which is
what is refuted.

**Doctrinal adjudication (the note's ground, checked against what it cites).** Dignāga,
*Pramāṇasamuccayavṛtti* V.2–11 rejects four positive candidates for the word's referent
(*bheda*, *jāti*, *sambandha*, *jātimat*); V.11 concludes *tenānyāpohakṛc chrutiḥ*; **V.25cd–38
is the load-bearing passage** — the "other" that is excluded behaves **differently for
synonyms, for coordinate incompatibles, and for sub/superordinate pairs**. A Boolean complement
in a fixed universe is **uniform and symmetric**: it cannot distinguish those three cases, since
$\neg$ knows nothing of how a term was formed. So the doctrine does not merely fail to be a
complement by accident of notation; **the scope-asymmetry is precisely the content that an
untyped complement destroys.** That is the note's point and it is correct.
*(Citation via the note, which reads the primary text; I did not decode a Sanskrit or Tibetan
edition, and the note's own reading is the warrant here.)*

**Verdict.** **Note right, J3 wrong as stated.** The correct residue of J3 is narrower and worth
keeping: *if* one supplies a two-place exclusion relation $R$ on terms — which is what
§2 of the note says the tradition itself makes typed — then $(-)^{\perp\perp}$ over that $R$ is
a closure, and the §7 theorems apply to *that* object. That is a **conditional** convergence
whose hypothesis is exactly the contested definition (row 5.16's missing item vi above). By
the transmission's own rule 5.1 — समता प्रमाणेन, साम्येन न — J3's identification is
**by resemblance**, which is the ledger's finding, reached here independently.

### 3.2 — absence and its three slots (row 5.5): **the note is right, and the transmission refutes itself**

**The claim at issue.** The transmission (l. 262–263) writes the seven padārthas as a tuple
$(\delta,\gamma,\kappa,\Sigma,\upsilon,\iota,\varnothing)$ with abhāva as the entry $\varnothing$,
and boxes $\text{अभावः}\ne\text{शून्यशब्दः}$. `notes/ABHAVA.md` §1 gives absence three named
slots — *pratiyogin* (counterpositive), *anuyogin* (locus), *avacchedaka* (limitor) — and writes
$\text{abhāva}(p,\ell,\alpha)\equiv\forall x\in\ell:\neg p_\alpha(x)$.

**Adjudication, three findings.**

1. **Type.** The other six padārthas are **categories** (kinds of entity); the seventh is written
   as a **constant**, $\varnothing$. Even before any doctrine, that is a type error inside the
   tuple: six sorts and one element. The note's $\text{abhāva}(p,\ell,\alpha)$ is a **relation
   of arity three**, which is a category-sized object and fits the slot the other six occupy.
2. **The transmission contradicts its own box.** $\text{अभावः}\ne\text{शून्यशब्दः}$ says absence
   is not the empty word. Writing $\varnothing$ — the empty-set glyph, and the *first* member of
   the §0 seed alphabet (l. 30) — as the seventh entry **performs the identification the box
   forbids**, in the same two lines. This is not a disagreement between the note and the
   transmission; it is an inconsistency internal to the row, and it is the sharpest thing
   available against it.
3. **Both parties are anachronistic, but only one harmfully.** From §1.5.2 above: the
   *Vaiśeṣikasūtra* has **six** padārthas; abhāva is Śrīdhara (991) / Śivāditya (c. 1150). The
   three-slot analysis with *avacchedaka* is later still — **Navya-Nyāya**, Gaṅgeśa's line
   (14th c.), and `ABHAVA.md` says so ("then with great precision in Navya-Nyāya"), sourcing
   Annambhaṭṭa's *Tarkasaṅgraha* §§57, 80. So the note applies a 14th-century calculus to a
   Vaiśeṣika label — **harmless**, because it offers the calculus as the better formalisation
   and dates it. The transmission applies a 12th-century count under the label वैशेषिक
   **as if it were the school's own** — an attribution error, and one no reader of the row
   could detect.

**Verdict.** **Note right, transmission wrong, and wrong twice over** — the formalisation
(the ledger's finding, sustained) and the attribution (new here). The ledger's headline for
5.5, "the corpus is ahead of the transmission here", is correct and understated.
