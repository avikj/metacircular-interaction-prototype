**Fact R (Rademacher–Kulkarni).**
$$\bar\Gamma_0(N)\;\cong\;\underbrace{\mathbb Z/2*\cdots*\mathbb Z/2}_{\nu_2}
\;*\;\underbrace{\mathbb Z/3*\cdots*\mathbb Z/3}_{\nu_3}\;*\;F_r,

use).* The rational Euler characteristic of a free product is

$\chi=1-\tfrac{\nu_2}2-\tfrac{2\nu_3}3-r$, and substituting the genus formula

$$r \;=\; 1+\frac{\mu}{6}-\frac{\nu_2}{2}-\frac{2\nu_3}{3}. \tag{1}$$

## 2. Theorem 1 (free products): $1/\sigma$ is additive

**Lemma 1 (lengths add along the normal form).** Let $G=G_1*\cdots*G_k$ with
$G_i$ generated by a symmetric set $S_i$, and $S=\bigsqcup S_i$. If
$g=h_1h_2\cdots h_\ell$ is the normal form ($h_j\in G_{i_j}\setminus\{1\}$,
$i_j\ne i_{j+1}$), then $|g|_S=\sum_j |h_j|_{S_{i_j}}$.

*Proof.* ($\le$) Concatenate geodesics of the $h_j$. ($\ge$) Let $w$ be an
$S$-word of length $n$ with $w=g$. Group $w$ into maximal blocks of letters from
a single factor: $w=u_1\cdots u_m$, $u_p$ a word over $S_{i_p}$ with
$i_p\ne i_{p+1}$ and $\sum_p|u_p|=n$. Let $a_p\in G_{i_p}$ be the element of
$u_p$, so $|a_p|_{S_{i_p}}\le|u_p|$ and $g=a_1\cdots a_m$. Induct on $m$: if some
$a_p=1$, delete it (this can merge two neighbouring blocks of the same factor
into one, replacing $a_{p-1},a_{p+1}$ by their product, whose factor-length is
$\le$ the sum by the triangle inequality in $G_{i_{p-1}}$); either way the number
of blocks strictly drops and $\sum_p|a_p|_{S_{i_p}}$ does not increase. If no
$a_p=1$ and no two neighbours share a factor, the expression *is* the normal
form, which is unique (normal form theorem for free products), so
$\sum_j|h_j|=\sum_p|a_p|\le\sum_p|u_p|=n$. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (Chiswell).** With the hypotheses of Lemma 1,
$$\boxed{\;\frac{1}{\sigma_G(x)}\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sigma_{G_i}(x)}\;-\;(k-1)\;}$$
as formal power series in $\mathbb Z[[x]]$.

*Proof.* Put $a_i(x)=\sigma_{G_i}(x)-1$ (the nontrivial elements of $G_i$ by
length; $a_i(0)=0$). By Lemma 1 and uniqueness of normal forms, $G$ is in
length-preserving bijection with the set of alternating tuples
$(h_1,\dots,h_\ell)$, $\ell\ge0$. Let $f_i(x)$ be the generating function of the
nonempty alternating tuples whose **last** syllable lies in $G_i$, and
$T=\sum_i f_i$, so $\sigma_G=1+T$. Deleting the last syllable of such a tuple
gives an arbitrary alternating tuple that does not end in $G_i$, and this is a
weight-respecting bijection:
$$f_i \;=\; a_i\Bigl(1+T-f_i\Bigr)
\;\Longrightarrow\; f_i(1+a_i)=a_i(1+T)
\;\Longrightarrow\; f_i=(1+T)\,\frac{a_i}{\sigma_{G_i}} .$$
(The division is legitimate: $\sigma_{G_i}(0)=1$, so $\sigma_{G_i}$ is a unit in
$\mathbb Z[[x]]$.) Summing over $i$ and writing
$U:=\sum_i\bigl(1-1/\sigma_{G_i}\bigr)=k-\sum_i 1/\sigma_{G_i}\in x\mathbb Z[[x]]$:
$$T=(1+T)U\;\Longrightarrow\;T=\frac{U}{1-U}\;\Longrightarrow\;
\sigma_G=1+T=\frac1{1-U}=\frac{1}{\;\sum_i 1/\sigma_{G_i}-(k-1)\;}. \qquad\square$$

**Check against the corpus.** $F_2=\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z$, $\sigma_{\mathbb Z}=(1+x)/(1-x)$:
$1/\sigma=2\frac{1-x}{1+x}-1=\frac{1-3x}{1+x}$, so $\sigma=(1+x)/(1-3x)$ —
identically Theorem 2 of `TRACE_CORPUS_GROWTH_DENSITY`, whose $4\cdot3^{n-1}$ is
thereby the $k=2$ instance of a formula that also covers every other level.

## 3. Theorem 2: the closed form for every level $N$

Factor series in the alphabet $S_N$: $\sigma_{\mathbb Z/2}=1+x$,
$\sigma_{\mathbb Z/3}=1+2x$, $\sigma_{\mathbb Z}=(1+x)/(1-x)$, and
$k=\nu_2+\nu_3+r$ factors.

**Theorem 2.** For every $N\ge1$, with respect to $S_N$,
$$\boxed{\;\sigma_{\bar\Gamma_0(N)}(x)\;=\;\frac{(1+x)(1+2x)}{1-Dx-Ex^{2}}\;},
\qquad
D=\frac{\mu+2\nu_3-3}{3},\qquad E=\frac{2(\mu-\nu_3+3)}{3},$$
and $D,E$ are non-negative integers. Consequently the sphere sizes satisfy the
exact two-term recursion
$$c_n=D\,c_{n-1}+E\,c_{n-2}\quad(n\ge3),\qquad
c_0=1,\; c_1=D+3,\; c_2=D^2+3D+E+2 .$$

*Proof.* Theorem 1 gives
$$\frac1{\sigma}=\frac{\nu_2}{1+x}+\frac{\nu_3}{1+2x}+r\,\frac{1-x}{1+x}-c,
\qquad c:=\nu_2+\nu_3+r-1 .$$
Put over the common denominator $(1+x)(1+2x)$. The numerator is
$$(\nu_2+r-rx)(1+2x)+\nu_3(1+x)-c(1+3x+2x^2)
=(\nu_2+\nu_3+r-c)+(2\nu_2+\nu_3+r-3c)x-2(r+c)x^2 .$$
The constant term is $1$ by definition of $c$. Writing
$D:=-(2\nu_2+\nu_3+r-3c)=\nu_2+2\nu_3+2r-3$ and $E:=2(r+c)=2(\nu_2+\nu_3+2r-1)$
gives $1/\sigma=(1-Dx-Ex^2)/((1+x)(1+2x))$, which is the boxed formula.
Substituting (1) eliminates $\nu_2$ and $r$:
$$D=\nu_2+2\nu_3+2\Bigl(1+\tfrac\mu6-\tfrac{\nu_2}2-\tfrac{2\nu_3}3\Bigr)-3
=\frac{\mu+2\nu_3-3}{3},\qquad
E=2\Bigl(1+\frac{\mu-\nu_3}{3}\Bigr)=\frac{2(\mu-\nu_3+3)}{3}.$$
Integrality: $r\in\mathbb Z$ in (1) forces $\mu\equiv3\nu_2+4\nu_3\pmod 6$, hence
$\mu+2\nu_3\equiv3\nu_2\equiv0\pmod3$ and $\mu\equiv\nu_3\pmod 3$; both numerators
are divisible by $3$. Non-negativity is clear from the $\nu_2,\nu_3,r$ forms
($r\ge0$, and $D\ge0$ unless $\nu_2+2\nu_3+2r<3$, i.e. only $N=1,2$ where $D=0$).
The recursion is the statement that
$(1-Dx-Ex^2)\sigma=(1+x)(1+2x)=1+3x+2x^2$ has zero coefficients past $x^2$;
the three initial values are read off. $\square$

**Corollary 2.1 (rationality, with the denominator named).** The growth series
of $\bar\Gamma_0(N)$ in the alphabet $S_N$ is rational with denominator of
degree $2$ for **every** level. The corpus knew "virtually free $\Rightarrow$
rational" abstractly; the denominator is $1-Dx-Ex^2$ and nothing larger.

## 4. Theorem 3: the growth rate, exactly

**Theorem 3.** The exponential growth rate
$\lambda_N=\lim_n c_n^{1/n}$ of $\bar\Gamma_0(N)$ in $S_N$ is the largest root
of $\lambda^2-D\lambda-E=0$:
$$\lambda_N=\frac{D+\sqrt{D^2+4E}}{2}
=\frac{(\mu+2\nu_3-3)+\sqrt{(\mu+2\nu_3+9)^2-72\,\nu_3}}{6}.$$
In particular, **when $\nu_3=0$**,
$$\boxed{\;\lambda_N=\frac{\mu}{3}+1\;\in\;\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\;}$$
and when $\nu_3>0$, $\lambda_N$ is a quadratic irrational (or integer) of the
displayed discriminant. **$\lambda_N$ does not depend on $\nu_2$, on the genus,
or on the number of cusps except through $\mu$.**

*Proof.* $1-Dx-Ex^2$ has one positive root $\rho$ (its reverse polynomial
$\lambda^2-D\lambda-E$ has exactly one positive root by Descartes, as $E>0$), and
$\lambda_N=1/\rho$ is that root: $\sigma$ is a rational function whose poles are
the roots of $1-Dx-Ex^2$, the numerator $(1+x)(1+2x)$ has no positive root, and a
positive-coefficient series has its radius of convergence at a positive pole
(Pringsheim), so the growth rate is $1/\rho=\lambda_N$; the recursion of Theorem 2
then gives $c_n\sim C\lambda_N^n$ ~~exactly~~ **for every level except $N=1$
(see the correction below).**
For the discriminant, $9(D^2+4E)=(\mu+2\nu_3-3)^2+24(\mu-\nu_3+3)
=\mu^2+4\nu_3^2+4\mu\nu_3+18\mu-36\nu_3+81=(\mu+2\nu_3+9)^2-72\nu_3$.
For $\nu_3=0$ this is $(\mu+9)^2$, so
$\lambda_N=\bigl[(\mu-3)+(\mu+9)\bigr]/6=(\mu+3)/3$; and $3\mid\mu$ there
(shown in the proof of Theorem 2), so $\lambda_N\in\mathbb Z$. $\square$

## 5. What this says about a growth exponent the corpus is quoting

`notes/THE_MACHINE.md` line 59 summarises the trace lane as

The exponent $3$ is **correct as stated and wrong as used.** Precisely:

1. It is exact for the *sub-corpus* $F_m$ in its own alphabet $\Sigma_m$ — that
   theorem stands untouched, and Theorem 1 above re-derives it.
2. It is **not** the density of the payload group. In the canonical alphabet
   $S_N$ the payload group $\bar\Gamma_0(N)$ has density $\log\lambda_N$ with
   $\lambda_N=\mu/3+1$ (for $\nu_3=0$), which is $3$ only at $\mu=6$, i.e. only
   at $N\in\{4,5\}$. At $N=12$ it is $9$; asymptotically
   $\lambda_N\sim\mu(N)/3$, unbounded, with
   $\mu(N)\gg N\log\log N$ along primorials. So "$\log 3$ bounds any encoding"
   is a **lower** bound at levels $N\le3$ and is *violated as an upper bound* at
   every level with $\mu>6$: an encoding of level-$N$ payloads needs
   $\log_2\lambda_N$ bits per letter, not $\log_2 3$.
3. This is the failure mode `CLAUDE.md` §"Corollary, learned the hard way"
   names exactly: **a constant measured (here: derived) at one scale hides its
   scaling.** $\log 3$ was derived at a fixed sub-object; the $N$-dependence
   was invisible, and the summary line dropped the sub-object and kept the
   number. The repair is the same shape as `HOLOGRAM.md` §7's: the constant is
   a function, $\log(\mu(N)/3+1)$, and it is now derived, not estimated.

No measurement was ever run for the exponent $3$ (the note is honest and exact),
so this is not an `exp27`-style fitted-constant failure. It is the *other*
failure the protocol warns about: an exact constant transported off its object.

## 6. Theorem 4 (Mirzakhani's lens): counting the closed orbits

Spheres count elements; the geometry is in the *closed* orbits. Let
$G=G_1*\cdots*G_k$ as above and let $M(x)$ be the $k\times k$ transfer matrix
$$M(x)_{ij}=\begin{cases} a_j(x)=\sigma_{G_j}(x)-1,& i\ne j\\ 0,& i=j.\end{cases}$$

**Theorem 4.** For $\ell\ge2$, the elements of $G$ that are conjugate to no
element of any factor, of cyclically-reduced syllable length $\ell$, fall into
conjugacy classes counted with the $|\cdot|_S$-grading by
$$\mathcal N_\ell(x)\;=\;\frac1\ell\sum_{d\mid\ell}\varphi(d)\;
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M(x^{d})^{\ell/d}\bigr).$$

*Proof.* (i) Every element is conjugate to a cyclically reduced one, and two
cyclically reduced elements of syllable length $\ge2$ are conjugate iff their
syllable sequences are cyclic permutations of one another (conjugacy theorem for
free products, Magnus–Karrass–Solitar Thm 4.6). So the classes in question are
the necklaces: orbits of the cyclic group $C_\ell$ acting by rotation on
cyclically-alternating tuples $(h_1,\dots,h_\ell)$, $i_j\ne i_{j+1}$ cyclically.
(ii) $\operatorname{tr}(M(x)^\ell)$ is by definition the $|\cdot|_S$-graded count
of such marked tuples (Lemma 1 makes the grading additive, so the matrix product
tracks total length); this is the "periodic points of period $\ell$" count of the
alternation dynamics. (iii) Burnside: a rotation of order $d$ fixes exactly the
tuples that are $d$-fold repetitions of a cyclically-alternating tuple of length
$\ell/d$, and repetition multiplies every length by $d$, i.e. substitutes
$x\mapsto x^d$; there are $\varphi(d)$ rotations of order $d$ in $C_\ell$.
Averaging over $C_\ell$ gives the formula. $\square$

**Example ($N=4$, $G=F_2$).** $k=2$, $a_1=a_2=2x/(1-x)=:a$, $M=\binom{0\;a}{a\;0}$,
so $\operatorname{tr}(M^m)=2a^m$ for even $m$ and $0$ for odd $m$ — alternation
between two factors forces even period, as it must. Then
$\mathcal N_2(x)=\tfrac12\bigl(2a(x)^2\bigr)=a(x)^2=4x^2/(1-x)^2$, whose $x^2$
coefficient is $4$: the classes $[AB],[AB^{-1}],[A^{-1}B],[A^{-1}B^{-1}]$, with
$BA\sim AB$ correctly identified. $\mathcal N_4(x)=\tfrac14(2a^4+ 0 + \varphi(2)\cdot
2a(x^2)^2)$ etc.

Since $\operatorname{tr}(M^\ell)$ grows like $\lambda_N^{\,\ell\cdot(\text{scale})}$,
Theorem 4 gives the closed-orbit count as $\lambda^n/n$ up to the divisor
correction — the group-theoretic shadow of the prime geodesic theorem, obtained
here by counting rather than by a trace formula.
