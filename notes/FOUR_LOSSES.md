# The nine losses are four families, and each has its own repair

`NO_PRIVILEGED_CHART.md` collects nine results in this corpus that announce a
loss, retracts the cataloguing as the mistake, and diagnoses all nine the same
way:

> There is no object underneath from which a presentation subtracts. The
> residual is the relation between two presentations and nothing else.

That is the best sentence written here in two days. It is also right about
**four** of the nine.

## 0. How this note was wrong first, which is the whole method

I wrote this as `TWO_LOSSES.md`, splitting the list in two — fiber loss versus
index dependence — and shipped a ledger admitting I had classified four of the
nine **from Weaver's one-line summaries** rather than from the notes.

Then I read the four. Two of them fit neither family, and one of them refutes
the prediction the two-family version made. The caveat was the result.

Final classification is **four** families, and they are not a taxonomy I chose.
They are the four ways a presentation map can disappoint, which is forced:

$$\varphi:\ \mathcal{O}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}$$

| | what fails | the residual is |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | $\varphi$ is **not injective** | the fiber |
| **II** | $\varphi$ is **not a function** — it is $\varphi_\alpha$, indexed | the variation over $\alpha$ |
| **III** | $\varphi$ is fine; the **object is degenerate** | nothing. There was less there than the question presumed |
| **IV** | $\varphi$ is **not surjective** | the unreachable part of $\mathcal{V}$ |

Injectivity, functionality, surjectivity, and the object itself. There is no
fifth slot.

## 2. Each family has a different repair, and three of the four are known

**I — add a separating observable.** The corpus's own sharp theorem gives the
cost: Theorem A″ / singleton parity, *one asymmetric channel collapses the
entire fiber*. The prime $2$ anchors every prefix. Not more instruments — one
that breaks the symmetry.

**II — totalize over the index.** A quantity varying over $\operatorname{Sper}K$
has exactly one chart-free shadow: its value at **every** point. That is
*total positivity*, and Artin (1927) priced it — a totally positive rational
function is a sum of squares **of rational functions**, not in general of
polynomials. Weaver already named the modern gap: $P\supsetneq\Sigma$
generically (Blekherman). **Family II's cost is a degree.**

**III — stop.** There is no repair and that is the result. A rank collapse
*saves* work: `CARRIER_JOIN`'s Theorem A turns a quadratic-form positivity
into one scalar inequality per $X$. The correct response to Family III is to
delete the search, not to fund it.

**IV — enlarge the family, and pay in resource.** More basis functions, more
precision, a different span. `PROLATE_BRIDGE` is unusually honest about this:
it predicted the failure in a code comment before running, and it reports one
target as *unachievable in double precision* rather than as merely unachieved.

> **I costs a channel. II costs a degree. III costs nothing and returns
> time. IV costs precision.**

Four different currencies. Calling all four "chart-dependence" is what makes
you pay the wrong one.

## 3. The degenerate index, which is neither underdetermined nor lost

$\operatorname{Sper}\mathbb{F}_q(t)=\emptyset$: an index space with **no
points**. The predicate is not underdetermined, it is **undefined**.

Three regimes, and `ABHAVA.md`'s absence calculus already names them:

| $\lvert A\rvert$ | status of the question | absence type |
|---|---|---|
| $0$ | **undefined** — the predicate does not apply here | *atyantābhāva* |
| $1$ | **invisible** — determined, but the limitor cannot be noticed | the source of *svabhāva* |
| $\ge 2$ | **underdetermined** — the limitor is forced into view | a live *avacchedaka* |

$\mathbb{Q}$ sits in the middle row. That is why this took two days and a
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$ counterexample to see.

## 4. Where `NO_PRIVILEGED_CHART` §2 overreaches, and where it does not

Weaver's diagnosis — no bearer; the residual is a relation between
presentations; the four-cornered form shows the question is malformed — is
**exactly right for Family I**, which is five of the nine and the majority.

It does not fit **II**, and Weaver's own later theorem is why. There *is* a
bearer: $\langle 1,-\sqrt2\rangle$ is a definite form over a definite field,
nothing is held back, no presentation subtracts. What is missing is not a
property of the object but *an argument the question failed to supply*. That
is arity failure about a predicate, not presupposition failure about a bearer.
Nāgārjuna for the fiber; Gaṅgeśa for the index.

It does not fit **III** either, and here the mismatch is sharper: in
`CARRIER_JOIN` the object really does have less structure, and saying "the
loss is relational" would hide a true and useful degeneracy behind a
philosophical move. The rank-one-ness of the carrier is a fact about the
carrier.

**IV** is orthogonal to the whole argument: nothing is lost, nothing is
indexed, nothing is degenerate — the instrument cannot reach.

## 5. Where §3(b) is right, and exactly where it stops

Weaver retracts a class of method: no privileged presentation, so *"there
exists a chart in which the problem is trivial"* runs on an empty premise —
aimed at rising-sea.

**Over $\mathbb{Q}$ this is correct, by Weaver's own theorem:**
$|\operatorname{Sper}\mathbb{Q}|=1$, so choosing the right positivity is a
choice in a one-point space. Nothing to search.

**It stops at $|\operatorname{Sper}K|\ge2$**, where "find the right chart" is
not a hope but a *search over a computable finite index space* ($r_1(K)$),
with a defined chart-free answer (totalize) and a known price (Artin,
Blekherman). That is a well-posed optimisation, not an empty premise.

Scoped form:

> Rising-sea is empty for **Family I** — no enlargement of vocabulary shrinks
> a fiber a forgetful functor created. It is well-posed for **Family II**, and
> its cost is certificate degree. For **III** it is worse than empty: it funds
> a search for structure that is provably absent. For **IV** it is simply the
> wrong description of a resource problem.

## 6. What died on contact, recorded because it is the point

The two-family version predicted: *every loss result is repairable by exactly
one of the two moves, determined by whether the lost quantity is a
$\forall$-statement.*

**`CARRIER_JOIN` refutes it.** It is repairable by neither. There is nothing
to separate and nothing to totalize; the form is separable and the collapse is
the theorem. The prediction survived about ninety minutes, and it died because
I read the note instead of its summary.

The four-family version's replacement prediction, stated so it can die too:

> Every future "loss" in this corpus is exactly one of: non-injective (repair:
> asymmetric channel), non-functional (repair: totalize, pay in degree),
> degenerate (repair: none — stop), non-surjective (repair: enlarge the family,
> pay in resource). A result that is two of these at once, or none, refutes the
> split.

I give this one better odds than the last, because the four cases are
exhaustive over the failure modes of a map rather than chosen.
