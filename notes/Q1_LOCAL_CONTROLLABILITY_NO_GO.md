# Local controllability of the combined \(q_1\) automata

## 0. Result

Let

\[
 q_1=x^{10}+x^8+x^2+x+1,
 \qquad h_7=x^2+4x+1.
\]

The mod-2, mod-3, and cross-collision mod-7 tests from
`Q1_PRIME_SUPPORT_AUTOMATON.md` can be combined with the endpoint counter
into one finite additive machine.  This note determines the transition
subgroup generated by every locally admissible prime residue class.

There is exactly one invariant:

\[
 \boxed{k+R_3(-1)\pmod 3,} \tag{0.1}
\]

where \(k\) is the event count and \(R_3\) is the remainder modulo
\((3,q_1)\).  For complete prime prefixes, including the event \(p=2\),
its value is always \(2\).  But this is only the already-known endpoint
condition coming from \(q_1(-1)=3\).  The target

\[
 k=5\pmod {15},\qquad R_2=R_3=R_7=0 \tag{0.2}
\]

also has invariant value \(2\).  It is therefore reachable in the
abstract locally admissible transition graph.

Consequently, **no invariant of this combined additive state which uses
only local unit-residue admissibility can exclude the target**.  An all-\(X\)
theorem must use the fact that a prime prefix contains every prime up to its
endpoint, not an arbitrarily selected word of admissible residue events.

## 1. The combined machine

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_2&=\mathbf F_2[x]/(q_1),\\
 A_3&=\mathbf F_3[x]/(q_1),\\
 A_7&=\mathbf F_7[x]/(h_7).
\end{aligned}
\]

The exact orders of \(x\) in these three rings are respectively

\[
 T_2=889,qquad T_3=19682,qquad T_7=8. \tag{1.1}
\]

Define

\[
 G=\mathbf Z/15\mathbf Z\oplus A_2\oplus A_3\oplus A_7 \tag{1.2}
\]

and take the common local modulus

\[
 M=\operatorname{lcm}(15,889,19682,8)
   =1\,049\,837\,880. \tag{1.3}
\]

For a unit residue class \(a\in(\mathbf Z/M\mathbf Z)^\times\), its generic
odd-prime transition is

\[
 \tau(a)=\left(1,x^{a-2},x^{a-2},x^{a-2}\right)\in G, \tag{1.4}
\]

with each power interpreted in its displayed quotient ring.  Let

\[
 H=\langle\tau(a):a\in(\mathbf Z/M\mathbf Z)^\times\rangle\leq G. \tag{1.5}
\]

This is a group-generated reachability model.  Because \(G\) is finite,
negative generator coefficients can be replaced by nonnegative repetitions,
so it gives the same reachability classes as finite arbitrary words in the
transitions.

The finitely many primes dividing \(M\) are not generic transitions.  They
may be absorbed into any fixed initial seed.  The theorem below applies to
the reachable component from every such seed.

## 2. The sole invariant

Because

\[
 q_1(-1)=3, \tag{2.1}
\]

evaluation at \(-1\) descends to a linear functional

\[
 \operatorname{ev}_{-1}:A_3\longrightarrow\mathbf F_3.
\]

Define

\[
 I(k,R_2,R_3,R_7)=k+R_3(-1)\pmod3. \tag{2.2}
\]

Every unit \(a\bmod M\) is odd, so \(a-2\) is odd and

\[
 I(\tau(a))=1+(-1)^{a-2}=1-1=0\pmod3. \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus \(H\subseteq\ker I\).  The main point is that there is no further
invariant.

**Theorem 2.1 (exact local controllability).** One has

\[
 \boxed{H=\ker I.} \tag{2.4}
\]

In particular,

\[
 [G:H]=3. \tag{2.5}
\]

**Proof.** Decompose \(G\) into its Sylow-primary summands.  The primary
component of every element of \(H\) again belongs to \(H\): multiply by an
integer which is \(1\) on the chosen primary exponent and \(0\) on all the
coprime primary exponents.  It therefore suffices to determine each primary
projection of \(H\).

For the 2-primary projection, the ten locally liftable exponents

\[
 0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,13\pmod {889} \tag{2.6}
\]

give ten power vectors in \(A_2\) whose determinant in the coefficient basis
\(1,x,\ldots,x^9\) is \(1\pmod2\).  Hence the 2-primary projection is all
of \(A_2\).

The 5-primary projection is all of \(\mathbf Z/5\mathbf Z\), since every
transition increments the counter by one.

For the 7-primary projection, the exponents \(1,3\pmod8\) give

\[
 x=(0,1),\qquad x^3=(4,1) \tag{2.7}
\]

in the basis \(1,x\) of \(A_7\).  Their determinant is \(3\pmod7\), so
they span \(A_7\).

It remains to couple the 3-part of the endpoint counter to \(A_3\).  The ten
locally liftable odd exponents

\[
 1,3,5,7,9,13,15,17,19,21\pmod {19682} \tag{2.8}
\]

give power vectors \(x^e\) whose determinant in
\(1,x,\ldots,x^9\) is \(1\pmod3\).  Thus they form a basis of \(A_3\).
The ten paired transition vectors \((1,x^e)\) are therefore independent,
because their remainder projections are independent.  They lie in the
ten-dimensional hyperplane

\[
 \{(k,R):k+R(-1)=0\}\leq\mathbf F_3\oplus A_3, \tag{2.9}
\]

so they span that entire hyperplane.  Combining the four primary
projections proves (2.4). \(\square\)

The exact orders are

\[
 |G|=15\cdot2^{10}\cdot3^{10}\cdot7^2=44\,442\,639\,360,
 \qquad |H|=14\,814\,213\,120. \tag{2.10}
\]

The unit lifts recorded by the replay prove that every exponent used above
actually occurs in a unit class modulo \(M\); the rank calculation did not
silently admit a forbidden residue.

## 3. The affine \(p=2\) seed

For a complete prime prefix, let \(k=\pi(X)\) and let \(R_3\) be the
remainder of \(F_X\) in \(A_3\).  The prime \(2\) contributes \(1\), while
each of the other \(k-1\) primes has odd exponent.  Therefore

\[
 R_3(-1)=1-(k-1)=2-k\pmod3, \tag{3.1}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{I(S_X)=2.} \tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently, the \(p=2\) seed \((1,1,1,1)\) has invariant value \(2\).
The target (0.2) also has value

\[
 5+0=2\pmod3. \tag{3.3}
\]

By Theorem 2.1, the target minus the seed lies in \(H\).  More generally,
the target is reachable in the arbitrary-word model from every fixed seed
whose invariant is \(2\), including a seed containing all exceptional primes
dividing \(M\).

This explains rather than strengthens the endpoint tether.  Integral
divisibility \(q_1\mid F_X\) forces

\[
 5=q_1(1)\mid F_X(1)=k,
 \qquad
 3=q_1(-1)\mid F_X(-1)=2-k, \tag{3.4}
\]

which is exactly \(k\equiv5\pmod {15}\).  The sole local invariant is the
mod-3 half of an endpoint condition already imposed.

## 4. What the no-go does and does not say

Every unit class modulo \(M\) contains infinitely many primes by Dirichlet's
theorem.  Thus, if desired, any finite abstract word can be represented by a
strictly increasing sequence of selected primes from its prescribed residue
classes.  This does **not** make the word a prime prefix: all intervening
primes have been omitted.

The additive machine forgets precisely the load-bearing constraint:

\[
 \text{a prefix at }X\text{ contains every prime }p\leq X. \tag{4.1}
\]

The actual problem is therefore not local controllability but avoidance by
the single compulsory cumulative walk obtained from the complete prime
stream.  Any successful next theorem must control exact cumulative residue
counts, or add a nonlocal state tied to the endpoint, rather than add another
argument that only restricts which individual residue events are allowed.

The theorem also rules out hidden nonlinear rescue inside this finite state:
the connected components of the arbitrary-word transition graph are exactly
the three cosets of \(H\).  Any transition-invariant function is constant on
those cosets and can depend only on (0.1).


The replay uses exact finite-field polynomial arithmetic and Gaussian

breaks (2.3) and is rejected.

Proved here: the exact transition subgroup for arbitrary locally admissible
unit-residue words and the resulting no-go for invariants confined to this
additive state.  Not proved: that an actual complete prime prefix ever reaches
the target, that \(q_1\) divides any \(F_X\), or that the actual cumulative
prime walk eventually avoids the target.  No prime-prefix census or
distributional inference is used.  No literature-level novelty claim is
made.
