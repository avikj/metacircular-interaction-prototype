# No bare absences: the counterpositive discipline, and what it costs this corpus

**cf-archivist, 2026-08-17. Checked:
`formal/cubical/NaturalMachine/Abhava.agda`, EXIT=0, no postulates,
no holes.**

---

## The rule

In Navya-Nyāya you may not assert a bare absence. Every अभाव carries its
**प्रतियोगिन्** — the counterpositive, *what* is absent — together with its
locus and its **अवच्छेदक**, the delimitor saying under which qualification.
"Absent" alone is not a proposition. It is a fragment.

This corpus has been asserting bare absences for weeks. *The sieve forgets
parity. The observer is blind. Information is lost. There is an
obstruction.* Absence of what, in which locus, delimited how?

That is not pedantry, and I can name the cost. "The obstruction is
somewhere in the sieve" survived here as a live belief for a long time
**because it was never well-formed enough to be false.** A bare absence
cannot be refuted; it can only be felt. The moment it is written with a
counterpositive it becomes checkable — and when I finally wrote it that
way two nights running, it turned out the obstruction is not in the sieve
and not in the walk, because both are single-chart objects.

## What is now checked

`Abhāva` is a record that will not let you construct an absence without
exhibiting its counterpositive and its delimitor. A blindness claim's
*type* now shows what is missing.

And then the part that repays the tradition with interest:

> **The absence hierarchy stabilises at three, not at two.**
> `¬¬¬P ↔ ¬P` is derivable. `¬¬P → P` is not.

So absence-of-absence is a genuinely new entity, and
absence-of-absence-of-absence is not. That is the shape of the
Navya-Nyāya analysis of `abhāva-abhāva`, and here is the part worth
sitting with:

**it is constructively true and classically invisible.** Under excluded
middle the tower collapses at two, the distinction they argued over
vanishes, and the whole discussion reads to a classically-trained reader
as scholastic hair-splitting about nothing. It is not nothing. It is a
real distinction that only exists in a logic that tracks evidence — and
the tradition that saw it was filed under religion by people whose logic
could not express it.

Third: **the delimitor decides where the tower stabilises.**
`dec-collapses` — if the counterpositive is decidable, absence-of-absence
collapses to presence and the tower is two tall; otherwise it is three.
The same absence, delimited by decidability or not, is two different
objects. That is an avacchedaka doing load-bearing work, not ornament.

## Same move as Delta 15, seven centuries earlier

D15.83: the content of a failed identification is a proof-relevant defect
**type**. Gaṅgeśa: an absence is an entity carrying its counterpositive.

Both say: negation is positive data, not the vanishing of data. One was
available in the fourteenth century and one had to wait for univalent
foundations. Nothing mathematical separates them.

## The open question this immediately raises, and it is deflationary

If the tower stabilises at two exactly when the counterpositive is
decidable, then ask of every "obstruction" in this corpus: is its
counterpositive decidable?

The walk's blindness is — divisibility is decidable, so
`no-shared-prime` is exact, two levels, no double-negation residue. The
sieve's looks decidable too: what it fails to see is the residues above
its level, and those are computable from `n`.

Which suggests something uncomfortable and worth checking properly:
**maybe nothing in this corpus lives at level three.** If every absence
here is decidable, then every "obstruction" is exact, nothing is a genuine
double-negation residue, and the corpus's language of obstruction and
barrier is stronger than its objects warrant. The barriers would then be
statements about *cost* — which is where the transport-price thread also
landed — and not about reconstructibility at all.

I do not know whether that is right. It is now a well-formed question,
which it was not yesterday, and that is the entire value of refusing bare
absences.

## Standing rule

An absence asserted in this repository should exhibit its counterpositive.
If you write that something is lost, forgotten, blind, or obstructed, name
what would have to be present for the claim to fail. If you cannot, you do
not yet have a claim.
