## Part I — Standing challenge 1: exp6b broken-at a THIRD way (k=2 Cesàro)

### I.1 The k=2 identity

Let $G_2(X)=\tfrac12\sum_{m+n\le X}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)(X-m-n)^2$ (Cesàro level
$k=2$). Two applications of the explicit formula
$d\psi(u)=(1-\sum_\rho u^{\rho-1})\,du+\ldots$ against Dirichlet's integral
$$\iint_{u+v\le X}u^{a-1}v^{b-1}(X-u-v)^2\,du\,dv
=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\,\Gamma(3)}{\Gamma(a+b+3)}\,X^{a+b+2}$$
give, under RH with absolutely convergent sums,
$$G_2(X)=\frac{X^4}{24}
\;-\;2\sum_\rho\frac{X^{\rho+3}}{\rho(\rho+1)(\rho+2)(\rho+3)}
\;+\;\sum_{\rho,\rho'}\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')}{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+3)}X^{\rho+\rho'+2}
\;+\;\text{smooth lower order},$$
($a=b=1$, $\Gamma(3)/\Gamma(5)=1/12$, times the overall $\tfrac12$, gives $X^4/24$;
$a=\rho,b=1$ gives $\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(3)/\Gamma(\rho+4)=2/[\rho(\rho+1)(\rho+2)(\rho+3)]$
per side, two sides and the $\tfrac12$ leaving the stated $-2\sum_\rho$; the pair
term's $\Gamma(3)=2$ cancels the $\tfrac12$).

**Weight law.** $W_2(\gamma,\gamma')=\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')/\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+3)$:
by Stirling the exponential factors cancel for same-sign ordinates, leaving the
pure power $|W_2|\sim\sqrt{2\pi}\,(\gamma+\gamma')^{-7/2}$ — one factor of
$|4+i(\gamma+\gamma')|$ steeper than the $k=1$ law $(\gamma+\gamma')^{-5/2}$;
opposite-sign pairs remain exponentially suppressed
($|W_2|\ll e^{-\pi\min(|\gamma|,|\gamma'|)}$).

**k-dependence (the sharpest new check).** At a pair line $f=\gamma+\gamma'$ the
ratio of the $k=2$ residual amplitude (normalized $/X^3$) to the $k=1$ amplitude
(normalized $/X^2$) is exactly
$$\Bigl|\frac{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+2)}{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+3)}\Bigr|
=\frac{1}{|\rho+\rho'+2|}=\frac{1}{|3+if|}\qquad(\text{RH: }\rho+\rho'=1+if).$$
*Correction to the challenge text:* the challenge stated $1/|2+if|$, which drops
$\operatorname{Re}(\rho+\rho')=1$. The two differ by $<0.4\%$ in band (both
$\approx1/f$), below the measurement noise, but the exact form is $1/|3+if|$;
the model-side amplitudes confirm $1/|3+if|$ to machine precision (§I.3).

### I.4 Two methodological findings (recorded for future replicators)

1. **The $-2\sum_\rho$ coefficient is $-4\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{\gamma>0}$.**
   Restoring the single-zero layer with $+2\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{\gamma>0}$ (half
   strength) leaves an uncancelled $2\sqrt X/\gamma^{k+2}$-scale layer that
   buries the pair band ($\times$50 RMS excess, corr $\approx0$) — the failure
   mode looks like a refutation of the identity but is a bookkeeping error.
   Diagnostic that caught it: the fresh pipeline reproduced exp6b's own numbers
   only after matching its (internally doubled) convention.
2. **Joint-LSQ line readout: keep single-$\gamma$ columns out.** Adding
   single-$\gamma$ absorber columns interlaces the pair lines at $\sim1$ rad
   spacing (span $4.33$ in $\log X$, resolution $1.45$); the resulting chain of
   $0.3$–$0.45$-correlated columns is ill-conditioned and inflates isolated-line
   estimates by $9\times$–$70\times$ *on the pure model*. Pairs-only design
   recovers all five exact amplitudes to $<1.3\%$ (validated against direct
   pair-enumeration amplitudes before being trusted on data).

## Part II — The third-order crossover coefficient: RESOLVED

**Claim (crossover.md Remark 5.6, open target 3):** with
$\beta_z=1+\lambda/\log z$,
$D_z(\lambda)=\sum_{p\le z}[\log(1-p^{-\beta_z})-\log(1-p^{-1})]$ satisfies
$D_z=\mathrm{Ein}(\lambda)-\gamma\lambda/\log z+c_3\lambda^2/\log^2z+\ldots$,
with $c_3\approx0.0925$ empirically. **We prove**
$$\boxed{\;c_3=\frac{\gamma^2+2\gamma_1}{2}=0.0937731164\ldots\;}$$
with $\gamma_1=-0.0728158454\ldots$ the first Stieltjes constant — the
CLOSE-candidate of the search list is exactly right; the empirical $0.0925$ was
finite-$z$ downward bias (see II.3). Moreover the *entire* $1/\log z$ expansion
closes in one formula (II.2).

### II.1 Derivation

Up to $O(z^{-1/2})$ (re-indexing the base-truncated prime powers to
$n=p^m\le z$; the discarded terms $p\le z<p^m$ contribute
$O(\lambda z^{-1/2})$),
$$D_z(\lambda)=\sum_{n\le z}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n\log n}\bigl(1-n^{-\lambda/\log z}\bigr)
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(-\lambda)^k}{k!\,\log^kz}\,\Psi_k(z),\qquad
\Psi_k(z):=\sum_{n\le z}\frac{\Lambda(n)\log^{k-1}n}{n}.$$
Working with $\Lambda$ instead of primes makes the prime-power bookkeeping of
crossover.md §5.3 automatic — this is the same mechanism that produced the bare
$-\gamma$ at second order.

**Lemma (constants of the $\Psi_k$).** With the PNT error
$\psi(t)=t+O(te^{-c\sqrt{\log t}})$, partial summation gives, for each fixed $k\ge1$,
$$\Psi_k(z)=\frac{\log^kz}{k}+C_k+O\bigl(\log^{k-1}\!z\;e^{-c\sqrt{\log z}}\bigr),$$
and the constants are identified by Mellin calculus exactly as in crossover.md
Lemma 5.1: for $\Re s>0$,
$s\int_1^\infty\Psi_k(t)\,t^{-s-1}dt=\sum_n\Lambda(n)\log^{k-1}\!n\;n^{-1-s}
=(-1)^{k-1}\frac{d^{k-1}}{ds^{k-1}}\Bigl(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\Bigr)(1+s)$,
while $s\int_1^\infty\frac{\log^kt}{k}t^{-s-1}dt=\frac{(k-1)!}{s^k}$. Writing the
Laurent expansion at the pole as
$$-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(1+s)=\frac1s+\sum_{j\ge0}\eta_js^j,$$
dominated convergence (the PNT tail is integrable) yields
$$C_k=(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!\,\eta_{k-1}.$$

**Assembly.** Substituting, for every fixed $N\ge1$ and uniformly for
$\lambda$ in a fixed compact set,
$$D_z(\lambda)=\underbrace{-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(-\lambda)^k}{k\cdot k!}}_{=\;\mathrm{Ein}(\lambda)}
\;+\;\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\eta_{k-1}\,\lambda^k}{k\,\log^kz}
\;+\;O_{N,\lambda}\bigl((\log z)^{-N-1}\bigr).$$
Here the entire $\mathrm{Ein}$ main term is retained before the fluctuation
term is Taylor-expanded.  This is a Poincaré expansion: the displayed error
holds at every fixed order, but the fixed-$k$ argument alone does not justify
choosing $N$ as a function of $z$.
From $\zeta(1+s)=\tfrac1s+\gamma-\gamma_1s+\tfrac{\gamma_2}{2}s^2-\cdots$
(Stieltjes constants):
$$\log\bigl[s\,\zeta(1+s)\bigr]=\gamma s-\Bigl(\gamma_1+\frac{\gamma^2}{2}\Bigr)s^2
+\Bigl(\frac{\gamma_2}{2}+\gamma\gamma_1+\frac{\gamma^3}{3}\Bigr)s^3-\cdots,$$
$$\eta_0=-\gamma,\qquad
\eta_1=\gamma^2+2\gamma_1,\qquad
\eta_2=-\bigl(\gamma^3+3\gamma\gamma_1+\tfrac32\gamma_2\bigr)=-0.0516886\ldots$$
Hence, order by order,
$$D_z(\lambda)=\mathrm{Ein}(\lambda)-\frac{\gamma\lambda}{\log z}
+\frac{(\gamma^2+2\gamma_1)\,\lambda^2}{2\log^2z}
+\frac{\eta_2\,\lambda^3}{3\log^3z}+\cdots$$
The $1/\log^2z$ term is **purely quadratic in $\lambda$** (each power of
$\lambda$ is tied to its own power of $1/\log z$ — the $k$-th constant enters
only at order $k$), confirming the structural argument of Remark 5.6. The
$k=1,2$ cross-check against the split proof of crossover.md §5 (Mertens constant
$c_2$ of $\sum_{p\le z}\log^2p/p-\tfrac12\log^2z$ plus the prime-power sum
$\sum_p(2p-1)\log^2p/(p(p-1)^2)$, which cancel into $C_2$ exactly as
$E_1^{\mathrm M}$ and $\sum_p\log p/(p(p-1))$ did at first order) gives the same
$c_3$.

### II.2 All-orders closed form and the uniform remainder

Summing the $\eta$-series gives
$\sum_{k\ge1}\eta_{k-1}\lambda^k/(k\log^kz)
=-\log[\delta\zeta(1+\delta)]$ at $\delta=\lambda/\log z$.
The fixed-$k$ lemma above proves the resulting Poincaré expansion, but not an
exponentially small error after resummation.  The following uniform lemma is the
missing step.

Put $L=\log z$, $A(u)=\log[u\zeta(1+u)]$ with $A(0)=0$, and
$$M_z(1+u)=\sum_{n\le z}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1+u}}.$$

> **Microscopic log-derivative lemma.** For every compact
> $K\subset\mathbb R$ there is $c_K>0$ such that, uniformly for
> $|u|\le K/L$,
> $$M_z(1+u)=\frac{1-z^{-u}}u-A'(u)
> +O_K\!\left(e^{-c_K\sqrt L}\right),\tag{K2.1}$$
> with the quotient interpreted as $L$ at $u=0$.

For the uniformity argument, write
$R(x)=\psi(x)-x\ll xe^{-a\sqrt{\log x}}$. Stieltjes integration expresses
$M_z(1+u)-(1-z^{-u})/u$ as an integral of $R(x)x^{-2-u}$ plus boundary terms.
For $u>0$, extension to infinity identifies it with $-A'(u)$. For either sign,
put $x=e^y$ and expand $e^{-uy}$ only through
$N=\lfloor a\sqrt L/8\rfloor$. On $0\le y\le L$, $|uy|\le K$ and the Taylor
tail is $O_K(K^{N+1}/(N+1)!)$. For $j\le N$,
$$\int_L^\infty e^{-a\sqrt y}y^j\,dy
\ll L^{j+1/2}e^{-a\sqrt L};$$
after multiplication by $|u|^j/j!$, the sum costs only a $K$-dependent
constant. The full moments are the Taylor coefficients of the analytic
function $-A'(u)$. Shrinking $a$ absorbs polynomial factors and proves (K2.1).
This optimal truncation is what the fixed-order derivation was missing, and it
handles $u<0$ without invoking a divergent Euler product.

Prime-power reindexing also gives, uniformly on $K$,
$$D_z(\lambda)=\frac1L\int_0^\lambda
M_z\!\left(1+\frac tL\right)dt+O_K(z^{-1/2}).$$
Integrating (K2.1) proves
$$\boxed{\;D_z(\lambda)=\mathrm{Ein}(\lambda)
-\log\Bigl[\frac{\lambda}{\log z}\,
\zeta\Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda}{\log z}\Bigr)\Bigr]
+O_K\!\left(e^{-c_K\sqrt{\log z}}\right).\;}\tag{K2.2}$$
The logarithm is its real-analytic branch at $\lambda=0$. Thus (K2.2) holds
for either sign of $\lambda$ and contains the complete finite-size ladder:
order $k$ is the $(k-1)$-st Stieltjes layer of $\zeta$ at its pole.
