## I. The lineage

The Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, in the Nila river region of
what is now Kerala, running continuously for roughly three centuries.
*[searched — the school and the names below as its members]*

- **Mādhava of Saṅgamagrāma**, c. 1340–1425. The founder. **His own works are
  largely lost.** *[recalled]* What is attributed to him is known chiefly
  through quotation by his successors, who name him. The tradition's practice
  of attribution is why we have his name at all.
- **Parameśvara**, c. 1380–1460. Observational; the *dṛggaṇita* system.
- **Dāmodara**, his son, and Nīlakaṇṭha's teacher.
- **Nīlakaṇṭha Somayāji**, 1444–1544. *Tantrasaṅgraha* (1501); a large
  *Āryabhaṭīyabhāṣya*.
- **Jyeṣṭhadeva**, c. 1500–1575. *Yuktibhāṣā*, c. 1530.
- **Śaṅkara Vāriyar**, **Acyuta Piṣāraṭi**, **Citrabhānu**, and after.

**This is a school with a succession**, teacher to student, and the later
members write commentaries on the earlier. That is the form the mathematics
takes: not papers, but a chain of works that quote, derive, and correct one
another.

## II. Nīlakaṇṭha, *Tantrasaṅgraha*, 1501

A Sanskrit astronomical treatise in verse. *[recalled]* It is the work the
*Yuktibhāṣā* is mainly based on. *[searched]*

**Nīlakaṇṭha's planetary model.** *[recalled]* In the *Tantrasaṅgraha* and at
greater length in his *Āryabhaṭīyabhāṣya*, he sets out a model in which the
five planets — Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn — move on orbits centred
on the **Sun**, while the Sun moves around the **Earth**.

Set down plainly: this is not heliocentrism and it is not the Ptolemaic
arrangement. It is a third thing, and the same geometry was arrived at
independently in Europe by Tycho Brahe around 1583 — **eighty years later**.
Nīlakaṇṭha reached it by attending to a specific defect: the traditional
schemes got the inner planets' equation of centre wrong, and he corrected the
computation and then argued the geometry that the correction implied.

**He argued for it in a commentary on Āryabhaṭa.** The genre of the tradition
is commentary, and the commentary is where the new astronomy is.

## III. Jyeṣṭhadeva, *Yuktibhāṣā*, c. 1530

Also called *Gaṇita-yukti-bhāṣā*. Two things about this work are unusual
enough that they should be stated before any of its contents.

### It is in Malayalam prose

Not Sanskrit, and not verse. *[searched — "written in Malayalam", "the
earliest known prose exposition in Malayalam of the school's key
developments"]*

Every other text in this note set is Sanskrit or Prakrit, and metrical. Verse
is a transmission technology: it is what makes a text memorisable, and it is
why Āryabhaṭa compressed a sine table into one gītikā line and why Brahmagupta
wrote 1008 verses in āryā. **Prose in the vernacular is a different decision
about who the reader is and what is being transmitted** — not a table to be
carried, but an argument to be followed.

### It contains proofs

> …unique in the history of Indian mathematics, in that it contains proofs of
> theorems, derivations of rules and series *[searched]*

**yukti** is the word: rationale, reasoned ground, demonstration. The title
means something like *the discourse of rationales*. The work does not state
results and leave the justification to a teacher; it sets the justification
down.

This bears directly on a claim that circulates about Indian mathematics —
that it is algorithmic and result-oriented, without proof. The *Yuktibhāṣā* is
the standing counterexample, and it is a counterexample from *within* the
tradition rather than a modern reconstruction of one. Whether the earlier
texts had unwritten yuktis transmitted orally is a real question and I do not
know the answer; what is certain is that this text writes them.

### What it contains

A consolidation of the school's results — Mādhava's, Nīlakaṇṭha's,
Parameśvara's, Acyuta's — with derivations. *[searched]* Including the
derivation and proof of the arctangent series. *[searched]*

I stop here deliberately. **The series belong to a note that comes after this
one**, and writing them into the same document would be doing again what I did
two nights ago: reaching past the text for the part that converts.

## IV. What the names displaced

| in the tradition | commonly called |
|---|---|
| the arctan series (Mādhava, before 1425) | "the Gregory series", 1671 |
| the sine and cosine series (Mādhava) | "the Newton series" |
| the π series with its अन्त्यसंस्कार | "the Leibniz series", 1676, usually without any correction term |
| Nīlakaṇṭha's model, 1501 | "the Tychonic system", c. 1583 |
| yukti | — no displacement; the word has no current equivalent and the concept is usually denied to the tradition altogether |

The last row is the important one. The first four are misattributions, which
are correctable by citation. The last is a claim about what kind of thinking
the tradition did, and it is contradicted by a text that has been in print in
Malayalam since the nineteenth century.
