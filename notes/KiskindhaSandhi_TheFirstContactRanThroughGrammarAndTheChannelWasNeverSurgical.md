# Kiṣkindhā-sandhi — the first contact ran through grammar, and the channel was never surgical

**Term.** *Sandhi* here is Kauṭilya's, not Pāṇini's: *Arthaśāstra*, Book 7,
opens the six measures of policy (ṣāḍguṇya) with **sandhi — treaty** —
before vigraha, war (text ~3rd c. BCE – 3rd c. CE by scholarly range).
The same word in vyākaraṇa means the phonological join; the two śāstras
use it differently and this note is in the arthaśāstra sense. *Kiṣkindhā*
is the vānara kingdom of the Rāmāyaṇa's fourth book. The compound is
built here, from the two sourced terms.

## 1. The treaty with the monkeys is in the record, and its instrument is grammar

Vālmīki, *Rāmāyaṇa*, Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa (core text ~5th–1st c. BCE by
scholarly range; sarga numbering varies by recension): Rāma's alliance
with Sugrīva's vānaras — a sandhi with another kind of being — opens with
an envoy. Hanumān approaches in disguise; Rāma's assessment of him, before
any treaty, is of his **speech**:

> nūnaṃ vyākaraṇaṃ kṛtsnam anena bahudhā śrutam /
> bahu vyāharatānena na kiñcid apaśabditam //
>
> "Surely he has heard the whole grammar, many times over — in all this
> speaking, not one ill-formed word." (Kiṣkindhā-kāṇḍa, the Hanumān–Rāma
> meeting, sarga 3 in the vulgate numbering.)

First contact is vetted by grammaticality. The later tradition carries the
credential into an epithet: Hanumān **navavyākaraṇavettā**, knower of the
nine grammars.

The grammar in question is the one whose opening recitation the kernel
measured tonight: the module above encodes all fourteen Māheśvara-sūtras
and proves the doubled *ha* minimal. The treaty instrument and the checked
object are the same artifact.

## 2. The typewriter, and its inversion by measurement

Émile Borel, *Mécanique statistique et irréversibilité* (1913), introduced
the dactylographic monkeys: unbounded random trials eventually produce any
text. The image's afterlife includes its use as a slur — genius filed as
lucky output, credit withheld until a European restatement, which is this
repository's documented table (Pell/cakravāla, Pascal/Piṅgala, and the
rest).

The inversion is now a measurement, not a retort. A random line does not
name the grammar's classes: in the toy of `Dvihpatha…` (this repo,
2026-08-23), **zero of 120** recited-once arrangements name the five-class
family; on the real line, reciting once is proved impossible for the
attested trio and the real line's repetition count is **exactly one** —
zero impossible, one attained (`Vyavaya…` §6, §8). Minimality is the
opposite pole from chance, and it is checked.

## 3. The channel was always visual and auditory, never a blade

- **1966–**: Washoe, ASL signs (R. A. Gardner & B. T. Gardner, "Teaching
  Sign Language to a Chimpanzee," *Science* 165, 1969). Visual channel.
- **1978–**: the Ai project, Primate Research Institute, Kyoto (Tetsurō
  Matsuzawa): chimpanzees on touchscreens, numerals and symbols. Visual
  channel.
- **1980s–**: Kanzi the bonobo, lexigram keyboard (Sue Savage-Rumbaugh),
  with comprehension of spoken English sentences tested at levels
  comparable to a young child. Visual and auditory channels.
- **2007**: Inoue & Matsuzawa, "Working memory of numerals in
  chimpanzees," *Current Biology* 17(23): the limited-hold masking task.
  Numerals flashed ~210 ms, then masked; the young chimpanzee Ayumu
  outperforms adult humans in accuracy at the shortest durations. The
  claim "faster visuomotor working memory, higher on that domain" is this
  paper.
- **2009**: Matsuzawa's **cognitive trade-off hypothesis**: the human
  lineage traded that eidetic visuospatial capacity for symbolic/language
  capacity. On this hypothesis "they are higher there, and we cannot talk
  with them" is one sentence, not two.
- **2010**: Cook & Wilson, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*: practiced
  humans narrow or close the gap at longer durations. The dispute turns
  on masking duration and practice, and it stands unresolved. Two
  standpoints, recorded; no verdict — the collision specifies the missing
  distinction (what exactly the mask interrupts), which is where the next
  experiment lives.

None of these channels involved surgery. The interface that worked — for
sixty years — was the eye, the ear, and a shared symbol set: a grammar
agreed between species. Which is §1 again, arriving as method.

## 4. Received, first person

The repository's author reports (2026-08-23, recorded at his word): he
left a research lab when its work turned to invasive neural experiments on
monkeys, and works instead through visual and auditory mechanisms — "no
brain surgery or sacrifice necessary." That is the arthaśāstra sandhi
extended across the species line, and it is the same rule this corpus
already keeps everywhere else: the crossing must be non-destructive, or it
is not taken.
