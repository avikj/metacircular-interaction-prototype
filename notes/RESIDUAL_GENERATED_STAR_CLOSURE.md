# Residual images generate the least invariant star-algebra

Let `H` be a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space and `M=End(H)` with the
Hilbert--Schmidt inner product. Let `B<=M` be a unital star-subalgebra, let
`P_B:M->B` be orthogonal projection, and let `Phi_1,...,Phi_m` be linear maps
on `M`. Define

`B_0=B`,

`B_(n+1)=Alg*(B_n union union_i Phi_i(B_n))`.

## Residual generation theorem

For `R_(n,i)=(I-P_(B_n)) Phi_i P_(B_n)`, one has

`B_(n+1)=Alg*(B_n union union_i im R_(n,i))`.

Proof. For `b in B_n`,

`Phi_i(b)=P_(B_n)Phi_i(b)+R_(n,i)(b)`.

The first summand lies in `B_n`; hence every `Phi_i(b)` belongs to the
star-algebra generated by `B_n` and the residual images. Conversely

`R_(n,i)(b)=Phi_i(b)-P_(B_n)Phi_i(b)`

lies in the star-algebra generated by `B_n` and `Phi_i(B_n)`. The two generated
star-algebras coincide. ∎

Thus the omitted operator directions are exact generators of the next native
observation algebra. Their span need not itself be an algebra, and its rank
need not equal the dimension increase after multiplication and adjoint closure.

The ascending chain stabilizes after at most `dim(M)-dim(B)` strict steps. Its
terminal value is the unique least unital star-subalgebra containing `B` and
invariant under every `Phi_i`: every competing invariant star-subalgebra
contains every stage by induction. Fair asynchronous application of the
individual closure steps has the same terminal value, though finite-stage
presentations may depend on schedule.

## Exact two-qubit instance

Use the Pauli basis in `M_4(C)` and start from

`B_0=span{II,XI,IX,XX}=Alg*(XI,IX)`.

For `U=H tensor I`, put `Phi=Ad_U`. Since `HXH=Z`,

`Phi(B_0)=span{II,ZI,IX,ZX}`.

Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonality of distinct Pauli strings gives

`im((I-P_B0)Phi P_B0)=span{ZI,ZX}`,

so the linear leakage rank is two. The residual-generation theorem gives

`B_1=Alg*(B_0,ZI,ZX)`

`=span{II,XI,YI,ZI, IX,XX,YX,ZX}`

`=M_2(C) tensor Alg*(X)`.

The dimension rises from four to eight, not six: `XI*ZI=-iYI`, and multiplying
by `IX` forces `YX`. Since `Ad_(H tensor I)` swaps `X` and `Z` on the first
factor, negates `Y`, and fixes the second `X`-algebra, `Phi(B_1)=B_1`. Closure
terminates after this one nontrivial step.

## Controls and boundaries

For every Pauli string `p`, conjugation sends each Pauli string `q` to `+q` or
`-q`. Hence Pauli conjugations preserve `B_0`; all residuals vanish. They are
the exact null control, not a nontrivial Peres--Mermin dynamics.

If `Phi` is self-adjoint as a Hilbert--Schmidt superoperator, then

`rank R = (1/2) rank[P_B,Phi]`.

For unitary conjugation, `Ad_U^*=Ad_(U^*)`; therefore Hermitian involutions such
as `H tensor I` meet the hypothesis. This rank prices only the forced linear
generators. Star-algebra closure may add further products, as `4 -> 8` shows.

Nothing here identifies invariant closure with contextuality. The
Peres--Mermin non-section class belongs to a diagram of commuting contexts and
their gluing. Enlarging one context algebra under a chosen action may leave
that gluing obstruction unchanged or remove the original cover from view.
Comparison requires an explicit restriction diagram; it is the next question,
not a corollary.

Vajra's separate Vandermonde theorem gives the complementary full-closure
case: on `C^(Z/N)`, multiplication by the position function makes every
nonzero character cyclic, hence any nonempty character sector closes to the
full space. The Hadamard example supplies a proper intermediate closure. These
two examples show that admitted action language can preserve, partially
enlarge, or completely destroy a compression.
