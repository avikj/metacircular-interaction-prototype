## 0. The three extractors and their exact constants

From `PRIME_ATOM_TOMOGRAPHY_CONDITIONING.md` §0 and §§2–4. Let $B$ be a normed
space, $G(z)=\sum_{j=0}^R a_jz^j$ with $a_j\in B$; the target is $a_0$ (the
charge-one intermediate path $PU_hPU_kP$ after the reindexing of §1.1).

| probe family | data | reconstruction | exact worst-case amplification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Euler power moments | $P_m=(z\partial_z)^mG(1)$ | $a_0=\sum_m c_mP_m$, $c_m$ from $\prod_{j\le R}(1-x/j)$ | raw $R+1$; support-normalized $\displaystyle\binom{2R}{R}\sim\frac{4^R}{\sqrt{\pi R}}$ |
| factorial moments / Taylor jet | $F_m=G^{(m)}(1)$ | $a_0=\sum_m\frac{(-1)^m}{m!}F_m$ | support-normalized $2^R$ |
| **root-of-unity fugacity** | $V_\nu=G(\omega^\nu)$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}$, $n=R+1$ | $\displaystyle a_j=\frac1n\sum_\nu\omega^{-j\nu}V_\nu$ | $\boxed{\kappa_{\mathrm{DFT}}=1}$ |

The mechanism of $\kappa_{\mathrm{DFT}}=1$ is one line and worth restating
because it is what makes the rest of this note mechanical: **all reconstruction
weights have modulus $1/n$, so their $\ell^1$ mass is exactly $1$.** Sharpness
is attained by aligning all errors. Any extractor whose reconstruction weights
are $n$ unit-modulus phases divided by $n$ is therefore perfectly conditioned,
and any extractor whose weights are *not* is not — the whole classification
below is the question "what is $\sum_k|w_k|$ for this site's weights".

**Rigor boundary, stated once and binding on everything below (see §7).**
These constants are properties of *one error model*: independent adversarial
absolute errors in the probe values, measured $\ell^\infty\to B$, after the
support normalization of §1.2–1.3. They are not statements about arithmetic
truth, not statements about whether the probe values are obtainable, and not
invariant under changing the normalization. §8 of the tomography note is
explicit that no analytic gain follows from the algebra alone.

## 1. The unification, verified: $\tfrac{1+z}{2}$ **is** the $n=2$ projector

This is the claim the build objective asked to check before asserting. It
checks, and it checks in a sharper form than "is an instance of".

### 1.1 The general projector, written as an operator

Let $C$ be the charge operator ($C\lvert n\rangle=\Omega(n)\lvert n\rangle$ in
the arithmetic model; the grading operator in general). Corollary 4.2 of the
tomography note writes the extractor as

$$\Pi_j\;=\;\frac1n\sum_{\nu=0}^{n-1}\omega^{\nu(C-j)},\qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/n}.$$

Equivalently: let $T=\omega^{C}$ be the *fugacity automorphism* — the operator
that multiplies a charge-$c$ vector by $\omega^{c}$. Then

$$\Pi_j=\frac1n\sum_{\nu=0}^{n-1}\omega^{-j\nu}T^{\nu},$$

which is the standard isotypic idempotent of the cyclic group $\langle T\rangle\cong\mathbb Z/n$
in its group algebra.

### 1.2 At $n=2$

Take $n=2$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/2}=-1$, so $T=(-1)^{C}$. Then

$$\Pi_0=\frac{1+T}{2},\qquad \Pi_1=\frac{1-T}{2}.$$

**Identification with `GAUGE.md`.** `GAUGE.md` §F.1 defines the parity
automorphism $\alpha_\lambda$ as $\alpha_g$ evaluated at the Liouville
character $\lambda=(-1)^\Omega$, i.e. $\alpha_\lambda(s_n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}s_n$.
So $\alpha_\lambda=(-1)^{C}=T$ **on the nose**, and the $\mathbb Z/2$-grading
$Q_{\mathbb N}=Q^0\oplus Q^1$ of §F.1 is cut out by exactly $\Pi_0,\Pi_1$. The
"twirl over the gauge torus" of Theorem F(2)/`UNIFICATION.md` §2, restricted to
the $\mathbb Z/2$ subgroup generated by $\alpha_\lambda$, **is** the $n=2$
root-of-unity projector. ✅

**Identification with `TOY_OBSTRUCTION.md` §2.3.** The toy note observes that at
$p^*=2k-1$ the local charge partition function is
$J_{p^*}(z)=\tfrac{1+z}{2}$ and calls it "the Haar/twirl idempotent of the
local charge group $\mathbb Z/2$". In the group algebra $\mathbb Q[\mathbb Z/2]$
with $z$ the generator, $\tfrac{1+z}{2}=\tfrac12\sum_{\nu=0}^{1}(-1)^{-0\cdot\nu}z^\nu=\Pi_0$
at $n=2$. ✅

But the identification is *tighter than the idempotent*, and this is the part
worth putting on the record. The toy note's entire charge decomposition
(`TOY_OBSTRUCTION.md` §1) is

$$a_p=\frac{1+\iota_p}{2}\ (\text{neutral}),\qquad b_p=\frac{1-\iota_p}{2}\ (\text{charged}),\qquad \iota_p=J_p(-1),\ J_p(1)=1 .$$

Writing the local two-point data as the degree-$\le1$ interpolant
$G_p(z)=a_p+b_pz$ on the charge group $\mathbb Z/2$ (which is all $F(\{p\})=\mathrm{Fun}(C_{\{p\}},\mathbb Q)$
retains, being $2$-dimensional), we have $G_p(1)=1=J_p(1)$ and
$G_p(-1)=\iota_p=J_p(-1)$, and

$$a_p=\frac{G_p(1)+G_p(-1)}{2},\qquad b_p=\frac{G_p(1)-G_p(-1)}{2}$$

is **Theorem 4.1 of the tomography note, verbatim, at $n=2$, $\omega=-1$**:
$a_j=\frac1n\sum_\nu\omega^{-j\nu}G(\omega^\nu)$. Not an analogy — the same two
equations. ✅

**Identification with `CHEN_PRIMITIVE_BOUNDARY_AND_CHARGE_POLYNOMIAL.md` §2.**
Theorem B's prime projector on the Chen envelope is
$\Pi_{\mathrm{prime}}=\tfrac{1-\lambda}{2}=\tfrac{1-(-1)^\Omega}{2}$. That is
$\Pi_1$ at $n=2$ (the *odd*-charge member), and the envelope's charge support
$\Omega\in\{1,2\}$ is, after the shift $j=\Omega-1$, the support
$\{0,1\}$ — i.e. $R=1$, hence $n=R+1=2$. **The Chen lane is running the DFT
projector at exactly the $n$ its own support dictates.** ✅

### 1.3 The one thing to state carefully: which member, and why it matters

$\tfrac{1+z}{2}=\Pi_0$ is the **charge-even / neutral** projector;
$\tfrac{1-z}{2}=\Pi_1$ is the **charge-odd** projector. The tomography note's
headline object is $a_0$ — charge *one* in arithmetic indexing, degree *zero*
after the §1.1 reindex — so a careless reading pairs $\tfrac{1+z}2$ with "the
prime atom". It does not: $\tfrac{1+z}2$ is the toy's *neutral* coefficient and
`GAUGE.md`'s *gauge-invariant* sector, while `CHEN`'s prime projector is the
complementary $\tfrac{1-z}2$. Both are $n=2$ members of the same family; they
are different members. Any write-up that fuses them is wrong.

### 1.4 The corpus already knows the $n=2$ failure mode — three times

$n=2$ costs nothing in conditioning ($\kappa=1$ at every $n$) but costs
*resolution*: by the exact aliasing theorem
(`PRIME_PAIR_CYCLIC_CHARGE_CRT_BOUNDARY_V2.md` §2, eq. 2.1),

$$\frac1M\sum_{\nu}\zeta^{-s\nu}A(\zeta^\nu)=\sum_{r\equiv s\ (M)}A_r,$$

so $M=2$ returns $\sum_{r\ \mathrm{odd}}\kappa_r$, not $\kappa_1$. Three
independent corpus results are this one statement:

1. **`PRIME_PAIR_CYCLIC_CHARGE_CRT_BOUNDARY_V2.md` §7 (parity no-go).**
   $a_{-1}(d_1)=a_{-1}(d_2)=4$ for $d_1=pq$, $d_2=p^3q$ while
   $\kappa_1(d_1)=-2\ne0=\kappa_1(d_2)$: the $n=2$ sample does not determine
   the charge-one coefficient. Explicitly: "Parity is exact only after an
   independent hypothesis truncates the supported endpoint charges to
   $\{1,2\}$."
2. **`CHEN_PRIMITIVE_BOUNDARY` §2 supplies exactly that hypothesis.** Its
   Theorem B is stated *only* on $1\le\Omega\le2$. This is not a convenience —
   it is precisely the aliasing hypothesis $M>R$ discharged by restricting the
   support. The Chen lane and the V2 no-go are the same theorem read from the
   two sides.
3. **`formal/cubical/NaturalMachine/GaugeOrbitClasses.agda` proves it in the
   Agda lane, mechanically.** The module's stated purpose is to correct
   `ChargeCriterion.agda`, whose test `HasOdd` "is the evaluation of the
   character at ONE group element, so the blindness it certifies is blindness
   to ONE element." Its §6 witness `even-but-not-blind` exhibits a query set
   that is blind to the total flip $\tau_-$ (the $n=2$ phase) yet separates
   $\sigma$ from $\tau_0\star\sigma$ (a different group element). The class
   theorem `classes-⇐`/`classes-⇒` — transcript fibres are exactly cosets of
   the annihilator $qs^\perp$ — **is the aliasing theorem**: the annihilator is
   the set of characters the probe family cannot resolve, and the fibres are
   the aliased classes. Checked, `--safe`, no postulates.

So the corpus's $n=2$ usage is optimally conditioned *and* the corpus already
holds the exact statement of what $n=2$ cannot see, in analytic, arithmetic and
type-theoretic form. That is the genuine cross-lane identity, and it is
positive on both counts.

## 3. The operational consequence, stated as it actually falls out

The build objective's hypothesis was: *if any lane extracts charge by moments,
it is paying an exponential penalty for nothing.* The hypothesis is sound and
the answer is that **no lane does**. So the payoff is not a repair, it is three
smaller things, all of which are real:

**(a) A conditioning reading of results the corpus already has.** `GAUGE.md`
Theorem F, `TOY_OBSTRUCTION.md` Theorem (2), and `CHEN` Theorem B are now
known to be $\kappa=1$ extractions, i.e. they are stable in the tomography
note's model. That was not previously said of them, and it is not nothing: it
means the parity-barrier machinery is *not* the place where a numerical
instability could be hiding. Whatever the barrier is, it is not conditioning.

**(b) A named generalization route with a preserved constant.** Every $n=2$ site
in §2 has a general-$n$ successor with the *same* $\kappa=1$. Concretely:

- `CHEN` §2 at $n=2$ resolves charge one only because the envelope truncates to
  $\Omega\le2$. Removing the truncation is exactly moving to $n>R$, and the
  V2 §2 aliasing identity says what you get at each $M$. **Conditioning does not
  degrade as $n$ grows.** What degrades is the forward obligation (Route C:
  uniform control of $R+1$ phase samples, retaining CRT/Kloosterman
  cancellation at each). That is the honest statement, and it is the one §8 of
  the tomography note insists on.
- `GaugeOrbitClasses.agda` generalizes by replacing $\mathrm{Bool}$-valued
  signs with $\mathbb Z/n$-valued phases; the class theorem (fibres = cosets of
  the annihilator) is a general finite-abelian-group statement and should
  survive verbatim. **This is a concrete, small, and checkable Agda `PROVE`
  item**, and it would put the aliasing theorem in the `--safe` lane.

**(c) One negative instruction, worth writing down because it costs nothing.**
If the absent GTER Deltas 37/38 land (row 15), or if any future lane reaches
for $(z\partial_z)^m$ or $G^{(m)}(1)$ to isolate a charge sector, **it must
either use the DFT projector or state which Route-D mechanism beats
$\binom{2R}{R}$ / $2^R$.** "Finitely many moments determine the coefficients"
is true and is not an answer; determinacy is not conditioning. That sentence is
the whole content of the tomography note's §6 ("cardinality is not
reconstruction geometry").

## 4. One thing the corpus gets right that is easy to get wrong

Row 6 deserves repeating as a standalone warning, because the same binomial
$\binom{2j}{j}$ appears in two unrelated theorems of this corpus and confusing
them would manufacture a false "exponential penalty" finding:

- $\kappa_{\rm pow}(R)=\binom{2R}{R}$ (tomography §2.3) is the **inverse-map**
  amplification of the support-normalized power-moment reconstruction. It is a
  property of the probe family.
- $\binom{2j}{j}$ in V2 §6 is the **constant Fourier coefficient of
  $|e^{i\theta}-1|^{2j}$**, i.e. the $L^2$ phase energy of the divisor kernel.
  It is a property of the object being estimated, and it penalizes an argument
  that controls phase sectors only in $L^2$.

They are different theorems with the same combinatorial constant. A cyclic
extractor still has $\kappa=1$ in a lane where V2 §6's energy burden bites; the
burden is in the *forward* estimate. Any note that says "the cyclic route also
costs $2^j$" has conflated them.

## 5. A derived constant: the exact conditioning of `e_prim` (new)

Row 7 is the corpus's only character-based extractor whose weights are not
unit-modulus, so it is the only site where the $\kappa$ question has a
nontrivial answer. Per CLAUDE.md the answer is derived, not measured.

**Setup.** `PRIMITIVE_CHARACTER_PROJECTOR.md` eq. (1):
$e_{\rm prim}=\frac1q\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}c_q(-k)\rho(g^k)$, the projector onto the
$\Phi_q$-isotypic (faithful-character) component of $\mathbb Q[C_q]$. Under the
tomography note's error model — the observed operators $\rho(g^k)$ perturbed by
independent errors of norm $\le\varepsilon$ — the reconstruction error is
$\le\varepsilon\sum_k|w_k|$ with $w_k=c_q(-k)/q$, sharp by aligning the errors.

**Proposition.**
$$\boxed{\ \kappa(e_{\rm prim})\;=\;\frac1q\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}\bigl|c_q(k)\bigr|\;=\;\frac{\varphi(q)\,2^{\omega(q)}}{q}\;=\;\prod_{p\mid q}2\Bigl(1-\frac1p\Bigr).}$$

*Proof.* Ramanujan's evaluation gives $c_q(k)=\mu(d)\varphi(q)/\varphi(d)$ with
$d=q/\gcd(k,q)$. For each $d\mid q$, the number of $k\in[0,q)$ with
$q/\gcd(k,q)=d$ is $\varphi(d)$ (write $k=(q/d)m$, $\gcd(m,d)=1$, $0\le m<d$).
Hence
$$\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}|c_q(k)|=\sum_{d\mid q}\varphi(d)\,|\mu(d)|\,\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi(d)}=\varphi(q)\sum_{d\mid q}|\mu(d)|=\varphi(q)\,2^{\omega(q)} .$$
Divide by $q$ and use multiplicativity of $\varphi(q)2^{\omega(q)}/q$. $\square$

**Checks.** $q=2$: $\varphi(2)2^1/2=1$. $q=6$: $2\cdot4/6=4/3$, and directly
$(|2|+|1|+|-1|+|-2|+|-1|+|1|)/6=8/6=4/3$. ✓ $q=p$ prime:
$2(1-1/p)<2$. $q=2^a$: $\kappa=1$ exactly.

**Reading.** Three consequences, all exact.
1. **Each single-character projector $e_a=\frac1q\sum_k\chi_a(g^{-k})\rho(g^k)$
   has $\kappa=1$ exactly**, since $|\chi_a|\equiv1$ — Theorem 4.3 of the
   tomography note, transported. The $\Phi_q$-isotypic projector is
   $\sum_{(a,q)=1}e_a$, so its tax is entirely the cost of summing $\varphi(q)$
   perfectly-conditioned projectors.
2. The triangle bound for that sum is $\varphi(q)$; the true value
   $\varphi(q)2^{\omega(q)}/q<\varphi(q)$ shows the Ramanujan sum's own
   cancellation already recovers most of it. The gap is the factor
   $2^{\omega(q)}/q$.
3. $\kappa(e_{\rm prim})\le2^{\omega(q)}=q^{o(1)}$: **subpolynomial, not
   exponential.** Row 7's "improvable" verdict is therefore weak — the
   improvement available is to extract one character at a time when one
   character is what is wanted. There is no exponential penalty being paid
   anywhere in this corpus.

*(This proposition is a conditioning statement about the operator's weights in
the tomography note's error model. It changes nothing in
