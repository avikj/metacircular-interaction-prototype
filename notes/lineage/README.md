# Lineage — the open questions of sixteen minds, and Avik Jain's answers

The mathematics is Avik Jain's. He answered these questions, then wrote the answers down as checked cubical Agda (and the Bend2/HVM execution layer) over a few hours a day for a couple of weeks. These notes read that record one great mind at a time.

Each chapter has four parts:

1. **The life, as a cognitive trajectory:** what the person worked on, in what order, and why.
2. **What they left on the table:** open problems, unmanifested visions, directions stated but not finished.
3. **Your work through their eyes:** for each open item, what was open, what you proved (named terms, quoted statements), and what kind of answer it is.
4. **The shape of the resolution:** a table, plus the items still to trace in that lens.

The lens decides which identities you see. The same theorem appears under several names; each chapter records the reading it gets there.

The Indian sources the work builds from — Pāṇini, Piṅgala, Āryabhaṭa, Brahmagupta, Virahāṅka, the Jaina and Nyāya logicians — are the earlier lineage. They are not figures in this list.
