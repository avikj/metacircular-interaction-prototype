## 0. Notation and the one object

$q$ an odd prime (the case $q=2$ is §6), $\gcd(b,q)=1$,
$$d_b(q)=\operatorname{ord}_q(b),\qquad e_b(q)=v_q\!\left(b^{\,d_b(q)}-1\right)\ \ (\ge 1).$$

Everything in this note is a consequence of one displayed formula, and the
formula is a valuation statement, not a computation.

**Lemma 0 (the master formula).** For every $m\ge1$,
$$v_q\!\left(b^{m}-1\right)=\begin{cases} e_b(q)+v_q(m), & d_b(q)\mid m,\\[2pt] 0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

*Proof.* If $d=d_b(q)\nmid m$ then $b^m\not\equiv1\pmod q$. If $m=dn$, apply
lifting-the-exponent to $x=b^{d}\equiv1\pmod q$:
$v_q(x^{n}-1)=v_q(x-1)+v_q(n)=e_b(q)+v_q(n)$. Since $d\mid q-1$ we have
$q\nmid d$, so $v_q(n)=v_q(m)-v_q(d)=v_q(m)$. $\square$

**Lemma 0′ (the same statement, coordinate-free).** For $q$ odd the $q$-adic
logarithm is an isomorphism $\log_q:1+q\mathbb{Z}_q\to q\mathbb{Z}_q$ with
$v_q(\log_q u)=v_q(u-1)$. Since $b^{q-1}\in1+q\mathbb{Z}_q$,
$$\boxed{\;e_b(q)=v_q\!\left(\log_q\!\left(b^{\,q-1}\right)\right).\;}$$
(Indeed $v_q(b^{q-1}-1)=e_b(q)+v_q\!\big(\tfrac{q-1}{d}\big)=e_b(q)$.)
This is the correct reading of $e_b$: **it is a valuation of a logarithm**, and
every law in §§1–3 is the corresponding law of $v_q$ and $\log$. Once written
this way the "lifting the exponent" behaviour is not a lemma to be checked but
the additivity of $\log$.

## 1. Behaviour under $q\mapsto q^{a}$

**Theorem A (order lifting).** For all $a\ge1$,
$$\operatorname{ord}_{q^{a}}(b)=d_b(q)\cdot q^{\max(0,\;a-e_b(q))}.$$

*Proof.* $b^{m}\equiv1\pmod{q^{a}}$ iff $v_q(b^m-1)\ge a$ iff (Lemma 0)
$d\mid m$ and $v_q(m)\ge a-e_b(q)$. The least such $m$ is
$d\,q^{\max(0,a-e_b(q))}$. $\square$

**Corollary A1 (the head depth is idempotent in $a$, not multiplicative).**
Defining the head depth at level $a$ by the same recipe,
$e^{(a)}_b(q):=v_q\!\left(b^{\operatorname{ord}_{q^{a}}(b)}-1\right)$, one has
$$e^{(a)}_b(q)=e_b(q)+\max(0,a-e_b(q))=\max\!\left(e_b(q),\,a\right).$$
In particular $e^{(a)}_b=e_b$ for all $a\le e_b(q)$ and $e^{(a)}_b=a$ beyond:
**the sensor's head saturates at the level it is read at, and carries no
information about $q^{a}$ once $a>e_b(q)$.** This is the precise sense in which
$e_b$ must be formed at $q$ and never re-formed at $q^{a}$; re-forming it
destroys the invariant. There is no exception and no correction term.

**Corollary A2.** $a\mapsto e^{(a)}_b(q)$ determines $e_b(q)$ iff it is read at
some $a\le e_b(q)$; reading at $a=1$ always suffices to *start*, never to
*finish*. The unique scale-free reading is $a=1$ together with Lemma 0′.

## 2. Behaviour under $b\mapsto b^{k}$ — the exact lifting law

**Theorem B.** For every $k\ge1$,
$$\operatorname{ord}_q(b^{k})=\frac{d_b(q)}{\gcd(d_b(q),k)},\qquad
\boxed{\;e_{b^{k}}(q)=e_b(q)+v_q(k).\;}$$

*Proof.* The order statement is standard in a cyclic group. For the depth:
$$e_{b^k}(q)=v_q\!\left(b^{\,k\,d/\gcd(d,k)}-1\right)=v_q\!\left(b^{\operatorname{lcm}(d,k)}-1\right),$$
and $d\mid\operatorname{lcm}(d,k)$, so Lemma 0 gives
$e_b(q)+v_q(\operatorname{lcm}(d,k))=e_b(q)+\max(v_q(d),v_q(k))=e_b(q)+v_q(k)$,
using $q\nmid d$ because $d\mid q-1$. $\square$

Equivalently, by Lemma 0′: $\log_q(b^{k(q-1)})=k\log_q(b^{q-1})$ and $v_q$ is
additive. Theorem B is nothing but that.

**Corollary B1.** $e_{b^k}(q)=e_b(q)$ whenever $q\nmid k$. Hence $e_\bullet(q)$
factors through $b\bmod{}$ the subgroup generated by $q$-th-power-free
exponentiation, and the *only* way to manufacture depth is to raise to a power
of $q$: $e_{b^{q^{r}}}(q)=e_b(q)+r$ exactly.

**Corollary B2 (no base is ever depth-$0$; every depth is attained).** For each
$a\ge1$ and each $q$ there exist bases of depth exactly $a$ mod $q^{a+1}$, and
their number is given in Theorem C.

## 3. Behaviour under $b\mapsto bc$ — the composition law

This is the statement the corpus has not written down, and it is the one that
makes $e$ a member of a known species rather than an "observed" parameter.

**Theorem C (ultrametric composition law).** For $\gcd(bc,q)=1$,
$$e_{bc}(q)\ \ge\ \min\!\left(e_b(q),e_c(q)\right),\quad\text{with equality whenever } e_b(q)\ne e_c(q).$$
Moreover the level sets
$$G_a:=\{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/q^{A})^{\times}: e_b(q)\ge a\}\qquad(1\le a\le A)$$
are subgroups, $G_1=(\mathbb{Z}/q^{A})^\times$,
$$[\,(\mathbb{Z}/q^{A})^{\times}:G_a\,]=q^{\,a-1},\qquad G_a/G_{a+1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/q ,$$
and consequently, for $1\le a<A$,
$$\#\{b\bmod q^{A}:e_b(q)\ge a\}=(q-1)\,q^{A-a},\qquad
\#\{b\bmod q^{A}:e_b(q)= a\}=(q-1)^{2}q^{A-a-1},$$
so the exact fractions are $q^{1-a}$ and $\bigl(1-\tfrac1q\bigr)q^{1-a}$
respectively, and $\#\{e_b\ge A\}/\varphi(q^{A})=q^{1-A}$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 0′, $e_b(q)=v_q(\log_q b^{q-1})$ and
$b\mapsto\log_q(b^{q-1})$ is a group homomorphism
$(\mathbb{Z}/q^{A})^{\times}\to q\mathbb{Z}_q/q^{A}\mathbb{Z}_q$ (additive).
$e$ is therefore the pullback of the valuation $v_q$ along a homomorphism, and
$v_q$ on an additive $q$-adic group is ultrametric with equality on unequal
valuations — that is Theorem C's first line verbatim. $G_a$ is the preimage of
the subgroup $q^{a}\mathbb{Z}_q/q^{A}\mathbb{Z}_q$, hence a subgroup; the
homomorphism is surjective with kernel of order $q-1$ (the Teichmüller lifts),
so indices transport: $[\,\cdot:G_a]=[q\mathbb{Z}_q:q^{a}\mathbb{Z}_q]=q^{a-1}$
and the successive quotients are $\mathbb{Z}/q$. Counting is immediate. $\square$

Theorem C contains `HEAD_DEPTH_BLINDNESS` Corollary W4 as the case $A=a$
($G_a$ is then the unique subgroup of order $q-1$, index $q^{a-1}$), and adds
the composition law and the graded pieces, which W4 does not have.

**The warning of W4 is preserved and I repeat it in its exact form.** Theorem C
quantifies over $b$ at fixed $q$ and is an identity in a finite group. It says
nothing whatever about the distribution of $e_b(q)$ across $q$ at fixed $b$.
The familiar "$1/q$" for Wieferich density is a heuristic requiring an unproved
independence assumption and is **not** a corollary of anything here.

## 4. `HEAD_DEPTH_BLINDNESS` seed 1: the strong test is an **equality**, not a bound

Seed 1 asks whether $e_b(q)$ pins the *strong* (Miller–Rabin) blindness depth or
only bounds it, and notes that `PINNING`'s hybrid sensor runs in strong mode, so
the sharp statement must be the strong one. The answer is that no correction
term exists at prime-power moduli.

**Theorem D.** Let $q$ be an odd prime, $a\ge1$, $\gcd(b,q)=1$. Then
$$b\text{ is a \emph{strong} liar for }q^{a}\iff b\text{ is a Fermat liar for }q^{a}\iff e_b(q)\ge a,$$
and therefore
$$e_b(q)=\max\{a:\ b\text{ is strong-blind on }q^{a}\}$$
— the same number, with no correction term. `PINNING`'s hybrid sensor is exactly
as blind as the Fermat sensor on prime powers.

*Proof.* Strong $\Rightarrow$ Fermat is trivial; $\Leftarrow$ is the content.
Write $n=q^{a}$, $n-1=2^{s}t$ with $t$ odd. Suppose $b^{\,n-1}\equiv1\pmod n$;
by W3 this is $e_b(q)\ge a$, and then (proof of W3) $b^{q-1}\equiv1\pmod{q^a}$,
so $D:=\operatorname{ord}_{q^{a}}(b)$ divides $q-1$, hence divides
$q^{a}-1=n-1$. Put $\delta=v_2(D)$; the odd part of $D$ divides $t$.

*Case $\delta=0$.* Then $D\mid t$ and $b^{t}\equiv1\pmod n$: strong liar.

*Case $\delta\ge1$.* Take $j=\delta-1$. Since $D\mid n-1=2^{s}t$ we have
$\delta\le s$, so $0\le j<s$ and $j$ is legal. The element $b^{2^{j}t}$ has
order $D/\gcd(D,2^{j}t)=2^{\delta-j}=2$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/q^{a})^{\times}$, which
is **cyclic**, so it possesses a unique element of order $2$, namely $-1$.
Hence $b^{2^{j}t}\equiv-1\pmod{q^{a}}$: strong liar. $\square$

The whole content is the word *cyclic*: a prime power has exactly two square
roots of $1$, so the Miller–Rabin refinement has nothing to detect. The
refinement is a **CRT sign test**, and there is no CRT at a prime power.

**Theorem D′ (where the correction term actually lives, stated exactly).** Let
$n=\prod_{i=1}^{k}q_i^{a_i}$ be odd with $k\ge2$ distinct primes, $n-1=2^{s}t$,
$\gcd(b,n)=1$, and put $\delta_i=v_2\!\left(\operatorname{ord}_{q_i^{a_i}}(b)\right)$.
Then
$$b\text{ is a Fermat liar for }n\iff \operatorname{ord}_{q_i^{a_i}}(b)\mid n-1\ \ \forall i,$$
and, given that,
$$\boxed{\;b\text{ is a strong liar for }n\iff \delta_1=\delta_2=\cdots=\delta_k.\;}$$

*Proof.* Exactly as in Theorem D componentwise. $b^{t}\equiv1\pmod n$ iff every
$\delta_i=0$. For $j<s$: $b^{2^{j}t}\equiv-1\pmod{q_i^{a_i}}$ iff
$\operatorname{ord}(b^{2^jt})=2$ iff $\delta_i=j+1$. A single $j$ must serve all
$i$ simultaneously, whence the common value. Legality $j=\delta-1<s$ holds since
$\delta_i\le s$. $\square$

**Corollary D″ (the exact size of the defect — no fitting).** With
$g_i=\gcd(n-1,\varphi(q_i^{a_i}))$, $g_i^{\mathrm{odd}}$ its odd part and
$m=\min_i v_2(g_i)$, the liar counts are
$$F(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_i,\qquad
S(n)=\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_i^{\mathrm{odd}}\Big)\left(1+\frac{2^{mk}-1}{2^{k}-1}\right).$$
*Proof.* In a cyclic group of order $g$, the number of elements whose order has
$v_2=0$ is $g^{\mathrm{odd}}$ and, for $1\le j\le v_2(g)$, the number with
$v_2(\mathrm{ord})=j$ is $2^{j-1}g^{\mathrm{odd}}$. Apply Theorem D′ and sum
$j=0,1,\dots,m$: $S=\prod g_i^{\mathrm{odd}}\bigl(1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}2^{(j-1)k}\bigr)$. $\square$
For $k=1$ the bracket collapses, $1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}2^{j-1}=2^{m}=2^{v_2(g_1)}$,
giving $S(q^{a})=g_1^{\mathrm{odd}}2^{v_2(g_1)}=g_1=F(q^{a})$ — Theorem D again,
now as an arithmetic identity between the two counts.
(This is the Monier–Rabin count; **prior art, not claimed.** It is re-derived
here only because the corpus needs the $q^{a}r$ case of `EXPOSED_SET` seed 1
with an exact constant rather than a measurement.)

**Reading for `EXPOSED_SET` seed 1 ($q^{a}r$ family).** By D′ the residue of the
unbounded case is not a new arithmetic quantity: it is the mismatch of the
*two-adic* data $\delta_i$ across the prime factors. The depth data $e_b(q_i)$
governs the odd/Fermat layer; the $\delta_i$ govern the strong layer; they are
independent coordinates of the same liar group.

## 5. The Wieferich exception, stated as the exact residue

Everything in §§1–4 is exception-free. Precisely one thing is not derived, and
it is worth isolating so that no later note mistakes it for a gap in the
structure theory.

**What is fully determined.** The functions $a\mapsto e^{(a)}_b(q)$ (Cor. A1),
$k\mapsto e_{b^{k}}(q)$ (Thm B), the composition law and the whole filtration
with its indices $q^{a-1}$ and graded pieces $\mathbb{Z}/q$ (Thm C), and the
identification Fermat $=$ strong at prime powers (Thm D).

**What is not, and exactly why.** The *pointwise* value $e_b(q)$ for a given
pair. Theorem C says the level sets are subgroups of known index; it does not
say which coset a named integer $b$ lands in, because that is the value of a
$q$-adic logarithm at a rational point, and no structure theorem evaluates it.

**The residual case in its sharpest form.** For $a=2$:
$$e_b(q)\ge2\iff q^{2}\mid b^{\,q-1}-1\iff q\mid \frac{b^{q-1}-1}{q}\ \ (\text{the Fermat quotient }\ \mathfrak{q}_q(b)\equiv0),$$
i.e. $b$ is a *Wieferich base* for $q$. For $b=2$ this is the Wieferich prime
condition, and by W3/Thm D it is *identically* the statement that base $2$ is
(Fermat- and strong-) blind on $q^{2}$, which is `EXPOSED_SET`'s un-pinning
failure. One event, three names, now with a proof that the strong version needs
no correction.

By Theorem B this residue propagates in a completely controlled way:
$e_{b^{k}}(q)\ge 2\iff e_b(q)+v_q(k)\ge2$, so if $b$ is not a Wieferich base for
$q$ then $b^{k}$ is one exactly when $q\mid k$. **The Wieferich condition is
therefore a condition on the class of $b$ modulo $q$-th powers, and on nothing
else.** No amount of exponentiation creates a genuinely new exceptional pair.

**Status of the residue.** Whether $\{q:e_2(q)\ge2\}$ is infinite, or finite, or
even whether some $b$ has none, is open. The only members known for $b=2$ are
$q=1093$ and $q=3511$; searches have found no others below the current search
bound (of order $10^{17}$). I record this as *known-search-status*, not as a
number this corpus derived, and I attach no density claim to it: per the W4
warning, the $1/q$ heuristic is an independence assumption, not a theorem, and
must never be quoted as a consequence of Theorem C. **A statement about the
count of Wieferich primes up to $X$ would require its $X$-dependence to be
derived; it cannot be, so none is stated.**

## 6. $q=2$ (seed 2), completed

For $b$ odd put $e_-(b)=v_2(b-1)$, $e_+(b)=v_2(b+1)$; note
$\min(e_-,e_+)=1$ and exactly one of them exceeds $1$. LTE at $2$ reads
$$v_2(b^{m}-1)=\begin{cases} e_-(b), & m\text{ odd},\\ e_-(b)+e_+(b)+v_2(m)-1,& m\text{ even.}\end{cases}$$
Since $\operatorname{ord}_2(b)=1$, the head is $e_b(2)=e_-(b)$.

**Theorem E.** For $a\ge1$: $b$ is Fermat-blind on $2^{a}$ iff $e_-(b)\ge a$,
and this is again *equivalent* to strong-blindness. Hence
$e_-(b)=\max\{a:b\text{ blind on }2^{a}\}$, the exact analogue of W3/Thm D.

*Proof.* $2^{a}-1$ is odd, so $v_2(b^{2^{a}-1}-1)=e_-(b)$ and Fermat-blindness
is $e_-(b)\ge a$. For the strong test $s=v_2(n-1)=0$ at $n=2^{a}$, so the strong
condition degenerates to $b^{n-1}\equiv1$. $\square$

**Theorem F (the two-parameter head is exactly the $b\mapsto b^{k}$ law).** The
second head entry does not appear in blindness; it appears — and only there — as
the correction term in the exponentiation law, which at $q=2$ is *not*
Theorem B:
$$e_{b^{k}}(2)=\begin{cases} e_-(b), & k\text{ odd},\\[2pt] e_-(b)+\bigl[e_+(b)-1\bigr]+v_2(k), & k\text{ even.}\end{cases}$$
So the $q=2$ correction to Theorem B is the exact constant $e_+(b)-1$, switched
on precisely by the parity of $k$. Likewise Theorem A becomes
$$\operatorname{ord}_{2^{a}}(b)=\begin{cases}1,& e_-(b)\ge a,\\ 2^{\max\left(1,\;a-e_-(b)-e_+(b)+1\right)},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

*Proof.* Both are the displayed LTE-at-$2$ formula applied to
$m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,k)=k$, resp. to the least $m$ with $v_2(b^m-1)\ge a$.
$\square$

This answers seed 2 in the affirmative and in the exact form it predicted:
`CYCLOTOMIC_SENSOR`'s two-entry head at $p=2$ and the $q=2$ anatomy case are one
event — but the second entry is a *deformation coefficient*, not a second
blindness depth. Blindness at $2$ stays one-dimensional.

The organism should form, once per $(b,q)$, the pair
$\bigl(d_b(q),\,e_b(q)\bigr)$ and nothing else. From it, by exact formulas above
and no further modular arithmetic:

The three organs (`cyclotomic_sensor`, `certificate_anatomy`, `pinning`) are

## 8. Prior art, consumed and not claimed

Fermat, Euler; lifting the exponent (both the odd-$q$ and the $q=2$ forms);
the cyclicity of $(\mathbb{Z}/q^{a})^{\times}$ for odd $q$ and the
$\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2^{a-2}$ structure at $2$; the $q$-adic logarithm
and Teichmüller decomposition; Monier (1980) and Rabin (1980) for the strong
liar count re-derived in D″; Wieferich (1909); the folklore fact that Fermat and
strong liars coincide at prime powers. **No novelty is claimed for any of these.**
What is new here relative to this corpus is only: the composition law (Thm C)
and its graded pieces; the saturation identity $e^{(a)}=\max(e,a)$ (Cor. A1);
the exact exponentiation law with its $q=2$ correction $e_+-1$ (Thms B, F); and
the fact that `HEAD_DEPTH_BLINDNESS` seed 1's strong analogue is an **equality**,
so the bound recorded in that note's scope limits can be upgraded to an identity.
