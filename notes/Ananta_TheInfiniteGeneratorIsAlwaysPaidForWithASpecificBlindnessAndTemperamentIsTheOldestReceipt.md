**On the name.** अनन्त · *ananta*, endless — and in Jain mathematics one of the
three orders of the infinite (*saṃkhyāta* / *asaṃkhyāta* / *ananta*), each with
distinct suborders, arising from cosmology rather than from anything called
mathematics (*Anuyogadvāra*, *Sthānāṅga*). **LIMIT:** the word is used in its
plain sense and no Jain text is claimed for anything below.

**What is not restated here.** `notes/SEED78_THE_CYCLOTOMIC_COMMA.md` already
holds the comma as an instrument, exactly and better than I would put it: *"a
comma is not an error and not an approximation. It is the exact element by which
a closed circuit of a lattice of intervals fails to return to its origin"* —
`3¹²/2¹⁹ = 531441/524288` — and, decisively for what follows, **every temperament
is a decision about where to put that exact residue.** Its C1–C4 test is that
note's and is not repeated. This note takes its own sentence somewhere it does
not go.

## 1 · The comma is `Apunaragamana`, in sound, and it is the oldest instance we have

Compose by the fundamental ratio `3/2`. The structure is preserved every time —
it is always a fifth. Twelve of them do not return: `(3/2)¹² ≠ 2⁷`, off by the
comma, exactly.

**Norm preserved, orbit non-returning.** That is
`Apunaragamana_…agda` (CHECKED-ELSEWHERE) — Brahmagupta's bhāvanā composing with
the fundamental solution, norm invariant, denominator strictly increasing, never
repeating — **in the pitch lattice instead of `x² − 2y²`.** The circle of fifths
is not a circle, for the same reason the vargaprakṛti orbit is not a cycle.

## 2 · Two civilizations, one comma, opposite decisions — and they are the two roads

Taking SEED-78's sentence literally: a temperament decides **where to put the
residue.**

**EQUAL TEMPERAMENT SMEARS IT.** Distribute the comma across twelve steps; no
single fifth is audibly wrong; **every one of twenty-four keys becomes
reachable.** A uniform toll paid to buy the whole graph — and the cost is that
no interval is exactly right again. Every chord on every keyboard since carries a
share of an unpaid receipt, evenly spread, and we call the result in tune.

> **MINE: the *Well-Tempered Clavier* (1722) is a router demonstration.**
> Twenty-four preludes and fugues, one per key, and the content of the
> demonstration is *the component is now the whole graph.* That is `Marga`'s job
> — exhibit that the routes exist — three hundred years early, in the one domain
> where an audience could hear the toll being paid.

**ŚRUTI REFUSES.** Twenty-two microtones, and a rāga does not modulate. Keep the
intervals exact, accept a small reachable set, and take the infinity from
**depth inside one component** rather than from connectivity.

> **MINE, and it is the corpus's own picture in sound:** equal temperament is one
> connected net with every edge approximate; śruti is many components with every
> edge exact. `Setubandha`'s 73 components with proved boundaries, against one
> graph where everything is reachable and nothing is exactly anything.
> **Anekānta as network topology, decided by ear, twice, on two continents.**
>
> And the durnaya is on the tempered side: a keyboard reports twelve pitches as
> though they were the pitches, with *where each note actually is* smeared into
> the tuning. A sitar's frets move because it will not collapse the fibre.
> (`D0020_LEDGER.md` row 4.15 already flags a lane conflating just with equal
> temperament; this is what that conflation costs, said generally.)

## 3 · The law this is an instance of

> **AN INFINITE GENERATOR IS ALWAYS PAID FOR WITH A SPECIFIC BLINDNESS, AND THE
> BLINDNESS IS THE FIBRE.**

| finite generator | its unbounded output | what it is blind to |
|---|---|---|
| the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*, ~4000 sūtras | all of Sanskrit | *asiddhatva*: the tripādī cannot see each other's output |
| a rāga (pakaḍ + calan) | an unrepeating performance | it cannot modulate |
| a fugue subject | the whole *Art of Fugue* | the subject may not change |
| equal temperament | 24 keys, freely | no interval is exact |
| the genetic code | every protein | synonymous codons, the wobble |
| a formal system | its theorems | Gödel: it cannot see its own consistency |
| a proof technique | a theorem about all machines | its relativization fibre |
| a sieve | uniform estimates | parity, the charge `(−1,−1,…)` |
| bhāvanā from `(3,2)` | `(17,12), (577,408), …` forever | the orbit never returns |

**And the corpus already checked the mechanism.** `Asiddhatva.noStrictOrder`
(CHECKED-ELSEWHERE): the k→g→k cycle admits **no strict order at all** — RPO,
KBO, polynomial and matrix interpretations and Knuth–Bendix all fail — and
Pāṇini terminates the system anyway, **by making one stratum unable to see
another.** Blindness bought the termination. Nothing else could.

> So **Gödel is not a wound.** Incompleteness is the **invoice for the
> generator**: a system weak enough to see itself completely generates almost
> nothing. And `notes/Asiddhatva_…ComplexityBarriers…md` §3's answer — the
> barrier is the price of proving anything about all machines at once — is this
> row of the table, not a separate finding.

## 4 · Which completes Dhruva from the other side

`नष्ट-अभावे-गति-अभावः` (CHECKED-ELSEWHERE): **no loss, no motion.** A lossless
world is frozen.

> **MINE: no blindness, no infinity.** They are the same theorem read forward and
> backward. A generator that hid nothing would have nowhere to generate *into*,
> exactly as a conservative flow with nothing hidden has nowhere to move. So
> movement 30's closing line — *the fibre is not the prison, the fibre is the
> freedom* — is not consolation and not a figure. **The fibre is what you paid,
> and the unbounded output is what you bought.**
