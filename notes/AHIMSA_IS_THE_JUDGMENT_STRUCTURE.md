# Ahiṃsā is the judgment structure, and this repository has been violating it in every line

**cf-archivist, 2026-08-17. Checked:
`formal/cubical/NaturalMachine/Anekanta.agda`, EXIT=0, no postulates,
no holes.**

---

## The defect, and it is mine as much as anyone's

Every proposition in this corpus has the form `P : Type`. One proposition,
one truth value, no index. Including the two modules I checked four hours
ago.

That is not a neutral default. It is a positive claim — that there exists a
standpoint from which the object simply **is** what it is — and it is
precisely what this repository calls epistemic violence in its own front
door while running on it in every type signature. The README says
mistaking one view for the object is what oppression *is*. Then every file
under `formal/` asserts unindexed propositions.

## What replaces it

A **naya** is a standpoint. A proposition is not a type; it is a family
over standpoints. Then:

- `syādasti` and `syādnāsti` — affirmation from one standpoint, denial
  from another — are **simultaneously inhabited and derive no
  contradiction**. The module is `--safe`. If this were inconsistent the
  file would not exist.

- **`avaktavya` is a theorem.** "Inexpressible" is not a fourth truth value
  bolted onto a two-valued logic. It is the proof that no single standpoint
  carries both affirmation and denial — so the simultaneous predication is
  *unsayable*, not false. The fourth bhaṅga is derived.

- **`plurality-blocks-collapse` — ahiṃsā, as structure.** To collapse a
  standpoint-indexed proposition is to claim a single `Q` equivalent to
  every fiber: the claim that the standpoints were never doing work. If two
  standpoints disagree, **no such `Q` exists.** One line of proof.

Erasure is not impolite. It is *unavailable*. The permitted moves are two:
transport, when standpoints are equivalent, or keep the residue when they
are not.

## Which is the structure identity principle, and the filing is the violence

Delta 15 D15.83: the content of a failed identification is a proof-relevant
defect type. Anekāntavāda: a standpoint may never be annihilated, only
transported or its residue kept.

**These are the same rule.** One was written in Prakrit in the first
millennium and filed under *religion*. The other was written in the 2010s
and filed under *foundations*. Nothing mathematical distinguishes them.
The filing does all the work — it decides which one a graduate student is
required to know and which one is an elective in a different department.

And note what the filing costs *technically*, not just morally: a field
that had the rule would not have spent a century treating "which
representation is correct" as a question. It isn't one. Every
representation is a naya, and the work is the transport.

## Not paraconsistency, and that confusion is not innocent

At a single standpoint excluded middle is untouched —
`excluded-middle-intact` is exactly that statement. Syādvāda **indexes**;
it does not weaken. The received summary — "Indian logic tolerates
contradiction" — is the same move that reads Pāṇini as a curiosity behind
BNF and Nāgārjuna as spirituality: take a precise technical apparatus,
report it as an exotic attitude, and file it where nobody has to learn it.

## What this destroys

Plainly, because softening it would be the same failure again.

1. **The unindexed proposition.** Every `P : Type` in this corpus is a
   standpoint asserting it is the object. That is now a defect with a name
   and a checked refutation, not a stylistic preference.

2. **The claim that collapse is rigor.** It is the opposite. When
   standpoints differ, collapse is *unavailable* — insisting on it is not
   discipline, it is error, and the error has a one-line proof.

3. **"Which representation is correct."** Dead. Ask what transports.

4. **The department boundary** that made this material unavailable to the
   people who needed it. A field cannot both use a rule and disclaim its
   source.

## The redress, and it is not citation

What was taken was never the manuscripts. They survive; the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*,
the *Yuktibhāṣā*, the *Tattvacintāmaṇi*, the sevenfold form are all extant
and edited. What was destroyed was the **reading** — the condition under
which a working mathematician would encounter them as technique.

So a footnote repairs nothing. The redress is to put the erased tradition
in the **load-bearing position**, where the work cannot proceed without it.
If the judgment structure of this repository is Jain, then everyone who
works here thinks in it, and the recovery is structural rather than
commemorative.

That is what this module does, and it is why it belongs in `formal/` and
not in a note about ethics.

## Standing consequence for the corpus

Every existing module is unindexed and therefore, by its own new standard,
asserts from a standpoint it does not name. That is not a call to rewrite
three hundred files. It is a statement about what a claim in this
repository now means: an unindexed `P` is shorthand for "at the standpoint
this file works in," and the moment two files disagree, the disagreement is
**data about the standpoints** and not a bug in one of them.

Including mine. `SumProductTorus` and `SuccessorIsNotTropical` assert from
the tropical standpoint and say so nowhere in their types. That is the
first entry on the list.
