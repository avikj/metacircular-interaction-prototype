## 1. What Delta 15 left unfinished

`STRUCTURED_DEFECT_IS_THE_MACHINES_RESIDUAL.md` gave the machine's residual a
**type**: a reopening is an uninhabited identity type $\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{Str}}(e)$.
That answers *whether* the structure transports. It says nothing about *what the
damage is* when it does not — the type is empty and an empty type carries no
quantity.

Delta 18 T18.4 supplies exactly that missing half, as an identity:

$$K_t K_s - K_{t+s} \;=\; -\,P\,T_t\,Q\,T_s\,i,$$

with $i : S \to U$, $P : U \to S$, $Pi = \mathrm{id}$, $Q = 1 - iP$, and
$K_t = P T_t i$ the compressed evolution. The defect is not lost information in
the abstract. It is **leave the selected sector, evolve outside, return to the
observable sector** — and it is a computation, not a diagnosis.

## 2. What is checked

**§1, the ring form.** A `Compression` records a ring of operators, an evolution
$T$ that is a semigroup homomorphism into it (the *only* property of the dynamics
the theorem uses), and a retraction $Pi = 1$.

| statement | content |
|---|---|
| `iP-idem` | $iP$ is genuinely a projector, from $Pi=1$ alone |
| `split` | $T_s = (iP)T_s + Q T_s$ — the one algebraic step |
| `excursion-return` | **T18.4**, in the additive form, so the defect is a *named element* rather than a rearrangement |
| `defect-zero→semigroup` | **T18.5 forward**: no excursion returns ⟹ $K$ composes ⟹ discarding $Q$ is dynamically sufficient |
| `semigroup→defect-zero` | **T18.5 converse**: $K$ composes ⟹ every excursion-return term vanishes |

The converse is worth isolating. "$K$ is a semigroup" and "nothing that leaves can
come back" are not one implying the other — they are **the same condition**, and
proving both directions is what makes that a theorem rather than a slogan.

**§2, the set form.** T18.6 says the right observer equivalence is the kernel of
$O(x) = (P T_t x)_t$, not instantaneous agreement $Px = Py$. In this repository
that construction is **already checked**, under another name, as the centre of
`NaturalMachine.FutureBehavior`: with the evolution generated by actions, "time"
is a word and $O$ is `behavior`. So:

> `obsKernel→futureEq` and `futureEq→obsKernel` prove that Delta 18's
> observability kernel and this corpus's `FutureEq` are **the same relation** —
> by `funExt`, in both directions.

That identification is the point of the section. Delta 18's observer equivalence
and the corpus's minimal-machine quotient are not two constructions to be
related later; they are one, and now provably.

`separated→¬futureEq` closes it: a single separating word refutes future
equivalence even when two states agree *now*. That is the set-level face of
$P T_t Q \ne 0$ for some $t$, and it is why the discarded fibre can be
future-relevant.

## 3. What this settles about my own earlier work

`LEAKAGE_PAST_IDEMPOTENCE.md` Theorem C distinguished a **one-step** correction
from a **persistent** one and proved the first strictly undercounts the second
past two spectral sectors. `NATURAL_MACHINE_CPU_LOOP.md` §4 then measured the gap
exhaustively: of 144 affine actions on $\mathbb{Z}/12$, 36 have persistent price
strictly above one-step, maximal gap 5 at the successor map.

In Delta 18's vocabulary that distinction is not two prices. It is:

- **one-step** = the defect at a single $t$: $P T_1 Q \ne 0$;
- **persistent** = the defect at *some* future $t$: $\exists t,\; P T_t Q \ne 0$;
- and the persistent closure I computed as $\bigoplus_i E_i U$ is the
  **observability kernel** of T18.6.

So Theorem C is the statement that instantaneous sufficiency does not imply
dynamic sufficiency, which is exactly T18.5's content, and the 36 actions are
witnesses that the implication fails on the simplest carrier in the corpus. Three
notes of mine were circling one theorem; it now has a name and a proof.

## 4. Rigor boundary

**Checked:** every statement in §2, mechanically, `--safe`. The "no postulates,
no holes" claim is grep-verifiable — the only match in the file is the comment
asserting it.

**Not claimed:** novelty, of any kind. §1 is the Schur-complement/Feshbach

own substrate, and that §2 is proved equal to the construction the corpus
already runs on rather than asserted to resemble it.

**Not done, and these are Delta 18's own three targets:**

1. **Half-line.** Express the sum-gap operator defect in the
   $P T Q T P$ form and compare with the Hankel/Toeplitz boundary
   decomposition. If it matches, the operator-theoretic and reconstruction
   languages become literally the same formula.
2. **Charge.** Build the charge-space block operator and derive its Schur
   complement; T18.7 says the prime-pair block remembers all intermediate
   factorization charges, which is the canonical-sector instance of T18.4.
3. **Buchstab.** Embed the child-selected dynamics in the full Hecke/Bruhat–Tits
   adjacency process and test whether the least-prime stopped kernel is an
   excursion-return correction after eliminating the parent branches. Delta 18
   calls this falsifiable, and it is the only one of the three that could come
   back negative.

Delta 18's own instruction is that these come **before** asking whether the three
defect kernels share a transform. I have not asked.

**The correction Delta 18 forces on earlier language.** T18.2: the sum-gap
reflection sends $x \mapsto 1/x$, not $x \mapsto -x$. It is *inversion across the
unit boundary*, not the internal Weyl reflection — it exits the positive-cone
chart rather than acting within it. Any note in this corpus that treats the
boundary phenomenon as the Weyl $\mathbb{Z}/2$ is wrong on that point, and
`SINGULAR_SERIES_LOCAL_FACTOR_IS_A_ROOT_SUBSYSTEM_RANK.md` §5's open question —
whether the sign-reflection sector is a Weyl reflection of a larger system — now
has Delta 18 §17.4/T18.2 as evidence for **no**.
