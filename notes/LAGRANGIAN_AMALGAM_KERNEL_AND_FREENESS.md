## 0. Notation

`V = F₂^{2n}` with the symplectic form `⟨·,·⟩`. For `v ∈ V` write `P_v` for a
Hermitian Pauli representative (unique up to sign); `P_u P_v = (−1)^{⟨u,v⟩} P_v P_u`,
and distinct `P_v` are orthogonal in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, hence
linearly independent.

For an isotropic subspace `L ⊆ V`, put `Alg*(L) = span_C {P_v : v ∈ L}`. This is
well defined independently of the sign choices, and:

> **Lemma 0.** For `L` isotropic of dimension `m`, `Alg*(L)` is a commutative unital
> `*`-algebra of dimension `2^m`, and `Alg*(L) ≅ C[L] ≅ C[F₂^m]`.
>
> *Proof.* Pick a basis `u₁,…,u_m` of `L`. The `P_{u_i}` are commuting Hermitian
> involutions. The map `F₂^m → P_n`, `a ↦ ∏ P_{u_i}^{a_i}`, is a homomorphism (the
> factors commute) and lands in `±P_{Σ a_i u_i}`; it hits `±I` only at `a = 0`,
> where it is `+I`, because `u₁,…,u_m` are independent. So the image is elementary
> abelian of order `2^m`, its elements are `2^m` distinct Pauli strings, hence
> independent, and their span is `Alg*(L)`. ∎

(The twisting 2-cocycle of `Alg*(L)` is therefore a coboundary. This is exactly the
"choose a Hermitian section" step of `collab/messages/vajra/to_madhavi_pm_clifford_cycle.md`,
restricted to one isotropic context.)

## 1. The local sign is a theorem, not a datum

This is the whole of §3 in one proposition, so it goes first.

> **Proposition 1.** Let `L ≠ M` be Lagrangian subspaces of `F₂^{2n}` with
> `dim(L ∩ M) = n − 1`. Then for **every** `u ∈ L \ M` and **every** `v ∈ M \ L`,
>
> `⟨u,v⟩ = 1`,
>
> i.e. `P_u` and `P_v` anticommute. There is no configuration in which they commute.
>
> *Proof.* `L` is isotropic, so `⟨u, L ∩ M⟩ = 0`. Since `dim(L ∩ M) = n − 1 = dim M − 1`
> and `v ∈ M \ (L ∩ M)`, we have `M = (L ∩ M) ⊕ ⟨v⟩`. If also `⟨u,v⟩ = 0` then
> `⟨u, M⟩ = 0`, so `u ∈ M^⊥ = M` (Lagrangian), contradicting `u ∉ M`. ∎

Three lines, no case split. **The "local Clifford sign" that the open question
proposed to compose around a cycle is the constant function `−1` on its entire
domain of definition.** A quantity with no freedom cannot carry a class. §3 makes
this quantitative; §2 first supplies the object it is a statement about.

## 2. The kernel, for all `n` and all intersection dimensions

Fix Lagrangians `L, M ⊆ F₂^{2n}`, put `D = L ∩ M`, `k = dim D`. Write
`R = Alg*(L)`, `C = Alg*(M)`, `D_alg = Alg*(D)`, and

`B = Alg*(L ∪ M) = Alg*(L + M)`,

the `*`-algebra generated by the two contexts. Let

`U = R *_{D_alg} C`

be the algebraic amalgamated free product and `π : U → B` the canonical surjection.

> **Lemma 2.1 (the pairing of complements is perfect).** Choose complements
> `L = D ⊕ L′`, `M = D ⊕ M′`. Then `⟨·,·⟩ : L′ × M′ → F₂` is nondegenerate; in
> particular `dim L′ = dim M′ = n − k` and there are dual bases `u₁,…,u_{n−k}` of
> `L′` and `v₁,…,v_{n−k}` of `M′` with `⟨u_a, v_b⟩ = δ_{ab}`.
>
> *Proof.* If `u ∈ L′` has `⟨u, M′⟩ = 0`, then since `⟨u, D⟩ = 0` (both inside the
> isotropic `L`) we get `⟨u, M⟩ = 0`, so `u ∈ M^⊥ = M`, so `u ∈ L ∩ M = D`, so
> `u = 0`. Symmetrically on the right. ∎
>
> (Proposition 1 is the case `k = n − 1`.)

Presentation. By Lemma 0 and Lemma 2.1, over `D_alg` (which is central in `U`)

- `R = D_alg[x₁,…,x_{n−k}]`, `x_a* = x_a`, `x_a² = 1`, `[x_a, x_b] = 0`;
- `C = D_alg[z₁,…,z_{n−k}]`, same relations;
- `U` imposes **no** relation between the `x`'s and the `z`'s.

Equivalently `U ≅ C[D] ⊗ C[F₂^{n−k} * F₂^{n−k}]`, infinite-dimensional as soon as
`k < n`.

> **Theorem A (kernel classification).** `ker π` is the two-sided `*`-ideal
> generated by the `(n − k)²` elements
>
> `x_a z_b − (−1)^{δ_{ab}} z_b x_a`,  `1 ≤ a, b ≤ n − k`,
>
> that is: `n − k` **anticommutators** (`a = b`) and `(n−k)² − (n−k)` **commutators**
> (`a ≠ b`). Moreover
>
> `U / ker π ≅ B ≅ M_{2^{n−k}}(C) ⊗ C[F₂^k]`,  `dim_C B = 4^{n−k} · 2^k = 2^{2n−k}`,
>
> with `C[F₂^k] = Alg*(D)` the centre of `B`.

*Proof.* Let `I` be the ideal displayed. `I ⊆ ker π` because
`π(x_a)π(z_b) = (−1)^{⟨u_a,v_b⟩} π(z_b)π(x_a) = (−1)^{δ_{ab}} π(z_b)π(x_a)`.

*(i) Spanning.* Fix a basis `w₁,…,w_k` of `D`. Modulo `I` every generator pair
commutes up to sign, and `w_i² = x_a² = z_b² = 1` with the `w_i` central, so every
word is a scalar times a monomial `w^α x^β z^γ` with `α ∈ F₂^k`, `β, γ ∈ F₂^{n−k}`.
Hence `dim (U/I) ≤ 2^k · 2^{n−k} · 2^{n−k} = 2^{2n−k}`.

*(ii) The target.* `dim(L+M) = dim L + dim M − dim(L∩M) = 2n − k`, and
`L + M = D + L′ + M′` with the three dimensions `k + (n−k) + (n−k) = 2n−k` summing
to `dim(L+M)`, so the sum is direct. Now `B = span_C {P_v : v ∈ L+M}`: this span is
closed under multiplication (`P_u P_v = ±i^? P_{u+v}` and `L+M` is closed under `+`),
under `*`, and contains all generators, so it is `Alg*(L ∪ M)`. **Isotropy is not
needed here** — only that distinct Pauli strings are Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal,
hence linearly independent. So `dim_C B = |L+M| = 2^{2n−k}` exactly.

*(iii) Independence.* The monomial `w^α x^β z^γ` maps to `±P_{v(α,β,γ)}` with
`v(α,β,γ) = Σ α_i w_i + Σ β_a u_a + Σ γ_b v_b`, and `(α,β,γ) ↦ v(α,β,γ)` is a
bijection onto `L + M` by the direct-sum decomposition in (ii). So the `2^{2n−k}`
monomials have linearly independent images, hence are themselves independent in
`U/I`. With (i), `dim(U/I) = 2^{2n−k} = dim B`, and the induced surjection
`U/I → B` is an isomorphism. Therefore `ker π = I`.

*(iv) Structure.* First, the radical of `⟨·,·⟩` restricted to `W = L + M` is exactly
`D`: it contains `D`, since `D ⊆ L` gives `⟨D, L⟩ = 0` and `D ⊆ M` gives `⟨D, M⟩ = 0`;
conversely if `v = d + Σβ_a u_a + Σγ_b v_b` lies in the radical then `⟨v, v_b⟩ = β_b = 0`
and `⟨v, u_a⟩ = γ_a = 0` (all other pairings vanish because `d, v_b ∈ M` and
`u_a, d ∈ L`), so `v = d ∈ D`.

Now split. `Alg*(D) ≅ C[F₂^k]` by Lemma 0. `Alg*(L′ ⊕ M′)` is generated by
`x₁,…,x_{n−k}, z₁,…,z_{n−k}`: Hermitian involutions with `x_a z_a = −z_a x_a` and all
other pairs commuting — that is precisely the Pauli algebra on `n−k` qubits, i.e.
`M_{2^{n−k}}(C)`, of dimension `4^{n−k}`. The two factors commute elementwise
(`⟨D, L′⟩ = ⟨D, M′⟩ = 0`), so multiplication gives an algebra map
`Alg*(D) ⊗ Alg*(L′ ⊕ M′) → B`. It is surjective (the image contains all generators
and is an algebra) and the dimensions agree, `2^k · 4^{n−k} = 2^{2n−k} = dim B` by
(ii). Hence it is an isomorphism:

`B ≅ M_{2^{n−k}}(C) ⊗ C[F₂^k]`,

and by the radical computation `C[F₂^k] = Alg*(D)` is exactly the centre. ∎

> **Corollary A1 (classification by one integer).** The whole square
> `(D_alg → R, D_alg → C, → B)` together with the ideal `ker π` depends, up to
> `*`-isomorphism, **only on the pair `(k, n−k)`** — indeed the presentation above
> mentions nothing else. In particular, all two-context data with the same
> `dim(L ∩ M)` are isomorphic.

> **Corollary A2 (Mādhavī's theorem is the case `(n,k) = (2,1)`).** Then `n − k = 1`:
> one anticommutator, no commutators, `B ≅ M₂(C) ⊗ C[Z/2]`, `dim B = 8`. This is
> `notes/PAULI_TWO_CONTEXT_AMALGAM.md` verbatim, and the present proof is the same
> word-reduction argument carried out uniformly.

*Sanity check of the count.* `4^{n−k}2^k = 2^{2n−k}`: at `(2,1)`, `4·2 = 8 = 2³` ✓.
At `k = n` (i.e. `L = M`), the ideal is empty, `U = R = B`, `dim = 2^n` ✓.

## 3. Refutation of the composition conjecture

The question posed at the end of `notes/PAULI_TWO_CONTEXT_AMALGAM.md`, and again in
`collab/messages/shilpin/pm_star_closure_gluing_square.md`:

> "Determine whether the product of the resulting local Clifford signs around the
> unique parity cycle is exactly the generator of the existing `F₂` cokernel."

**REFUTED.** Three independent reasons, in increasing strength.

**(a) The signs are constant, so the cycle product is `+1`.**
Each Peres–Mermin row and column is a Lagrangian of `F₂⁴` (`n = 2`): two
independent commuting Paulis, e.g. row 1 `= span{XI, IX}` with `XX = XI + IX`, row 3
`= span{XZ, ZX}` with `YY = XZ + ZX`. Each row meets each column in exactly one
observable, so `k = 1` at all nine cells. By Corollary A1 all nine local amalgams
are isomorphic, and by Proposition 1 the local sign is `ε_{ij} = −1` at every cell.
The incidence graph of the cover — rows and columns as vertices, one edge per cell —
is `K_{3,3}`, which is **bipartite**, so every cycle has even length. Therefore for
every cycle `γ`,

`∏_{e ∈ γ} ε_e = (−1)^{|γ|} = +1`,

while the PM cokernel class is the nonzero element of `F₂` (`notes/PM_SECTION_VS_COCYCLE.md`,
`formal/cubical/NaturalMachine/PauliWeyl.agda`, `formal/cubical/PMNoSection.agda`).
`+1 ≠ −1`. There is no cycle, "unique parity" or otherwise, on which the two agree.

**(b) The local sign has no freedom at all.** Proposition 1 shows `ε = −1` is forced
by symplectic geometry for *every* pair of distinct Lagrangians meeting in a
hyperplane, in *every* `F₂^{2n}`. It is not a datum of the PM square; it is a
theorem about the ambient form. A constant function carries zero bits and can be a
representative of no nonzero class, under any assembly rule — not only "product
around a cycle".

**(c) The two live at different tensor orders.** The kernel records the *commutator
form* `⟨u,v⟩`, a **bilinear** (pairwise) datum. The PM class evaluates the *central
extension 2-cocycle* `β` on **triples** `u + v + w = 0` — each PM line has three
observables and the sign is the phase of a triple product. Pairwise data cannot
determine the class: two sections `s` and `s′ = (−1)^t s` have the same commutator
form and different local product phases (`β ↦ β + δt`). This is exactly the
diagnosis in `collab/messages/vajra/to_madhavi_pm_clifford_cycle.md`; the present
note upgrades it from "the local kernel is insufficient" to "the local kernel is a
function of `dim(L ∩ M)` alone", with a computed value on both sides.

**What survives.** The *least* diagram that can carry the class is one row against
**all three** columns at once (or the six-context cover with its 2-cells), because
the sign lives on a triple. The pairwise amalgam is provably too small. Formulating
the three-fold colimit and its kernel is **OPEN**; I did not attempt it.

**Falsifier I designed for my own claim, and its result.** If `ε` were non-constant
anywhere, (a) and (b) collapse. I looked for a pair of distinct Lagrangians in
`F₂^{2n}` meeting in a hyperplane with commuting complementary generators.
Proposition 1 shows there is none, and its proof exhibits the obstruction (`u` would
lie in `M^⊥ = M`). The claim survives its own control.

## 4. The kernel generators are the mixed free cumulants

Assigned frontier field: random matrix theory — universality, free probability,
moments and cumulants. The bridge here is exact, not decorative.

Equip `B` with the restriction `τ` of the normalized trace on `M_{2^n}(C)`:
`τ(P_v) = 0` for `v ≠ 0`, `τ(1) = 1`. Let `E : B → Alg*(D)` be the orthogonal
projection `P_v ↦ P_v` for `v ∈ D`, `↦ 0` otherwise; it is the `τ`-preserving
conditional expectation onto the centre (bimodularity: `P_d P_v P_{d′} = ±P_{v+d+d′}`
and `v ∈ D ⟺ v + d + d′ ∈ D`), and it is faithful.

> **Theorem D.** With `x_a = P_{u_a}`, `z_b = P_{v_b}` as in §2:
>
> 1. All mixed free cumulants of order `< 4` vanish: `κ₁(x_a) = κ₁(z_b) = 0` and
>    `κ₂(x_a, z_b) = τ(x_a z_b) = 0` (since `u_a + v_b ≠ 0`).
> 2. `κ₄(x_a, z_b, x_a, z_b) = τ(x_a z_b x_a z_b) = (−1)^{⟨u_a, v_b⟩} = (−1)^{δ_{ab}}`.
> 3. Hence the `(n−k)²` numbers `κ₄(x_a, z_b, x_a, z_b)` are **exactly the signs
>    appearing in the generators `x_a z_b − (−1)^{δ_{ab}} z_b x_a` of `ker π`**
>    (Theorem A). The lowest-order nonvanishing mixed free cumulants and the
>    generators of the amalgam kernel are the same finite list of `±1`'s.
> 4. Consequently `R` and `C` are **never** free with amalgamation over `Alg*(D)`
>    unless `L = M`.

**Which cumulants these are.** (1)–(3) are the **scalar** free cumulants with respect
to `τ`, not operator-valued (`D`-valued) ones. That is deliberate and it is enough:
the numbers being compared with the kernel signs are scalars. (4) is proved
separately below and does **not** go through cumulants at all, so no scalar fact is
being smuggled into an operator-valued conclusion.

*Proof.* (1) is immediate. For (2): the moment–cumulant formula gives
`τ(a₁a₂a₃a₄) = Σ_{π ∈ NC(4)} κ_π`. Since all `κ₁` vanish, only partitions without
singletons contribute; the non-crossing ones are `{1234}`, `{12}{34}`, `{14}{23}`
(`{13}{24}` is crossing, which is precisely why the *free* rather than classical
cumulant is the right invariant here). The two pair partitions contribute products
of `κ₂(x_a,z_b) = 0`. So `κ₄ = τ(x_a z_b x_a z_b)`, and
`x_a z_b x_a z_b = (−1)^{⟨u_a,v_b⟩} x_a² z_b² = (−1)^{δ_{ab}}·1`.
(3) is the comparison of (2) with Theorem A. (4): if `L ≠ M` then `n − k ≥ 1`, and
taking `a = b` gives an alternating word in `E`-centred elements with
`E(x_a z_a x_a z_a) = −1 ≠ 0`, which freeness with amalgamation forbids. ∎

Three remarks, each of which is the point.

- **The direction of the identification.** Freeness ⟺ vanishing of mixed free
  cumulants is Speicher's theorem (CITED, standard; confirmed at the level of
  search metadata, no full text read). What is added here is that in this family
  the *nonvanishing* cumulants are not merely an obstruction to freeness — they are
  a **presentation of the kernel**. The quantity free probability computes and the
  quantity operator-algebra amalgamation computes are literally the same numbers.

- **It re-derives §3(b) from the analytic side.** `κ₄ = ±1` always, never `0`, and
  the value is determined by `⟨u_a, v_b⟩`. A cumulant with no freedom detects no
  class. Two lenses, one verdict.

- **What separates the universal object from the physical one, numerically.** In
  `U` with its free-product state, `τ_U(x_a z_a x_a z_a) = 0`. In `B`,
  `τ_B(x_a z_a x_a z_a) = −1`. The difference `−1` is `κ₄`, is the kernel generator,
  and is the entire content of "the physical representation is a quotient of the
  universal amalgamation". §5 is about that number.

*Novelty*: not claimed. The computation is elementary and the ingredients are
standard; I found no source stating the identification and I opened none.

## 5. Where Boltzmann and Martin-Löf disagree, with the disagreement computed

The two assigned lenses, applied to the same object.

**Martin-Löf — what is a canonical element of this type?** The type is "unital
`*`-algebra generated by `R` and `C` over `D`". This type has an *initial* object,
and initiality is what canonicity means: `U = R *_{D} C`, unique up to unique
isomorphism, with a canonical normal form (reduced alternating words). `B` has no
universal property whatsoever; it is one quotient among the many that the ideal
lattice of `U` offers. On this lens the canonical answer is infinite-dimensional
and `τ_U(x z x z) = 0`.

**Boltzmann — count the microstates.** The microstates are the `2^{2n−k}` Pauli
strings of `L + M`. They are finite, they are the things that exist, and they span
`B`. On this lens `U` is not an object at all: it has no microstates, and its
"normal forms" are words nothing realises. The answer is `B`, and
`τ_B(x z x z) = −1`.

**The disagreement is exactly `−1`, and it has a name.** The two lenses assign
different values to one and the same computable observable, and the difference is
the mixed free cumulant `κ₄`, which is the generator of `ker π`. This is not a
metaphor and not a slogan: it is the number `−1`, derived twice.

**Which lens is right about what.** Boltzmann is right that the sign is forced —
§1's Proposition 1 is a counting-side statement (`u ∈ M^⊥ = M` is a dimension
count) and it is what kills the composition conjecture. Martin-Löf is right that
`B` is not canonical — which is why the conjecture was *attractive*: if `B` were
canonical, its defining relation would be a canonical local invariant and one could
hope to glue it. It is not; and the thing that *is* canonical (`U`) is the same for
all nine cells too, by Corollary A1. Both lenses converge on the refutation from
opposite ends, and this is the only site in my draw where they converge. The site
where they do not is `notes/PORT_IS_A_BASE_POINT.md` §4, and there they part with
the opposite sign: counting sees plenty where canonicity sees none.

## 8. Least-sure step, stated for a hostile reader

**Theorem A(i), the sorting step.** Everything in Theorem A rests on the claim that
modulo `I` every word in `w, x, z` reduces to a monomial `w^α x^β z^γ`, giving
`dim(U/I) ≤ 2^{2n−k}`. I justified this by "modulo `I` every generator pair commutes
up to sign, and the squares are `1`". That is a *rewriting* argument stated in one
sentence, and rewriting arguments are exactly where hidden cases live: I did not
exhibit a terminating confluent rewrite system, nor a diamond lemma / Bergman
verification of the overlap ambiguities (`x_a z_b z_c`, `x_a x_b z_c`, and the
`w`-central overlaps). I believe there is nothing there — every ambiguity resolves
because the signs are scalars and scalars commute — but a hostile reader should
attack precisely this, because if any overlap failed to resolve, `dim(U/I)` could
exceed `2^{2n−k}`, `U/I → B` would stop being injective, and Theorem A would degrade
to `I ⊆ ker π` with the reverse inclusion unproved. Note that the *lower* bound in
(iii) is safe regardless: the images really are independent.

Two smaller exposures, both already flagged in the text:

- **§3(a) reads "cycle" as a cycle of the `K_{3,3}` row/column incidence graph.** If
  the questioner meant a cycle in another complex — the 2-complex with the nine cells
  as 2-cells, say — the parity argument does not directly apply. §3(b) and §3(c) are
  immune to that reading, which is why three reasons were given and not one.
- **Theorem D(3) compares scalar cumulants with the kernel's signs.** They are equal
  as numbers. Anyone reading it as an operator-valued (`D`-valued) cumulant statement
  is reading more than is written, and D(4) is proved without cumulants for that
  reason.

A second, softer exposure: **§3(a) assumes the incidence graph is `K_{3,3}`**, i.e.
that "cycle" in the open question means a cycle of the row/column incidence graph.
