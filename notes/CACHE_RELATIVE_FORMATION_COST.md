# Marginal construction cost is a transition on retained mathematics

`PROBE_COST_DESCENT_NO_GO` showed that integer construction cost cannot be
attached to a residue-valued probe without choosing a lift. Fix now the least
positive lift when nonzero and the standard left-to-right binary construction
from the formed unit (1). Even after these choices, marginal cost is not a
static property of the probe or its decision tree.

## Fixed policy and cache state

For (n\ge1), read its binary expansion from left to right. Starting at (1),
each next bit performs a doubling and, when the bit is (1), an addition of
the retained unit. This produces a deterministic chain

\[
1=x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_m=n,                                \tag{1}
\]

where every step is either (x\mapsto x+x) or (x\mapsto x+1), and

\[
m=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor+\operatorname{popcount}(n)-1.  \tag{2}
\]

Let (K\subset\mathbb N_{>0}) be the retained cache, containing (1). The
declared cached-binary policy resumes from the latest member of (1) already in
(K), executes the remaining steps, and retains every result.

## Second descent no-go

**Theorem 1.** Marginal additions and newly retained intermediates are not
functions of the target integer—even with the lift and construction policy
fixed. They are functions of the pair ((K,n)).

**Proof.** For (n=13), the chain is

\[
1,2,3,6,12,13.                                         \tag{3}
\]

From (K_0=\{1\}), five additions are new. From
(K_1=\{1,2,3,6\}), only (6+6=12) and (12+1=13) are new. From a cache
containing (3), none is new. The target and semantic probe are identical in
all three cases, while both requested costs differ. ∎

Thus the Pareto vector `(queries, new additions, retained intermediates)` is
not a static annotation on a decision tree. A tree acts on a cache state and
changes the costs of later trees.

## Exact transition theorem

Write

\[
\Phi_n(K)=(K',T),                                      \tag{4}
\]

where (T) is the suffix of (1) executed from the latest cached prefix and
(K'=K\cup\{\text{results in }T\}).

**Theorem 2.** For fixed (n) and the declared policy:

1. (Phi_n(K)) is deterministic;
2. every step in (T) has both operands already available;
3. replaying (T) reconstructs (K') and certifies (n\in K');
4. if (K\subseteq L) and both caches contain prefixes of (1), then the
   marginal addition count from (L) is at most that from (K);
5. applying (Phi_n) twice is idempotent on cost: the second marginal count
   is zero.

**Proof.** The finite ordered chain (1) has a unique latest cached member.
Every suffix step consumes its predecessor and possibly (1), so induction
proves availability and replay. A larger prefix-closed cache can only move the
resume index forward, proving monotonicity. The first transition retains (n),
so the second resumes at the terminal member. ∎

## Observable formation event

The failed static Pareto vector forces a new state coordinate:

\[
\boxed{\text{formation state}=(\text{semantic objects},K,\text{provenance})}. \tag{5}
\]

This is not generic system architecture. Equation (3) is an exact
counterexample to omitting (K), while Theorem 2 supplies the smallest
executable repair for the chosen construction policy. Accepted construction
literally changes the cost of subsequent mathematics by enlarging (K).

In particular, forming center (13) also forms (2,3,6,12); a later request
for center (12) costs zero. This is future metric compression generated by
the arithmetic trace itself.

## Executable certificate and boundary

`machinery/cache_relative_formation.py` emits the deterministic trace,
enlarged cache, marginal additions, and new intermediates. Verification
replays operand availability and the transition. Tests cover (3), monotonicity,
reuse, the uncached formula (2), and malformed states.

Proved: Theorems 1–2 for this fixed algorithm and prefix-cache policy. No
optimal addition-chain claim is made.

Not proved: Pareto-optimality of a probe decision tree after cache state is
included; optimal eviction under bounded memory; reuse across non-prefix
addition chains; or a scalar exchange rate among queries, additions, and
retained integers.
