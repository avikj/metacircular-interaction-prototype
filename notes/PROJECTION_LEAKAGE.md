# Finite multiplier/window leakage

This note isolates an exact finite Fourier identity suggested by the
projection-leakage formalization in the source archive.  The identity is
elementary harmonic analysis.  No novelty is claimed.

## 1. Conventions

Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, written additively, of order $N$, with
character group $\widehat G$.  Use normalized counting measure:

$$
 \langle f,g\rangle=\mathbb E_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{g(x)},\qquad
 \widehat f(\chi)=\mathbb E_xf(x)\overline{\chi(x)},
$$

$$
 f(x)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}\widehat f(\chi)\chi(x).
$$

Define normalized convolution and correlation by

$$
 (a*b)(x)=\mathbb E_y a(x-y)b(y),\qquad
 (a\star b)(h)=\mathbb E_x\overline{a(x)}b(x+h).
$$

Then

$$
 \widehat{a*b}(\chi)=\widehat a(\chi)\widehat b(\chi),\qquad
 \widehat{a\star b}(\chi)=
 \overline{\widehat a(\chi)}\widehat b(\chi).
$$

For a multiplier symbol $p:\widehat G\to\mathbb C$, put

$$
 \kappa(r)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}p(\chi)\chi(r),\qquad
 \widehat{P_pf}(\chi)=p(\chi)\widehat f(\chi).
$$

Thus $P_pf=\kappa*f$.  For a window $w:G\to\mathbb C$, let
$M_wf(x)=w(x)f(x)$.  Our sign convention is

$$
 [M_w,P_p]=M_wP_p-P_pM_w.
$$

## 2. Exact leakage theorem

**Theorem 2.1 (finite multiplier/window commutator).** With the conventions
above,

$$
 \boxed{
 [M_w,P_p]f(x)
 =\mathbb E_y(w(x)-w(y))\kappa(x-y)f(y)
 =\mathbb E_r(w(x)-w(x-r))\kappa(r)f(x-r).
 }
$$

In the orthonormal character basis,

$$
 \boxed{
 \langle\chi,[M_w,P_p]\psi\rangle
 =(p(\psi)-p(\chi))\widehat w(\chi\psi^{-1}).
 }
$$

Consequently,

$$
 \boxed{
 \|[M_w,P_p]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\frac1{N^2}\sum_{r\in G}|\kappa(r)|^2
       \sum_{x\in G}|w(x)-w(x-r)|^2
 }
$$

and, equivalently,

$$
 \boxed{
 \|[M_w,P_p]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\sum_{\chi,\psi\in\widehat G}
 |p(\psi)-p(\chi)|^2
 |\widehat w(\chi\psi^{-1})|^2.
 }
$$

If $w=\mathbf1_A$, with $A+r=\{a+r:a\in A\}$, then

$$
 \boxed{
 \|[M_A,P_p]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\frac1{N^2}\sum_{r\in G}|\kappa(r)|^2
 |A\mathbin\triangle(A+r)|.
 }
$$

The commutator therefore vanishes exactly when $A+r=A$ for every
$r\in\operatorname{supp}\kappa$.  Equivalently, $A$ is a union of cosets of
the subgroup generated by $\operatorname{supp}\kappa$.  Dually, vanishing is
equivalent to

$$
 p(\eta\psi)=p(\psi)
$$

for every $\eta\in\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\mathbf1_A}$ and every
$\psi\in\widehat G$.

$$
 \langle\chi,M_w\psi\rangle=\widehat w(\chi\psi^{-1}),
$$

and multiplication on the right and left by the diagonal operator $p$ gives

$$
 N^{-1}(w(x)-w(y))\kappa(x-y).

$\mathbf1_A(x-r)=\mathbf1_{A+r}(x)$, and the inner sum is the cardinality of
the symmetric difference.  The vanishing criteria follow because every

## 3. Finite-sieve specialization

Take $G=\mathbb Z/W\mathbb Z$ and define

$$
 e_W(x)=\mathbf1_{(x,W)=1},\qquad
 \alpha_W=\mathbb E e_W=\frac{\varphi(W)}W,
$$

$$
 \mathfrak S_W(h)=\alpha_W^{-2}(e_W\star e_W)(h).
$$

For squarefree $W$, direct CRT counting gives

$$
 \mathfrak S_W(h)=\prod_{p\mid W}
 \begin{cases}
 p/(p-1),&p\mid h,\\
 p(p-2)/(p-1)^2,&p\nmid h.
 \end{cases}
$$

This is exactly the truncated Hardy--Littlewood singular series.  Center it
by

$$
 \kappa_W=\mathfrak S_W-1
$$

and let $P_Wf=\kappa_W*f$.  Its symbol is

$$
 \boxed{
 p_W(\chi)=\alpha_W^{-2}|\widehat e_W(\chi)|^2
 -\mathbf1_{\chi=1}\ge0.
 }
$$

Thus $P_W$ is positive and self-adjoint, but it is generally **not an
idempotent projection**.  For every $A\subset\mathbb Z/W\mathbb Z$,

$$
 \boxed{
 \|[M_A,P_W]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\frac1{W^2}\sum_{h\bmod W}
 |\mathfrak S_W(h)-1|^2|A\mathbin\triangle(A+h)|.
 }
$$

Replacing $p_W$ by its support projection would discard the Fourier
amplitudes that encode the singular series.

## 4. Exact small checks

### Sign and normalization on $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$

Let $p(0)=p(2)=1$, $p(1)=p(3)=0$, and $A=\{0,1\}$.  Then

$$
 \kappa=(2,0,2,0),\qquad
 Pf(x)=\frac12(f(x)+f(x-2)).
$$

In the order $(0,1,2,3)$, the only nonzero entries of $[M_A,P]$ are

$$
 C_{0,2}=C_{1,3}=\frac12,\qquad
 C_{2,0}=C_{3,1}=-\frac12.
$$

Hence $\|C\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=1$.  The displacement formula has only $r=2$,
where $|A\triangle(A+2)|=4$, and gives
$4|2|^2/4^2=1$.  The character-side formula also gives $1$.

### Arithmetic check at $W=6$

Here $e_6=\mathbf1_{\{1,5\}}$, $\alpha_6=1/3$, and

$$
 \mathfrak S_6=(3,0,3/2,0,3/2,0),\qquad
 \kappa_6=(2,-1,1/2,-1,1/2,-1).
$$

For $A=\{0,1,2\}$, the symmetric-difference sizes for shifts $0,\ldots,5$
are

$$
 (0,2,4,6,4,2).
$$

Therefore

$$
 \|[M_A,P_6]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\frac{0+2+1+6+1+2}{36}=\frac13.
$$

Direct matrix multiplication gives the same value.

## 5. Theorem/interpretation boundary

The following are exact:

1. the physical and spectral commutator formulas;
2. the Hilbert--Schmidt identities and coset vanishing criterion;
3. the finite-sieve singular-series specialization;
4. positivity of the centered sieve multiplier.

The theorem does **not** supply:

1. the linear Hardy--Littlewood main term—the Hilbert--Schmidt expression
   squares the centered local kernel;
2. a prime-pair asymptotic or any way around the sieve parity barrier;
3. a zeta-zero sector—the latter is global/archimedean explicit-formula data;
4. uniform control in a joint $W$, observation-window, and shift limit;
5. an RH-sensitive positivity criterion—Hilbert--Schmidt positivity here is
   automatic.

A genuine singular-series/zero-sector join would require a second operator
carrying the completed-zeta linear and quadratic sectors, together with a
controlled comparison or adelic factorization and uniform joint limits.
