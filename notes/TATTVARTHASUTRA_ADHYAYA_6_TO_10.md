## Adhyāya 6 — आस्रव, the influx

### 6.1
> **kāyavāṅmanaḥkarma yogaḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> The action of body, speech and mind is *yoga*.

*Yoga* here is not the discipline. It is **activity** — vibration of the
soul's space-points caused by its association with body, speech and mind.

### 6.2
> **sa āsravaḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> That is influx.

Activity itself is the influx. Karma flows in because the soul *acts*, not
because it acts badly. This is why 6.5's distinction is needed at all.

### 6.3–6.4
> **śubhaḥ puṇyasya** — auspicious [activity is the influx] of merit.
> **aśubhaḥ pāpasya** — inauspicious, of demerit. *[recalled]*

### 6.5 — the distinction that carries the chapter
> **sakaṣāyākaṣāyayoḥ sāmparāyikeryāpathayoḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> Of those with passions and those without, [the influx is] *sāmparāyika* and
> *īryāpatha* respectively.

- **sāmparāyika** — influx that *binds*, because passion (kaṣāya) is present.
- **īryāpatha** — literally "of the path of movement": the influx of one
  acting without passion. It touches and falls away; it does not bind.

So activity is not the problem. **Passion is.** A liberated-in-life being
still acts, still has influx, and accumulates nothing.

### 6.6 — the causes of sāmparāyika influx
> **indriyakaṣāyāvratakriyāḥ pañcacatuḥpañcapañcaviṃśatisaṃkhyāḥ pūrvasya
> bhedāḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> The senses, the passions, the non-vows and the activities — five, four,
> five and twenty-five in number — are the divisions of the former.

5 + 4 + 5 + 25 = **39**. The four kaṣāyas are **krodha, māna, māyā, lobha**
(anger, pride, deceit, greed). *[recalled]* The twenty-five *kriyā* I do not
list; I would get them wrong.

*(6.7 onward assign specific influxes to specific karmas — what conduct
draws which of the eight. I do not set these out. The chapter's method is to
make the mechanism specific, and my recall is not specific enough to
represent it.)*

## Adhyāya 7 — the vows

### 7.1
> **hiṃsānṛtasteyābrahmaparigrahebhyo viratir vratam** *[recalled]*
>
> Abstinence from injury, falsehood, theft, unchastity and possession is a
> vow.

Five. Stated as *viratiḥ* — turning away — not as a command.

### 7.2
> **deśasarvato'ṇumahatī** *[recalled]*
>
> Partially or wholly, [they are] the small and the great [vows].

**aṇuvrata** for the householder, **mahāvrata** for the mendicant. The same
five vows, differing in extent, not in kind.

### 7.11
> **maitrīpramodakāruṇyamādhyasthyāni sattvaguṇādhikakliśyamānāvineyeṣu**
> *[recalled]*
>
> Friendliness toward all beings, delight in the meritorious, compassion for
> the afflicted, and equanimity toward the ill-behaved.

### On हिंसा
The definition given in this chapter is **pramattayogāt prāṇavyaparopaṇaṃ
hiṃsā** *[recalled]* — injury is the severing of vitalities *through
passionate activity*. The qualification is the doctrine: it is the
*pramatta-yoga*, the careless or passion-driven act, that makes injury
injury. Ahiṃsā is accordingly not a rule about outcomes.

## Adhyāya 8 — बन्ध, bondage

### 8.1 — the five causes
> **mithyādarśanāviratipramādakaṣāyayogā bandhahetavaḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> Wrong view, non-abstinence, negligence, passion and activity are the causes
> of bondage.

Five, and they are shed in that order along the guṇasthāna ladder — wrong
view first, activity last. The last to go is the one that is not a fault.

### 8.2
> **sakaṣāyatvāj jīvaḥ karmaṇo yogyān pudgalān ādatte** *[recalled]*
>
> Because it has passions, the soul takes in matter fit for karma.

**Taking in matter.** Not incurring a debt, not acquiring a merit-score:
literally drawing in *pudgala*, which Adhyāya 5 established as one of the
substances. Karma is physical.

### 8.3 — THE FOUR ASPECTS
> **prakṛtisthityanubhāgapradeśās tadvidhayaḥ** *[searched]*
>
> Nature, duration, fruition and quantity are its kinds.

- **prakṛti** — what species of karma it is: what it will obscure or produce.
- **sthiti** — how long it stays bound before it must ripen.
- **anubhāga** (anubhava) — with what intensity it will fruit.
- **pradeśa** — how much karmic matter, counted in space-points.

Every binding has all four at once. Two of them — sthiti and anubhāga —
are determined by *kaṣāya*; the other two by *yoga*. *[recalled]* Which is
6.5 restated at the level of bondage: passion sets how long and how hard,
activity sets what kind and how much.

### 8.4 — THE EIGHT PRAKṚTIS
> **ādyo jñānadarśanāvaraṇavedanīyamohanīyāyurnāmagotrāntarāyāḥ** *[searched]*
>
> The first [prakṛti-bondage] is of eight kinds: knowledge-obscuring,
> perception-obscuring, feeling-producing, deluding, life-determining,
> body-making, status-determining, and obstructive.

The classical division of these eight into **ghātiyā** (destructive of the
soul's own qualities — jñānāvaraṇa, darśanāvaraṇa, mohanīya, antarāya) and
**aghātiyā** (the remaining four) is commentarial rather than in these
sūtras. *[recalled]* It is what makes 10.1 intelligible.

*(8.5 onward give the sub-divisions — 5, 9, 2, 28, 4, 42, 2, 5 — and then
maximum and minimum durations for each. I do not set the durations out.)*

## Adhyāya 9 — संवर and निर्जरा

### 9.1
> **āsravanirodhaḥ saṃvaraḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> Stopping the influx is saṃvara.

### 9.2 — by what means
> **sa guptisamitidharmānuprekṣāparīṣahajayacāritraiḥ** *[searched]*
>
> That [is achieved] by restraint, carefulness, virtue, contemplation,
> endurance of hardships, and conduct.

Six means, and the rest of the chapter unpacks each.

### 9.4–9.5 — gupti and samiti
> **gupti** — three: of body, speech and mind. *[recalled]*
> **samiti** — five: **īryā** (walking), **bhāṣā** (speaking), **eṣaṇā**
> (accepting alms), **ādānanikṣepaṇa** (taking up and setting down),
> **utsarga** (disposal). *[recalled]*

Gupti is *restraint from*; samiti is *care in*. One stops, the other
qualifies. Both are needed because a mendicant cannot cease acting.

### 9.6 — THE TEN DHARMAS
> **uttamakṣamāmārdavārjavaśaucasatyasaṃyamatapastyāgākiñcanyabrahmacaryāṇi
> dharmaḥ** *[searched]*
>
> Supreme forbearance, modesty, straightforwardness, purity, truthfulness,
> self-restraint, austerity, renunciation, non-possession, and celibacy —
> [these ten] are dharma.

Each carries **uttama**. The list is the *daśalakṣaṇadharma*.

### 9.7 — THE TWELVE अनुप्रेक्षा
> *[recalled — the twelve, in the order I have them]*
>
> **anitya** (impermanence), **aśaraṇa** (helplessness), **saṃsāra**,
> **ekatva** (solitariness), **anyatva** (otherness — the soul is other than
> the body), **aśuci** (impurity of the body), **āsrava**, **saṃvara**,
> **nirjarā**, **loka** (the shape of the world), **bodhidurlabha**
> (the rarity of enlightenment), **dharma** (the well-taught path).

Contemplations, repeated deliberately. Four of the twelve are the tattvas
themselves — āsrava, saṃvara, nirjarā and the world — which is to say the
metaphysics of Adhyāyas 5–8 is *itself* prescribed as a practice, not only
as an account.

### 9.9 — the परीषह
Twenty-two hardships to be borne without disturbance — hunger, thirst, cold,
heat, insect-bite, nakedness, and so on. *[recalled: the count 22 and the
first several. I will not list all twenty-two; I would supply wrong ones.]*

### 9.18 — the five conducts
> **sāmāyikachedopasthāpanāparihāraviśuddhisūkṣmasāmparāyayathākhyātam
> iti cāritram** *[recalled]*
>
> Equanimity, re-initiation, purity by exclusion, subtle passion, and the
> perfect — these are conduct.

An ordered ascent, ending in **yathākhyāta** — conduct "as declared", the
conduct of one in whom the deluding karma is gone.

### 9.19–9.20 — तपस्
> External six: **anaśana** (fasting), **avamaudarya** (reduced eating),
> **vṛttiparisaṃkhyāna** (restricting what one will accept),
> **rasaparityāga** (giving up delicacies), **viviktaśayyāsana** (solitary
> lodging), **kāyakleśa** (mortification). *[recalled]*
>
> Internal six: **prāyaścitta** (expiation), **vinaya** (reverence),
> **vaiyāvṛttya** (service), **svādhyāya** (study), **vyutsarga**
> (abandonment of the body-sense), **dhyāna** (meditation). *[recalled]*

**Tapas appears twice** — as the seventh of the ten dharmas (9.6) and here as
the means of nirjarā. Austerity both stops influx and sheds what is already
bound.

### On निर्जरा
Shedding is of two kinds *[recalled]*: **savipāka** — karma falling away
when it ripens of itself, which happens to everyone and gains nothing — and
**avipāka**, brought about deliberately by tapas before its time. Only the
second is a path.

## Adhyāya 10 — मोक्ष

### 10.1
> **mohakṣayāj jñānadarśanāvaraṇāntarāyakṣayāc ca kevalam** *[recalled]*
>
> From the destruction of the deluding karma, and from the destruction of
> knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring and obstructive karma,
> [arises] omniscience.

The **four ghātiyā** karmas, and in that order: delusion first, then the
other three together. **Kevala-jñāna is what remains when obscuration is
removed.** It is not an acquisition. Omniscience is the soul's own condition,
uncovered.

### 10.2
> **bandhahetvabhāvanirjarābhyāṃ kṛtsnakarmavipramokṣo mokṣaḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> By the absence of the causes of bondage and by shedding, the complete
> release from all karma is liberation.

Both halves are required: **stop the causes** (8.1's five) **and shed what is
bound** (9's tapas). Neither alone.

### 10.5–10.7 — the ascent
> **tadanantaram ūrdhvaṃ gacchaty ā lokāntāt** *[recalled]*
>
> Immediately after, it goes upward to the end of the loka.

And it stops there — because beyond the loka there is no *dharma*, the medium
of motion (5.17), so motion is impossible. **The liberated soul's rest at the
summit is not a destination chosen; it is where movement ceases to be
possible.** The cosmology of Adhyāya 5 is load-bearing for the soteriology of
Adhyāya 10, and this is the sūtra where that becomes visible.

The reasons given for the upward motion *[recalled]* are by analogy —
the momentum of prior association, the absence of the karma that weighed it,
the natural upward tendency of the soul, as a gourd freed of clay rises
through water.
