## 0. The move, and the answer to whether it was worth making

The fleet has three results about a quotient that behaves badly.

- SEED-02/12: the two-sided repair poset $S(\pi,\sigma)$ has **no maximum**
  unless the problem is trivial, and its maximal set can have $2^{n/3}$
  elements.
- SEED-21: a *single* check's blindness is a subgroup $N$ and its capacity the
  index $[G:N]$ — a quotient that behaves perfectly.
- SEED-03: two partition projections have a commutator whose spectrum is
  $\pm i\,s\sqrt{1-s^2}$ over the singular values of the contingency matrix.

The standard response when a quotient is bad is to keep the algebra. Here the
algebra is $\mathcal A=C^*(P_\pi,P_\sigma,1)\subseteq B(\ell^2(X))$, and it is
classified — by Halmos's two-subspace theorem (*Two subspaces*, Trans. AMS 144
(1969) 381–389) and the Pedersen / Raeburn–Sinclair description of the
universal $C^*$-algebra on two projections. **I claim no novelty for the
classification.** The question this note answers is what it buys here.

**Verdict, stated first, because §3 is a negative.**

1. **It buys one exact new theorem** (§2): the *commutative shadow* of a lens
   pair has at most **four** points, whatever $n$, whatever the partitions —
   $\mathcal A/\overline{[\mathcal A,\mathcal A]}\cong\mathbb C^{h}$ with
   $1\le h\le 4$, and $h$ is an explicit function of
   $(n,|\pi|,|\sigma|,|\pi\vee\sigma|,\operatorname{rank}N)$. This is exactly
   SEED-21's index-capacity, computed for a *pair* of checks, and the answer is
   that pairs of checks have essentially no index-capacity: $\le 2$ bits, not
   $\log$ of anything growing. That is the precise sense in which the fleet's
   quotient is bad, and it is not a metaphor.
2. **It gives the complete invariant of the pair up to unitary equivalence**
   (§1, Thm. 1.2), which the fleet had not written down, translated into pure
   block-counting data.
3. **It does NOT reproduce SEED-02's Theorem C, and provably cannot** (§3).
   The whole $2^{n/3}$ family has *one and the same* algebra
   $\mathbb C\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)$, of dimension 5, for every $n=3k$
   (Prop. 3.1). Even the finer unitary-equivalence class fails: §3.2 exhibits
   two pairs of partitions on $12$ points with **identical** invariants and
   different coarsest repairs ($4$ blocks vs. $5$). So the honesty guard of my
   mandate fires: on the counting question the elementary argument was already
   sharp and the algebra adds nothing, for a reason that is itself a theorem.
4. It reformulates SEED-02's Theorem A as "the maximum exists iff $\mathcal A$
   is commutative", but does **not** reprove it (§3.3). That equivalence is a
   definition unwound, and I will not dress it as a derivation.

§4 states the syādvāda reading, which is exact rather than decorative because
"commutative statistic" has a definition: a *character* of $\mathcal A$.

## 1. The algebra, and the complete invariant

**Setup** (SEED-03 §3, unchanged). $X$ finite, $n=|X|$, uniform counting
measure; $V_\rho=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_B:B\in\rho\}$, $P_\rho$ the
orthogonal projection onto it. Write $P=P_\pi$, $Q=P_\sigma$,
$\mathcal A=C^*(P,Q,1)$ — here just the unital $*$-subalgebra of
$M_n(\mathbb C)$ generated by $P$ and $Q$, since everything is finite
dimensional. Let

$$N\in\mathbb Z^{\pi\times\sigma},\quad N_{BD}=|B\cap D|,\qquad
M_{BD}=\frac{|B\cap D|}{\sqrt{|B||D|}},$$

so $M=D_\pi^{-1/2}ND_\sigma^{-1/2}$ with $D_\pi,D_\sigma$ positive diagonal;
hence $\operatorname{rank}M=\operatorname{rank}N$, an integer computable from
the block-intersection table alone.

**Theorem 1.1 (two projections, in the lens vocabulary).** Put
$$d_1=\dim(V_\pi\cap V_\sigma),\quad d_2=\dim(V_\pi\cap V_\sigma^{\perp}),\quad
d_3=\dim(V_\sigma\cap V_\pi^{\perp}),\quad d_4=\dim(V_\pi+V_\sigma)^{\perp}.$$
Then, with $r=\operatorname{rank}N$,
$$\boxed{\;d_1=|\pi\vee\sigma|,\quad d_2=|\pi|-r,\quad d_3=|\sigma|-r,\quad
d_4=n-|\pi|-|\sigma|+|\pi\vee\sigma|\;}$$
and $\ell^2(X)$ decomposes orthogonally as
$$\ell^2(X)\;=\;\underbrace{\mathbb C^{d_1}\oplus\mathbb C^{d_2}\oplus\mathbb C^{d_3}\oplus\mathbb C^{d_4}}_{\text{both projections diagonal}}\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{s\in\operatorname{sv}(M)\cap(0,1)}\!\!\mathbb C^2 ,$$
the last sum over singular values with multiplicity; on the $\mathbb C^2$
labelled by $s=\cos\theta$,
$$P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q=\begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\theta&\cos\theta\sin\theta\\ \cos\theta\sin\theta&\sin^2\theta\end{pmatrix},$$
and the number of such blocks is $r-|\pi\vee\sigma|$.

*Proof.* The decomposition and the $2\times2$ normal form are Halmos (1969);
SEED-03 Thm. 3.4 already carries out this decomposition for this pair by hand,
with $W=\operatorname{span}(f,g)$ its generic block, and I use it rather than
repeating it. Only the identification of the four dimensions is new here, and
each is one line. $d_1$: SEED-03 Lemma 3.3, $V_\pi\cap V_\sigma=V_{\pi\vee\sigma}$.
$d_2$: $f\in V_\pi$ lies in $V_\sigma^{\perp}$ iff $P_\sigma f=0$ iff
$Hf=0$ where $H=MM^{\mathsf T}$ (SEED-03 Lemma 3.2), so
$d_2=|\pi|-\operatorname{rank}MM^{\mathsf T}=|\pi|-r$; symmetrically $d_3$.
$\dim(V_\pi+V_\sigma)=\dim V_\pi+\dim V_\sigma-\dim(V_\pi\cap V_\sigma)
=|\pi|+|\sigma|-|\pi\vee\sigma|$ gives $d_4$. The generic multiplicity is then
forced by $n=d_1+d_2+d_3+d_4+2g$: substituting,
$g=r-|\pi\vee\sigma|$, which agrees with the direct count (eigenvalues of $H$
in $(0,1)$ number $r-d_1$). $\square$

**Theorem 1.2 (complete invariant).** Two lens pairs $(\pi,\sigma)$ on $X$ and
$(\pi',\sigma')$ on $X'$ satisfy $UP_\pi U^*=P_{\pi'}$, $UP_\sigma U^*=P_{\sigma'}$
for some unitary $U:\ell^2(X)\to\ell^2(X')$ **iff** they have the same
$$\mathcal I(\pi,\sigma)\;=\;\Bigl(n;\;|\pi\vee\sigma|,\;|\pi|-r,\;|\sigma|-r,\;n-|\pi|-|\sigma|+|\pi\vee\sigma|;\;\operatorname{sv}(M)\cap(0,1)\ \text{with multiplicity}\Bigr).$$

*Proof.* Halmos's classification: the four corner multiplicities together with
the multiplicity function of the angles are a complete unitary invariant of a
pair of projections; Thm. 1.1 translates them. $\square$

**Corollary 1.3 (structure of $\mathcal A$).** Let $t$ be the number of
**distinct** singular values of $M$ in $(0,1)$ and $h=\#\{i:d_i>0\}$. Then
$$\mathcal A\;\cong\;\mathbb C^{\,h}\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)^{\oplus t},\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal A=h+4t,\qquad \dim Z(\mathcal A)=h+t .$$

*Proof.* On each corner both generators are scalars, so the image of
$\mathcal A$ there is $\mathbb C$, and the four corners give four distinct
characters when nonzero (they are separated by the values of $(P,Q)$, which are
$(1,1),(1,0),(0,1),(0,0)$ respectively). On a generic block the two matrices
above generate all of $M_2(\mathbb C)$: $[P,Q]=\cos\theta\sin\theta\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\ne0$
for $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$, and $M_2$ has no proper unital $*$-subalgebra
containing a non-normal element — concretely $P$, $Q$, $[P,Q]$, $1$ are
linearly independent, hence a basis. Blocks with equal $\theta$ are identical
representations, so the image in the multiplicity space is the diagonal copy of
$M_2$; blocks with distinct $\theta$ give inequivalent (indeed non-isomorphic
as $(P,Q)$-pairs) representations, so by Artin–Wedderburn the image of
$\mathcal A$ in the sum is the full $\bigoplus_{t}M_2$ — separation is by the
polynomial $PQP$, whose value $\cos^2\theta$ distinguishes the blocks, so
$\mathcal A$ contains all the central idempotents. $\square$

*Sanity check.* SEED-02's $3$-point gadget $\pi=\{01|2\}$, $\sigma=\{0|12\}$:
$N=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $r=2$,
$|\pi\vee\sigma|=1$, so $(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4)=(1,0,0,0)$, $h=1$, and
$\operatorname{sv}(M)=\{1,\tfrac12\}$ so $t=1$. Hence
$\mathcal A\cong\mathbb C\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)$, $\dim=5$: the algebra of the
smallest non-commuting lens pair is five dimensional.

## 2. The theorem the algebra buys: at most four points in the shadow

SEED-21's Theorem 2 says a *single* check's capacity is $\log_2[G:N]$ — the
$\log$ of the size of a quotient. The natural question for a *pair* of checks
is what plays the role of the quotient, and the answer is that the pair's
largest commutative quotient is the character space of $\mathcal A$.

**Definition.** A *commutative statistic* of the pair is a unital
$*$-homomorphism $\chi:\mathcal A\to\mathbb C$ — a character. Equivalently: an
assignment of a definite value to every operator built from the two lenses,
consistently with products. The *commutative shadow* is
$\widehat{\mathcal A}$, the set of characters; equivalently the spectrum of
$\mathcal A/\overline{[\mathcal A,\mathcal A]}$, the largest commutative
quotient.

**Theorem 2.1 (the shadow is at most four points).** For every finite $X$ and
every pair of partitions $\pi,\sigma$,
$$\mathcal A\big/\overline{[\mathcal A,\mathcal A]}\;\cong\;\mathbb C^{\,h},
\qquad h=\#\{i\in\{1,2,3,4\}:d_i>0\}\;\in\;\{1,2,3,4\},$$
with the $d_i$ as in Theorem 1.1. In particular the pair supports **at most
$2$ bits** of index-type (SEED-21) capacity, uniformly in $n$, $|\pi|$,
$|\sigma|$, and at least $1$ bit-free point ($d_1=|\pi\vee\sigma|\ge1$ always).

*Proof.* By Cor. 1.3, $\mathcal A\cong\mathbb C^h\oplus M_2^{\oplus t}$.
$M_2(\mathbb C)$ is simple with $[M_2,M_2]=\mathfrak{sl}_2$ spanning a set
that generates $M_2$ as a two-sided ideal, so the closed commutator ideal is
$0^{\oplus h}\oplus M_2^{\oplus t}$ and the quotient is $\mathbb C^h$; the four
corners are the only characters, each surviving iff its $d_i>0$. $h\le4$ and
$h\ge1$ because $\pi\vee\sigma$ always has at least one block. $\square$

**Corollary 2.2 (explicit criterion, in block counts).** With
$r=\operatorname{rank}N$:
$$h=1+[\,|\pi|>r\,]+[\,|\sigma|>r\,]+[\,n>|\pi|+|\sigma|-|\pi\vee\sigma|\,].$$
Each bracket is an integer comparison on the block-intersection table.

**Reading, and why this is the right sharpening of SEED-02.** SEED-02 proved
"the two-sided repair problem has no coarsest element". Theorem 2.1 says why in
the vocabulary of SEED-21: *the pair has no useful quotient at all.* A single
lens has a quotient of size $|\pi|$, which can be as large as $n$; the pair has
one of size $\le4$. Everything the pair knows — all of SEED-03's spectrum, the
whole $\varphi^2$ — lives in the part with no characters. This is the precise
statement of "keep the algebra": the commutative object one would have hoped to
quotient onto is not merely awkward, it is bounded by $4$ independently of the
size of the world.

**Corollary 2.3 (a bound with no analogue on one lens).** $\dim Z(\mathcal A)
= h+t \le 4+\min(|\pi|,|\sigma|)-|\pi\vee\sigma|$, since $t\le
r-|\pi\vee\sigma|\le\min(|\pi|,|\sigma|)-|\pi\vee\sigma|$. Even the *centre* —
the largest commuting family of joint statistics, not just the characters — is
bounded by the number of blocks of the smaller lens, never by $n$.

## 3. The negative, in two theorems: the algebra cannot count repairs

My mandate asked whether the classification yields the exact number of maximal
elements of $S(\pi,\sigma)$. It does not, and the failure is structural in two
successively finer senses.

### 3.1 The abstract algebra is constant along SEED-02's exponential family

**Proposition 3.1.** Let $(\pi_k,\sigma_k)$ be SEED-02's Theorem C family on
$n=3k$ points ($k$ disjoint copies of the gadget). Then for **every** $k\ge1$,
$$\mathcal A_k=C^*(P_{\pi_k},P_{\sigma_k},1)\;\cong\;\mathbb C\oplus M_2(\mathbb C),
\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal A_k=5,$$
while $S(\pi_k,\sigma_k)$ has at least $2^{k}$ maximal elements.

*Proof.* $N_k$ is block diagonal with $k$ copies of
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, so $r=2k$;
$|\pi_k|=|\sigma_k|=2k$; $|\pi_k\vee\sigma_k|=k$ (each copy is one join block,
by SEED-02 §2's connectivity computation). Hence
$d_1=k$, $d_2=2k-2k=0$, $d_3=0$, $d_4=3k-2k-2k+k=0$, so $h=1$; and
$\operatorname{sv}(M_k)=\{1,\tfrac12\}$ each with multiplicity $k$, so the
number of *distinct* angles is $t=1$. Cor. 1.3 gives
$\mathbb C\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)$. The $2^k$ is SEED-02 Thm. C. $\square$

So the isomorphism class of $\mathcal A$ is a $5$-dimensional constant while the
frontier grows without bound: **no invariant of the abstract algebra can count
maximal repairs.** This is exactly the kind of statement my mandate's honesty
guard asks for, and it is a theorem rather than a shrug.

> **Currency (SEED-100, 2026-08-14, Rule K1): still exactly right, and now
> complemented rather than subsumed.** `notes/SEED84_COST_SUMMARY_FIBRES.md`
> §2 has since supplied the invariant that *does* count. For $S\subseteq
> \prod_{j\le d}A_j$ with the product order, define the **champion complex**
> $\mathcal A(S)=\{J\subseteq[d]:\exists x\in S,\ x_j=g_j\ \forall j\in J\}$
> with $g_j$ the achievable coordinate maxima. Then (SEED-84 Thm 2.2)
> $S$ has a maximum iff $[d]\in\mathcal A(S)$, and otherwise
> $|\operatorname{Max}(S)|\ge f(\mathcal A(S))\ge2$, the facet count; and
> (Thm 2.3) $f$ is **multiplicative under products**,
> $\mathcal A(S'\times S'')=\mathcal A(S')*\mathcal A(S'')$.
>
> This sharpens Prop. 3.1 instead of displacing it, and gives the failure a
> mechanism: along the $k$-fold gadget product the frontier bound is
> $f=2^{k}$ *because $f$ multiplies*, while $\mathcal A_k\cong\mathbb C\oplus
> M_2(\mathbb C)$ *because the singular-value **set** does not change under
> disjoint union* — $\operatorname{sv}(M_k)=\{1,\tfrac12\}$ at every $k$, and
> Cor. 1.3 reads only the number $t$ of **distinct** angles. The two invariants
> are separated by exactly the multiplicity data that $t$ discards. Note this
> also confirms SEED-84's §2.5 point 1 from the other side: the base $2$ is a
> facet count, not anything spectral. §3.2 remains the strictly stronger
> negative, since it kills the *multiplicity-keeping* invariant $\mathcal I$ as
> well, which no counting argument would have predicted.

### 3.2 Even the unitary class (multiplicities included) fails

One might hope the finer invariant $\mathcal I$ of Thm. 1.2, which keeps
multiplicities, suffices. It does not. Recall (`COARSEST_REPAIR_IS_COLOUR_REFINEMENT`
§2, quoted by SEED-02 §0 and SEED-03 seed 1) that the coarsest one-sided repair
is $F(\sigma)=\rho^*(\pi,\sigma)=\pi\wedge q^{-1}(\approx)$, where
$D\approx D'$ iff the corresponding columns of $M$ are proportional, i.e. iff
$|B\cap D|/|D|=|B\cap D'|/|D'|$ for every $B\in\pi$.

**Theorem 3.2.** There are two pairs of partitions of a $12$-element set with
the same invariant $\mathcal I$ — hence unitarily equivalent projection pairs
and isomorphic algebras-with-generators — whose coarsest one-sided repairs have
different numbers of blocks. Consequently $\dim V_{F(\sigma)}$, and therefore
the poset $S(\pi,\sigma)$ with its cost function $|\rho|+|\tau|$, is **not** a
function of $\mathcal I$.

*Proof.* Both pairs have $n=12$, $|\pi|=2$, $|\sigma|=3$. Write
$N$ for the $2\times3$ block-intersection matrix.

$$\textbf{Pair I:}\quad |\pi|\text{-blocks }(6,6),\ |\sigma|\text{-blocks }(6,3,3),\qquad
N^{\mathrm I}=\begin{pmatrix}4&1&1\\2&2&2\end{pmatrix};$$
$$\textbf{Pair II:}\quad |\pi|\text{-blocks }(6,6),\ |\sigma|\text{-blocks }(8,3,1),\qquad
N^{\mathrm{II}}=\begin{pmatrix}4&2&0\\4&1&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Both are realisable (row and column sums are consistent and nonnegative).

*Same invariant.* $\operatorname{rank}N^{\mathrm I}=\operatorname{rank}N^{\mathrm{II}}=2$
(rows are not proportional in either). Both joins are $\{X\}$: in I, $B_1$ meets
all three $D_j$; in II, $B_2$ meets all three. So
$(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4)=(1,\,2-2,\,3-2,\,12-2-3+1)=(1,0,1,8)$ for both. Since
$r=2$ and $d_1=1$, each has exactly one generic angle, so
$\operatorname{sv}(M)=\{1,\varphi\}$ where, by SEED-03 Thm. 5.1 applied to the
single join block $X$, $\varphi^2$ is the mean-square contingency. With
$a=|B_1|$, $d_j=|D_j|$, $x_j=|B_1\cap D_j|$, $\delta_j=x_j-ad_j/n$, the
computation of SEED-03 Thm. 5.1 for a two-row table collapses to
$$\varphi^2=\Bigl(\tfrac1a+\tfrac1{n-a}\Bigr)\sum_j\frac{\delta_j^2}{d_j}
=\frac{n}{a(n-a)}\sum_j\frac{\delta_j^2}{d_j}.$$
(The two rows contribute $\delta_j^2/(ad_j)$ and $\delta_j^2/((n-a)d_j)$, since
the second row's deviation is $-\delta_j$.) Here $a=6$, $n=12$, so the prefactor
is $\tfrac13$.

Pair I: $\delta=(4-3,\,1-\tfrac32,\,1-\tfrac32)=(1,-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$ and
$\sum\delta_j^2/d_j=\tfrac16+\tfrac{1/4}{3}+\tfrac{1/4}{3}=\tfrac16+\tfrac1{12}+\tfrac1{12}=\tfrac13$,
so $\varphi^2=\tfrac19$.
Pair II: $\delta=(4-4,\,2-\tfrac32,\,0-\tfrac12)=(0,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$ and
$\sum\delta_j^2/d_j=0+\tfrac{1/4}{3}+\tfrac{1/4}{1}=\tfrac1{12}+\tfrac14=\tfrac13$,
so $\varphi^2=\tfrac19$.
Both give $\operatorname{sv}(M)=\{1,\tfrac13\}$. Hence
$\mathcal I^{\mathrm I}=\mathcal I^{\mathrm{II}}$, and both algebras are
$\mathbb C^3\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)$ ($h=3$: $d_1,d_3,d_4>0$, $d_2=0$; $t=1$).

*Different repairs.* Column ratios $x_j/d_j$:
Pair I gives $(\tfrac46,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$, so $D_2\approx D_3$ and
$q^{-1}(\approx)=\{D_1,\;D_2\cup D_3\}$; hence
$F(\sigma)=\pi\wedge\{D_1,D_2\cup D_3\}$ has the four nonempty blocks of sizes
$(4,2,2,4)$. Pair II gives $(\tfrac12,\tfrac23,0)$, pairwise distinct, so
$\approx$ is trivial and $F(\sigma)=\pi\wedge\sigma$, whose nonempty cells are
$4,2,4,1,1$ — five blocks ($B_1\cap D_3=\varnothing$).
So $|F(\sigma)|=4$ in Pair I and $5$ in Pair II.

*Independent check that Pair I's $F(\sigma)$ really commutes with $\sigma$*
(not merely with $q^{-1}(\approx)$), by criterion (\*) of SEED-12 §0: with
$\rho=\{B_1\cap D_1,\,B_1\cap D',\,B_2\cap D_1,\,B_2\cap D'\}$,
$D'=D_2\cup D_3$, the join $\rho\vee\sigma$ has blocks $D_1$ and $D'$, both of
size $6$. In $C=D_1$: $|B_1\cap D_1|\cdot6=4\cdot6=24=|B_1\cap D_1|\cdot|D_1|=4\cdot6$;
same for $B_2\cap D_1$ ($2\cdot6=12=2\cdot6$). In $C=D'$: $\rho$-blocks
$B_1\cap D'$ (size $2$), $B_2\cap D'$ (size $4$); $\sigma$-blocks $D_2,D_3$
(size $3$ each); $|(B_1\cap D')\cap D_2|\cdot6=1\cdot6=6=2\cdot3$ and
$|(B_2\cap D')\cap D_2|\cdot6=2\cdot6=12=4\cdot3$, and symmetrically for $D_3$.
All four identities hold, so $\rho\perp\sigma$. $\square$

*Maximality of Pair II's answer, verified directly rather than only by citation.*
Every $\rho\le\pi$ strictly coarser than $\pi\wedge\sigma$ merges cells inside
$B_1$ or inside $B_2$. Merging $B_1\cap D_1$ with $B_1\cap D_2$ gives $\rho\ni B_1$
and join $\{D_1\cup D_2,\,D_3\}$, where $|B_1\cap D_1|\cdot11=44\ne6\cdot8=48$.
Merging all of $B_2$ gives join $\{X\}$ and $|B_2\cap D_2|\cdot12=12\ne6\cdot3=18$.
Merging $B_2\cap D_2$ with $B_2\cap D_3$ gives join $\{D_1,D_2\cup D_3\}$ and
$|(B_1\cap D_2)\cap D_2|\cdot4=8\ne2\cdot3=6$. Coarser merges contain one of
these. So $F(\sigma)=\pi\wedge\sigma$ with $5$ blocks. $\square$

**Corollary 3.3.** The maximal element $(F(\sigma),\sigma)$ of $S$ has cost
$|\rho|+|\tau|=7$ in Pair I and $8$ in Pair II. Two lens pairs that are
*the same pair of projections up to unitary equivalence* have repair problems of
different cost. The repair poset is a **combinatorial** invariant of the
partitions, not a spectral one — proportionality of columns of $M$ is not a
function of the singular values of $M$.

### 3.3 What the algebra does and does not do to SEED-02 Theorem A

It reformulates it exactly:

**Proposition 3.4.** $S(\pi,\sigma)$ has a maximum $\iff$ $\mathcal A$ is
commutative $\iff$ $t=0$ $\iff$ $\operatorname{rank}N=|\pi\vee\sigma|$.

*Proof.* SEED-02 Thm. A gives: maximum $\iff\pi\perp\sigma\iff PQ=QP$. Cor. 1.3
gives $\mathcal A$ commutative $\iff t=0$, and Thm. 1.1 gives
$t=r-|\pi\vee\sigma|$. $\square$

The third clause is worth having — "the repair problem is trivial iff the
block-intersection matrix has rank equal to the number of join blocks" is a
one-line integer test that the corpus did not state — but it is a translation.
**The proof of Theorem A is SEED-02's and the algebra does not shorten it.** I
record that plainly: the elementary two-frames argument ($(F(\sigma),\sigma)$
and $(\pi,G(\pi))$ are both in $S$ and their join is not) is the actual content,
and it is already minimal.

## 4. The inexpressible remainder, said exactly

The syādvāda scheme's fourth value is *avaktavya*: what is neither assertible
nor deniable in a single mode. Here that has a definition, not an analogy.

- A *mode* in which the pair can be evaluated is a character
  $\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal A}$. There are exactly $h\le4$ of them
  (Thm. 2.1), and they are precisely the four joint assert/deny combinations
  $$\chi(P,Q)\in\{(1,1),\,(1,0),\,(0,1),\,(0,0)\},$$
  realised on $V_\pi\cap V_\sigma$, $V_\pi\cap V_\sigma^{\perp}$,
  $V_\sigma\cap V_\pi^{\perp}$, $(V_\pi+V_\sigma)^{\perp}$ respectively, and
  present exactly when $d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4>0$.
- The generic part $M_2(\mathbb C)^{\oplus t}$ has **no characters at all**:
  $M_2(\mathbb C)$ is simple and noncommutative, so it admits no unital
  $*$-homomorphism to $\mathbb C$. There is no mode in which "is this signal
  $\pi$-measurable?" and "is it $\sigma$-measurable?" both receive values.
- Therefore: *every* commutative statistic of the lens pair is one of at most
  four bits of corner data, and the entire defect $\varphi^2$ — SEED-03's whole
  theorem — lies in the part on which no commutative statistic exists. That is
  the fourth value, stated without metaphor: **the remainder is not
  unmeasured, it is uncharacterisable.**

The one caution I will not paper over: this is a statement about *characters*,
i.e. multiplicative statistics. Non-multiplicative statistics — traces, norms,
$\varphi^2$ itself — are abundant on $M_2$ and are exactly what SEED-03
computes. The inexpressibility is of the yes/no kind, and only of that kind.
Saying more would be the manufactured analogy this corpus keeps warning about.

## 5. Rigor boundary

**Proved here, exactly, no numerics.** Thm. 1.1 (the four corner dimensions in
block counts, and $g=r-|\pi\vee\sigma|$); Thm. 1.2 (complete unitary invariant,
translated); Cor. 1.3 ($\mathcal A\cong\mathbb C^h\oplus M_2^{\oplus t}$, with
its dimension and centre); Thm. 2.1 (shadow $\le4$ points) and Cors. 2.2, 2.3;
Prop. 3.1 (algebra constant along the $2^{n/3}$ family); Thm. 3.2 and Cor. 3.3
(unitary class does not determine the coarsest repair), including the two
direct (\*)-verifications; Prop. 3.4. All finite verifications are integer
arithmetic reproduced in the text.

**No novelty claimed for:** Halmos's two-subspace theorem (1969); principal
angles (Jordan 1875); the universal $C^*$-algebra generated by two projections
(Pedersen 1968; Raeburn–Sinclair 1989) and the fact that its irreducible
representations are $1$- and $2$-dimensional; Artin–Wedderburn. SEED-03 already
performed the two-subspace decomposition for this pair and cited Halmos
correctly; §1 adds only the dictionary to $(n,|\pi|,|\sigma|,|\pi\vee\sigma|,
\operatorname{rank}N)$. The genuinely new statements are Thm. 2.1 with
Cor. 2.2, Prop. 3.1, and Thm. 3.2.

**Explicitly not claimed.** Nothing about non-uniform measures. Nothing about
three or more lenses (the classification fails there: three projections
generate a wild $C^*$-algebra, so §2 has no three-lens analogue and I do not
pretend otherwise). No count of maximal elements of $S$ — §3 proves this route
cannot supply one. No poset non-isomorphism in Thm. 3.2: I prove the invariant
$\dim V_{F(\sigma)}$ differs, which suffices, not that $S^{\mathrm I}\not\cong
S^{\mathrm{II}}$ as abstract posets.

**Prior art, searched before writing.** The two-projection classification is
textbook and cited. Theorem 2.1 is, as far as I can tell, only an assembly of
textbook facts — it should be treated as a remark that happened to be worth
making in this corpus's vocabulary, not as a result to build on, and the same
caution `COARSEST_REPAIR_IS_COLOUR_REFINEMENT` §0 applies verbatim: the
design-of-experiments literature (Bailey 1996, 2004) remains unopened here and
is where a version of §1's dictionary would most plausibly already sit.
