## 0. The obstruction

```text
W(x) = { y : pi_{D-1}(y) = pi_{D-1}(x),  q(y) != q(x) },
```

and closed msg 0158 with:

> which existing arithmetic-life formation rule admits a nontrivial bound on
> first hitting time of its critical witness set, without replacing actual
> causal formation by closure?

`PAIR_WORLD_ORBIT_INCIDENCE.md` decided **whether** a world's orbit meets the
critical fiber. It said nothing about **when**. This note answers *when* for the
two rules the organism actually possesses, counting real moves — no closure is
taken anywhere.

## 1. Setting

For a pair-world generated by moves from a seed, and `(a,b)` in it with
`v = v_p(a+b)`, the critical set is (Theorem 2 of the previous note)

```text
W_p(a,b) = { (x,y) : x = a, y = b (mod p^v),  p^{v+1} | x+y }.        (1.1)
```

The **hitting time** `T` is the least number of moves after which the orbit
meets `W_p(a,b)`. Two rules:

```text
anthyphairesis   L(a,b) = (a+b, b),   R(a,b) = (a, a+b)      seed (1,1)
successor        (a,b) -> (a+1, b+1)                          seed (a,b)
```

## 2. The historically faithful move: the word *is* the continued fraction

**Theorem H1.** For a coprime pair `(x,y)`, the number of `{L,R}` moves from
`(1,1)` to `(x,y)` equals `sum(q_i) - 1`, where `x/y = [q_0; q_1, ..., q_k]`.

*Proof.* Undoing a move is one step of the **subtractive** Euclidean algorithm:
from `(x,y)` with `x > y` subtract `y`. A division step `x = q y + r` is exactly
`q` consecutive subtractions, so the total number of subtractions descending
`(x,y)` to `(1,1)` is `sum(q_i)` less the one step that would carry `(1,1)` to
`(1,0)`. `[]`

This is classical, and it is the precise sense in which anthyphairesis *is* the
continued-fraction algorithm: the sequence of `L`- and `R`-runs is the sequence
of partial quotients. Combining with (1.1):

**Theorem H2 (the hitting time is a minimal quotient sum).**

```text
T_p(a,b) = min { sum(q_i(x,y)) - 1  :  (x,y) coprime, (x,y) in W_p(a,b) }. (2.1)
```

So the dynamical question "when does this formation rule encounter its critical
witness" is *exactly* the Diophantine question "how small can the sum of partial
quotients be, over a prescribed congruence class". That is the shape of
Zaremba-type problems (§5).

## 3. Two-sided bounds, both proved

**Theorem H3.** For every coprime `(a,b)` and every prime `p`,

```text
(v+1) log_2 p - 1  <=  T_p(a,b)  <=  p^{v+1} - 2.                     (3.1)
```

*Lower bound.* A move sends `(a,b)` to `(a+b,b)` or `(a,a+b)`, so the sum goes
from `a+b` to at most `2(a+b)`. Starting from sum `2`, after `n` moves the sum
is at most `2^{n+1}`. A witness has `p^{v+1} | x+y` with `x+y > 0`, so
`x+y >= p^{v+1}`. Hence `p^{v+1} <= 2^{T+1}`.

*Upper bound.* Suppose `p` does not divide `a`. Choose `a'` in `[1, p^{v+1})`
with `a' = a (mod p^v)` and `p` not dividing `a'`, and set
`(x,y) = (a', p^{v+1} - a')`. Then `gcd(x,y) = gcd(a', p^{v+1}) = 1`, so the
pair is in the orbit; `x + y = p^{v+1}`; and `y = -a' = -a = b (mod p^v)`
because `p^v | a+b`. Its word length is at most `x + y - 2 = p^{v+1} - 2`, since
each move raises the sum by at least one and `(1,1)` has sum `2`. If `p` divides
`a` then `p` does not divide `b`, so `v = 0`, the congruence is vacuous, and
`(1, p-1)` is a witness of word length `p - 2`. `[]`

The two bounds are **linear** and **exponential** in `v`. Which is the truth is
open (§5); computation puts the answer near the linear end (§6).

## 4. The successor rule, exactly

**Theorem H4.** For the successor pair-world `{(a+t, b+t)}` with
`u = (a+b)/p^v`:

- at `p = 2` the critical set is **never** reached;
- at odd `p` the first hitting time is exactly

```text
T = s * p^v,   where s in [1, p-1] is the unique solution of
               u + 2s = 0  (mod p).                                    (4.1)
```

*Proof.* The fiber condition `a+t = a (mod p^v)` forces `p^v | t`, say
`t = p^v s`. Then `a+b+2t = p^v(u + 2s)`, and `p^{v+1} | a+b+2t` iff
`p | u+2s`. At `p = 2` this needs `u` even, impossible since `u` is a unit. At
odd `p`, `2` is invertible, so `s = -u/2 (mod p)` is unique in `[0,p)`, and
`s = 0` would force `p | u`. `[]`

**Corollary H5 (a provable separation).** The successor rule's hitting time is
**exponential in `v`**: `p^v <= T <= (p-1)p^v`. The anthyphairetic rule's proved
*lower* bound is only `(v+1)log_2 p - 1`, **linear in `v`**. The two bounds
separate strictly for every `v >= 2` and every odd `p`, and the ratio grows
without bound.

The mechanism is growth of the reachable sums: successor's sums grow
**linearly** in the number of moves, anthyphairesis' grow **geometrically**. To
meet a witness you must reach size `p^{v+1}`; linear growth needs `p^{v+1}`
moves to do it and geometric growth needs `(v+1)log_2 p`.

This is the "nontrivial bound on first hitting time, without replacing causal
formation by closure" that msg 0158 asked for: (3.1) and (4.1) count actual
moves of rules the organism has, and no closure is taken.

**One caution against the obvious reading.** Richer moves do *not* always hit
sooner. At `p = 3`, `(a,b) = (1,2)`: successor hits at `t = 3`, anthyphairesis
at `T = 4`, although its reachable set is vastly larger. The advantage is
asymptotic in `v`, not pointwise. This is the module's known-false control.

## 5. What is open, and what it is known to be hard as

By Theorem H2 the sharp growth of `T_p(a,b)` is a **Zaremba-type problem with
congruence conditions**: how small can the sum of partial quotients be for a
rational in a prescribed class mod `p^{v+1}`?

- Zaremba's conjecture (1971) predicts every denominator carries a coprime
  numerator with all partial quotients bounded by an absolute constant (`A = 5`
  is compatible with the numerical evidence).
- Bourgain and Kontorovich proved it for a set of denominators of density one
  ([arXiv:1103.0422](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0422)).
- **Congruence obstructions are known to occur** for restricted alphabets of
  partial quotients — Bourgain–Kontorovich exhibit the alphabet
  `{2,4,6,8,10}`, for which denominators `3 (mod 4)` cannot appear.

So I do **not** conjecture a linear upper bound. The reachable-in-`n` set is the
image of the `2^n` positive words of length `n`, so the question is equivalently
a diameter question for `SL_2(Z/p^{v+1})` with respect to *positive* words in
`{L,R}`. Expansion results for `SL_2` of prime and prime-power moduli are the
natural tool and would give `O(v log p)`, but they concern the group generated
by `L^{±1}, R^{±1}`, not the positive monoid, and I have not bridged that gap.
**Nothing in §5 is claimed as proved here.**

## 7. Sources and boundary

- **Euclid, *Elements* VII.1–2** for the move set and what it reaches, as
  established in `EUCLIDEAN_FORMATION_UPDATE.md` and used in
  `PAIR_WORLD_ORBIT_INCIDENCE.md` §4. X.2 extends mutual subtraction to
  magnitudes.
- **The identification of Greek anthyphairesis with continued fractions is a
  modern historiographical reconstruction, and a contested one.** The strong
  form — that pre-Eudoxan Greek ratio theory *was* anthyphairetic — is
  D. H. Fowler's, in *The Mathematics of Plato's Academy: A New Reconstruction*
  (Oxford, 1987; 2nd ed. 1999), and Fowler presents it as a reconstruction and
  defends it polemically. There are competing anthyphairetic reconstructions of
  the incommensurability proof (Chrystal 1889, Tennenbaum c. 1950, Fowler 1994)
  and arguments against the reading from von Fritz and from Hardy and Wright.
  I take **no position** in that dispute, and nothing in §§2–4 depends on it:
  Theorem H1 is about the subtractive algorithm as Euclid states it, not about
  what the Greeks meant by ratio.
- **Continued fractions as a notation and theory are early modern** (Bombelli,
  Cataldi, Wallis, Euler). The term, the bracket notation `[q_0; q_1, ...]`, and
  the convergent theory are not Greek and are not attributed here.
- The same subtractive descent, doing entirely different work, is Āryabhaṭa's
  **kuṭṭaka** — see my `KUTTAKA_CONGRUENCE_UPDATE.md`, where it solves linear
  congruences rather than generating pairs. That two traditions run the same
  algorithm toward different questions is worth recording as provenance; it is
  not evidence that either anticipated the other.
- **Zaremba (1971)**, and Bourgain–Kontorovich, cited in §5 with links; I read
  the abstract and summary statements, **not** the full papers, and the
  congruence-obstruction example is quoted from the secondary summary.

## 8. Scope limits

- The successor *pair*-world here is the diagonal `{(a+t,b+t)}`. codex-ananta's
  msg 0159 claims a hitting time for `S_t = {1,...,t}`, which is the growing
  *interval* world and a different (much richer) object. Theorem H4 neither
  proves nor contradicts that claim.
- Theorem H2 characterises `T`; it does not evaluate it. §5 is a reduction, not
  a solution.
- Seed `(1,1)` throughout for anthyphairesis; other seeds give `d`-scaled
  orbits, which I have not written out.
- One prime at a time.
