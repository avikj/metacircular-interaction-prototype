## Adhyāya 2 — जीव

### 2.1 — the five bhāvas
> **aupaśamikakṣāyikau bhāvau miśraś ca jīvasya svatattvam audayikapāriṇāmikau ca**
> *[recalled]*
>
> The suppressional and the destructional states, and the mixed, are the
> soul's own nature — and the fruitional and the inherent.

Five *bhāvas*, and the sūtra says **svatattvam**: they are the soul's *own*
reality, not accidents of it.

- **aupaśamika** — from suppression (upaśama) of karma. The karma is present
  and quiescent.
- **kṣāyika** — from destruction (kṣaya). Gone, not quiescent.
- **kṣāyopaśamika** (*miśra*, mixed) — partial destruction with suppression of
  the rest.
- **audayika** — from fruition (udaya): the state the soul is in *because*
  karma is presently ripening.
- **pāriṇāmika** — inherent, owing to nothing karmic at all.

### 2.2 — their divisions
> **dvinavāṣṭādaśaikaviṃśatitribhedā yathākramam** *[searched — the counts]*
>
> Of two, nine, eighteen, twenty-one and three divisions respectively.

2 / 9 / 18 / 21 / 3. **[searched: these five counts are confirmed by search
results; the Sanskrit is recalled.]**

*(2.3–2.7 enumerate the members of each. I do not set them out; I am not
confident of the lists in order.)*

### 2.8 — the defining mark
> **upayogo lakṣaṇam** *[searched]*
>
> Upayoga is the characteristic [of the soul].

**upayoga** — the soul's applied consciousness, its being-turned-toward. Not a
faculty the soul has; the mark by which it is soul at all. This is the Jaina
answer to what a jīva *is*, and it is neither substance-without-content nor a
bundle: it is that which cognizes, essentially.

### 2.9
> **sa dvividho'ṣṭacaturbhedaḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> It is of two kinds, of eight and of four divisions.

**jñānopayoga** (eight — the five knowledges of 1.9 plus three erroneous
kinds) and **darśanopayoga** (four). *Darśana* here is not "faith" as in
samyagdarśana of 1.1–1.2 but the undifferentiated apprehension that precedes
determinate knowing — the term does double duty and the commentaries are
explicit that it does.

### 2.10 — the great division
> **saṃsāriṇo muktāś ca** *[searched]*
>
> [Souls are] transmigrating and liberated.

### 2.11
> **samanaskāmanaskāḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> [Transmigrating souls are] with mind and without mind.

### 2.12–2.14 — mobile and immobile
> **saṃsāriṇas trasasthāvarāḥ** *[recalled]*
> **pṛthivyaptejovāyuvanaspatayaḥ sthāvarāḥ** *[recalled]*
> **dvīndriyādayas trasāḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> The transmigrating are mobile and immobile. Earth, water, fire, air and
> plants are immobile. From the two-sensed upward are mobile.

**Earth, water, fire and air are ensouled.** Not "have life in them" as a
figure — each is a class of *jīva* with one sense. This is doctrine, not
poetry, and it is load-bearing: ahiṃsā's scope follows from it, and so does
the Jaina account of why liberation requires the conduct it requires.

### 2.15–2.21 — the senses
> **pañcendriyāṇi** — five senses. *[recalled]*
> **dvividhāni** — of two kinds. *[recalled]*
> **nirvṛttyupakaraṇe dravyendriyam** — the physical sense is its formation
> and its instrument. *[recalled]*
> **labdhyupayogau bhāvendriyam** — the psychical sense is capacity and
> application. *[recalled]*
> **sparśanarasanaghrāṇacakṣuḥśrotrāṇi** — touch, taste, smell, sight,
> hearing. *[recalled]*
> **sparśarasagandhavarṇaśabdās teṣām arthāḥ** — their objects are touch,
> taste, smell, colour, sound. *[recalled]*
> **śrutam anindriyasya** — scripture is the object of the mind. *[recalled]*

Each sense is doubled: a **dravyendriya** (the organ, physical) and a
**bhāvendriya** (the capacity and its exercise). The sense is not the organ.

### 2.22–2.25 — how many senses each class has
> **vanaspatyantānām ekam** *[recalled]*
> **kṛmipipīlikābhramaramanuṣyādīnām ekaikavṛddhāni** *[recalled]*
>
> Up to plants, one [sense]. Worms, ants, bees, humans and so on increase by
> one each.

Worms two, ants three, bees four, humans five. The ladder is stated by
example, with animals named.

### 2.36 (approximate number) — the five bodies
> **audārikavaikriyikāhārakataijasakārmaṇāni śarīrāṇi** *[recalled; number
> uncertain]*
>
> Gross, transformable, projectable, luminous, and karmic — the bodies.

Five **śarīra**. The last, **kārmaṇa-śarīra**, is the karmic body itself: karma
is not a moral ledger but a *body*, made of matter, that the soul carries.
This is the point at which the Jaina system diverges most sharply from every
other Indian account of karma, and it is why Adhyāya 5's matter-theory is not
a digression.

*(The remainder of Adhyāya 2 treats the bodies' properties, their occupancy,
birth-modes — sammūrcchana, garbha, upapāda — and the kinds of birth-place.
I do not set these out with confidence.)*

## Adhyāya 3 — the lower and middle worlds

*[recalled throughout; I have no search anchor for this chapter and my
confidence is lower than for 2 and 5.]*

The chapter describes **naraka** — the seven hells, their earths (ratnaprabhā,
śarkarāprabhā, and so on), their extents and the lifespans and sufferings of
those born there — and then the **madhyaloka**, the middle world: Jambūdvīpa
with Mount Meru at its centre, the concentric continents and oceans, the
regions (kṣetra) and the mountain ranges (varṣadhara) dividing them, and the
*karmabhūmi* — the regions where conduct bears liberating fruit — as against
the *bhogabhūmi*, the regions of enjoyment where it does not.

Two things are worth setting down because they are structural rather than
scenic:

1. **The cosmos is not created and not destroyed.** It is beginningless. There
   is no cosmogony in this system, and therefore no creator; the loka has a
   determinate shape and eternal duration.
2. **Liberation is possible only from the karmabhūmi, and only as a human.**
   The geography is soteriological. It is a map of where the path is available.

## Adhyāya 4 — the celestial beings

*[recalled; low confidence on detail.]*

Four classes of **deva**:

- **bhavanavāsin** — residential
- **vyantara** — peripatetic
- **jyotiṣka** — the luminaries: suns, moons, planets, constellations, stars
- **vaimānika** — the empyrean, itself divided into *kalpopapanna* (within the
  heavens proper) and *kalpātīta* (beyond them, including the graiveyaka and
  anuttara)

The jyotiṣka are gods. The sun and moon are not objects in a sky; they are
classes of embodied soul, in motion, and their motion is what time is measured
by in the human region.

**Gods are not liberated and cannot liberate.** They are in saṃsāra, with
longer lives and better circumstances, and they must be reborn human to take
the path. The system has no salvific deity, and this chapter is where that is
made unavoidable rather than argued.

## Adhyāya 5 — अजीव

### 5.1
> **ajīvakāyā dharmādharmākāśapudgalāḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> The non-soul substances are dharma, adharma, ākāśa and pudgala.

### 5.2
> **dravyāṇi jīvāś ca** *[recalled]*
>
> These, and souls, are the substances.

### 5.3–5.4
> Eternal, fixed in number, without form. Souls too, except that they have
> upayoga. *[recalled, paraphrased — I do not trust my wording]*

### 5.5–5.6
> **āākāśād ekadravyāṇi** — dharma, adharma and ākāśa are each a single
> substance. *[recalled]*
> **niṣkriyāṇi ca** — and motionless. *[recalled]*

### 5.7–5.16 — extension
The **pradeśa** count: dharma, adharma and each jīva have innumerable
(asaṃkhyāta) space-points; ākāśa has infinite (ananta); pudgala from one
upward. *[recalled]*

### 5.17 — THE FUNCTION OF DHARMA AND ADHARMA
> **gatisthityupagraho dharmādharmayor upakāraḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> The assistance of dharma and adharma is [respectively] to motion and to rest.

**dharma** here is not virtue. It is the medium that makes motion possible — a
substance whose sole function is to be the condition of movement, as water is
for a fish, in the standard illustration. It does not move anything; it permits
what moves to move. **adharma** likewise for rest. Nothing else in Indian
philosophy has these two, and they are what make the Jaina loka a bounded
region: the loka is exactly where dharma and adharma extend, and beyond it,
the *aloka*, motion is impossible — not forbidden, impossible.

### 5.18
> **ākāśasyāvagāhaḥ** *[recalled]*
>
> The function of space is to give room.

### 5.19–5.20
> **śarīravāṅmanaḥprāṇāpānāḥ pudgalānām** *[recalled]*
>
> Bodies, speech, mind, and the in- and out-breath are [functions] of matter.

**Speech and mind are material.** So is karma. The soul is not made of matter,
but everything by which it is bound is.

### 5.21
> **sukhaduḥkhajīvitamaraṇopagrahāś ca** *[recalled]*
>
> And [matter assists in] pleasure, pain, life and death.

### 5.22 — time, and a dissent recorded in the text
> **kālaś cetyeke** *[recalled]*
>
> "And time," say some.

The sūtra reports a disagreement rather than settling it. The Digambara
tradition takes kāla as a genuine sixth dravya; the Śvetāmbara tradition
largely does not. **Umāsvāti sets down that the schools differ.** That is the
text's own method: the dispute is part of the record.

### 5.23–5.28 — pudgala
Atoms (**aṇu**) and aggregates (**skandha**); the qualities of matter are
touch, taste, smell and colour; aggregates form by *bheda* (splitting),
*saṃghāta* (combining), or both; binding (**bandha**) between atoms occurs by
degrees of oiliness (snigdha) and dryness (rūkṣa), with stated conditions on
which degrees bind. *[recalled; the binding conditions I do not state because
I would get the numbers wrong.]*

### 5.29 — WHAT IT IS TO BE
> **utpādavyayadhrauvyayuktaṃ sat** *[searched — see companion note]*
>
> That which is, is endowed with origination, cessation, and persistence.

### 5.30
> **tadbhāvāvyayaṃ nityam** *[searched — see companion note]*

### 5.31
> **arpitānarpitasiddheḥ** *[searched — see companion note]*

### 5.38 (Digambara numbering)
> **guṇaparyayavad dravyam** *[recalled]*
>
> A substance is that which has qualities and modes.

**guṇa** persist; **paryāya** come and go. 5.29's three-fold character follows
from this pair, and so does anekāntavāda: the same thing is permanent *qua*
guṇa and impermanent *qua* paryāya, and neither predication is a concession to
the other.

## What is still missing from this repository

Adhyāyas 6–10 — āsrava (the influx of karma and its causes), bandha (its four
aspects: prakṛti, sthiti, anubhāga, pradeśa — nature, duration, intensity,
quantity), saṃvara (stopping it: gupti, samiti, dharma, anuprekṣā, parīṣahajaya,
cāritra), nirjarā (shedding it, and tapas), and mokṣa. That is five of the
seven tattvas — the entire karmic mechanism and the path — and none of it is
here.

I am not writing it from recall in the same session I wrote this. The parts I
would get wrong are exactly the parts that matter: the numbered lists.
