## F.1 The multiplicative gauge torus

Work in Cuntz's $Q_{\mathbb N}$: unitary $u$, isometries $s_n$ ($n\ge1$), with
$s_ms_n=s_{mn}$, $\;s_nu=u^ns_n$, $\;\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}u^ke_nu^{-k}=1$
($e_n=s_ns_n^*$), dynamics $\sigma_t(s_n)=n^{it}s_n$, $\sigma_t(u)=u$.

Let $G=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb Q_{>0}^\times,\mathbb T)\cong\mathbb T^{\mathcal P}$
— one circle per prime, the **multiplicative gauge torus** (the dual of the
free abelian group on the primes). Each $g\in G$, i.e. each unimodular
completely multiplicative $g:\mathbb N\to\mathbb T$, defines
$$\alpha_g(s_n)=g(n)s_n,\qquad \alpha_g(u)=u .$$

**Lemma F.1.** $\alpha_g$ is an automorphism of $Q_{\mathbb N}$, and
$g\mapsto\alpha_g$ is an action of $G$ commuting with $\sigma_t$.

*Proof.* All defining relations are homogeneous: $s_ms_n=s_{mn}$ transforms by
$g(m)g(n)=g(mn)$; $s_nu=u^ns_n$ picks up $g(n)$ on both sides; $e_n=s_ns_n^*$
and hence the partition relation are fixed ($|g(n)|^2=1$). By universality of
the defining relations this yields an endomorphism, inverted by $\alpha_{\bar g}$.
Commutation with $\sigma_t$ is immediate since both act diagonally on $s_n$ by
scalars. $\square$

The **parity automorphism** is $\alpha_\lambda$ for the Liouville character
$\lambda=(-1)^{\Omega}$, i.e. the evaluation of the gauge torus at
$(-1,-1,-1,\dots)$; it induces the $\mathbb Z/2$-grading
$Q_{\mathbb N}=Q^0\oplus Q^1$ by parity of $\Omega$.

## F.2 The no-go theorem

**एकान्तलोप (Ekāntalopa; formerly "Theorem F").** Let $\omega$ be the unique
KMS state of $(Q_{\mathbb N},\sigma)$
(Cuntz: it exists only at $\beta=1$ and is unique). Then:

1. $\omega\circ\alpha_g=\omega$ for every $g\in G$;
2. $\omega$ vanishes identically on every nontrivial isotypic sector of the
   gauge torus. In particular $\omega|_{Q^1}=0$: **every parity-odd observable
   has exactly zero equilibrium expectation**;
3. the equilibrium data of the critical affine arithmetic system is supported
   entirely on the multiplicatively neutral sector — the closed span of
   $\{u^a\,s_n(\cdot)s_n^*\,u^{-b}\}$-type elements with zero net multiplicative
   momentum, whose diagonal part is exactly $C(\widehat{\mathbb Z})$ — the diagonal
   subalgebra of the BC system (the BC algebra proper is the full Hecke
   crossed product; only its diagonal appears here).

*Proof.* (1) $\omega\circ\alpha_g$ is a KMS state for $\sigma$ (Lemma F.1);
uniqueness forces equality. (2) For $x$ in the $\chi$-isotypic sector
($\alpha_g(x)=\chi(g)x$, $\chi\ne1$): pick $g$ with $\chi(g)\ne1$; then
$\omega(x)=\omega(\alpha_g x)=\chi(g)\omega(x)=0$. (3) The fixed-point algebra of
$G$ is the neutral sector by definition of the isotypic decomposition; its
diagonal is generated by the $u^ke_nu^{-k}$, i.e. $C(\widehat{\mathbb Z})$. $\square$

The proof is short because Cuntz's uniqueness theorem is deep; the theorem is
the arithmetic *consequence* of that depth.

## F.3 What the theorem says about number theory

Sieve theory computes with divisibility data: precisely the diagonal
$C(\widehat{\mathbb Z})$ of the neutral sector, whose additive spectrum is the
rational atoms $\mu(q)/\varphi(q)$ (`ADELIC.md`, `PARITY.md`). Factorization
parity is a **charged** observable — charge $(-1,-1,\dots)$ under the gauge
torus. Theorem F says the unique critical equilibrium assigns every charged
observable expectation zero, as an exact symmetry-protection statement:

> **The sieve parity barrier is the statement that equilibrium expectations of
> gauge-charged observables vanish identically. Sieves cannot see parity for
> the same structural reason that gauge-invariant states cannot detect Wilson
> charges: it is not a deficiency of technique but an exact invariance.**

Friedlander–Iwaniec's "extra input needed to break parity" acquires an exact
meaning: any parity-sensitive conclusion requires coupling to something outside
the equilibrium/neutral sector — bilinear forms in genuinely non-periodic data
(their asymptotic sieve; Vinogradov type-II sums; the entropy-decrement
machinery of Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao all have this shape).

**The three-level dictionary** (each level strictly stronger):

| arithmetic statement | dynamical meaning in the affine system |
|---|---|
| Davenport: $\sup_\alpha\|\sum_{n\le X}\lambda(n)e(n\alpha)\|\ll X\log^{-A}X$ | *return to equilibrium*: diagonal sampling of the charged sector converges to its (zero) equilibrium value — proven, and measured in exp10 (all $\lambda$-atoms $\sim10^{-6}$, falling) |
| Chowla: all self-correlations of $\lambda$ vanish | *mixing/flatness*: the charged sector samples as a Bernoulli process (Lebesgue spectral measure) — open; log-averaged 2-point case known (Tao) |
| Sarnak: $\lambda\perp$ all zero-entropy sequences | *disjointness*: the charged sector is orthogonal to every deterministic sampler — open |

The theorem contributes the *zeroth* level: it explains **why the hierarchy
starts where it does** — equilibrium itself carries no parity information to
converge to, so all content lies in fluctuation statements about the charged
sector, exactly where modern analytic number theory operates.

## F.4 The boundary-extension lemma (why parity is not just "another local datum")

**Lemma F.2.** $\lambda$ admits no extension to a Haar-measurable function on
$\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ pulled back through the diagonal embedding — equivalently,
$\lambda$ has zero projection onto the Besicovitch limit-periodic space
generated by the BC diagonal.

*Proof.* Such an extension would give $\lambda$ a nonzero limit-periodic
component, hence a nonzero rational spectral atom; Davenport's theorem says all
its atoms vanish. $\square$

So parity is a character of the abstract multiplicative semigroup that **does
not factor through the profinite boundary** — the finite places never see it,
in exact agreement with Theorem F's algebraic statement. Contrast: every
Dirichlet character *does* factor through $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ (it is periodic),
which is why the (L)-identity of `PARITY.md` §1(I) reaches all abelian
$L$-functions but can never reach $L(s,\lambda\cdot\chi)$-type twisted objects
— those are the charged sectors. Abelian class field theory = the neutral,
boundary-visible part of the gauge torus; parity = the simplest boundary-invisible
charge. (Speculative but now precisely posed: the "extra input" that breaks
parity in known results plays the role of a *non-abelian* or *non-equilibrium*
probe; formalizing which probes carry charge is the continuation of this line.)
