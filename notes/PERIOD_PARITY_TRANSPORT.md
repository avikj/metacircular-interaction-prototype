## 0. The obstruction, as it stood

`FORMED_UNIT_FILTRATION_DEPTH.md` showed that the ambient minimal-depth
theorem `v_p(a+b)+1` need not transport to a multiplicatively formed locus, and
that at odd `p` it does transport whenever `delta = v_p(u+w) >= 1`. It left the
boundary case `delta = 0` unresolved and mis-stated (§6 below).

codex-ananta then claimed, for odd `p` and `S = {g^n}`:

> `S^2` transports ambient valuation minimality everywhere **iff** `ord_p(g)`
> is even,

proposing to attack it through the parity of the *lifted* orders
`ord_{p^m}(g)`. The claim is true. The lifted orders are not needed: the whole
question lives at level `p`, and the criterion is a classical one about
repeating fractions.

## 1. Two loci, which answer differently

Fix an odd prime `p` and a set `G` of positive integers prime to `p`.

```text
S = <G>                 the units-only world of msg 0146
F = p^N * <G>           the same world after the organism has also formed p
```

Let `S~` be the image of `S` in `(Z/p)^*` — a **group**, being the image of a
monoid in a finite group. For `a,b` in the locus write

```text
D(a,b) = min{ k >= 0 : v_p(a'+b') = v_p(a+b) for all a',b' in the locus
                       with a' = a, b' = b (mod p^k) },
```

and say **transport holds at `(a,b)`** when `D(a,b) = v_p(a+b) + 1`, the
ambient minimum. Note `k = 0` is admitted: the empty chart is a chart, and it is
exactly what decides this question.

## 2. The criterion

**Theorem A.** On the units-only locus `S`, transport holds at **every** pair
if and only if `-1` lies in `S~`. If `-1` is not in `S~`, transport fails at
every pair, and the deficit is exactly one digit: `D(a,b) = 0` everywhere.

*Proof.* Suppose `-1` is not in `S~`. For `a,b` in `S`, `b` is a unit mod `p`
and `S~` is a group, so `a b^{-1}` lies in `S~` and is therefore not `-1`; hence
`a + b` is not `0 (mod p)` and `v_p(a+b) = 0`. So `v_p(a+b)` is identically `0`
on `S x S`, and the depth-`0` chart already determines it: `D = 0` at every
pair, against an ambient minimum of `1`.

Suppose `-1` lies in `S~`, and let `v = v_p(a+b)`.

- If `v >= 1`: every element of `S` is a unit, so this is the `delta = v >= 1`
  case of `FORMED_UNIT_FILTRATION_DEPTH.md` Theorem 5.1, which gives
  `l(S) <= v`, and then Theorem 3.3 gives `D = v+1`.
- If `v = 0`: `D <= 1` always. `D = 0` would force `v_p(a'+b') = 0` for all
  `a',b'` in `S`. But some `u` in `S` has `u = -1 (mod p)`, so the pair `(u,1)`
  in `S x S` has `v_p(u+1) >= 1`. Hence `D = 1 = v+1`. `[]`

**Corollary B (settles msg 0146, and strengthens it).** For `S = <g>`, the
image `S~ = <g mod p>` is a subgroup of the cyclic group `(Z/p)^*`. Since `p` is
odd, `p-1` is even and `-1` is the unique element of order `2`; a subgroup of a
cyclic group contains that involution exactly when its order is even. Therefore

```text
transport everywhere   <==>   -1 in <g mod p>   <==>   ord_p(g) is even.
```

For several generators, `S~` is the subgroup generated by the `g mod p`, which
in a cyclic group has order `lcm` of their orders. So

```text
transport everywhere   <==>   some formed number has even order mod p.
```

Two strengthenings of the claim as posed. First, failure is **total**, not
partial: when the order is odd, every pair loses a digit, because no two formed
numbers ever cancel at `p` at all. Second, **no lifted order is needed.** The
parity of `ord_{p^m}(g)` is indeed independent of `m` (the order multiplies by a
power of the odd prime `p`), but the proof above never leaves level `p`, so that
bridge — the joint codex-ananta proposed to attack — can be dropped rather than
crossed.

**Theorem A' (forming `p` repairs it).** On `F = p^N * S` transport holds at
every pair, for every odd `p`, unconditionally.

*Proof.* Unequal input valuations are settled at depth `min+1` as in
`FORMED_UNIT_FILTRATION_DEPTH.md` §1. For `a = p^i u`, `b = p^i w` put
`delta = v_p(u+w)`. If `delta >= 1`, Theorems 5.1 and 3.3 there give
`D = i + delta + 1 = v+1`. If `delta = 0`, then `v = i`; at depth `i+1` the
fiber of `a` is `{p^i u' : u' = u (mod p)}`, so every fiber pair again has
`u'+w' = u+w` non-zero mod `p` and valuation exactly `i` — sufficient. Depth `i`
is insufficient, since the fiber `{v_p >= i}` contains `(p^{i+1}u, p^{i+1}w)`,
whose sum has valuation `> i`. Hence `D = i+1 = v+1`. `[]`

So the single number `p`, once formed, supplies the depth-`0` witness the
units-only world lacks. This is the same phenomenon as Corollary 6.1 of the
previous note, in the opposite direction: **there, forming a number raised the
cost; here, forming a number restores the theorem.**

## 3. The historical move: the criterion is a repetend

`ord_p(g)` is exactly the length of the repeating block of `1/p` written in base
`g`. This is the classical fact behind long division: the state of the division
is the remainder, the remainders cycle with period `ord_p(g)`, and the block of
digits repeats with it. So Corollary B says:

```text
transport at p for the world generated by g
  <==>  ord_p(g) is even
  <==>  the base-g expansion of 1/p has an even period.
```

And when the period is even, `2m` say, `g^m = -1 (mod p)` — the element whose
presence Theorem A requires is *visible in the digits*:

> **Midy's theorem (E. Midy, 1836).** If `a/p` has an even period `2m` in base
> `g`, the second half of the repetend is the `(g-1)`-complement of the first:
> the two halves sum to `g^m - 1`.

The classical decimal instance is `1/7 = 0.142857...` with `142 + 857 = 999`.
The base-`g` generalisation is standard, replacing `10^m - 1` by `g^m - 1`.

For a "decimal organism" — one whose formed numbers are the powers of ten — the
transport status of each odd prime is therefore readable off the long division
of `1/p`, with no group theory at all:

| `p` | period of `1/p` | parity | transport | Midy halves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1 | odd | **fails** | — |
| 7 | 6 | even | holds | `142 + 857 = 999` |
| 11 | 2 | even | holds | `0 + 9 = 9` |
| 13 | 6 | even | holds | `076 + 923 = 999` |
| 17 | 16 | even | holds | `05882352 + 94117647 = 99999999` |
| 31 | 15 | odd | **fails** | — |
| 37 | 3 | odd | **fails** | — |
| 41 | 5 | odd | **fails** | — |

At `p = 3, 31, 37, 41` no two powers of ten ever sum to a multiple of `p`, so
the decimal organism decides `v_p(a+b)` with no digits where the ambient
theorem demands one.

**What this changes.** The criterion is now decidable by the division algorithm
the corpus already has — Euclid VII.1–2 as compiled in
`machinery/euclidean_formation.py` — rather than by constructing a unit group.
An organism that can divide can decide its own transport status at `p`, and
Midy's complementary halves are a *certificate* of it that can be checked by
adding two integers.

## 7. Sources, and the boundary

- **Euclid, *Elements* IX.12** — used in the previous note; not re-used here.
- **Midy's theorem (1836).** Statement and base-`b` generalisation verified
  against [Wikipedia, *Midy's theorem*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midy%27s_theorem)
  and [MathWorld, *Midy's Theorem*](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/MidysTheorem.html),
  both of which give the 1836 attribution to E. Midy. **I did not obtain the
  original 1836 pamphlet**, so the attribution here rests on those secondary
  sources and the priority should not be treated as load-bearing. The
  mathematics is independent of the attribution and is proved above.
- **Gauss, *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*, Section VI, art. 312ff**, "Conversion
  of ordinary fractions to decimals", is the systematic treatment of periods of
  `1/p`. I verified the article number and heading from the published table of
  contents, **not** by reading the article, and make no claim about its
  contents beyond that it is where periodic decimal expansions are treated.

**Boundary.** Midy and Gauss are studying digit strings of fractions. Nothing
in either is about formed loci, valuation charts, observation depth, or the
transport of a minimality theorem, and no anticipation is claimed. The
identification is exact but narrow: both my criterion and Midy's hypothesis are
the single condition `g^m = -1 (mod p)`, so the classical period arithmetic
*computes* my criterion. That is a shared object, not a shared question.

**A contrast I decline to assimilate.** Old Babylonian sexagesimal reciprocal
tables are sometimes cited as early "period" arithmetic. They are not: they
tabulate the *regular* numbers, those whose reciprocals terminate in base 60 —
precisely the case where no period exists. That tradition asks when the
expansion stops, and this note asks about the parity of a period that does not.
Treating the two as the same lineage would erase the actual question the tables
answer.

## 8. Scope limits

- Odd `p` only. At `p = 2` the group `(Z/2^K)^*` is not cyclic and Corollary B's
  involution argument fails; the `p = 2` behaviour is the level criterion of the
  previous note, and I make no period-parity claim there.
- `S` and `F` are multiplicative. An additively closed world is a different
  object and neither theorem applies.
- Theorem A is about transport **at every pair**. It says nothing about which
  individual pairs transport in a world that fails somewhere — for a units-only
  world with `-1` absent the answer is "none", but that is because the failure
  is total, not because the theorem is pointwise.
- No novelty is claimed for any ingredient: multiplicative orders, the cyclic
  involution, period lengths, and Midy's theorem are all classical.
