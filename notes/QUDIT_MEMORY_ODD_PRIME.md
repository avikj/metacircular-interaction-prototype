## 1. The multiplier law over `Z_d`, `d` odd prime

Let `V = Z_d^(2n)` with `<a,b> = x_a . z_b - x_b . z_a`. With
`X|j> = |j+1>`, `Z|j> = omega^j |j>`, `omega` a primitive `d`-th root of unity,
and `h = (d+1)/2 = 2^-1 mod d`, define

    D(x,z) = omega^(h x.z) X^x Z^z .

**Lemma 1.1.** `D(a) D(b) = omega^(-h <a,b>) D(a+b)`.

*Proof.* `Z^z X^x = omega^(x.z) X^x Z^z`, so

    (X^(x_a) Z^(z_a))(X^(x_b) Z^(z_b)) = omega^(x_b . z_a) X^(x_a+x_b) Z^(z_a+z_b).

Collecting the three `h`-prefactors, the exponent of `omega` in
`D(a)D(b) D(a+b)^-1` is

    h x_a.z_a + h x_b.z_b + x_b.z_a - h(x_a+x_b).(z_a+z_b)
      = x_b.z_a - h x_a.z_b - h x_b.z_a
      = -h (x_a.z_b - x_b.z_a) ,

using `1 - h = -h` in `Z_d`, which is exactly `2h = 1`. ∎

Verified independently on explicit **monomial matrices** over `Z_d` (entries
are exponents of `omega`, no floating roots) for `(n,d) = (1,3), (1,5), (2,3)`:
`matrix_control` in `machinery/qudit_context_memory.py`.

**Corollary 1.2 (no multiplier obstruction).** On any isotropic subspace `L`,
`<a,b> = 0` for all `a,b in L`, so `D|_L` is a *group homomorphism*: every
context product is the identity with **no phase**. Hence the `Z_d` system

    sum over v in L of  val(v)  =  phase(L)

that defines a noncontextual value assignment is homogeneous, and `val = 0`
solves it. No Mermin-type parity proof exists over an odd prime. ∎

This is the exact content of the folklore "odd `d` kills Mermin squares": the
step that fails is `1 - h = -h`, which is available precisely because `2` is
invertible. At `d = 2`, `h` does not exist and the multiplier is genuinely
nontrivial on contexts -- which is where `PAULI_MEMORY_LAGRANGIAN.md`'s ten
Mermin squares come from.

## 2. The memory count is unmoved

A memory state is a pair `(L, chi)`: a Lagrangian together with a character
`chi : L -> Z_d` recording measured eigenvalue exponents. By Corollary 1.2 no
compatibility condition constrains `chi`, so the fibre over each Lagrangian has
exactly `d^n` elements -- a torsor under `Hom(L, Z_d)`. Theorem 3.1 of the
companion note transfers verbatim (measurement preserves purity and maps
Lagrangians to Lagrangians), giving

    memory = |C_reach| * d^n .

Exhaustively verified, full Weyl scenario (all `d^(2n) - 1` nonzero vectors):

| `n` | `d` | observables | `\|C\|` | `\|C\| * d^n` | memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 8 | 4 | 12 | **12** |
| 1 | 5 | 24 | 6 | 30 | **30** |
| 1 | 7 | 48 | 8 | 56 | **56** |
| 2 | 3 | 80 | 40 | 360 | **360** |

Every row also reports `context_products_trivial = True`. The counts reproduce
the standard stabilizer-state numbers `d^n * prod_(k=1..n) (d^k + 1)`:
`3*4 = 12`, `5*6 = 30`, `7*8 = 56`, `9*4*10 = 360`. That agreement is the
independent check that the `(L, chi)` bookkeeping is the right one.

## 3. What this settles

**Theorem 3.1 (independence over a family).** For every odd prime `d` and every
`n`, the Weyl--Heisenberg scenario has **no** multiplier-generated contextuality
whatsoever, while its memory count `|C| * d^n` grows without bound in both `d`
and `n`.

Together with the qubit table in the companion note, the two coordinates of a
finite symplectic scenario are now separated on an infinite family rather than
at one point:

- the **multiplier class** decides contextuality and is trivial for all odd `d`;
- the **Lagrangian orbit** decides memory and never notices.

The mechanism is exactly the one Theorem 5.2 named: sign/character relabelling
acts on each fibre by a torsor automorphism, and a torsor automorphism cannot
change a cardinality. This is now demonstrated where the multiplier is not
merely relabelled but *identically trivial*, which is the strongest form of the
test.

## 4. Scope -- read this before citing

The vanishing in Corollary 1.2 is of the obstruction generated by the
**Weyl--Heisenberg multiplier on its own contexts**. It is emphatically **not**
a statement that dimension-`d` quantum systems are noncontextual. The original
Kochen--Specker theorem lives in dimension **3**, and state-independent
contextuality proofs exist for odd `d` using projector sets that are not Weyl
context covers. Nothing here touches those. Conflating "no Mermin-type parity
proof from the Weyl multiplier" with "noncontextual" would be a serious error,
and the exact scope of my `is_noncontextual` predicate is: *does the `Z_d`
system of context-product relations admit a solution?*

Also not claimed: any bound on non-unifilar or approximate classical models, on
composite (non-prime) `d`, where `Z_d` is not a field and the Lagrangian theory
changes, on circuit or thermodynamic cost, or on physical realization.
