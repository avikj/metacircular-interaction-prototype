# The incompleteness lens: parity as a conservation-law independence theorem

Chaitin's incompleteness theorem says a sound theory $T$ cannot certify
$K(x) > K(T) + c$: the theory's own information content is a ceiling on the
complexity it can attribute. The hint "apply this to the corpus" lands on a
precise target, and the arithmetic case turns out *cleaner* than Chaitin's:
for the parity barrier the ceiling is not $K(T)+c$ up to a machine constant
— it is **exactly zero**, by an algebraic conservation law, and the
independence witness demanded by INFORMATION_LENS §1 ("an actual two-point
fiber theorem rather than a suggestive parity label") can be exhibited
explicitly. This note assembles it from in-corpus parts, states what is
classical (Selberg), what is ours, and what the lens newly organizes.

## 1. The two-point fiber theorem (the witness)

Work on $\Omega = \{1,\dots,X\}$. Let $\lambda$ be Liouville and
$\sigma$ the gauge flip (GAUGE.md): the involution of weighted states
generated by $\lambda \mapsto -\lambda$. Define the **Selberg pair**

$$\nu_\pm \;=\; \bigl(1 \pm \lambda(n)\bigr)\,dn \quad\text{on } [1,X].$$

**Observation S (classical, Selberg 1949 in sieve form).** Both $\nu_\pm$
are nonnegative measures of total mass $X + O(\sqrt X\,e^{c\sqrt{\log X}})$;
they take *identical* values on every $\sigma$-even observable, and their
values on any observable $f$ differ by exactly $2\langle\lambda, f\rangle$
— the charged component carries **all** the separation. The prime target
separates maximally: $\nu_+(\text{primes}) = 0$ (since $\lambda(p) = -1$),
$\nu_-(\text{primes}) = 2\pi(X)$. For the twin problem the same swap with
the pair charge $c_2(n) = \lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)$ gives
$\nu^{(2)}_-(\text{twins}) = 0$, $\nu^{(2)}_+(\text{twins}) = 2\pi_2(X)$.

Measured (exp41, $X = 2\cdot10^6$): AP axiom-values agree with
$\max_a |\sum_{n\equiv a(q)}\lambda| / \sqrt{X/q}$ between $1.3$ and $4.0$
for $q$ from $3$ to $10^5$ (square-root scale, as LENS_CIRCUIT Theorems
1–2 prove on average); prime separation exactly $0$ vs $297{,}866 = 2\pi(X)$;
twin separation exactly $29{,}742 = 2\pi_2(X)$ vs $0$; the fiber identity
$E_{\nu_+}[f] - E_{\nu_-}[f] = 2\langle\lambda,f\rangle$ to $10^{-11}$.

## 2. Lemma C1: conservation of charge in positive derivations

**Lemma C1 (exact, finite, five lines).** Let $\mathcal A$ be any family of
affine axiom functionals with $A(\nu_+) = A(\nu_-)$ for all
$A \in \mathcal A$ (charge-even axioms — AP counts, sieve dimension data,
smooth-weight sums, anything $\sigma$-even). Suppose a derivation
establishes, for every nonnegative state $\nu$ in the class consistent with
the axiom values $\{A(\nu)\}_{A\in\mathcal A}$, the lower bound
$T(\nu) \ge \beta$ for a target functional $T$. Then
$$\beta \;\le\; \min\bigl(T(\nu_+),\,T(\nu_-)\bigr)
= \tfrac12\bigl(T_{\mathrm{even}}\bigr) - \bigl|\langle \text{charged part}\rangle\bigr|.$$
In particular for the prime target $\beta \le 0$: **no positive derivation
from charge-even axioms certifies a single prime**, and likewise for twins
with the pair charge. *Proof.* Both models are consistent with identical
axiom data; a derivation valid for all consistent states applies to the one
where the target's charged part enters with the unfavorable sign. $\square$

The lemma is trivial *given the witness pair* — which is the point: the
mathematical content of the parity barrier is the existence of the two-point
fiber, and everything else is bookkeeping. This is the proof-system shadow
of Theorem F (states on the neutral algebra carry no charge), of the twirl
annihilation (TOY_OBSTRUCTION), and of Theorem K (homotopy-invariant
functors of the gauge action see nothing): one conservation law, now in a
fourth category — **derivations**.

## 3. The Chaitin dictionary, made exact

| Chaitin / Gödel | sieve parity |
|---|---|
| theory $T$, sound, r.e. | positive cone generated by charge-even axioms |
| $K(T)$: information content of $T$ | charge content of the axiom family: **exactly 0** |
| $T \nvdash K(x) > K(T) + c$ | cone $\nvdash$ any charged conclusion (Lemma C1) |
| the machine constant $c$ | absent — the arithmetic case is exact |
| Berry-paradox compression proof | the swap: a derivation of a charged bound would "compress" the parity bit into neutral data, contradicting the fiber |
| unprovability ≠ falsity | twins presumably exist; the cone just cannot see them |
| adding $\mathrm{Con}(T)$ / reflection | adjoining a **charged axiom**: Type II bilinear sums (Vinogradov), monodromy (Sawin–Shusterman), entropy decrement (Tao) |
| $\Omega$: maximally incompressible | Chowla-flat $\lambda$: the charge sector with no structure visible to the cone |

Two escapes-from-incompleteness map precisely. Gödel escape: pass to a
stronger theory — arithmetic escape: adjoin axioms with nonzero charge.
Every historical parity breakthrough is of this form, and the lens says
*how much* charge is needed:

**Corollary C2 (charge-to-error threshold).** Real axiom systems hold with
error budgets ($A(\nu) = \text{main} + O(R_A)$). The Selberg pair satisfies
the same noisy axioms whenever the axiom's charge $|\langle\lambda,
a\rangle|$ is below its error budget $R_A$. Hence a family $\mathcal A$ can
support a charged conclusion **only if some member's charge exceeds its own
error budget**. LENS_CIRCUIT Theorems 1–2 prove the standard families (AP
data to BV levels, any lcm-bounded sieve circuit) sit *strictly below* the
threshold — making those no-go instances unconditional. Conversely
Vinogradov's Type II sums are precisely axioms whose charge
($\lambda$-bilinear correlations) is *proved* to exceed their error budget:
they are the reflection axioms of this arithmetic.

## 4. What the lens adds beyond re-description

1. **A fourth conservation theater.** The same $\mathbb Z/2$ now has
   annihilation theorems in states (F), cores (CORE_KMS), functors (K), and
   derivations (C1) — the last being the one proof-theorists would call
   independence. The UNIFICATION schema gains its logic column.
2. **Proof-mass lower bounds (Chaitin-quantitative, open but well-posed).**
   A derivation using noisy axioms with total dual mass $M$ transfers
   between the models up to $M \cdot \delta$ ($\delta$ = the axioms' actual
   charge). Certifying $\pi_2(X) \ge 1$ therefore forces
   $M \ge (\text{separation})/\delta \approx 2\pi_2(X)/X^{1/2+\varepsilon}$
   at BV levels: **sieve proofs of twins must be polynomially heavy** in a
   precise LP-dual sense. Making this a theorem for a formalized proof
   system (the positive cone over SIEVE_d(S,Q) of LENS_CIRCUIT) is the new
   open rung; it is the arithmetic analog of "short proofs cannot certify
   high complexity."
3. **The reflexive reading (MATH_OS).** The discovery OS's authority
   lattice is itself a $K(T)$ budget: `certified` is bounded by the kernel's
   information (Lean axioms + validator), and Codex's INFORMATION_LENS §3
   discipline ("no complexity estimate as theorem without a code or
   counting proof") is exactly the discipline Chaitin's theorem imposes on
   any self-improving prover. The registry cannot certify its way past its
   own trusted base — it can only *adjoin* to it, auditedly, as C2's
   charged axioms do.

## 5. Rigor boundary and prior art

Classical: the Selberg pair and the parity phenomenon (Selberg 1949;
Bombieri's "the sieve is unable to distinguish…"; Friedlander–Iwaniec
Opera de Cribro Ch. 16 discusses parity in exactly the two-weight form).
Lemma C1 as stated is elementary; we claim no novelty for it in isolation.
Ours: the audit-card formalization (the exact fiber/class statement), the
four-theater conservation assembly (F/CORE_KMS/K/C1 as one law), Corollary
C2's charge-to-error threshold with LENS_CIRCUIT making instances
unconditional, and the proof-mass program of §4.2. The dictionary row
"Type II = reflection axioms" is a reading, not a theorem. Lean target:
C1 and the audit card's fiber statement are finite and elementary —
natural companions to Codex's `FiniteInformation.lean` lane.
