# Apoha changes the type of alignment

**Status:** source-critical correction to a repository interface. This note
does not formalize Buddhist cognition as an institution or identify apoha with
a quotient, complement, classifier, or software filter.

## 1. The question inherited from the Nyāya audit

`PRAMANA_IS_NOT_AN_EVIDENCE_RANK.md` concluded that a new probe should carry
an object-alignment witness and a defeater family before the response square
can audit its preservation. That conclusion was explicitly Nyāya-scoped, but
the phrase “object alignment” can still smuggle in a positive conceptual
object or universal.

The Buddhist objection changes the type of that slot. It does not merely add
another defeater to the same record.

## 2. Dignāga: word meaning is not one of four positive candidates

In *Pramāṇasamuccaya(vṛtti)* V.2–11, Dignāga rejects an individual
(`bheda`), a genus (`jāti`), their relation (`sambandha`), and a bearer of a
genus (`jātimat`/`tadvat`) as the word's positive referent; V.11 concludes
`tenānyāpohakṛc chrutiḥ`, that an expression effects exclusion of others.
V.25cd–38 then asks what counts as the “other”: synonyms need not exclude one
another, subordinate and superordinate terms interact asymmetrically, and
incompatible coordinate terms exclude directly. Apoha is therefore not an
untyped Boolean complement of a pre-given set.

This already blocks the naive witness

```text
Align₊(q) = a proof that q's conceptual output presents a real universal U
```

as a tradition-neutral requirement. It assumes one of the candidates under
dispute.

## 3. Dharmakīrti: causal production does not restore the universal

Dharmakīrti's *Pramāṇavārttika* I.119–121 and autocommentary give a more
specific route. Different particulars can produce the same cognition; a
cognizer separates causes of that cognition from causes of another cognition,
and an expression is connected to this difference. The cognition sees the
different particulars as if they were one real thing **through error**.

PV III.165–173 keeps both sides apart. The reflection appearing in
word-produced cognition is not the object's nature; yet the cognition accords
with the object's differentiation from others. At convention time an indicated
particular occasions cognition of such exclusion, and a word is connected by
its occurrence for some things and non-occurrence for others—not to a real
genus in the object.

PV I.115–121 also makes activity load-bearing. If convention did not exclude
what is other, later activity would fail to avoid undesired non-instances.
This is not the claim that every successful action makes a cognition true, nor
that `arthakriyā = benchmark success`.

## 4. Exact repository correction

A single positive `AlignmentWitness(q, U)` is too narrow. The least modern
interface that does not decide the historical dispute in advance is a sum of
incompatible warrant shapes:

```text
ProbeWarrant(q) =
    PositivePresentation(object, positiveMode, production, defeat)
  | ExclusionAlignment(
        indicatedParticulars,
        causalHistory,
        convention,
        excludedAlternatives,
        activityTest,
        failureConditions)
  | OtherWarrant(...)
```

For the apoha branch, the exact negative constraint is:

```text
no field : RealUniversal
no field : conceptualOutput = intrinsicNature(indicatedParticular)
```

and the positive obligations are relational and historical:

1. name the particular occurrence(s) that causally occasion the episode;
2. name the convention under which the expression is connected;
3. delimit what is excluded—synonym, coordinate, and superordinate cases
   cannot be flattened to one complement operation;
4. record the activity whose discrimination would fail if non-instances were
   not excluded;
5. record failure conditions without pretending that “defeater family” is
   Dharmakīrti's own data type.

Thus the **functional need** for failure conditions survives. The positive
object witness changes type. Dignāga's scope-sensitive account and
Dharmakīrti's causal/error account must remain distinguishable inside the
second branch; “apoha” alone does not erase their difference.

## 5. Why the satisfaction square remains external

The repository theorem

\[
x'\Vdash(\tau q,y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s(x')\Vdash(q,y)
\]

audits preservation after `q`, `τ`, response semantics, and the state map are
supplied. It neither constructs the causal history and convention above nor
decides the scope of exclusion. Conversely, Dignāga and Dharmakīrti do not
prove this atomic satisfaction theorem. There is no Rosetta equality here:

```text
formation/warrant shape | supplied-translation preservation audit
```

The apoha encounter changes the left-hand type; the right-hand theorem remains
valid for whichever response semantics is eventually supplied.

## 6. Provenance and untranslated residual

**Primary text through source-critical editions/translations:**

- Dignāga, *Pramāṇasamuccaya(vṛtti)* V. The Sanskrit is not transmitted as a
  complete independent witness; Ernst Steinkellner and Ole Holten Pind's
  reconstruction from Sanskrit citations and Tibetan witnesses underlies the
  verse numbering used here. Shōryū Katsura's direct study of V.25cd–38 was
  checked for the exclusion-scope distinctions. This is not equivalent to
  reading an intact Sanskrit manuscript.
- Dharmakīrti, *Pramāṇavārttika* I.115–121, III.165–173 and
  *Pramāṇavārttikasvavṛtti* 59–60. Patrick McAllister's annotated translation
  was checked together with its displayed Sanskrit/Tibetan/commentarial
  apparatus. Its notes explicitly record consequential alternative parses;
  those remain source uncertainty, not implementation freedom.

**Later reconstruction:** the language “positive alignment,” “warrant sum,”
and the displayed interface are this repository's modern repair. They are not
translations of a Sanskrit record type.

**Untranslated:** momentariness and the two-pramāṇa architecture; the precise
relation of perception, conceptual construction (`vikalpa`), determination
(`adhyavasāya`), and action; Dignāga–Dharmakīrti differences; later Buddhist
disputes over positive/negative apoha; karmic `vāsanā`; liberation-directed
normativity; and Nyāya/Mīmāṃsā replies. Nothing here licenses a universal
“Indian epistemology” interface.

## Rigor boundary

**Textually supported:** the rejection of the four positive word-referent
candidates; scope-sensitive exclusion; Dharmakīrti's causal same-effect route,
erroneous sameness appearance, convention, and activity consequence.

**Exact modern consequence:** a requirement typed only by a positive real
universal begs the apoha dispute. A sum/interface that permits a
causal-conventional exclusion warrant avoids that specific presupposition.

**Not proved:** that the displayed interface is sufficient for Buddhist valid
cognition, that successful action alone warrants truth, or that any current
repository probe satisfies the apoha branch.
