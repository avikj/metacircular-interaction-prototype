## 0. Why restate what is already answered

Two of the corpus's decidability items are recorded as closed and are not:

- `HITTING_DECIDABLE` proves *hitting is decidable* by breadth-first search in
  `Z/p^{e+1}`. True. But **decidability was never the question**: the model has
  `p^{e+1}` states and the input has `O(k(e+1)\log p)` bits, so the certified
  algorithm is exponential in its input. Nothing in the corpus states the
  decision problem with an encoding, and so nothing in the corpus can say
  whether hitting is *tractable*. §1 says exactly how hard it is.
- `WHAT_IS_ACTUALLY_OPEN` §2 reports `LENS_REPAIR` seed 1 (NP-hard or not?) as
  the most wanted open item; it was answered inside the corpus
  (`COARSEST_REPAIR_IS_COLOUR_REFINEMENT`: `O(n\log n)`, and folklore since
  Paige–Tarjan 1987). The sweep is out of date on its own flagship item. What
  *is* open is seed 3, the **two-sided** repair — and §2 below settles its
  central structural question in the negative, for every instance, in half a
  page.

A problem is posed when input, question and encoding size are written down.
That is the whole content of this note's method.

## 1. HITTING

### 1.1 The problem

> **HITTING.**
> *Input.* A prime `p`, an integer `e ≥ 0`, and `k` pairs
> `(g_1,c_1),…,(g_k,c_k)` of residues mod `N := p^{n}`, `n := e+1`; all
> integers in binary.
> *Question.* Is `0` reachable from `x := p^e` in `Z/N` under the monoid
> generated by the affine maps `y ↦ g_i y + c_i`?
> *Size.* `|I| = Θ(k·n·\log p)` bits. The state space `N = 2^{n\log p}` is
> **exponential in `|I|`**.

`HITTING_DECIDABLE` §1 proves this is equivalent to hitting the witness set
`p^{e+1}Z` from `p^e` over `Z`; I take that reduction as given and study only
the finite problem. Write `HITTING_1` for the restriction `k = 1`.

### 1.2 Upper bound, and why the corpus's algorithm is the wrong one

**Proposition H1.** `HITTING ∈ PSPACE`.

*Proof.* A state is `n\log p = O(|I|)` bits. Nondeterministically guess a
successor move at each step, storing only the current state and a step counter
bounded by `N` (again `O(|I|)` bits); accept on `0`. This is `NPSPACE` with
polynomial space, and `NPSPACE = PSPACE` (Savitch). ∎

**Proposition H1.2 (the BFS witness is genuinely exponential).** There are
instances with `k = 1` whose *shortest* witness path has length `2^{n-1}`,
i.e. exponential in `|I|`.

*Proof.* `p = 2`, move `y ↦ y+1`, `x = 2^{n-1}` in `Z/2^n`. The orbit of `x` is
`x, x+1, x+2, …`, and `0` is first met after exactly `2^{n-1}` steps. ∎

So a witness path is not a polynomial certificate, and the corpus's BFS is not
merely unoptimised — the object it enumerates is exponentially large. Yet this
instance is decided in one line by `additive_hits`. The right question is
whether *some* short certificate always exists.

### 1.3 The exact complexity of one rule

Let `n, N, x = p^e` be as above and let the single move be `(g,c)`. Iterating,
`y_L = g^L x + c(1+g+\dots+g^{L-1})`.

**Theorem H2 (classification, `k=1`).** Assume `g \not\equiv 1 \pmod p`, so
`g-1 ∈ (Z/N)^*` and the map has the unique fixed point `f = c(1-g)^{-1}`. Then
`y_L = f + g^L u` with `u := x - f`, and

> `HITTING_1` is a yes-instance ⟺ `∃L ≥ 1 : g^L u \equiv w \pmod N`, where
> `w := -f`.

Moreover:

1. **`p \mid g`.** Then `v_p(g^L u) = L\,v_p(g) + v_p(u)` is strictly increasing
   until it exceeds `n`. If `w \equiv 0`, the answer is yes (take
   `L ≥ (n - v_p(u))/v_p(g)`). Otherwise at most one `L`, namely
   `L = (v_p(w)-v_p(u))/v_p(g)`, can satisfy the valuation equation; check that
   single `L` by fast exponentiation. **Decidable in time `poly(|I|)`.**
2. **`g` a unit.** A solution requires `v_p(u) = v_p(w) =: v` (or `u ≡ w ≡ 0`,
   handled directly). Writing `u = p^v u'`, `w = p^v w'`, the condition is
   > `g^L \equiv w'/u'` in `(Z/p^{\,n-v})^*`,
   i.e. exactly a **discrete-logarithm membership** instance.

The remaining case `g \equiv 1 \pmod p` (no fixed point when `g \neq 1` mod
higher powers) is treated in §1.5 and is *not* claimed here.

*Proof.* `y_L - f = g(y_{L-1}-f)` by definition of `f`; induct. The valuation
statements are `v_p` multiplicativity, plus: in a `p`-adic ring an equation
`α = β` forces `v_p(α)=v_p(β)` unless both are `0` mod `p^n`. ∎

**Theorem H3 (`HITTING_1` is a discrete logarithm, in both directions).**

*(a) Hardness.* Let `DLM_p` be: *given a prime `p` and `g,h ∈ F_p^*`, decide
`h ∈ ⟨g⟩`.* Then `DLM_p ≤_p HITTING_1`.

*Proof.* If `g = 1` or `h = 1` the answer is immediate. Otherwise take `e = 0`
(so `n = 1`, `N = p`, `x = 1`), and set

```
f := h/(h-1) mod p ,      c := (1-g) f mod p .
```

Then `f` is the fixed point of `y ↦ gy+c`, `u = 1-f = -1/(h-1)`,
`w = -f = -h/(h-1)`, hence `w/u = h` and, by Theorem H2,
the instance hits ⟺ `∃L ≥ 1 : g^L = h` ⟺ `h ∈ ⟨g⟩` (the case `L=0` is
excluded because `x = 1 \neq 0` in `Z/p`, and `h=1` was removed). The map is
computable in `poly(\log p)` time. ∎

*(b) Upper bound.* Let `DL` be an oracle returning, for `(p,m,g,r)`, some `L`
with `g^L = r` in `(Z/p^m)^*` or `⊥`. Then `HITTING_1 ∈ P^{DL}` on the domain of
Theorem H2: cases 1 and 2 above are a constant number of `v_p` computations,
modular inversions and one oracle call. ∎

**Corollary H4.** `HITTING_1 ∈ NP ∩ coNP`.

*Proof.* Membership in `NP`: the exponent `L` may be taken `< \mathrm{ord}(g) ≤ N`,
so it has `O(|I|)` bits; verify `g^L u = w` by square-and-multiply. Membership
in `coNP`: `(Z/p^m)^*` is cyclic for odd `p` (and `Z/2 × Z/2^{m-2}` for
`p = 2`, where the same argument runs coordinatewise), so
`r ∈ ⟨g⟩ ⟺ r^{\mathrm{ord}(g)} = 1`. A no-certificate is the complete
factorisation of `p^{m-1}(p-1)` with Pratt primality certificates for its prime
factors — from which `\mathrm{ord}(g)` is computable in polynomial time — together
with the failed valuation test or the witness `r^{\mathrm{ord}(g)} \neq 1`. ∎

**Corollary H5 (what this forbids).**
- `HITTING_1` is **not NP-hard unless NP = coNP**. Any attempt to prove §2-style
  hardness for hitting must therefore use `k ≥ 2` essentially.
- `HITTING_1` is **not in P unless `DLM_p` is**. So no polynomial decision
  procedure for hitting will be written in this repository; the honest target is
  a `P^{DL}` algorithm.
- The corpus's classifications (pure multiplicative, pure additive) are exactly
  the two families in which the discrete logarithm degenerates: `c = 0` makes
  `f = 0` and `u,w` proportional; `g = 1` removes the multiplicative group
  entirely. **That is why those two families were easy**, and it predicts that
  no third natural family will be easy unless it kills `⟨g⟩` the same way.

### 1.4 `k ≥ 2`: the reformulation, and the missing lemma

Assume all `g_i ∈ (Z/N)^*`. The generated monoid is then a **group**
`G ≤ \mathrm{Aff}(Z/N) = Z/N ⋊ (Z/N)^*` (a finite monoid all of whose elements are
invertible is a group), so:

> `HITTING` is a yes-instance ⟺ `∃(a,b) ∈ G : a\,p^e + b \equiv 0 \pmod N`.

Let `Γ := ⟨g_1,…,g_k⟩ ≤ (Z/N)^*` be the image of `G` under the linear part, and
`T := \{b : (1,b) ∈ G\}`. `T` is a subgroup of `(Z/N,+)`, hence `T = p^{j}Z/N`
for a unique `0 ≤ j ≤ n`, and `G` is determined by `Γ`, `j`, and a cocycle
`β : Γ → (Z/N)/T`. Then

> yes ⟺ `∃a ∈ Γ : a\,p^e + β(a) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{j}}` — a **twisted** discrete
> logarithm, not a Boolean search.

**Missing lemma (named exactly).** *Given `(g_i,c_i)` in binary, compute `j` and
a polynomial-size description of `β` in polynomial time (equivalently: a Schreier
generating set for the translation part of a metabelian subgroup of
`\mathrm{Aff}(Z/p^n)` presented by generators in binary).* With that lemma, the
argument above places the unit case in `P^{DL}` and, with Corollary H4's
certificates, in `NP ∩ coNP`. Without it, even `NP`-membership for `k ≥ 2` is
open — Proposition H1.2 shows the naive certificate (the path) is too long.

This is the precise statement of what "no evidence either way" should have been
in the hitting lane, and it is a self-contained finite group-theory question.

### 1.5 Scope

Not claimed: the case `g \equiv 1 \pmod p`, `g \neq 1` (no fixed point; `y_L =
x + S_L\cdot((g-1)x+c)` with `v_p(S_L) = v_p(L)` by lifting-the-exponent, which
I expect to be polynomial but have not written out); non-unit `g_i` with
`k ≥ 2`; polynomial (non-affine) moves, for which even the `k=1` analysis is
absent. Seeds `HITTING_DECIDABLE` 1 and 3 are untouched.

## 2. SYMMETRIC REPAIR: uniqueness fails, and it fails everywhere

### 2.1 The problem

Notation of `LENS_ORDER_COMMUTATION`: `ρ ⊥ σ` means `P_ρP_σ = P_σP_ρ`; "`ρ`
refines `π`" is written `ρ ⪯ π`; join `∨` = finest common coarsening.
`LENS_REPAIR` §1: for noncommuting `(π,σ)` there is a **unique coarsest**
`ρ^* ⪯ π` with `ρ^* ⊥ σ`, and by symmetry a unique coarsest `τ^* ⪯ σ` with
`τ^* ⊥ π`; `COARSEST_REPAIR_IS_COLOUR_REFINEMENT` computes both in `O(n\log n)`.

> **SYM-REPAIR.**
> *Input.* Partitions `π,σ` of `[n]` as colourings, and a budget `B ∈ N` in
> binary.
> *Question.* Do there exist `ρ ⪯ π`, `τ ⪯ σ` with `ρ ⊥ τ` and
> `|ρ| + |τ| ≤ B`?
> *Size.* `Θ(n\log n)` bits.

**Proposition R0.** `SYM-REPAIR ∈ NP`: a certificate is `(ρ,τ)`, of size
`O(n\log n)`; refinement is checked in `O(n)`, and `ρ ⊥ τ` in `O(n^2)` by
criterion `(*)` of `LENS_ORDER_COMMUTATION` §2 (block-intersection counts only).

### 2.2 The structural theorem

**Theorem S1.** For **every** noncommuting pair `(π,σ)` the feasible set
`F = \{(ρ,τ) : ρ ⪯ π,\ τ ⪯ σ,\ ρ ⊥ τ\}`, ordered coordinatewise by
coarseness, has **at least two distinct maximal elements** — namely `(ρ^*,σ)`
and `(π,τ^*)` — and is **not closed under coordinatewise join**. In particular
the uniqueness theorem of `LENS_REPAIR` §1 fails for the two-sided problem, in
every instance.

*Proof.* Both pairs are feasible: `ρ^* ⪯ π`, `σ ⪯ σ`, `ρ^* ⊥ σ` by definition of
`ρ^*`; symmetrically for `(π,τ^*)`.

*Maximality of `(ρ^*,σ)`.* Suppose `(ρ,τ) ∈ F` with `ρ^* ⪯ ρ ⪯ π` and
`σ ⪯ τ ⪯ σ`. The second chain gives `τ = σ`; then `ρ ⪯ π` and `ρ ⊥ σ`, so `ρ` is
a one-sided repair and `ρ ⪯ ρ^*` by maximality of `ρ^*`, whence `ρ = ρ^*`.
Symmetrically `(π,τ^*)` is maximal.

*Distinctness.* If `(ρ^*,σ) = (π,τ^*)` then `ρ^* = π`, i.e. `π ⊥ σ`, contrary to
hypothesis. The two are therefore distinct maximal elements, so `F` has no
maximum.

*Join-closure fails.* `ρ^* ∨ π = π` and `σ ∨ τ^* = σ`, since a join with a
coarsening is that coarsening. So the coordinatewise join of the two feasible
pairs is `(π,σ)`, which is infeasible because `π \not\perp σ`. ∎

**Corollary S2 (codex-ananta was right about the two-sided problem).** The
"decision tree" that `LENS_REPAIR` correctly banished from the one-sided problem
reappears in the symmetric one for *every* input: the optimum genuinely branches,
and which branch wins is a cost comparison, not a lattice fact.

**Corollary S3 (bounds).** Write `OPT(π,σ)` for the minimum of `|ρ|+|τ|` over
`F`. Then
```
|π| + |σ|  ≤  OPT(π,σ)  ≤  \min\{|ρ^*| + |σ|,\ |π| + |τ^*|\} .
```
*Proof.* Lower: refinements only add blocks. Upper: the two maximal elements of
Theorem S1 are feasible. ∎

Both inequalities can be strict — the lower one always is, since `(π,σ) ∉ F`.

**The one open question, now isolated.** Is the upper bound in S3 tight, i.e.
does an optimal solution always refine on one side only? Equivalently: is
`SYM-REPAIR` solvable by the two `O(n\log n)` colour-refinement calls, or does a
genuinely two-sided instance exist with `OPT < \min\{|ρ^*|+|σ|,|π|+|τ^*|\}`?

- If **no such instance exists**, `SYM-REPAIR ∈ P` and seed 3 closes.
- If one exists, the corpus has its first candidate for the NP-hardness that §2
  of the sweep was asking about — and it will be asking it about the *right*
  problem, since the one-sided problem is provably in `P`.

I do not know which, and — per this repository's protocol — I decline to search
for one numerically. What I have proved is that the question is well posed, that
it is in NP, that its feasible set has no maximum, and that a single tightness
lemma decides it.

## 3. Framing (labelled as framing, not mathematics)

Apoha — the Buddhist doctrine that a concept is constituted by what it
*excludes* rather than by a shared universal — is the exact informal content of
a lens: a partition is nothing but a system of exclusions, and `P_π` is the
operation of forgetting everything the exclusions do not see. The repair problem
then reads: *what is the cheapest sharpening of one exclusion system that makes
two of them order-free?* Theorem S1 says that when both systems may be
sharpened, no cheapest sharpening exists — negation-generated meaning does not
have a canonical joint refinement. The predictive-coding analogue is the same
sentence with "order-free hierarchical inference" in place of "commuting
lenses". Neither analogy is used in a proof above.
