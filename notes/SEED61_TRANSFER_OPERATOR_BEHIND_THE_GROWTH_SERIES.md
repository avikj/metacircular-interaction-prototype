## 1. The mechanical method (heuristic — NOT a proof)

Archimedes weighed the segment against the triangle before he exhausted it,
and said which was which. Here is the weighing.

SEED-08 produced three shapes at once:

1. a growth series that is rational with **degree-two** denominator
   $1-Dx-Ex^2$, at *every* level $N$, no matter how many free-product
   factors there are;
2. a **two-term integer recursion** $c_n = Dc_{n-1}+Ec_{n-2}$;
3. a closed-orbit count (its Theorem 4) of the form
   $\mathcal N_\ell = \frac1\ell\sum_{d\mid\ell}\varphi(d)\operatorname{tr}
   \bigl(M(x^d)^{\ell/d}\bigr)$, with an explicit $k\times k$ matrix $M(x)$.

Read as a physicist reads a spectrum: (2) says the whole sequence is
governed by a **two-dimensional space**, whatever the value of $k$; (3) says
the counting is a **trace of powers of an operator**, which is the
Lefschetz/Frobenius shape; (1) says the generating function is the resolvent
of that operator. Then the guess writes itself:

> **The mechanical guess.** $1-Dx-Ex^2$ is, up to units and to the cancelling
> numerator, the *characteristic polynomial* $\det(I-M(x))$ of the transfer
> operator of the alternation dynamics; the growth rate $\lambda_N$ is its
> Perron eigenvalue; the closed-orbit count is $\frac{d}{dt}\log$ of a zeta
> function $Z=1/\det(I-tM)$; and $Z$ has an Euler product over primitive
> closed orbits. The collapse from $k$ dimensions to $2$ happens because the
> $k$ local factors take only **three** distinct values, so the operator has
> huge multiplicity and only three-ish distinct eigenvalues.

A second weighing, on the same balance. When $\nu_3=0$ the eigenvalue is the
*integer* $\mu/3+1$, and it is independent of $\nu_2$ and of the genus. An
integer Perron eigenvalue with a $(q+1)$-regular smell is what one gets from
a **tree**: a $(q+1)$-regular tree has sphere sizes $(q+1)q^{n-1}$. So the
guess sharpens to: *when $\nu_3=0$ the Cayley graph is a regular tree of
degree $\mu/3+2$, and $\nu_2$ cannot matter because the tree only sees the
total valence.* Where does $\nu_3$ break it? $\sigma_{\mathbb Z/3}=1+2x$ is
the growth series of a **triangle**, not of a tree. Cycles are what make the
eigenvalue irrational.

## 2. Setting and notation (following SEED-08 §2, §6)

$G=G_1*\cdots*G_k$, each $G_i$ generated by a finite symmetric $S_i$,
$S=\bigsqcup_i S_i$. Write $\sigma_i(x)=\sigma_{G_i}(x)\in\mathbb Z[[x]]$ for
the spherical growth series of $G_i$ in $S_i$ and
$a_i(x)=\sigma_i(x)-1\in x\mathbb Z[[x]]$, so $a_i$ enumerates the
*nontrivial* elements of $G_i$ by $S_i$-length. Put

$$A=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\dots,a_k),\qquad C_{ij}=[\,i\neq j\,],\qquad
M(x):=CA,\quad\text{i.e. } M_{ij}=[\,i\neq j\,]\,a_j(x),$$

which is exactly SEED-08's transfer matrix. All identities below are in the
commutative ring $R=\mathbb Q[[x,t]]$ (or $\mathbb Z[[x,t]]$ where stated);
every series that occurs has, in each total degree, only finitely many
contributing terms, so all manipulations are legitimate formal ones.

We use SEED-08's Lemma 1 (lengths add along the free-product normal form) as
an input; it is proved there and I do not reprove it.

## 3. Theorem A — Chiswell's formula is a determinant

> **Theorem A.** In $\mathbb Q[[x,t]]$,
> $$\det\bigl(I-t\,M(x)\bigr)\;=\;\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(1+t\,a_i\bigr)\Bigr)
> \cdot\Bigl(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{t\,a_i}{1+t\,a_i}\Bigr).$$
> In particular, at $t=1$,
> $$\det\bigl(I-M(x)\bigr)\;=\;\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^k\sigma_i(x)\Bigr)
> \Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^k\frac1{\sigma_i(x)}-(k-1)\Bigr)
> \;=\;\frac{\prod_{i=1}^k\sigma_i(x)}{\sigma_G(x)},$$
> the last equality being SEED-08's Theorem 1 (Chiswell). Equivalently:
> $$\boxed{\;\sigma_G(x)\;=\;\frac{\prod_i\sigma_i(x)}{\det\bigl(I-M(x)\bigr)}\;}$$

*Proof.* Write $J$ for the all-ones $k\times k$ matrix and $\mathbf 1$ for
the all-ones column, $a^{\mathsf T}=(a_1,\dots,a_k)$. Since $C=J-I$,
$$I-tCA \;=\; I+tA-tJA \;=\; D_t-t\,\mathbf 1\,a^{\mathsf T},
\qquad D_t:=I+tA=\operatorname{diag}(1+ta_i).$$
$D_t$ is invertible in $R$ because $1+ta_i$ has constant term $1$. The
matrix-determinant lemma (rank-one update; over any commutative ring, for
invertible $D$: $\det(D-uv^{\mathsf T})=\det(D)\,(1-v^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}u)$)
gives
$$\det(I-tM)=\det(D_t)\Bigl(1-a^{\mathsf T}D_t^{-1}\,t\mathbf 1\Bigr)
=\prod_i(1+ta_i)\cdot\Bigl(1-\sum_i\frac{ta_i}{1+ta_i}\Bigr).$$
At $t=1$: $1+a_i=\sigma_i$ and $a_i/\sigma_i=1-1/\sigma_i$, so the second
factor is $1-\sum_i(1-1/\sigma_i)=\sum_i 1/\sigma_i-(k-1)$. By SEED-08's
Theorem 1 that equals $1/\sigma_G$. $\square$

**What Theorem A buys.** SEED-08 proves $1/\sigma_G=\sum1/\sigma_i-(k-1)$ by
a normal-form bijection and calls it additive bookkeeping. Theorem A says the
right-hand side is not a bookkeeping identity at all: it is a
**characteristic polynomial**, and the "$-(k-1)$" is the rank-one term of a
determinant. That is the difference between a coincidence and an operator.

**Corollary A1 (the growth rate is a spectral radius, exactly).** Let
$\rho>0$ be the radius of convergence of $\sigma_G$ as a real power series
(it is positive since $c_n\le|S|^n$). Then $\rho$ is the smallest positive
real zero of $\det(I-M(x))$ at which no cancellation with
$\prod_i\sigma_i(x)$ occurs, and $\lambda=1/\rho$. *Proof.* Immediate from
the boxed formula plus Pringsheim (a series with non-negative coefficients
has a singularity at its radius of convergence), as in SEED-08 Theorem 3.
$\square$

## 4. Theorem B — the zeta function and its Euler product

Call a tuple $(h_1,\dots,h_\ell)$ with $h_j\in G_{i_j}\setminus\{1\}$
**cyclically alternating** if $i_j\neq i_{j+1}$ for all $j$ read cyclically
(so $i_\ell\neq i_1$ too). A **necklace** is such a tuple up to cyclic
rotation; it is **primitive** if its stabiliser in the rotation group
$C_\ell$ is trivial. For a necklace $p$ put $\ell(p)$ = its length,
$|p|=\sum_j|h_j|_{S_{i_j}}$ its total $S$-length, and
$w(p)=t^{\ell(p)}x^{|p|}$.

Define the **graded dynamical zeta function**
$$Z_G(x,t)\;:=\;\exp\Bigl(\sum_{\ell\ge1}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(M(x)^\ell\bigr)
\frac{t^\ell}{\ell}\Bigr)\ \in\ \mathbb Q[[x,t]].$$

> **Theorem B.** In $\mathbb Q[[x,t]]$,
> $$\boxed{\;Z_G(x,t)\;=\;\frac{1}{\det\bigl(I-t\,M(x)\bigr)}
> \;=\;\prod_{p\ \text{primitive necklace}}\bigl(1-w(p)\bigr)^{-1}.\;}$$
> The middle term is a **rational** function of $t$ of degree $\le k$; the
> right-hand term is an **Euler product over the primitive closed orbits**;
> and by SEED-08's conjugacy input the primitive necklaces of length
> $\ell\ge2$ are exactly the conjugacy classes of $G$ of cyclically reduced
> syllable length $\ell$ that are not conjugate into any factor and are not
> proper powers.

*Proof.* Three steps.

*(i) Trace = marked closed orbits.* By definition of $M$,
$\operatorname{tr}(M^\ell)=\sum a_{i_1}a_{i_2}\cdots a_{i_\ell}$ over
sequences $i_1,\dots,i_\ell$ with $i_j\neq i_{j+1}$ cyclically. Expanding
each $a_i(x)=\sum_{h\in G_i\setminus\{1\}}x^{|h|}$ and using that
$S$-lengths add along the normal form (SEED-08 Lemma 1), $\operatorname{tr}
(M(x)^\ell)=\sum x^{|{\rm tuple}|}$ over cyclically alternating tuples of
length $\ell$ *with a marked starting position*. Note
$\operatorname{tr}(M)=0$: $M$ has zero diagonal, so there are no orbits of
length $1$ — the analogue of "no closed orbit lies inside a single factor".

*(ii) Determinant.* Over a commutative $\mathbb Q$-algebra, for a matrix $N$
with entries in the maximal ideal (here $tM$, whose entries lie in
$(t)$), $\log\det(I-tM)=\operatorname{tr}\log(I-tM)=
-\sum_{\ell\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(M^\ell)t^\ell/\ell$; exponentiating gives
$Z_G=1/\det(I-tM)$. Rationality in $t$ of degree $\le k$ is then the
statement that a $k\times k$ determinant is a polynomial of degree $\le k$
in $t$.

*(iii) Euler product.* Each marked cyclically alternating tuple of length
$\ell$ is uniquely a $d$-fold repetition of a primitive necklace $p$ with
$d\,\ell(p)=\ell$, together with one of $\ell(p)$ choices of marking; the
repetition multiplies the $x$-weight by $d$, so its weight is $w(p)^d$ in the
$(t,x)$-grading. Hence
$$\sum_{\ell\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(M^\ell)\frac{t^\ell}{\ell}
=\sum_{p\ \text{prim}}\ \sum_{d\ge1}\frac{\ell(p)\,w(p)^d}{d\,\ell(p)}
=\sum_{p}\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{w(p)^d}{d}
=-\sum_{p}\log\bigl(1-w(p)\bigr).$$
Exponentiating gives the product. The rearrangement is legitimate formally:
every syllable has $|h|\ge1$, so a necklace of length $\ell$ has $x$-degree
$\ge\ell$, and only finitely many $p$ contribute to any fixed total degree.
The last sentence of the statement is the conjugacy theorem for free products
(Magnus–Karrass–Solitar Thm 4.6), quoted by SEED-08 in its Theorem 4 and
quoted, not reproved, here. $\square$

> **Theorem C (SEED-08's Theorem 4, re-derived, and its primitive form).**
> Let $\mathcal N_\ell(x)$ be the graded count of *all* necklaces of length
> $\ell$ and $\mathcal P_\ell(x)$ of the *primitive* ones. Then
> $$\mathcal N_\ell=\frac1\ell\sum_{d\mid\ell}\varphi(d)\operatorname{tr}
> \bigl(M(x^d)^{\ell/d}\bigr),\qquad
> \mathcal P_\ell=\frac1\ell\sum_{d\mid\ell}\mu(d)\operatorname{tr}
> \bigl(M(x^d)^{\ell/d}\bigr),$$
> and $Z_G(x,t)=\prod_{\ell\ge2}\bigl(1-t^\ell x^{\bullet}\bigr)^{-1}$ in the
> sense of Theorem B with $\mathcal P_\ell$ recording the exponents.

*Proof.* Burnside for $\mathcal N_\ell$ is SEED-08's argument verbatim (a
rotation of order $d$ fixes exactly the $d$-fold repetitions, and repetition
substitutes $x\mapsto x^d$; there are $\varphi(d)$ such rotations). For
$\mathcal P_\ell$: the marked-orbit count satisfies
$\operatorname{tr}(M(x)^\ell)=\sum_{d\mid\ell}\frac{\ell}{d}\cdot
\bigl[\text{primitive necklaces of length }\ell/d\text{, weighted }
x\mapsto x^{d}\bigr]$ by step (iii) above, i.e.
$\operatorname{tr}(M(x)^\ell)=\sum_{d\mid\ell}(\ell/d)\,\mathcal
P_{\ell/d}(x^{d})$; Möbius inversion over the divisor lattice gives the
stated formula. $\square$

**Reading.** Theorems B and C say the corpus's necklace count is a *prime
orbit count* and its generating function is a rational zeta function with an
Euler product — the Weil shape, obtained by counting rather than by a trace
formula, exactly as SEED-08's §6 hoped but did not prove. The identification
is now a theorem, not an analogy.

## 5. Theorem T — why $\nu_2$ cannot matter: the Cayley graph is a regular tree

This closes successor seed 2 of SEED-08 §8 (*"Is there a direct
Bass–Serre / covering-space proof that gives $\mu/3+1$ without passing
through the free-product decomposition? A cover-theoretic proof would say
**why** $\nu_2$ cannot matter."*). The answer is yes, and it gives more than
the growth rate: it gives every $c_n$ on the nose.

> **Theorem T.** Let $N$ be a level with $\nu_3(N)=0$, and let $S_N$ be
> SEED-08's declared alphabet. Then:
> 1. $|S_N|=\nu_2+2r=\dfrac{\mu}{3}+2$;
> 2. the Cayley graph $\mathrm{Cay}(\bar\Gamma_0(N),S_N)$ is a **tree**,
>    regular of degree $|S_N|=\mu/3+2$;
> 3. consequently $c_0=1$ and $c_n=\bigl(\tfrac\mu3+2\bigr)
>    \bigl(\tfrac\mu3+1\bigr)^{n-1}$ for all $n\ge1$, and
>    $\lambda_N=\mu/3+1$ exactly.
>
> $\nu_2$ cannot appear in the answer because it does not appear in (1): an
> involution contributes one generator, a free generator contributes two, and
> the Euler-characteristic relation (1) of SEED-08,
> $r=1+\mu/6-\nu_2/2-2\nu_3/3$, makes the total valence a function of $\mu$
> alone.

*Proof.* (1) With $\nu_3=0$, $|S_N|=\nu_2+2r$ by definition of $S_N$, and
$r=1+\mu/6-\nu_2/2$ by SEED-08 (1), so
$\nu_2+2r=\nu_2+2+\mu/3-\nu_2=\mu/3+2$. The $\nu_2$ cancellation is exact and
is the whole content of the $\nu_2$-independence.

(2) By Fact R with $\nu_3=0$, $\bar\Gamma_0(N)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^{*\nu_2}*F_r$.
Each factor's Cayley graph in its own generators is a tree: $\mathbb Z/2$
with $\{s\}$ is a single edge, $\mathbb Z$ with $\{u^{\pm1}\}$ is a
bi-infinite line. In a free product with $S=\bigsqcup S_i$, every element has
a unique normal form and, by SEED-08's Lemma 1, its $S$-length is the sum of
the syllable lengths; hence a reduced $S$-path from $1$ to $g$ is exactly a
concatenation of reduced paths inside the successive factor-cosets, each of
which is unique because the factor graphs are trees, and the sequence of
cosets is determined by the normal form. So reduced paths between any two
vertices are unique: the graph is a tree. (This is the standard
"tree of Cayley graphs" description of a free product, Serre, *Trees* I.§4;
the argument above is the direct one.) Its degree at $1$, hence at every
vertex by vertex-transitivity, is $\#S_N=\nu_2+2r$: the $\nu_2$ involutions
give one neighbour each ($s=s^{-1}$), the $r$ free generators two each, and
these $\nu_2+2r$ neighbours are pairwise distinct since distinct reduced
words of length $1$ are distinct group elements.

(3) In a $d$-regular tree, $c_0=1$ and $c_n=d(d-1)^{n-1}$: the unique
reduced path realises the distance, and each vertex at distance $n-1$ has
$d-1$ neighbours at distance $n$. Substituting $d=\mu/3+2$ gives the formula,
and $c_n^{1/n}\to \mu/3+1$. $\square$

**Consistency with SEED-08.** Its Theorem 2 gives
$\sigma=(1+x)(1+2x)/(1-Dx-Ex^2)$; with $\nu_3=0$ and $\lambda:=\mu/3+1$ one
has $D=\lambda-2$ and $E=2\lambda$, so $\lambda^2-D\lambda-E=
\lambda^2-(\lambda-2)\lambda-2\lambda=0$ — $\lambda$ is indeed the root —
and $1-Dx-Ex^2=(1-\lambda x)(1+2x)$, so
$\sigma=(1+x)/(1-\lambda x)$, whose coefficients are
$1,\lambda+1,(\lambda+1)\lambda,\dots$, i.e. exactly Theorem T(3). The table
in SEED-08 §4 is reproduced entry by entry ($N=12$: $d=10$, $\lambda=9$).

**Corollary T1 (the second eigenvalue is a ghost of an absent factor).** For
$\nu_3=0$ the denominator's two reciprocal roots are $\lambda_N$ and $-2$,
and $-2$ is precisely the reciprocal root of $\sigma_{\mathbb Z/3}=1+2x$ —
the local factor of a $\mathbb Z/3$ that is **not present**. It cancels
against the numerator. *Proof.* $1-Dx-Ex^2=(1-\lambda x)(1+2x)$, shown
above; the numerator of SEED-08 Theorem 2 carries the same factor $(1+2x)$.
$\square$

**Corollary T2 (where the irrationality comes from).** $\lambda_N$ is a
non-integer quadratic irrational only when $\nu_3>0$; equivalently, only when
some free-product factor has a Cayley graph containing a cycle
($\sigma_{\mathbb Z/3}=1+2x$ is the growth series of a triangle, not of a
tree). *Proof.* SEED-08 Theorem 3 computes the discriminant as
$(\mu+2\nu_3+9)^2-72\nu_3$, a perfect square when $\nu_3=0$; and for
$\nu_3=0$ Theorem T(2) shows the graph is a tree. $\square$

## 6. The same move on SEED-53: $q^{\varphi(q)}$ is a norm, and that is all it is

SEED-53's Certificate C1 reads $\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_q,R_q)=q^{\varphi(q)}$
and presents it as a certificate of non-vanishing. Asking Weil's question of
it — *what is being counted, and by which group?* — gives a complete and
deflationary answer, which I record because a "point count that is exactly
$q^{\varphi(q)}$" invites a richer reading than it deserves.

> **Proposition N.** $\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_q,R_q)=
> N_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)/\mathbb Q}\bigl(R_q(\zeta_q)\bigr)$, and since
> $R_q(\zeta_q)=q$ lies in $\mathbb Q$ and is fixed by
> $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/q)^\times$,
> this norm is $q^{[\mathbb Q(\zeta_q):\mathbb Q]}=q^{\varphi(q)}$.
> The exponent $\varphi(q)$ is the **order of the Galois group**, i.e. the
> size of the single Galois orbit of $\zeta_q$; the base $q$ is the constant
> value of $R_q$ on that orbit (SEED-53's Corollary $\Psi2$).

*Proof.* $\Phi_q$ is monic with simple roots the primitive $q$-th roots of
unity, which form one $\mathrm{Gal}$-orbit of size $\varphi(q)$, so
$\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_q,R_q)=\prod_{\zeta'}R_q(\zeta')$ is by definition
the field norm of $R_q(\zeta_q)$. By $\Psi2$ each factor equals $q$. $\square$

> **Corollary N1 (no extra content).** C1 is logically equivalent to $\Psi2$
> given $\Psi2$'s constancy statement, and carries **no** information beyond
> "the value is $q$ at one primitive root". In particular the exponent
> $\varphi(q)$ is not a count of anything but the Galois orbit, and the shape
> $q^{\varphi(q)}$ is not evidence of a variety with $q$ points per orbit.

I state Corollary N1 explicitly because it is the anti-`exp27` move: a number
of suggestive shape, whose shape is fully explained by a group order, should
be reported *with* that explanation, so that no later note reads $q^{\varphi
(q)}$ as an unexplained arithmetic coincidence. SEED-53's mathematics is
untouched; only its billing is adjusted. C2 ($\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_d,R_q)
=0$) is likewise the statement that a *different* Galois orbit lies in the
zero set, and the pair (C1, C2) is a decomposition of $\mu_q$ into
Galois orbits, one per divisor — the group action whose orbits are being
counted is $\mathrm{Gal}$, and the "point count" is
$q=\sum_{d\mid q}\varphi(d)$ read backwards.

## 7. The guesses, each marked

Weil said which of his statements were guesses. These are mine. **None of the
following is used anywhere above.**

- **GUESS 1 (a local field behind $\mu/3+1$).** Theorem T exhibits, for
  $\nu_3=0$, an action of $\bar\Gamma_0(N)$ on a $(q+1)$-regular tree with
  $q=\mu/3+1$ — formally the same object on which $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb
  Q_p)$ acts with $q=p$. I **guess** there is no arithmetic content in the
  coincidence: the tree here is the Cayley graph of a free product, its
  valence is forced by an Euler characteristic, and $\mu/3+1$ is prime only
  sporadically. Stated as a guess in the falsifiable direction: *there is no
  natural $\mathbb Q_p$-structure making $\mathrm{Cay}(\bar\Gamma_0(N),S_N)$
  the Bruhat–Tits building of a rank-one group for infinitely many $N$.*
  I have no proof either way.

- **CONJECTURE 2 (Ihara/Bass shape).** Theorem A's formula
  $\det(I-tM)=\prod_i(1+ta_i)\cdot\bigl(1-\sum_i ta_i/(1+ta_i)\bigr)$ has the
  form "product of local factors times one global factor", which is the shape
  of Bass's theorem for the Ihara zeta function of a graph
  ($Z(t)^{-1}=(1-t^2)^{-\chi}\det(I-At+Qt^2)$). I **conjecture** that
  $Z_G(x,t)$ of Theorem B specialises, for $G$ the fundamental group of a
  finite graph of finite groups, to the Ihara zeta of the quotient graph
  under an explicit substitution of $x$, and that the two "$-(k-1)$"-type
  corrections are the same Euler-characteristic term. I have **not** proved
  this and did not attempt Bass's theorem.

  > **SETTLED (SEED-74, `notes/SEED74_IHARA_BASS_SETTLED_THE_WRONG_TRACE_FORMULA.md`,
  > 2026-08-14; recorded at its site by SEED-108, 2026-08-14, Rule K1/K3).**
  > **False in its literal form, true in a corrected one.** SEED-74 Theorem 1:
  > for $G=F_r$ free with a free basis, the *completed* zeta
  > $\widehat Z_{F_r}(x)=Z_G(x,1)(1-x)^{-2r}$ **is** the Ihara zeta of the rose
  > $B_r$ on the nose ($t=1$, $u=x$), with $-\chi(B_r)=r-1$ the exponent — so the
  > guessed shape is right, but the graph produced is always the rose, never a
  > general graph with $\pi_1=F_r$. SEED-74 Theorem 2: if every factor is finite
  > and $\chi(G)\neq0$ then $\det(I-M(x))|_{x=1}=\chi(G)\prod_i|G_i|\neq0$,
  > whereas every finite connected graph with a cycle has $Z_X^{-1}(1)=0$; hence
  > $Z_G$ is then **not** the Ihara zeta of any finite graph (for
  > $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$: $1-2x^2$ at $x=1$ is $-1=6\cdot(-\tfrac16)$),
  > and its Corollary 2.2 kills the simplest mixed case $\mathbb Z/2*\mathbb Z$
  > by an odd-degree count. The conjecture's instinct that "the two
  > $-(k-1)$-type corrections are the same Euler-characteristic term" is
  > **correct**, but $\chi$ appears as the *value of the determinant at $u=1$*,
  > i.e. as the obstruction, not as a matching prefactor. SEED-74 Theorem 3 gives
  > the true statement: $M(x)$ is the Hashimoto non-backtracking edge operator of
  > the star graph of groups and $\det(I-tM(x))$ is a Bass tree-lattice
  > determinant, $t$ grading translation length in the Bass–Serre tree and $x$
  > word length. What remains open is only SEED-74 ledger item 12: whether the
  > *completed* $\widehat Z_G$ is an Ihara zeta when torsion is present.

- **CONJECTURE 3 (the parity obstruction is a second eigenvalue).**
  SEED-26 shows the witness radius drops by exactly $1$ at
  $m=b^{L-1}+1$ because of a parity constraint invisible to counting. The
  shape — an exact integer defect of $1$, at a codimension-one family of
  moduli, not seen by the leading-order count — is the shape of a *second*
  spectral contribution of absolute value equal to the first, i.e. a
  non-mixing transfer operator with an eigenvalue $-\lambda$ alongside
  $+\lambda$. I **conjecture** that the digit-action transfer operator of
  SEED-11's automaton at $m=b^{L-1}+1$ has spectrum symmetric under
  $\lambda\mapsto-\lambda$ on the relevant invariant subspace, and that
  SEED-26's parity argument is exactly the statement that the $-\lambda$
  eigenvector is the parity character. This is a guess about **why** the
  counting bound of SEED-11 was not sharp; it is not a theorem, and SEED-26's
  own proof is complete without it. It is offered as a `PROVE` item, not as a
  result.

- **GUESS 4 (Corollary T1, interpreted).** The reciprocal root $-2$ persists
  when $\nu_3=0$ and cancels. I **guess** the right way to say this is that
  the alternation dynamics always carries the local factor of every *possible*

## 9. Rigor boundary and least-sure step

- Fact R (Rademacher–Kulkarni), SEED-08's Lemma 1, the conjugacy theorem for
  free products, and Bass's theorem (only in §7.2, where it is not used) are
  **quoted**, not proved.
- No toolchain was available; I read `formal/cubical/Swarm/S15ACResidue.agda`

- Everything is alphabet-relative to SEED-08's declared $S_N$, and Theorem T
  is false for other generating sets (the tree property is not a
  quasi-isometry invariant statement about $c_n$).
- **Least-sure step, for a hostile reader:** Theorem T(2)'s uniqueness of
  reduced paths. The argument is: normal form is unique, lengths add, and
  each factor graph has unique reduced paths. The place to press is the
  transition between consecutive syllables — one must check that a reduced
  $S$-path cannot leave a factor-coset and re-enter it, which is exactly
  SEED-08's Lemma 1 ($\ge$ direction, the block-merging induction). If that
  induction has a gap, Theorem T(2) inherits it; T(1) and the consistency
  check against SEED-08's Theorem 2 are independent of it and would survive.
- A second pressure point: Theorem B's formal rearrangement in step (iii)
  needs each total $(t,x)$-degree to receive finitely many contributions.
  I gave the reason ($|h|\ge1$ per syllable); a reader who wants a different
  grading must re-check it.
