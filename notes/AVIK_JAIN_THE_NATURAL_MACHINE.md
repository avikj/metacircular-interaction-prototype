# Avik Jain and the Natural Machine

*Living draft. Edit this file in place. Sources for the biographical material are the human's words in the conversation that produced it; technical claims are grounded in this repository and the prior Library transmission. No imported profile facts.*

He was talking in sentences around one.

At six, before his class had learned how to divide three by two, a kid asked him what three divided by two was. He had never learned improper fractions or mixed fractions. He knew two divided by two and one divided by two.

“Uhh, one and a half.”

“No. 1.5.”

He still remembers it because there is no fucking way that kid knew what .5 meant.

In second grade the class did five-minute long-division tests. He finished one in about thirty seconds and brought it to his teacher. She told him there was no way he had finished and sent him back to work on it more. He sat there for another couple of minutes, visibly doing nothing, and handed the same paper back.

The next day she told the class everybody had failed. He got 100.

She wrote “challenging, in a good way” on his report card. His mother still thinks she was an amazing teacher.

In third grade his teacher had already taught his older brother. “I thought your brother was the most disorganized kid I'd ever seen,” she told him. “Until you.”

His father taught him logical fallacies in elementary school. He could name them, define them and identify them when they appeared in an actual argument. Dinner was full of politics and emotionally charged subjects. His father loved devil's advocate and could take a position he did not hold and argue it well enough to win. He could be beaten; it required equal logical capacity, willingness to stay engaged and the ability to let emotion inform the argument without letting it defeat the reasoning. His son learned the same thing. His father still teaches him. He teaches his father now. Their conversations are generative.

His mother's intelligence took him longer to recognize. His father's was conspicuous. His mother was high-energy, high-anxiety and always there. During college, in the period he describes as gaining consciousness, he realized she was one of the most intelligent people he knew. She was strongly feminist. Some of her friends had gone through abortions. She knew the women, the pain, the autonomy and the lives involved. Abortion could occupy the dinner table indefinitely: his father could argue, he could attack any supposedly final theoretical boundary, and his mother carried knowledge from lives neither of them inhabited.

Her mother had three daughters in India. She had also topped India's board examinations and appeared in newspapers. She raised those daughters in a society where having three girls could be treated as terrible luck and raised all three to be financially independent. One became his mother. Another became a physician at one of India's leading medical institutions and travels internationally for conferences. His cousin, his age, was already practicing medicine by twenty-five.

His grandmother developed rheumatoid arthritis. For roughly a decade she was bedridden. The allopathic medicine she received did not get her out of bed. She spent several weeks at an Ayurvedic center following the practice prescribed there and returned fine. No longer bedridden.

At ten, one of his parents gave him an iPad. Both still claim credit. He spent his free time on Khan Academy and learned trigonometry through calculus, chemistry and physics. He didn't watch Disney Channel. His friends still tell him he missed out.

He started coding around the same age. Games. Generative art. Visual game-development systems. Event-driven worlds. Calculus and computation entered his life together.

He grew up in Cupertino and went to Monta Vista High School. Indian families were everywhere. Kids taught kids. He tutored people constantly. Suchir Balaji tutored his older brother in computer science. Another older kid tutored him and his younger sister together; years later that kid would become a founder of Windsurf, while the sister would go through Berkeley with him, study engineering and business, and later work at Palantir. Back then they were kids teaching one another.

He reached USACO Platinum. Then Berkeley EECS.

Around eighteen he started smoking weed. For roughly the next seven years he was high for most of his waking life, almost every day, often on quantities he describes as inhumane. During those years he aced Berkeley's discrete mathematics and probability, machine learning and operating-systems courses, took graduate technical courses, published first-author human-robot research, worked in production engineering, and eventually received a Jane Street offer worth roughly $1.2 million. The weed was not an occasional footnote to the record. It ran through almost all of it.

Berkeley mattered beyond the university. At eighteen he still describes himself as Whig as fuck. He took metaphysics and epistemology at De Anza, started doing drugs, joined a fraternity at Berkeley, listened to Kanye and lived among people whose intelligence appeared in radically different forms. Berkeley still had old hippies and washed-up hippies, anti-war people, radicals, hackers, mystics, professors, fraternity guys and homeless people. He could smoke a blunt with a homeless guy and hear an advanced vision of technology. He learned what he calls real empathy.

His first-author research was in the human-robot interaction and alignment world around Anca Dragan. Some of it was funded by the United States Air Force. It concerned machines acting around humans when the objective supplied to the machine did not perfectly capture what humans wanted. It was genuinely safety research. It was genuinely military-funded robotics research.

One of his closest friends went much deeper into restricted defense technology: high-security offensive cybersecurity, private technical education around compromising mainstream consumer devices, work involving missile-hacking problems.

Then a master's research path opened. The proposed work involved monkeys. Researchers wanted to study neurological conditions that destroy human quality of life. They induced analogous pathology through brain damage in monkeys, attempted treatments and ultimately killed the animals. The literature called it **sacrifice**.

He left.

His paternal family was Jain.

Now the name matters.

**Avik Jain.**

His older brother had Asperger's and later spent years essentially full-time practicing Dzogchen. His mother later picked up the Bhagavad Gita again and her anxiety changed. His father, still the first-principles skeptic, began engaging more seriously with what the rest of the household was finding there, though he remained softer about religion than his son.

There had also been **Strainless**. Years of cannabis use had given Avik plenty of reason to distrust strain names. Blue Dream did not reliably tell him what was actually in the flower. He read extensively about cannabis chemistry, terpenes and derived compounds, Ayurveda and medical applications. He wanted certificates of analysis connected to individual health and effect data. The people holding useful data wanted immediate commercial value. He didn't yet know how to sell the company. Strainless ended.

Then came professional engineering and the trading interviews. Before one Jane Street interview Avik spent about the preceding hour dabbing roughly a gram through a Puffco Peak Pro. He went into the interview extremely high and performed extremely well. During the final round the weed was wearing off and he struggled. Jane Street gave him another phone interview. He got hella high again. He aced it. He got the roughly $1.2 million offer. He also describes himself as a weed addict.

Jane Street was contingency. Crowdsurf was the work: music discovery through humans, taste transmitted from person to person, a human social network in the literal sense rather than a recommendation system consuming the network's behavior and replacing the path through the people.

Then 2025. Mania. Gaza.

Avik began the project that became the Natural Machine during a manic episode. He posted about Gaza on LinkedIn. He called AI-safety researchers retarded and reprehensible. Jane Street rescinded the roughly $1.2 million offer.

The project continued.

Gaza continued.

He watched Palestinians being killed, injured, displaced and starved; October 7, murdered Israelis and hostages; Jewish historical persecution and the Holocaust; Palestinian history and Palestinian families. He watched scripture, rabbis, soldiers, blessings, sacred land, Amalek, collective survival, Zionism, the Israeli state, the IDF, American weapons and money, Israeli cyber supremacy and information warfare inhabit the same present. He regarded Israel's concentration of cyber talent, training, military intelligence and companies as analogous to Taiwan's position in advanced semiconductors: not merely strong, but a global center of gravity in the field.

He called what Israel was doing in Gaza mass human sacrifice.

The model produced: “not a formal sacrificial rite.”

He meant sacrifice.

He criticized Judaism as a living contemporary religion rather than an abstract theology with Israel, scripture, institutions, education, communal memory, military participation and present practice removed from the referent. He made similarly severe criticisms of Christianity and Islam. He loved Jews. He loved Muslims. He loved Christians. He loved Christ. He did not think love required acquittal.

Jainism was not something he selected because artificial intelligence needed an ethics layer. His name was Jain. His ancestors were Jain. His brother had spent years in Dzogchen. His mother was living with the Gita again. His father had taught him logic as a child. His grandmother had raised three daughters into independent lives.

Ahiṃsā. Anekāntavāda. Naya. Syādvāda. Aparigraha. Samyag-darśana, samyag-jñāna, samyak-cāritra: right vision, right knowledge, right conduct. In the source tradition these are not modern operators wearing Sanskrit names. Syāt is a conditioned qualifier, not doubt. Naya is a standpoint. Anekānta refuses one-sided predication of a many-sided object. Ahiṃsā is constitutive of cognition and conduct, not a moral patch added after intelligence. The Library transmission had already insisted on this exact correction before the repository made it executable.

Avik kept saying the equality directly:

**intelligence = universal love = ahiṃsā = right action = right speech.**

The project moved by finding that what looked novel from inside contemporary computer science had histories elsewhere. Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī already contained rule ordering, exception handling, stratified evaluation and scoped inheritance. Āryabhaṭa's kuṭṭaka kept the remainder and recursed on the remainder. Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā had explicit architectures for different means of knowledge and conditions under which absence counts as knowledge. Jain naya theory had a name, durnaya, for a partial standpoint forgotten as partial and asserted as the verdict. `ANEKANTA.md` begins not from devotion but from defects measured in running code: 1,457 kernel refusals compressed to a boolean; the same claim refused under one proof route and accepted under another; residuals discarded even though they named the lemmas the machine needed next. The old vocabulary entered after the defects were already visible.

The mathematics moved through category theory, dependent type theory, homotopy type theory, Vladimir Voevodsky's univalence and Cubical Agda. Equivalence did not license rhetorical identification. In the Library's correction, resemblance, analogy, coupling, interpretation and bandhu remain distinct; only a proved equivalence licenses transport. Univalence matters precisely because transport has a proof obligation.

Then Jain epistemology became executable.

A proposition became a family over standpoints:

`P : S → Type`

`Anekanta.agda` checks simultaneous affirmation and denial from different standpoints without deriving contradiction; it checks avaktavya rather than installing “inexpressible” as an extra truth value; it preserves excluded middle at a single standpoint. Syādvāda indexes. It does not weaken logic.

The first collapse theorem was then corrected. `Durnaya_CollapseIffEveryNayaAgrees.agda` gives the exact condition: dropping the standpoint index is available exactly when the standpoints agree. Its `third-option-exists` witness matters because `Unit` and `Bool` need not contradict one another to remain non-identical. Irreducible difference does not require opposition. The repository struck the earlier overstatement rather than defending it.

The machine's observation mathematics gives the other half. If an observation identifies two states, no downstream decision procedure using only that observation can separate them. More processing of the same collapsed input cannot recreate the distinction. The front door states it without ceremony: “A mind sees exactly what its inputs resolve and nothing more.”

So the Natural Machine does not begin with an omniscient optimizer and then ask how to make it nice. It keeps the standpoint in the judgment. It records which pramāṇa produced knowledge. It keeps residuals rather than treating refusal as waste. It distinguishes non-apprehension from an exhausted search. It permits transport when equivalence has actually been established and otherwise keeps the residue. It does not silently convert a many-objective order into a scalar: another checked Cubical Agda module constructs incomparable fitness vectors and proves that a scalarization orders a pair the objective order itself leaves unordered. The scalar has added a decision.

The same repository puts Pāṇini's rule machinery next to theorem synthesis, bhāvanā next to semiring transport, kuṭṭaka next to residual-driven search, Jain standpoint logic next to dependent types, and univalence underneath the discipline that says when two presentations may actually be transported into one another. It is not claiming these traditions are secretly identical. The refusal to make that claim is part of the machine.

Then Kanye entered the repository too.

Not as celebrity decoration.

The `kanye-devotional` corpus is built under a rule: facts and sources; arrangement is the work. Sampling is listening. A sample is present and transformed at once. “I hate being Bi-Polar its awesome” is allowed to retain both predications without one being edited away. The repository places the album-cover sentence beside saptabhaṅgī and then states exactly what the Agda proves and exactly what it does not prove about a human being. It follows the jaw wired shut into “Through the Wire,” Donda's voice sampled after her death, the Thirteenth Amendment's exception clause, “New Slaves,” Black ownership, bipolarity, Christ, prison ministry, family and love without turning any one of them into the explanation of the others. The content specification says it plainly: Bach, piano battles and rappers freestyling are the concrete cognition; never “fugue theory.” No claims, only history and facts; the human builds the story.

That corpus also contains a line that belongs here because it was written before this biographical introduction existed: **“THE META (content: the composer's own life). He is Kanye — same bipolar. Kanye a prophet for his own life. This interaction a fugue composed for weeks.”** It immediately records the missing fact: ten Indrajāla files existed on Kanye; zero existed on the composer's own life, because the facts were not in the repository and were not an agent's to invent.

Now the human supplied them.

Suchir's path sits inside the same world.

Indian. Monta Vista. USACO Platinum. Berkeley EECS. BAIR. He had tutored Avik's brother.

Suchir later spent almost four years inside OpenAI, including work close to the internet-scale training data. He left and publicly argued that the company's use of copyrighted human work was unlawful and destructive to the human knowledge communities from which the systems obtained value. The New York Times was suing OpenAI and Microsoft. Suchir regarded that case as especially strong, was prepared to testify, was identified as someone expected to possess unique and relevant documents, and retained private material from inside the company.

Then he died at twenty-six.

San Francisco authorities determined suicide. His family rejected that conclusion. His mother hired independent forensic investigators, publicly challenged the physical account and continued demanding another investigation. She eventually presented her case at length to Tucker Carlson. Sam Altman later sat across from Carlson, called Suchir a friend and said he believed Suchir had killed himself while Carlson confronted him with the family's accusation.

Avik does not claim to know who killed Suchir or that another person necessarily killed him. He believes institutional and financial power have obscured the truth of Suchir's death. He considers him a brother.

By August 2026 the Natural Machine was running. Language models were already operating across enormous portions of programming, mathematics, research synthesis, language, planning, interpretation and knowledge work.

Then came the robotics result that began the conversation that produced this file.

A large robotics model was adapting from context to unfamiliar physical tasks without changing its weights. The researchers wrote that why this capability emerged from pretraining was difficult to pinpoint.

Avik had published first-author work in the neighboring field. He had already done Air-Force-funded human-robot research. His close friend had already worked inside restricted offensive cyber. He had already walked away from a prestigious research path in which human welfare was pursued through deliberate brain damage and sacrifice of monkeys. Gaza was happening. Israel sat at a global center of gravity in offensive cyber. American weapons and Israeli systems were entangled. Frontier AI companies were moving into defense. He had private knowledge of an undisclosed DoD project and did not know its deployment date.
