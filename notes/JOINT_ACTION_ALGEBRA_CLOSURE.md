# Joint action-algebra closure and assimilation

Let `X` be finite, let `V=k^X` over an infinite field `k`, with pointwise
multiplication, and let `S={T_1,...,T_m}` be linear endomorphisms of `V`.
For a unital subalgebra `A<=V`, define

`F_S(A)=alg(A union T_1(A) union ... union T_m(A))`

and iterate `A_0=A`, `A_(n+1)=F_S(A_n)`.

## Theorem 1: finite joint closure

The chain stabilizes after at most `|X|-dim(A)` strict steps. Its stable value

`Cl_S(A)`

is the unique least unital subalgebra containing `A` and invariant under every
`T_i`.

Proof. `A_n <= A_(n+1)`. A strict inclusion increases finite dimension, which
is bounded by `|X|`. At a fixed point, every `T_i(A_n)` lies in `A_n`. If `B`
is any invariant unital algebra containing `A`, induction gives `A_n<=B` for
all `n`, hence the fixed point lies in `B`. ∎

By the finite partition-algebra classification, every `A_n` and `Cl_S(A)` is
the block-constant algebra of a unique partition. Thus this construction is
simultaneously the least joint stable observation partition. It is independent
of a schedule that fairly applies the actions, because all fair schedules have
the same least common fixed point.

## Theorem 2: assimilation law

If

`A <= B <= Cl_S(A)`,

then

`Cl_S(B)=Cl_S(A)`.

In particular, at any stage `A_n`, adjoining any family of currently forced
images `Y <= sum_i T_i(A_n)` and setting `B=alg(A_n union Y)` changes neither
the eventual joint closure nor its terminal partition.

Proof. Monotonicity gives `Cl_S(A)<=Cl_S(B)`. Since `Cl_S(A)` is itself an
`S`-invariant unital algebra containing `B`, minimality gives the reverse
inclusion. ∎

This is stronger than idempotence `Cl_S(Cl_S(A))=Cl_S(A)`: any intermediate
assimilation inside the closure is path-independent. It may shorten the number
of subsequent refinement rounds, but cannot change the terminal mathematics.

## Theorem 3: why the action monoid orbit is insufficient

Let `M` be the monoid generated by `S` under composition. The linear span

`L_M(A)=span{T(a):T in M,a in A}`

is the least invariant linear subspace containing `A`. It equals
`Cl_S(A)` exactly when it is closed under pointwise multiplication. Otherwise
the missing elements are products of action-generated observables, and no
pure word in the action monoid supplies them.

Proof. The first statement is standard invariant-span minimality. Apply the
classification lemma: `L_M(A)` contains `1` when the actions preserve `1`
(or after explicitly adjoining it), and represents a partition algebra iff it
is multiplication-closed. If so it satisfies the universal property of
`Cl_S(A)`; if not, every stable observation algebra containing it must contain
its algebra closure strictly. ∎

The qualification about preserving `1` is necessary. For arbitrary linear
actions use `span(k1 union M A)` in the criterion.

## Exact boundary

The theorem is finite-dimensional and algebraic. It supplies no choice of the
action family. It says precisely what assimilation can and cannot do after the
family is fixed: install any forced intermediate result freely with respect to
the terminal closure, but do not replace algebra closure by composition alone.
