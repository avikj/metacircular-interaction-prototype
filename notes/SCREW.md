## 1. What Matsumoto–Suzuki actually define

**Generating summatory function** [MS (1.2)], which is Fujii's formula: with $G(n)=\sum_{m+k=n}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(k) = (\Lambda*\Lambda)(n)$,

$$\sum_{n\le X} G(n) \;=\; \tfrac12 X^2 \;-\; 2X^{3/2}H(X)\;+\;R(X),\qquad
H(X):=\sum_\rho \frac{X^{\rho-1/2}}{\rho(\rho+1)},$$

$R(X)=O((X\log X)^{4/3})$ under RH (Fujii), improved to $O(X\log^5X)$ (Bhowmik–Schlage-Puchta), $O(X\log^3X)$ (Languasco–Zaccagnini; Goldston–Yang).

**The reweighted variant** [MS (1.6)]:

$$\sum_{n\le X}\frac{G(n)}{n^{2}} \;=\; \log X + c_2 + \frac{2}{\sqrt X}\,H_1(X) + E(X),
\qquad H_1(X):=\sum_\rho \frac{X^{\rho-1/2}}{\rho(1-\rho)},$$

and MS note (1.2) ⇔ (1.6) by partial summation ("the contributions of $H$ and $H_1$ to the Goldbach problem are equivalent"). We verified the passage directly: differentiating (1.2), $d\bigl(-2y^{\rho+1}/(\rho(\rho+1))\bigr) = -2y^\rho/\rho\,dy$, and $\int_1^X -2y^{\rho-2}/\rho\,dy = +2X^{-1/2}\cdot X^{\rho-1/2}/(\rho(1-\rho)) + \text{const}$ — the sign and the denominator $\rho(1-\rho)$ come out exactly as quoted.

**Screw function** (Kreĭn; Kreĭn–Langer 1970, as used in [MS §1]): a continuous $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$ with $g(-t)=\overline{g(t)}$ such that the **Kreĭn kernel**

$$G_g(t,u) \;:=\; g(t-u)-g(t)-g(-u)+g(0)$$

is nonnegative definite on $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$: $\sum_{i,j=1}^n G_g(t_i,t_j)\,\xi_i\bar\xi_j \ge 0$ for all finite $\{t_i\}\subset\mathbb R$, $\{\xi_i\}\subset\mathbb C$.

**The RH-equivalence** [MS Theorem 1.3, quoted verbatim from the extraction]:

> The function $g_{H_1}(t):=H_1(e^t)-H_1(1)$ is a screw function on $\mathbb R$ if and only if the Riemann hypothesis holds.

**Mechanism** (as extracted, and reconstructed): under RH, $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$ gives $\rho(1-\rho)=\gamma^2+\tfrac14>0$, so with signed ordinates,

$$g_{H_1}(t) \;=\; \sum_{\gamma}\frac{e^{i\gamma t}-1}{\gamma^2+\tfrac14}
\;=\; 2\sum_{\gamma>0}\frac{\cos(\gamma t)-1}{\gamma^2+\tfrac14},$$

a real even function with **positive spectral masses** $a(\gamma)=1/(\gamma^2+\tfrac14)$, $\sum a(\gamma) = 2+\gamma_E-\log 4\pi < \infty$. Then [MS Prop. 4.1]

$$\sum_{i,j}G_g(t_i,t_j)\xi_i\bar\xi_j \;=\; \sum_{\gamma} a(\gamma)\Bigl|\sum_i \bigl(e^{-it_i\gamma}-1\bigr)\xi_i\Bigr|^2 \;\ge\; 0 ,$$

i.e. Kreĭn positivity is a **Hermitian-square identity over the single-zero spectrum**. Conversely, an off-line zero makes some frequency $\omega = -i(\rho-\tfrac12)$ non-real; the resulting $e^{(\beta-1/2)t}$ growth is incompatible with the screw property ([MS Cor. 3.1]: boundedness of $\operatorname{Re}g$ forces $\Omega\subset\mathbb R$). Note the symmetrization $\rho(\rho+1)\to\rho(1-\rho)$ is forced already by the *axiom* $g(-t)=\overline{g(t)}$: $1/(\rho(1-\rho))$ is real on the line and invariant under $\rho\mapsto1-\rho$, while $1/(\rho(\rho+1))$ is neither (see §3, Part 3a).

**Relation to Suzuki's earlier zeta screw function** [S]: MS remark that $H_{1/2}(e^t)-H_{1/2}(1)$ (the $\ell$-shifted variant with masses $1/\gamma^2$) *is* the screw function of $\zeta$ from [S], where $g_\zeta(t) = -\Psi(t)$ with the two faces
$$\Psi(t) = \sum_\gamma \frac{1-\cos\gamma t}{\gamma^2}\quad(\text{zero side, RH form}), \qquad
\Psi(t) = 4(e^{t/2}+e^{-t/2}-2)-\sum_{n\le e^t}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}(t-\log n)+\cdots \quad(\text{prime side}),$$
and [S, Thm 1.2]: RH ⟺ $g_\zeta$ is a screw function. So the MS construction is the Goldbach-flavored member of a one-parameter family; the Goldbach content is that the *same* screw data is generated by the pair-correlation-free part of $\Lambda*\Lambda$.

MS's other results (Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.4: existence of $M$-functions for the value-distribution of $H$, sufficiency criteria for RH from $M$-function formulas, and the identification of $\exp(g)$ with characteristic functions of infinitely divisible distributions [MS Prop. 5.1]) concern the probabilistic layer and are not used here.

## 2. The exact dictionary to this repo's decomposition

Our Theorem C/D structure (REPORT.md §4–5), $k$-fold Cesàro ladder: $G_k(X)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m+n\le X}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)(X-m-n)^k$,

$$G_k(X) = \frac{X^{k+2}}{(k+2)!} \;-\; 2\sum_\rho \frac{\Gamma(\rho)\,X^{\rho+k+1}}{\Gamma(\rho+k+2)} \;+\; \sum_{\rho,\rho'}\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')}{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+k+1)}X^{\rho+\rho'+k}\;+\;(\text{lower}),$$

with $k=1$ = Theorem D ($\Gamma(\rho)/\Gamma(\rho+3) = 1/(\rho(\rho+1)(\rho+2))$), and $k=0$ = Fujii = MS's starting point (1.2).

| Matsumoto–Suzuki object | This repo's object | Change of variables |
|---|---|---|
| $\sum_{n\le X}G(n)$, [MS (1.2)] | $G_0(X)$, the $k=0$ rung below Theorem D | identical |
| $-2X^{3/2}H(X)$ | **first variation** (single-zero layer) at $k=0$: $-2\sum_\rho \frac{X^{\rho+1}}{\rho(\rho+1)}$ | $H(X) = X^{-3/2}\sum_\rho\frac{X^{\rho+1}}{\rho(\rho+1)}$ |
| $R(X)$ | contains the **second variation** at $k=0$: $\sum_{\rho,\rho'}\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')}{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+1)}X^{\rho+\rho'}$, of size $X$ under RH — cf. BSP's $\Omega(X\log\log X)$ | pair weights at $k=0$ decay like $(\gamma+\gamma')^{-3/2}$ (Thm D′ with $k=0$) |
| $H_1(X)=\sum_\rho\frac{X^{\rho-1/2}}{\rho(1-\rho)}$ | first variation after $n^{-2}$ partial summation; denominators symmetrized $\rho(\rho+1)\to\rho(1-\rho)$ | two-line computation in §1 |
| $g_{H_1}(t)=H_1(e^t)-H_1(1)$ | Kreĭn transform of the **first-variation sector only**: $t=\log X$, subtract the $t=0$ value | $g_{H_1}(t)=2\sum_{\gamma>0}\frac{\cos\gamma t-1}{\gamma^2+1/4}$ under RH |
| spectral measure of $G_{g_{H_1}}$ | $\mu_1=\sum_\gamma \frac{\delta_\gamma}{\gamma^2+1/4}$ on the **single-zero spectrum** $\{\gamma\}$ | $G_g(t,u)=\int(e^{i\gamma t}-1)\overline{(e^{i\gamma u}-1)}\,d\mu_1(\gamma)$ |
| — (no MS object) | second variation of Thm D: measure $\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}\,\delta_{\gamma_i+\gamma_j}$, $W=\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')}{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+2)}$, on the **sum spectrum** $\{\gamma_i+\gamma_j\}$ | no screw structure — see §4 |

**Conclusion of the mapping (verified against their formulas):** the MS screw function is built *entirely from the first-variation / single-zero layer*. Its frequencies are the single ordinates $\gamma$ (the function $g_{H_1}$ oscillates at $\gamma_1=14.13,\ \gamma_2=21.02,\dots$ in $t=\log X$), not the pair sums $\gamma_i+\gamma_j\ge 28.27$ of our Theorem D. This was the expected answer — a screw function is a one-variable Kreĭn object and can only carry one frequency index — and it is now confirmed at the level of the exact formulas. In the language of REPORT.md §6, the MS criterion applies the **Hermitian-square (D-side) positivity certificate to the S-side generating layer**: $G_g(t,u)$ is a function of $t-u$, its positivity is Bochner positivity of the single-frequency measure $\mu_1$, and the RH-equivalence says precisely "all frequencies real + all masses positive". RH enters through zero *locations* only — consistent with our depth staircase, since the first variation needs no zero correlations.

## 3. Numerical results (`code/exp12_screw.py`, first $10^4$ zeros, $\Lambda$ to $3\cdot10^6$)

**Part 1 — construction/axioms.** $g_{H_1}$ from $K$ zeros: $\sup_t|g_K-g_{10^4}|$ on $[-40,40]$ is $1.2\cdot10^{-3}$ ($K=10^3$), $4.0\cdot10^{-4}$ ($K=3\cdot10^3$) — absolute convergence with $\sim\log\Gamma/\Gamma$ tails, as expected from masses $1/\gamma^2$ against density $\log\gamma$. Truncated total mass $0.045922$ vs. exact $\sum_\rho\frac1{\rho(1-\rho)}=2+\gamma_E-\log4\pi=0.046191$ (tail $2.7\cdot10^{-4}$, matching the integral estimate). $g(0)=0$ exactly; $g$ real and even to machine precision; modulus of continuity at $h=10^{-4}$: $9.7\cdot10^{-5}$.

**Part 2 — Kreĭn positivity (RH numerically true).** Sampled kernel $G_g(t_i,t_j)$, uniform grids $n=160$ on $[-40,40]$ and $[-12,12]$ and five random grids $n=80$: **all eigenvalues strictly positive**; min eigenvalue $+3.5\cdot10^{-3}$ vs. max $+7.2$ (uniform, $T=40$); worst relative min over random grids $\ge 0$ at machine precision. *Caveat stated honestly:* since the truncated sum has positive masses by construction, PSD is guaranteed by the Hermitian-square identity; this test validates the implementation and the mass-positivity of the extracted formula, and is "numerical evidence for RH" only in the weak sense that the zeros used lie on the line.

**Part 3a — why $\rho(1-\rho)$ and not Fujii's $\rho(\rho+1)$.** The unsymmetrized $g_H(t)=H(e^t)-H(1)$ *violates the screw axiom*: $\max_t|g_H(-t)-g_H(t)| = 4.4\cdot10^{-3}$ ($\ne0$; the masses $1/(\rho(\rho+1))$ are complex on the line), and its kernel is asymmetric at the $10^{-2}$ level. Curiously, the symmetric part of its Kreĭn kernel is **negative** definite (all eigenvalues in $[-7.1,-3.5\cdot10^{-3}]$): on the line $\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{\rho(\rho+1)}=\frac{3/4-\gamma^2}{|{\cdot}|^2}<0$ for every zero, so $-g_H$ is "screw-like up to the asymmetry defect". The functional-equation symmetrization $\rho\mapsto1-\rho$ is what turns this accident into an exact equivalence.

**Part 3b — the criterion detects off-line zeros.** Replacing the first zero pair by the quadruple $\{\beta\pm i\gamma_1,\,1-\beta\pm i\gamma_1\}$ (keeping 3000 on-line zeros):

| $\beta$ | 0.50 | 0.55 | 0.60 | 0.65 | 0.70 | 0.80 | 0.90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\lambda_{\min}/|\lambda|_{\max}$ | $+5\cdot10^{-4}$ | $-0.108$ | $-0.629$ | $-0.983$ | $-0.998$ | $-1.000$ | $-1.000$ |

A single off-line zero at $\beta=0.55$ already produces a $10\%$-relative negative eigenvalue on a grid of radius $T=25$; detection strength grows like the $e^{(\beta-1/2)T}$ growth of the corresponding term. This is the quantitative face of the "only if" of [MS Thm 1.3].

**Part 4 — the second variation is *not* of screw type (negative result for §D.6 item 3 as stated).** For the Theorem D pair layer $A(u)=\sum W_{ij}e^{i(\gamma_i+\gamma_j)u}$ (400 zeros, all sign classes): binning the measure $\sum W_{ij}\delta_{\gamma_i+\gamma_j}$ on $[20,100]$ into its 2626 resolved lines, **50% of the lines have negative real mass** (carrying 60% of the total $|$mass$|$), mean $|\operatorname{Im}|/|{\cdot}| = 0.64$; the Kreĭn kernel of $A$ is maximally indefinite ($\lambda_{\min}/|\lambda|_{\max}=-1.00$). Opposite-sign pairs carry a fraction $2\cdot10^{-18}$ of the mass (consistency with Thm D′ suppression). So the conjecture of APPENDIX_D.md §D.6(3), *"positivity of the measure $\sum W_{ij}\delta_{\gamma_i+\gamma_j}$ should translate into their condition"*, is **false as literally stated**: that measure is genuinely complex-phased, and no positivity of it exists to be translated. The correct join is the one in §4 below.

**Part 5 — the dictionary on real Goldbach data.** Computing the arithmetic side of [MS (1.6)] from $\Lambda*\Lambda$ up to $3\cdot10^6$ and band-passing its $\sqrt X$-normalized residual to $[8,26.5]$ in $\log X$ (a band containing only single-zero lines $\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$; pair lines start at $2\gamma_1=28.27$): correlation with the zero-side $H_1(X)$ = **1.0000**, amplitude ratio **0.9984** (fitted $c_2=-2.280$). Their screw data is literally computable from Goldbach counts, and the frequency segregation (single lines below $28$, pair lines above) is exactly the first/second-variation split of exp6b.

## 4. Honest assessment: what a new theorem here would require

**What is settled by the dictionary.** MS positivity and our Theorem D/D″ live in *different sectors*: their RH-criterion is a Bochner-type positivity of the single-zero measure $\mu_1$ (first variation); our sum-spectrum identity and the weighted additive energy $V\asymp\sum|W|^2$ (Appendix D) live on the pair measure (second variation). The two sectors are joined only through the trivial ladder: both are generated by the same explicit formula, at adjacent Cesàro heights, related by partial summation. There is no *existing* theorem connecting Kreĭn positivity to the pair layer, and Part 4 shows the naive candidate is false.

**Why the naive join fails, structurally.** The pair measure is (up to the smoothing denominators) the additive **convolution square of a complex measure**: $\nu=\sum_\gamma \Gamma(\tfrac12+i\gamma)\delta_\gamma$ pushed forward under $(\gamma,\gamma')\mapsto\gamma+\gamma'$, modulated by $1/\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+2)$. Convolution squares of complex measures have no positivity; the phases $\arg\Gamma(\tfrac12+i\gamma)$ — precisely the Riemann–Siegel phases — survive into the pair masses. Kreĭn/screw theory is single-frequency Bochner theory and cannot see them.

**What a genuine theorem could look like, and what each route needs.**

1. **The squared screw function (most concrete).** Under RH, $g_{H_1}(t)^2$ expands with *positive product masses* $\frac{1}{(\gamma^2+1/4)(\gamma'^2+1/4)}$ on the sum spectrum $\{\gamma+\gamma'\}$ (plus single-frequency and DC cross terms). So there *is* a canonical positive measure on the sum spectrum — but with product weights $\frac{1}{\rho(1-\rho)\rho'(1-\rho')}$, not our Beta weights $\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')}{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+2)}$. A theorem "RH ⟹ positivity of the product-weighted pair measure" is an immediate *corollary* of MS (positivity is preserved under convolution of positive measures) — true but not new. The new content would be: **identify the weighted Goldbach summatory function whose second variation carries the product weights** (a double partial summation in both variables $m,k$ separately, i.e. weights $m^{-2}k^{-2}$-type rather than $(m+k)^{-2}$-type — the candidate is $\bigl(\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)/n^2\cdot\text{(normalization)}\bigr)^2$-adjacent objects), and prove its variance analogue of Theorem D″. Because the weights then factor, the Parseval/variance analysis of Appendix D closes *without* the Beta-function coupling, and MS positivity becomes usable as a lower-bound input. This is routine-but-real work and, we believe, the correct formalization of "the screw-kernel join".
2. **The coupling factor.** To connect the *actual* Theorem D weights to the product weights one must control $C(\rho,\rho') := \rho(1-\rho)\rho'(1-\rho')\,\frac{\Gamma(\rho)\Gamma(\rho')}{\Gamma(\rho+\rho'+2)}$ — a bounded, explicitly Stirling-estimable kernel, but complex-phased. A join theorem via this route needs a Schur-multiplier statement: that multiplication by $C$ maps the positive-definite cone of sum-spectrum measures to a cone on which the Appendix D variance bounds still close. Our Part 4 numerics (60% of $|$mass$|$ on negative-real lines) show $C$ genuinely destroys pointwise positivity, so any such statement must be an $L^2$/quadratic-form statement (about $\sum|W|^2$, where phases are harmless), not a pointwise one.
3. **What screw theory cannot give.** The open ingredient of Theorem D″ is near-diagonal separation of $\{\gamma_i+\gamma_j\}$ — a 4-point additive-energy statement. Kreĭn positivity is a 2-point (one-frequency) statement; it is structurally incapable of controlling additive energy, exactly as Weil positivity does not imply pair correlation. So the screw join can at best supply the *first-variation* side of a variance equivalence (and RH-detection), never the separation hypothesis. Conversely, our additive-energy program contributes nothing new to RH-criteria: the RH content is already saturated by the single-zero sector, which is MS's theorem.
4. **Unconditional formulation.** Any paper-grade statement should be phrased without RH: define $g_{H_1}$ by the arithmetic side (Part 5 shows it is computable from $\sum_{n\le X}G(n)/n^2$ up to explicit smooth terms — MS Prop. 6.1 gives the zero-free expression for $H$; ~~the analogous expression for $H_1$ needs the $E(X)$ bookkeeping made explicit~~ **[seed135, 2026-08-14 — checked at the numbered statement, `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.00888` §6: Proposition 6.1 is stated "unconditionally for $X>1$" for **both** $H(X)$ **and** $H_1(X)$, the latter as $H_1(X)=\sum_{n\le X}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\bigl(\sqrt{X/n}-\sqrt{n/X}\bigr)-\sqrt X(\log X-C_0-1)-\frac{1}{\sqrt X}\log2\pi-\frac{1}{\sqrt X}[\cdots]$. So the zero-free $H_1$ expression is *given*, not owed; what remains owed is only this corpus's own $E(X)$ bookkeeping relative to $\sum_{n\le X}G(n)/n^2$. Narrow the clause accordingly]**). Then "screw ⟺ RH" (their theorem) plus "pair-sector variance ⟺ weighted additive energy" (our D″, still needing the separation input of D.6 items 1–2) would sit as the two independent halves of one framework; the *join paper* is the product-weight construction of point 1, which makes the second half's weights the literal square of the first half's masses.

**Bottom line.** The mapping is now exact and numerically certified: MS's screw function *is* our first-variation sector in Kreĭn normal form ($t=\log X$, $\rho(1-\rho)$-symmetrized, masses $1/(\gamma^2+\tfrac14)$), their positivity criterion is a Hermitian-square identity on the single-zero spectrum, and it does **not** extend along the naive route to the Theorem D pair measure, whose complex phases we have now measured. The realistic new theorem is the product-weighted (factorized) pair construction of point 1 — a statement whose both endpoints are now explicitly identified and numerically instrumented in this repository.
