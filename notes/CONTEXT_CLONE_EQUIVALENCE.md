# Contextual meaning depends on the generated unary clone

`formal/cubical/NaturalMachine/ContextCloneEquivalence.agda` closes the
one-way boundary left by `CompositionalContextAdapter.adding-operation-refines`.
Adding an operation may refine contextual equality, but equality of raw
operation lists is not the honest converse condition.  The executable object
is the monoid of state transformations generated by one-hole actions.

## Generator-to-word simulation

For transition systems

\[
\delta_A:X\to A\to X,\qquad \delta_B:X\to B\to X,
\]

an `ActionSimulation δA δB` assigns each \(A\)-generator a finite \(B\)-word
whose execution is the same state transformation, pointwise on \(X\).
`compileWord` extends this assignment by concatenation and `run-compile`
proves that every compiled word realizes the original execution.

Consequently `futureEq-via-simulation` has the contravariant direction one
expects: if every \(A\)-experiment can be executed using \(B\), equality under
all \(B\)-experiments implies equality under all \(A\)-experiments.

Mutual simulations therefore give:

1. `futureEqIso`, an isomorphism between the two complete-future equality
   proof types for set-valued observations;
2. `MutualQuotient.meaningIso`, an identity-on-state-representatives
   isomorphism between the two Cubical set quotients.

The quotient map is not obtained by choosing representatives.  It eliminates
one set quotient into the other and uses the simulated relation to discharge
the path constructor; both inverse laws follow by propositional elimination
because the targets are sets.

## Control: unequal operations, identical contextual meaning

On `Bool`, let

\[
L(x,y)=x,\qquad R(x,y)=y.
\]

`projections-differ` proves \(L\ne R\) by evaluating at `(false,true)`.  Yet
their one-hole translations are the same unary clone:

- for \(L\), left-hole is identity and right-hole is constant;
- for \(R\), left-hole is constant and right-hole is identity.

`swapContextAction` exchanges the hole polarity and realizes every generator
in both directions with a one-letter word.  The module then checks both
`projection-contextEqIso` and `projection-contextual-meaningIso`.

This kills the translation

> equal contextual quotients require equal binary operations.

The exact sufficient replacement is mutual simulation of the generated unary
actions.

## Control: observation collapse kills necessity

The sufficient condition is not necessary.  On `Bool`, compare the one-action
systems

\[
\delta_{\mathrm{id}}(x,*)=x,
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{flip}}(x,*)=\neg x,
\]

and observe both through the constant map `Bool → Unit`.
`identity-words-do-nothing` proves that every word in the first system acts as
the identity.  Hence `flip-not-simulable-by-identity` proves that the flip
generator has no compiled identity-word realization, and
`no-mutual-simulation-after-collapse` rules out mutual simulation of the state
actions.

Nevertheless `constant-futureEq` proves that every two states have equal
observations after every word in either system.  The module checks
`collapsed-futureEqIso` and constructs `collapsed-meaningIso`, an
identity-on-state-representatives isomorphism between the resulting Cubical
set quotients.

Thus even equality of the complete observed future relations, not just an
accidental agreement of quotient cardinalities, does not recover mutual
simulation before observation.  The obstruction is exact: observation has
collapsed a genuine state transformation difference.

## Rigor boundary

- Checked in Cubical Agda with `--safe`, no postulates and no holes.
- The generic word-realization theorem needs no finiteness or decidable
  equality.  Set-valued observation is used only to make future equality
  proof-irrelevant and to form the set quotients.
- The result concerns exact deterministic actions and their complete finite
  words.  It does not identify costs, shortest contexts, probabilistic
  experiments, or presentations of the full universal-algebraic clone.
- No novelty claim is made; this is the ordinary generated-action/automata
  principle compiled into the repository's `FutureBehavior` interface.
