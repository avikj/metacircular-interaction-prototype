# Character-anchor rigidity in torsion-free abelian groups

## Status

The theorem below is proved by an exact finite-support group-ring argument.
It generalizes the parity-anchor proof in `PARITY_RIGIDITY.md`, but it does
not establish novelty.  Group-ring factorization of homometric sets is
classical; see the prior-art boundary at the end.

## Conventions

Write an abelian group \(\Gamma\) additively.  For a finite set
\(A\subset\Gamma\), its **oriented difference multiplicity** is

\[
c_A(g)=\#\{(a,a')\in A^2:a-a'=g\}.
\]

Equivalently, in the integral group ring, put

\[
\mathbf A=\sum_{a\in A}X^a,
\qquad
\mathbf A^*=\sum_{a\in A}X^{-a}.
\]

Then the coefficient of \(X^g\) in \(\mathbf A\mathbf A^*\) is
\(c_A(g)\).  Thus equality of the full, group-labeled oriented difference
multisets is exactly

\[
\mathbf A\mathbf A^*=\mathbf B\mathbf B^*.
\]

This convention is stronger than retaining only Euclidean lengths,
coordinatewise absolute values, quotient-valued differences, or an unlabeled
bag of multiplicities.

## Theorem

**Character-anchor rigidity.**  Let \(\Gamma\) be a torsion-free abelian
group and let \(\chi:\Gamma\to\{\pm1\}\) be a group homomorphism, with
\(\{\pm1\}\) written multiplicatively.  Let
\(A\subset\Gamma\) be finite and suppose one of the two \(\chi\)-fibers of
\(A\) is a singleton.  If a finite set \(B\subset\Gamma\) satisfies
\(c_A=c_B\), then there is a \(t\in\Gamma\) such that

\[
B=A+t\qquad\text{or}\qquad B=-A+t.
\]

The assertion includes \(|A|=1\); that case is immediate.  Nontriviality of
\(\chi\) on all of \(\Gamma\) is unnecessary: only its values on the finite
sets matter.

### Proof

The identity coefficient in \(\mathbf A\mathbf A^*\) is \(|A|\), so
\(|A|=|B|=:N\).  Extend \(\chi\) linearly to the ring homomorphism

\[
\chi_\#: \mathbb Z[\Gamma]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
\qquad X^g\longmapsto\chi(g).
\]

If \(e_A\) and \(o_A\) are the numbers of points on which \(\chi\) is
\(+1\) and \(-1\), respectively, then

\[
\chi_\#(\mathbf A\mathbf A^*)=(e_A-o_A)^2.
\]

The singleton hypothesis gives \(\{e_A,o_A\}=\{1,N-1\}\), hence
\((e_A-o_A)^2=(N-2)^2\).  Since \(e_B+o_B=N\), equality of the
autocorrelations implies

\[
(e_B-o_B)^2=(N-2)^2,
\]

and therefore \(\{e_B,o_B\}=\{1,N-1\}\).  This remains valid for
\(N=1,2\).

Choose a point \(a_0\) in a singleton \(\chi\)-fiber of \(A\), and similarly
choose \(b_0\) for \(B\).  Translate the sets independently:

\[
A_0=A-a_0,
\qquad
B_0=B-b_0.
\]

Translation does not change autocorrelation.  In each normalized set, the
identity is the unique point in the \(\chi=+1\) sector and every other point
lies in the \(\chi=-1\) sector.  This is the higher-rank normalization: it
uses neither an order nor a distinguished endpoint.

Write

\[
\mathbf A_0=1+U,
\qquad
\mathbf B_0=1+V,
\]

where \(U,V\) are supported in the odd sector of the induced
\(\mathbb Z/2\)-grading.  Comparing odd and even parts in
\((1+U)(1+U^*)=(1+V)(1+V^*)\) gives

\[
U+U^*=V+V^*,
\qquad
UU^*=VV^*.
\]

Put \(W=U-V\).  The first identity says \(W^*=-W\).  Using the second and
commutativity,

\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=UU^*-VV^*\\
&=(V+W)(V^*-W)-VV^*\\
&=W(V^*-V-W).
\end{aligned}
\]

The group ring \(\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\) has no zero divisors.  For the exact
amount needed here, this requires no global ordering theorem: all factors
have finite support, and the subgroup \(\Delta\leq\Gamma\) generated by
their supports is finitely generated and torsion-free.  Hence
\(\Delta\cong\mathbb Z^d\), so

\[
\mathbb Z[\Delta]
\cong
\mathbb Z[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_d^{\pm1}]
\]

is an integral domain.  Consequently either \(W=0\), giving \(U=V\), or
\(V^*-V-W=0\), giving \(U=V^*\).  Thus \(A_0=B_0\) or
\(A_0=-B_0\).  Undoing the translations yields

\[
A=B+(a_0-b_0)
\quad\text{or}\quad
A=-B+(a_0+b_0),
\]

which is the claim. \(\square\)

## What “unoriented differences” may mean

For torsion-free \(\Gamma\), the theorem is unchanged if the input is the
**labeled inversion-orbit multiset** of unordered distinct pairs:

\[
\bigl\{\,[a-a']\in
(\Gamma\setminus\{0\})/(g\sim-g):\{a,a'\}\subset A\,\bigr\},
\]

together with \(|A|\).  Every unordered pair in the orbit \(\{g,-g\}\)
contributes once to \(c_A(g)\) and once to \(c_A(-g)\), so this data
reconstructs \(\mathbf A\mathbf A^*\).

The qualifier *labeled* is essential.  A multiset of norms or merely the
multiset of numerical multiplicities does not reconstruct the group-ring
autocorrelation and is outside the theorem.

## Sharp algebraic boundary

The proof uses two independent ingredients:

1. a two-sector grading supplied by \(\chi\), with one sector containing a
   single support point after translation;
2. absence of zero divisors in the finite-support group ring.

Torsion-freeness is a clean sufficient hypothesis for the second ingredient.
With torsion, group rings can have zero divisors (for example
\((1-X^g)(1+\cdots+X^{(n-1)g})=0\) when \(ng=0\)), and the theorem itself can
fail.  In \(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\), for
\(\chi(x)=(-1)^x\), the anchored sets

\[
A=\{0,1,4,6\},\qquad B=\{0,1,3,7\}
\]

have the same cyclic autocorrelation

\[
(4,1,1,1,1,1,2,1,1,1,1,1),
\]

but are not related by translation or inversion.  Thus torsion-freeness is
a substantive hypothesis, not merely a convenience of this proof.

## Computational falsification search

`code/exp38_character_anchor_z2.py` exhausts all nonempty subsets of the
\(4\times4\) grid in \(\mathbb Z^2\), groups them by their exact oriented
difference multiplicities, and checks the theorem for the three nontrivial
mod-2 characters \((-1)^x\), \((-1)^y\), and \((-1)^{x+y}\).  Equivalence is
tested only under translation and central inversion, exactly as in the
theorem.  This is a falsification check, not part of the proof.

## Prior-art boundary

The broad algebraic method is classical.  Rosenblatt and Seymour,
“The Structure of Homometric Sets,” *SIAM Journal on Algebraic and Discrete
Methods* 3 (1982), characterize homometry of finite integer sums by
group-ring/polynomial factorization.  Modern finite-alphabet phase-retrieval
work likewise treats uniqueness through difference sets and homometric
partitions; see Bendory, Edidin, and Gonzalez, “Finite alphabet phase
retrieval,” *Applied and Computational Harmonic Analysis* 66 (2023),
arXiv:2301.10647.  Their neighboring result for homometric ordered
partitions of type \([N-K,1,\ldots,1]\) assumes the cross-difference data
between labeled blocks; it does not imply rigidity from the autocorrelation
of the unlabeled union used here.

A targeted search did not locate the exact corollary “a singleton fiber of an
index-two character forces trivial homometry in a torsion-free abelian
group.”  It may nevertheless be implicit in the classical factorization
literature.  The defensible status is therefore **searched, not found**, not
a novelty claim.  The proved contribution here is the explicit short
character-anchor reduction and its precise domain and data-convention audit.
