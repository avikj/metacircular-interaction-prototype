## 1. Theorem F (GAUGE.md) — VERDICT: SOUND

### 1a. Homogeneity of ALL defining relations under α_g — checked, holds

Cuntz's definition (verified against the extracted text of math/0611541, Definition 3.1,
quoted): *"We define the C\*-algebra Q_N as the **universal** C\*-algebra generated by
isometries s_n, n ∈ N\*, with range projections e_n = s_n s_n\*, and by a unitary u
satisfying the relations s_n s_m = s_{nm}, s_n u = u^n s_n, Σ_{k=0}^{n−1} u^k e_n u^{−k} = 1."*
So (i) the relation set used in GAUGE.md is exactly Cuntz's, and (ii) universality is
available — α_g may be defined on generators. Relation-by-relation, with
α_g(s_n) = g(n)s_n, α_g(u) = u, g unimodular completely multiplicative:

- s_n\*s_n = 1 (isometry): picks up |g(n)|² = 1. Homogeneous (degree 0).
- s_m s_n = s_{mn}: LHS scales by g(m)g(n), RHS by g(mn); equal **precisely because g is
  completely multiplicative** (this is where the hypothesis is consumed). Homogeneous.
- s_n u = u^n s_n: LHS ↦ g(n)·s_n u; RHS ↦ u^n·g(n)s_n (u fixed). Both sides scale by the
  same scalar g(n). Homogeneous. (The u-power on the right carries no g since α_g(u)=u.)
- e_n = s_n s_n\* ↦ g(n)s_n·overline{g(n)}s_n\* = e_n; hence each summand u^k e_n u^{−k} of
  the partition relation is fixed, and Σ_k u^k e_n u^{−k} = 1 is preserved exactly. Fixed.
- u unitary: preserved trivially.

By universality α_g is a *-endomorphism; α_g ∘ α_{ḡ} = id on generators gives
automorphism; g ↦ α_g is a homomorphism T^P → Aut(Q_N). One point GAUGE.md does not
mention (harmless, standard): point-norm **continuity** of the action — on the dense
*-algebra of monomials α_g acts by finite products of g(p)'s, continuous in the product
topology, and automorphisms are isometric, so continuity extends. Note also the pleasant
consistency check: σ_t = α_{g_t} with g_t(n) = n^{it} — the dynamics is itself a
one-parameter subgroup of the gauge torus, so commutation [α_g, σ_t] = 0 is automatic
(G abelian), independently of the "acts diagonally" argument.

### 1b. Cuntz's uniqueness theorem — checked at the source, stronger than needed

Cuntz's Theorem 4.3 (extracted verbatim): *"There is a unique state ω on Q_N with the
following property: there exists a one-parameter automorphism group (σ_t) for which ω is
a 1-KMS-state and such that σ_t(u) = u, σ_t(e_n) = e_n for all n and t. Moreover ω = τ_0∘E
(τ_0 the canonical trace on the Bunce–Deddens algebra F), and the one-parameter group for
which ω is 1-KMS is unique and is the standard one, σ_t(s_n) = n^{it}s_n, σ_t(u) = u."*

So: (i) the dynamics is **exactly** the σ used in GAUGE.md — no discrepancy in the u-part
(σ_t(u) = u is in the theorem's hypotheses). (ii) GAUGE.md's phrase "it exists only at β=1
and is unique" is fully supported, by rescaling: a KMS_β state for σ (β ∈ (0,∞)) is a
1-KMS state for σ′_t := σ_{βt}, which also fixes u and e_n; Theorem 4.3 then forces
ω = τ_0∘E *and* σ′ = σ, i.e. n^{iβt} = n^{it} for all n, t, i.e. β = 1. KMS_0 (traces) is
excluded because Q_N is simple and purely infinite (Cuntz, same paper). No glossed
hypotheses found: the theorem is stated for the universal algebra (and Q_N's simplicity
would anyway collapse any universal-vs-concrete gap).

### 1c. The KMS-invariance arguments — re-derived independently

- ω∘α_g is 1-KMS for σ: if F_{x,y} witnesses KMS for (x,y), then F_{α_g x, α_g y} does,
  using α_g σ_t = σ_t α_g and that α_g is a *-automorphism. Uniqueness ⇒ ω∘α_g = ω. ✓
- ω(u^k a u^{−k}) = ω(a): two independent proofs. (a) Since σ_t(u) = u, u is entire
  analytic with σ_z(u) = u; the KMS identity ω(x σ_{iβ}(y)) = ω(yx) with y = u,
  x = a u^{−1} gives ω(a) = ω(u a u^{−1}); induct for k. (b) Ad(u^k) commutes with σ, so
  ω∘Ad(u^k) is 1-KMS; uniqueness again. ✓ (Holds for any KMS state at any β; no gap.)
- Isotypic vanishing: for α_g(x) = χ(g)x with χ ≠ 1, pick g with χ(g) ≠ 1, then
  ω(x) = χ(g)ω(x) = 0; extends to the closed span of charged monomials (= Q^1 for the
  parity character) by continuity of ω. ✓

### 1d. Minor flags (do not affect the theorem)

- **F(3) wording:** "whose diagonal part is exactly the BC algebra C(Ẑ)" — C(Ẑ) is the BC
  *diagonal*, not the BC algebra. Also worth stating the cleaner fact: neutral monomials
  collapse (via s_n u = u^n s_n) to u^j e_n u^k, so the full fixed-point algebra
  Q_N^G equals Cuntz's Bunce–Deddens algebra F = C\*(u, {e_n}), whose diagonal is C(Ẑ).
- **Lemma F.2 (sound-with-gap-fixed):** "extension to a Haar-measurable function on Ẑ
  pulled back through the diagonal embedding" is ill-posed for merely measurable F: the
  pullback evaluates F on the null set Z ⊂ Ẑ, so "λ = F∘ι" is not a well-defined
  hypothesis. Fix: state the lemma for **continuous** (or Riemann-integrable) F, where the
  proof works verbatim (a continuous F with all Fourier coefficients zero — forced by
  Davenport on the pullback — is identically 0, contradicting λ ≠ 0); or keep only the
  second, already-correct formulation "λ has zero projection onto the Besicovitch
  limit-periodic space", which is exactly what Davenport's theorem gives.

## 2. Theorem A′ (REPORT.md §2.1) — VERDICT: SOUND-WITH-GAP-FIXED (statement survives verbatim); Problem 8.1's parenthetical is BROKEN

### 2a. The normalizations — airtight

c_B(0) = |B| for 0-1 sets, and diam B = max{h : c_B(h) > 0} = deg F; translating min B to
0 gives G with G(0) ≠ 0 and deg G = deg F. No issue.

### 2b. The UFD step — conclusion correct, argument as written is loose; corrected proof

As written, "G is a product of a subset of {C's factors, F0, F̃0} of the right degree
containing exactly one of F0, F̃0" does not deal with multiplicities and does not say why
the palindromic part of G is forced to be exactly C. The correct argument (all in
Z[x], units ±1, monomials excluded by G(0) ≠ 0):

Every irreducible factor of G divides G·G̃ = F·F̃, so it lies in
{palindromic irreducibles P of C, F0, F̃0} up to sign. Reversal is multiplicative and an
involution on irreducibles, and mult_Q(G̃) = mult_{Q̃}(G). Hence:

1. For each palindromic irreducible P (P̃ = ±P) with multiplicity m_P in F: multiplicity
   in F·F̃ is 2m_P; if e_P = mult_P(G), then mult_P(G̃) = e_P too, so 2e_P = 2m_P, forcing
   **e_P = m_P exactly**. (This is the airtight version of "any mixed choice within the
   palindromic part changes nothing": there is no choice at all.)
2. For F0 non-reciprocal (F̃0 ≠ ±F0): mult of F0 in F·F̃ is 1, and
   mult_{F0}(G) + mult_{F̃0}(G) = mult_{F0}(G) + mult_{F0}(G̃) = 1, so G contains exactly
   one of F0, F̃0, to the first power.
3. Therefore G = ±C·F0 = ±F or G = ±C·F̃0 = ±F̃ up to sign; nonnegativity of
   coefficients (B is a set) fixes the sign +.

**Two case gaps in the written proof, both fixable:**

- **Φ_1 = x − 1 is anti-palindromic**, not covered by "cyclotomic polynomials Φ_m (m ≥ 2)
  are palindromic" — and Φ_1 is cyclotomic. It must be (and can be) excluded:
  F_X(1) = π(X) > 0, so (x−1) ∤ F_X. One line, currently missing. (With Φ_1 excluded,
  C̃ = ±C is correct: Φ_2, and all Φ_m, m ≥ 3, are palindromic.)
- **Reciprocal non-cyclotomic F0.** Such factors are not vacuous: reciprocal irreducible
  non-cyclotomic 0-1 polynomials exist — brute force (redteam_poly.py):
  minimal degree 6, e.g. **1 + x + x³ + x⁵ + x⁶**; degree 8: three, e.g. 1+x³+x⁴+x⁵+x⁸;
  degree 10: ten; degree 12: twenty-four. (Answering the referee question: yes, they
  exist, and already below degree 8.) The proof's phrase "exactly one of F0, F̃0" is
  degenerate when F̃0 = ±F0. But the theorem survives verbatim: in that case step 1 above
  applies to F0 as well (it is then a palindromic-up-to-sign irreducible), forcing
  mult_{F0}(G) = 1, hence G = ±C F0 = ±F, and B is a translate of P_X (which is then also
  its own reflection up to translation). So a reciprocal non-cyclotomic factor makes
  rigidity *more* rigid, not less. The corrected proof should treat "palindromic or
  anti-palindromic irreducible" uniformly in step 1 and "non-reciprocal" in step 2,
  instead of "cyclotomic vs non-cyclotomic".

With these fixes, Theorem A′ is correct as stated (its hypothesis — non-cyclotomic part
irreducible — is used only through "at most one reversal-orbit of non-palindromic
factors").

### 2c. REAL ERROR: Problem 8.1's "intermediate target" claim is false

REPORT §8 Problem 1: *"Intermediate target: no reciprocal non-cyclotomic factor (this
alone implies rigidity by the proof of Thm A′)."* **This is backwards.** Counterexample
(machine-verified, redteam_poly.py): the minimal homometric pair itself. For
A = {0,1,2,6,8,11}:

    F_A = Φ₄ · (1 + x + x⁴) · (1 − x³ + x⁵)

with both non-cyclotomic factors irreducible and **non-reciprocal** — F_A has *no*
reciprocal non-cyclotomic factor — yet A is not rigid: reversing the degree-5 factor
gives exactly F_B, B = {0,1,6,7,9,11} (verified: 1 − x² + x⁵ is the reversal of
1 − x³ + x⁵). Reciprocal factors are the *harmless* ones (reversal fixes them, step 2b.1);
the danger is ≥ 2 reversal-orbits of non-reciprocal factors (or one with multiplicity
≥ 2). **Corrected intermediate target:** *every irreducible non-cyclotomic factor of F_X
is reciprocal, except at most one, which occurs with multiplicity 1.* (By the corrected
proof this does imply rigidity; the stated target neither implies nor is implied by it.)

## 5. Theorem C (REPORT §4) — VERDICT: SOUND (one typo in §0)

### 5a. Lower-bound direction (holomorphy ⇒ zero-free region) — airtight

Suppose E(t) ≪ t^{−σ₀} (t → 0⁺), σ₀ ∈ [1/2, 1). (i) M_E(s) = ∫₀¹ E(t)t^{s−1}dt: on
Re s ≥ σ₀ + δ the integrand is dominated by C·t^{δ−1} ∈ L¹(0,1), E is continuous on
(0,1], so M_E is holomorphic on Re s > σ₀ (Fubini + Morera — the interchange is fully
justified by absolute convergence; no conditional convergence is invoked). (ii)
∫₁^∞ P t^{s−1}dt is entire (P(t) ≤ Ce^{−t} for t ≥ 1). (iii) For Re s > 1,
Γ(s)(−ζ′/ζ)(s) = 1/(s−1) − (log 2π)/s + M_E(s) + entire; the right side is holomorphic on
{Re s > σ₀} \ {1} (the s = 0 pole of (log 2π)/s is outside since σ₀ ≥ 1/2), so the left
side continues there with only the pole at s = 1. (iv) **Gamma factor near σ₀: no issue**
— Γ is holomorphic and *nonvanishing* on Re s > 0, so dividing by Γ(s) preserves
holomorphy and creates no spurious zeros/poles anywhere in Re s > σ₀ ≥ 1/2; M_E may blow
up as Re s ↓ σ₀, but only openness of the half-plane is used. (v) A zero ρ of ζ of any
multiplicity m ≥ 1 with Re ρ > σ₀ gives −ζ′/ζ a genuine pole (residue −m ≠ 0) at ρ —
contradiction. Hence Θ ≤ σ₀. Combined with the upper direction (E ≪ t^{−Θ} directly from
Σ_ρ|Γ(ρ)| < ∞, which holds since |Γ(σ+iγ)| ≪ |γ|^{σ−1/2}e^{−π|γ|/2} uniformly on the
strip against density log γ): Θ = inf{σ₀}. Statement (1) proved; no gap found.

### 5b. Statement (2) and the denominator

E = (P² − M²)/(P + M) needs P + M bounded below by c/t: Chebyshev alone gives
P(t) ≥ e^{−1}ψ(1/t) ≥ c/t, so no circularity (the note's "known and nonvanishing" is
even weaker than needed). Forward direction: RH ⇒ E ≪ t^{−1/2} (no ε needed) ⇒ error
O(t^{−3/2}). Equivalence with (1) ✓.

### 5c. Numerical confirmation of the central identity (redteam_thmC.py)

With Λ to 4·10⁶ and 2000 zeros: E(t) − [−2Re Σ_ρ Γ(ρ)t^{−ρ}] = +0.0385, +0.0195,
+0.00983, +0.00395 at t = 0.02, 0.01, 0.005, 0.002 — i.e. ≈ 1.97·t, exactly the linear
O(|z|) remainder R claimed in §3; and the zero-sum itself sits at the 10⁻⁸ level, matching
Σ_ρ|Γ(ρ)| = 1.14·10⁻⁹ times t^{−1/2}. The explicit formula for P, on which Theorems B, C,
D all rest, checks out numerically to the expected order.

### 5d. Flag: sign typo in §0

REPORT §0 (Theorem C summary line) writes "… = (1/t − log 2π)² + **O(t^{−3/2+ε})**". As
t → 0⁺, t^{−3/2+ε} = t^{−3/2}·t^{ε} is *smaller* than t^{−3/2}; RH gives O(t^{−3/2}),
which is **not** O(t^{−3/2+ε}). The body of Theorem C(2) has the correct exponent
**O(t^{−3/2−ε})**. Fix §0 to match the theorem.
