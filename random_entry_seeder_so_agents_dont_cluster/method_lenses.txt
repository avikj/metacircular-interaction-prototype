# Living people appear only through published method.

Panini -- write the rule system, then the metarules that order the rules
Aryabhata -- when the equation resists, pulverize it and keep the trace column
Brahmagupta -- find the composition law that makes solutions a group
Bhaskara II -- cycle a bad approximation deliberately until it becomes exact
Madhava -- push a finite identity to an infinite series and control the remainder
Nagarjuna -- show the four exhaustive alternatives share a false presupposition
Dignaga -- define the term by what it excludes, never by what it positively names
Dharmakirti -- ask what causal work the concept does before asking what it denotes
Gangesa -- delimit every relation; two relations between the same pair are different relations
Kumarila -- when rules conflict, order them; the ordering is the content
Bhartrhari -- ask whether the unit you are analyzing is the real bearer of meaning
Euclid -- refuse the step that has no stated ground
Archimedes -- bound it above and below and squeeze
Diophantus -- solve the special case completely and let the method generalize itself
Hypatia -- make the difficult text usable by others; commentary is research
Liu Hui -- approximate by a procedure whose error you can name
Seki Takakazu -- eliminate variables systematically; the bookkeeping is the theorem
al-Khwarizmi -- name the procedure, then the procedure becomes an object
Omar Khayyam -- cross two curves to solve what one equation cannot
Ibn al-Haytham -- design the experiment that would refute you
al-Biruni -- learn the other tradition's terms before comparing
Ibn Khaldun -- look for the structural cycle behind the particular events
Fermat -- descend: from any solution build a strictly smaller one
Euler -- compute boldly, then find why the computation was allowed
Gauss -- do not publish the scaffolding, but do build it
Galois -- replace the object by its group of symmetries
Riemann -- move to the complex plane and let the singularities speak
Dedekind -- define the object by the cut it makes in what you already have
Cantor -- ask whether two infinite collections can be matched
Poincare -- when the equation is unsolvable, study the qualitative flow
Hilbert -- state the problem list; the list is the contribution
Noether -- every symmetry is a conservation law; find the invariant
Ramanujan -- trust the form of the identity, then hand it to someone to prove
Weil -- guess the right cohomology by counting points
Godel -- encode the system inside itself and read off the limit
Turing -- ask what a machine could do, then what no machine can
von Neumann -- build the axioms that make the physics computable
Shannon -- separate the signal's content from its representation and count bits
Kolmogorov -- define randomness as incompressibility
Chaitin -- ask how many bits of axiom the theorem costs
Erdos -- find the probabilistic existence proof, then hunt the explicit one
Grothendieck -- rise to the generality where the problem becomes trivial
Serre -- write it so short that the mechanism is visible
Atiyah -- an index is a topological quantity computed analytically
Connes -- the space is the algebra of functions on it, even when there is no space
Thurston -- the goal is human understanding, not the shortest correct proof
Conway -- play with the small cases until the structure surrenders
Mandelbrot -- look at the same object at every scale at once
Wolfram -- enumerate the simple programs exhaustively and look at what happens
Feynman -- rederive it your own way or you do not know it
Dirac -- follow the formal beauty of the equation past what is currently justified
Boltzmann -- count the microstates
Prigogine -- study the system far from equilibrium, where structure forms
McClintock -- have a feeling for the organism; look at the exceptions
Margulis -- the units you assumed were individuals may be collaborations
Darwin -- variation plus selection plus time explains the appearance of design
Wiener -- the loop, not the parts, is the object
Ashby -- a regulator must have at least as much variety as what it regulates
Simone Weil -- attention is the faculty; look without imposing
Sofia Kovalevskaya -- take the singular case others avoided as the real problem
Sophie Germain -- work the general obstruction rather than the individual case
Emmy Noether -- ask what structure the proof actually used
Julia Robinson -- turn the decision problem into a diophantine one
Mirzakhani -- doodle the surface for years until the recursion appears

Langlands -- suspect that two unrelated theories are two faces of a correspondence
Voevodsky -- if the foundations cannot check your proof, change the foundations
Tao -- decompose into structured plus pseudorandom parts
Gowers -- ask what the natural proof strategy is and why it fails
Villani -- carry an inequality across fields until it becomes a geometry
Bourgain -- estimate everything; the estimate is the structure
Deligne -- purity: weights control what can appear
Milnor -- construct the counterexample that shows the intuition was wrong
Nash -- the embedding exists if you allow enough wrinkling
Ulam -- if you cannot solve it, simulate it and look
