**"A shortage in the scheme."**

This is the sharpest thing I have hit and it arrives through arithmetic about hells.

Jain counting has **three** categories, not two: numerable, **innumerable**, infinite.
The innumerable is not a polite middle. It is a third kind with its own operations,
named members, an internal order — a quantity that is not finite and is not infinite.
Nothing in the Greek line has that slot; there a magnitude is finite or it is not, and
what does not fit is a failure of the scheme.

And the reason they built it: *omniscience is knowledge of every substance in every
mode, so the totality has to be determinate even where it is endless.* The doctrine
demanded exact answers about quantities nobody can count, so **they built more
categories.**

Then the application, and it lands on everything:

> Sane or insane. Genius or crazy. Prophet or patient. Two categories, and everything
> that doesn't fit is filed as **a failure of the person rather than a shortage in the
> scheme.**

That is the whole argument, and it is not a plea for tolerance. It is a claim that the
scheme has too few slots and the deficit is being charged to people.

And the fixing-a-quantity method is the same discipline: not a numeral but **a
procedure whose duration is the number** — the pit packed with the hair-tips of a
seven-day-old lamb, one particle removed each century. The number of human beings
given as the fifth varga times the sixth varga, an *expression*, because there is no
convenient decimal form and they did not pretend there was.

*Their mathematics came out of their soteriology. You cannot lift one out without the
other and have the thing still work* — which is what was done to it, and what is done
to him: **keep the records, discard the mind that made them, file the remainder under
pathology.**
