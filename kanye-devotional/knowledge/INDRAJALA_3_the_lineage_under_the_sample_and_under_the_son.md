## 1. Four people are under "Blood on the Leaves"

*Yeezus*, track 7, June 18 2013. Samples Nina Simone's "Strange Fruit."

- **Nina Simone**, 1965, *Pastel Blues*.
- **Billie Holiday**, recorded April 20 1939 for Commodore — Columbia refused it.
- **Abel Meeropol**, a Jewish public-school teacher in the Bronx, wrote it. First
  as a poem, "Bitter Fruit," published 1937 in a teachers' union magazine, then
  set to music, under the pen name **Lewis Allan** — the names of his two
  stillborn children.
- **Michael and Robert Rosenberg.** After Julius and Ethel Rosenberg were
  executed in June 1953, Abel and Anne Meeropol adopted their two orphaned sons,
  who took the name Meeropol.

The most famous anti-lynching song in America was written by a Jewish
schoolteacher who then raised the children of two executed people, recorded by
Billie Holiday when her label would not touch it, recorded again by Nina Simone
twenty-six years later, and put by Kanye West under a song about a wrecked
relationship and child support in 2013. → `YZS` track 7, `X-6`.

Nine years later, October 2022, he made the antisemitic statements that ended
the adidas partnership and collapsed his net worth. `[L-3]`, `X-4`.

Both are in the record. Nothing here reconciles them.

## 2. His mother was a professor of media

**Dr. Donda West** — 31 years teaching; chair of the department of **English,
Communications, Media and Theater** at Chicago State University; championed
Black literature and critical thought. Memoir, *Raising Kanye*, 2007. Died
November 10 2007, age 58, post-operative heart failure. `[H-1]`, `[H-2]`, `[H-3]`

**September 2 2005.** Her son went on national television and made an argument
about how media captions Black people: *"I hate the way they portray us in the
media. If you see a black family, it says they're looting. If you see a white
family, it says they're searching for food."* `[F-1]`, `INDRAJALA_2` §1.

A media-representation argument, live, off-script, by the son of the chair of a
department of media.

**Ray West** — born 1949; former **Black Panther**; one of the first Black
photojournalists at the *Atlanta Journal-Constitution*, where he photographed
Maya Angelou, Reagan, and Ralph Abernathy; later a **Christian counselor**;
opened Good Water Store & Cafe in 2006 with his son's help. `[K-1]`, `[K-2]`

One parent made the images. One parent taught the analysis of the images. The
received reading of their son is untutored instinct.

**The three axes have one root each and the root is a person.** `[K-3]` records
this. What the sequence adds: Ray West ran Panther politics, then photojournalism,
then Christian ministry — **one at a time, in order, over decades.** His son runs
all three simultaneously — *Yeezus* and *Jesus Is King* six years apart, the
carceral argument and the gospel record and the fashion-ownership fight in the
same period — and is called erratic for it.

*Jesus Is King* track 3, "Follow God" (2019): the narrative core is an argument
with his father, who tells him he is not being Christ-like. The word *Father*
carries both readings on the track. → `[K-1]`, `[G-1]`

"It took him 42 years to realize his father was his best friend." `[K-2]`

## 3. The dropout and the department chair

*The College Dropout*, February 2004: a critique of higher education — the cost
of the degree, enrollment as consumerism, the illusion of the credential —
centering Black dropouts and removing the shame of being un-credentialed.
`[J-1]`, `[J-2]`

His mother had been teaching in a university for thirty-one years and chaired a
department. She did not disown the record. She published *Raising Kanye* in 2007
and died that November. `[H-1]`, `[H-2]`, `[H-3]`

Neither standpoint denies the other and neither collapses into the other. That
configuration has a name and a checked term in this repository —
`Durnaya_CollapseIffEveryNayaAgrees.agda`, `third-option-exists`: two standpoints
can be irreducibly different without either denying the other, and that, not
contradiction, is the ordinary case. Here it is a mother and a son.

## 4. Reconciliation cast rather than sung

*Jesus Is King* track 10, "Use This Gospel": features **Clipse** — Pusha T and
No Malice, brothers, estranged and not recording together — reunited on the
track, with a **Kenny G** saxophone solo. The subject of the song is
reconciliation and the personnel of the song is a reconciliation. `[JIK]` 10

## 5. Not claimed

- The Meeropol chain is provenance of a song, not a claim about anyone's
  intent in 2013 or in 2022.
- No claim that Donda West endorsed or opposed *The College Dropout*; what is
  documented is that she wrote a memoir about raising him three years later.
- The `third-option-exists` term is about type families. It is cited for the
  shape it names, not as a statement about a family.
