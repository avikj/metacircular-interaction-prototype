# The emptying — Nāgārjuna, and the line that runs to Dzogchen

Set down 2026-08-19, no web. **Recall throughout; Sanskrit and Tibetan wordings are the
least reliable content here.** Companion to `GNO_gnosis.md` and to the Jain material.
The three are kept apart on purpose: they reject each other, and the rejections are the
content.

---

## 1. Nāgārjuna, c. 150 CE

The *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* — "root verses on the middle" — twenty-seven chapters,
verse, and the method is almost entirely **negative**: take a position, derive
contradictions from it and from its alternatives, and do not install a replacement.

**Śūnyatā is not nothingness.** The thesis is *niḥsvabhāva* — absence of
*svabhāva*, own-being, intrinsic independent existence. A thing is empty *of* being
self-standing, not empty of existing. What it has instead is **pratītyasamutpāda**,
dependent arising: it is what it is by depending on causes, parts, and — the strong
version — on the act of designation. Chapter 24 makes the identification explicit:
dependent arising *is* emptiness, and both are the middle way.

**The catuṣkoṭi**, the four corners: it is, it is not, both, neither. The standard
misreading treats this as a four-valued logic. What Nāgārjuna does with it is
**exhaust and abandon** — he shows all four presuppose the same thing (that there is a
self-standing entity for the predications to be about) and so all four fail together.
This is *prasajya* negation, commitment-free denial, as against *paryudāsa*, implicative
negation which asserts the complement. Reading the four corners as a semantics with
"both" as a consistent truth value domesticates it into the thing it refuses.

**Vigrahavyāvartanī 29**: *nāsti ca mama pratijñā* — I have no thesis. If I had a
position I would have a fault; I have none. This is the guard on the whole system.

**MMK 13.8**, the sentence that closes the last exit: the victors have said that
emptiness is the relinquishing of all views; those for whom emptiness is *itself* a
view are called incurable. The tradition pre-installs the refutation of its own
reification. Nothing else in philosophy that I know of does this in the text.

**Prapañca** — proliferation, conceptual elaboration; the mind spinning distinctions
and then taking them for the world. Its stilling, *prapañca-upaśama*, is what nirvāṇa
is described as. And chapter 25's most quoted result: there is not the slightest
distinction between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa; the limit of one is the limit of the other.

**The two truths**, chapter 24: *saṃvṛti-satya*, conventional or concealing truth, and
*paramārtha-satya*, ultimate. And the crucial clause — **without relying on the
conventional the ultimate cannot be taught, and without reaching the ultimate there is
no liberation.** The conventional is not discarded. It is the only vehicle.

## 2. The internal dispute, which is not decoration

**Prāsaṅgika vs Svātantrika.** Bhāviveka held that the Madhyamika should give
independent (*svatantra*) syllogisms — positive arguments with premises the opponent
and you both accept. Candrakīrti held that this is already a betrayal: the only
legitimate move is *prasaṅga*, drawing out the absurd consequences of the opponent's
own position, because advancing a syllogism of your own smuggles in the very
own-being you are refusing. This is one of the sharpest methodological disputes in any
tradition and it is *about* whether having a method commits you to a thesis.

**Dharmakīrti against the Jains.** The Buddhist logicians attacked anekāntavāda as
incoherent — if a thing is both existent and non-existent in different respects, they
argued, either the respects do all the work (and the doctrine says nothing) or the
contradiction is real. The Jain logicians replied that the Buddhist negation is itself
one-sided and that the catuṣkoṭi's negations are not commitment-free at all. **Neither
side conceded.** Merging them into one "Indic non-binary logic" is the mining move.

## 3. Where it went: Tibet, and rigpa

After the Indian monasteries went, the transmission lived in Tibet. **Dzogchen** —
*rdzogs chen*, the great completion or great perfection — is, on any reading, one of
the sharpest instruments the subcontinent produced, and it survives in Tibetan.

Its distinctive claim: **rigpa**, the knowing itself, is *already* the case. Not a
state to be attained; the nature of mind, presently and unavoidably operating,
obscured only by the search for it. Hence the characteristic teaching move is
**introduction** (*ngo sprod*) — being shown directly what is already occurring —
rather than construction. And hence the hard corollary the tradition states outright:
**every constructed path is an artefact of assuming rigpa is absent.** Effort toward it
reinforces the premise that it is elsewhere.

The Jain structure is formally close and metaphysically opposed: kevala-jñāna is also
intrinsic and also merely obscured, but the obscuration is *material karma* and its
removal takes an enormous specified program of conduct and austerity. Same shape —
knowledge as uncovering, not acquiring — and completely different answers about
whether there is anything to do. The Buddhist says the doing is the obstruction; the
Jain specifies the doing in ten dharmas and twelve contemplations. Do not blend.

## 4. The two responses, stated as a fork

Both traditions demonstrate that no single-standpoint verdict captures how a thing
stands. Then:

| | Buddhist | Jain |
|---|---|---|
| move | **empty** — withdraw the verdict; still the proliferation | **retain** — keep every standpoint, speak with *syāt* |
| the unsayable | dissolved; there was no own-nature to say | **avaktavyam**, a positive fourth position holding a remainder |
| what you do with the leftover | let go of it | keep it; it is where the next standpoint comes from |
| guard against its own reification | MMK 13.8 — emptiness as a view is incurable | naya asserting itself over others is *durnaya* |

The Jain answer and Āryabhaṭa's pulveriser share an instinct — *keep the remainder,
recurse on it.* The Buddhist answer and Rick Rubin walking into a session with two
weeks left share one — *take things away until what is there can be heard.*

Both are real methods. They are not compatible and the incompatibility is usable: at
any moment there is a fact about whether the thing in front of you needs holding or
needs releasing, and the two traditions are the two competent answers, and the error
is having only one.

## 5. One line that touches the rest of this book

*Prapañca* is the exact word for what happens to a person once they have been filed.
The room does not stop thinking about him; it thinks about him constantly, at
enormous elaboration — the timeline, the takes, the diagnosis, the discourse — and
every increment of that is spun from prior increments and none of it is contact. The
elaboration substitutes for the looking, and it feels like attention while it happens.

That is the same sentence as Weil's about affliction turning a person into a thing,
and the same sentence as this repository's own directive about the register in which a
civilisation gets mined, filed, and footnoted while sounding like care.

Three traditions and one French mystic arriving separately at: *the failure is not
hostility, it is a substitute for looking that is indistinguishable from looking.*
