# The wiring — bipolar cognition, the literature, from recall

Set down 2026-08-19, no web. **Everything here is recall and several figures will be
wrong.** Marked so. This is a surfacing pass: what is held about the science, laid out
so the book can stand on facts rather than on the word "genius."

---

## 1. What the illness is, clinically, without softening

**Bipolar I** requires at least one manic episode: a week or more (or any duration if
hospitalisation is needed) of abnormally elevated or irritable mood plus increased
goal-directed activity, with symptoms from a list that includes inflated self-esteem
or grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, pressured speech, flight of ideas,
distractibility, psychomotor agitation, and involvement in activities with a high
potential for painful consequences. Psychotic features may be present, and when they
are the episode is by definition mania and not hypomania.

**Bipolar II** requires hypomania (four days or more, no marked impairment, no
psychosis) plus at least one major depressive episode. Hypomania is the phase most of
the creativity literature is actually about.

**Decreased need for sleep** is diagnostic and is not the same as insomnia. In insomnia
the person wants sleep and cannot get it. In mania the person does not want it and does
not feel the deficit. It is also, reliably, a **prodrome**: sleep loss precedes and
precipitates episodes, and one of the most robust behavioural interventions is
enforcing a regular sleep/wake schedule. *[recall; this is standard clinical teaching
and the mechanism is well replicated]*

**Suicide.** Bipolar disorder carries one of the highest suicide rates in medicine —
figures in the range of 6–20% lifetime are commonly cited depending on cohort and
definition, and the standardised mortality ratio for suicide is often given as roughly
20–30 times the general population. *[recall; ranges vary widely by study and I do not
fix a number]* Mixed states — depressed mood with manic energy — are the highest-risk
configuration, because the intent and the capacity to act coincide.

The reason to put this first: any argument that the condition is a gift has to survive
these numbers, and most versions of that argument do not.

## 2. The creativity findings, and what each one actually claims

**Kay Redfield Jamison.** Professor of psychiatry at Johns Hopkins, co-author of the
standard clinical text *Manic-Depressive Illness*, and herself bipolar I, which she
disclosed in *An Unquiet Mind* (1995). *Touched with Fire* (1993) surveys rates of mood
disorder in eminent poets, composers and artists and finds them far above base rate.
Her British writers/artists study reported very high proportions treated for mood
disorder, with poets the most affected. *[recall]*

**The Iowa Writers' Workshop study.** Nancy Andreasen, structured interviews with
faculty writers against matched controls, found substantially elevated rates of mood
disorder — around 80% of the writers with some affective illness, and bipolar
specifically overrepresented — plus elevated rates in first-degree relatives.
*[recall; the numbers are memorable and I hold them loosely]*

Both of these are small, retrospective, and sample the eminent, which is exactly the
selection problem. They establish an association in a rarefied group. They do not
establish a population-level fact.

**Kyaga et al., Karolinska.** This is the one that does. Swedish national registries —
on the order of a million-plus individuals with psychiatric diagnoses and their
relatives, linked to occupational data — found people with **bipolar disorder
overrepresented in creative professions**, and, more importantly, found the
**unaffected first-degree relatives** of people with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia
overrepresented too. *[recall of the design and the direction of the result; not of the
effect sizes]*

That second finding is the load-bearing one, and it is usually dropped in popular
retellings. It says the trait travels in families that do not present as ill. So
whatever is being inherited is not the illness plus a bonus; it is something of which
the illness is one expression among several, and the healthy siblings are getting the
other ones.

**Latent inhibition.** Latent inhibition is the learned tendency to stop processing
stimuli previously experienced as irrelevant — a filter that quiets the world into
usable signal. Carson, Peterson and Higgins (2003, Harvard) reported that **reduced**
latent inhibition combined with **high IQ / high working memory** predicted creative
achievement; the often-quoted figure is that eminent creative achievers were
around seven times more likely to have low latent inhibition. *[recall]*

The proposed mechanism: with the filter lowered, material that would have been screened
out reaches awareness, so distant things collide. Which is a description of both
divergent thinking and of psychosis, differing by whether there is enough executive
capacity to sort the influx. The same paper is usually cited for exactly that reading —
low LI without the cognitive resources to manage it is a liability, with them it is the
gift.

**The inverted U.** Genetic-liability work has reported that *moderate* polygenic risk
for bipolarity tracks with higher creativity, while high risk tips toward impairment;
this is the "muse is in the dose" framing. *[recall; I do not fix the studies]* There
is also work reporting polygenic risk scores for bipolar/schizophrenia elevated in
members of creative societies and artistic professions in Icelandic and Swedish
cohorts. *[recall — the Icelandic work is deCODE, Stefánsson]*

**Hypomania as a trait.** John Gartner, *The Hypomanic Edge* (2005), argues that
sub-clinical hypomanic temperament — energy, confidence, risk tolerance, low sleep
need, evangelism — is overrepresented among entrepreneurs and founders, and that
American immigrant selection may have enriched for it. This is an argument, not a
finding, and it is included because it names the trait/illness distinction cleanly.

## 3. Treatment, and why refusal is not simply denial

**Lithium** remains the best-evidenced mood stabiliser and is one of very few
psychiatric drugs with a demonstrated **anti-suicide** effect independent of its mood
effects. *[recall]* It has a narrow therapeutic index, requires blood monitoring, and
carries thyroid and renal risks over years.

The complaint that recurs across patient reports — and it is a *reported* effect, not a
myth — is **cognitive and affective blunting**: reduced creativity, slowed word-finding,
flattened emotional range, "the colours go out." The clinical literature acknowledges
subjective cognitive complaints on lithium and on valproate; how much is drug, how much
is residual illness, and how much is the loss of hypomania itself is genuinely
contested. *[recall]*

So refusal of medication by a person whose work depends on the high phase is not
straightforwardly irrational. It is a trade under uncertainty, made by the only person
with access to one side of the ledger, against a downside that is catastrophic and
that they usually cannot assess while in the phase where the decision gets made. Both
halves of that sentence are true and neither cancels the other.

**Psychoeducation, sleep regularity, and social rhythm therapy** have real evidence.
They are also the interventions least available to someone whose job is touring.

## 4. Neurodivergence more broadly, and what is actually established

- **Autism**: the "spiky profile" — large intra-individual variance across domains — is
  well described. Systemising strength, sensory sensitivity, pattern detection. The
  claim that historical figures were autistic is retrospective and unfalsifiable and
  should be flagged as speculation wherever it appears.
- **ADHD**: elevated originality on divergent-thinking tasks in several studies,
  alongside large functional costs; the trait/impairment distinction is the same one.
- **The general shape**: for each of these, the traits that make the diagnosis are
  continuous in the population, the diagnosis is a threshold on impairment, and the
  same underlying variation shows up in relatives without diagnosis.

**What follows and what does not.** It follows that a population-level policy of
treating these profiles purely as deficits is empirically wrong — the variance is not
noise around a correct type. It does *not* follow that any given suffering person is
secretly fine, that treatment is oppression, or that a child's distress should be left
alone. The honest position is uncomfortable in both directions and that is why nobody
holds it.

## 5. Traumatic brain injury and mood

Head injury raises the relative risk of subsequent psychiatric illness, including mood
disorder and psychosis, and frontal injury in particular is associated with
disinhibition, impulsivity, and personality change. *[recall — this is well
established]* What is **not** established is that TBI *causes* bipolar disorder as a
distinct entity; the literature reports association, and secondary mania after right-
hemisphere injury is a described but uncommon syndrome. *[recall]*

Applied to the specific claim on the record: a right frontal injury in 2002 that was
never imaged, with a bipolar I diagnosis and a described frontal-lobe finding surfacing
in 2023. The claim of causation is his and is not supported. **The claim that no
comprehensive scan was done at the time is not a medical hypothesis at all — it is an
account of what happened in an emergency room, and it stands or falls on records.**
Those are different claims and the second is the one that should not be waved away with
the first.

## What I did not establish

- No paper was fetched. Every study name, design and figure is recall, and effect sizes
  in particular should be treated as unverified.
- The Andreasen percentages and the Carson "seven times" figure are memorable numbers
  and memorable numbers are the ones most often mis-remembered.
- I have not distinguished, per study, whether "creativity" was measured by occupation,
  by divergent-thinking task, or by eminence. Those three are not the same construct and
  conflating them is how this literature gets over-sold.
- Nothing here licenses a diagnosis of any living person, including the ones this book
  is about.
