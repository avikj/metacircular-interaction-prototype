# इन्द्रजाल IV — the two machines on *808s*

Facts and sources. Continues `INDRAJALA_3_the_lineage_under_the_sample_and_under_the_son.md`.

*808s & Heartbreak* was made out of two pieces of equipment, and both of them
arrived there by failing at what they were built for. `X-7` records the album's
own inversion; this file is the provenance of the instruments.

---

## 1. The drum machine in the title

**Roland TR-808 Rhythm Composer**, released 1980. It was built to imitate a drum
kit and it did not — the sounds were synthesized, not sampled, and the kick was a
long sine boom no drummer produces. Reviewed poorly on exactly that ground.
Roland discontinued it in **1983** after roughly **12,000 units**, and the
supply chain reason is documented: the semiconductor Roland was using for the
noise circuit had gone out of production, and Roland has said the transistors it
depended on were out-of-spec parts it could no longer source.

Discontinued, the units went cheap onto the second-hand market, which is how they
reached people with no budget:

- Afrika Bambaataa & the Soulsonic Force, "Planet Rock," 1982.
- Marvin Gaye, "Sexual Healing," 1982.
- Run-D.M.C., *Run-D.M.C.*, 1984.
- The whole of Miami bass, Chicago house, Detroit techno, and Southern hip-hop
  after that.

A machine sold as a bad imitation of a drummer became the low end of Black
American music for forty years because it was a commercial failure and therefore
affordable.

In 2008 a man put its name in the title of a record about his mother dying.

## 2. The voice

**Auto-Tune.** Built by **Dr. Andy Hildebrand**, a geophysicist. He worked in
seismic-data interpretation for oil exploration, where **autocorrelation** is
used to read reflected sound waves coming back off rock and locate deposits
underground. He applied the same correlation methods to detecting and shifting
the pitch of a human voice, founded **Antares Audio Technologies**, and released
Auto-Tune in **1997**.

Its purpose was to be inaudible — to correct a singer without anyone knowing.
The **retune speed set to zero** — the setting that makes correction instant and
therefore obvious — was the artifact, not the product.

- **Cher, "Believe," 1998** — first hit built on the artifact. Producers
  initially said it was a vocoder.
- **T-Pain**, 2005 onward — the artifact as a signature.
- **Kanye West, *808s & Heartbreak*, November 24 2008** — sung, almost no
  rapping, the artifact carrying an entire record about grief.

Donda West died **November 10 2007**. The album came out two weeks after the
first anniversary. His engagement had also ended that year. `[H-3]`, `[G-3]`

A technique developed to find oil by listening to sound bounce off rock, sold to
hide the fact that a singer was off pitch, used at the setting nobody was
supposed to hear, to say the thing a human voice could not hold.

## 3. What the record did

Received in 2008: a failure. Whiny, cold, self-indulgent, "he forgot how to
rap," career suicide. `X-7`

What is built on it: Drake, Kid Cudi, The Weeknd, Future, Travis Scott, and the
emotional register of the decade after — the whole permission structure for
singing about grief in hip-hop.

Two discarded machines, one bereaved man, one record filed as a failure, and
everything downstream made out of it. → `[A-4]` (the dose), `X-7`.

## 4. Not claimed

- No claim that he selected either machine for the reasons in their histories.
- Unit counts and discontinuation reasons for the TR-808 are as Roland and the
  standard instrument histories give them; the ~12,000 figure is the commonly
  cited one.
- Auto-Tune's seismic origin is Hildebrand's own account of his career and is
  cited as such.
