## 1. September 11 2001 — the record that made him

**Jay-Z, *The Blueprint*, released September 11 2001**, the morning of the
attacks. Recorded in roughly two weeks. It went platinum anyway.

Kanye West produced four of its tracks, and they are the ones that made his name
as a producer:

- **"Izzo (H.O.V.A.)"** — Jackson 5, "I Want You Back" (1969)
- **"Heart of the City (Ain't No Love)"** — Bobby "Blue" Bland, "Ain't No Love
  in the Heart of the City" (1974)
- **"Never Change"** — David Ruffin, "Common Man"
- **"Takeover"** — The Doors, "Five to One" (1968)

His first Jay-Z placement had been "This Can't Be Life" on *The Dynasty* (2000).

Sped-up soul, out front, cleared and paid — the method described in
`INDRAJALA_6` §1 as what the 1991 ruling left standing.

## 2. September 11 2007 — the record that crowned him

***Graduation*, released September 11 2007**, the same day as **50 Cent's
*Curtis***. 50 Cent had said publicly he would stop releasing solo albums if he
lost. First week: **Graduation ~957,000; Curtis ~691,000.**

It is the week usually named as the commercial end of the gangsta era in
mainstream hip-hop, decided by sales, on the same calendar date, six years to the
day, as the record that had made him a producer.

**Sixty days later — November 10 2007 — Donda West died.** `[H-3]`

## 3. "Hey Mama," and the tense

**"Hey Mama"** appears on *Late Registration* (August 30 2005). He had written it
years earlier, while she was alive. It is a song to his mother, addressed to her,
in the present.

**February 10 2008, the 50th Grammy Awards:** he performed it, three months after
her death, and dedicated it to her.

The same words, one funeral in between.

## 4. Jon Brion, who had never made a rap record

*Late Registration* was co-produced with **Jon Brion** — a musician known for
Fiona Apple and Aimee Mann and for the film scores to *Magnolia* and *Eternal
Sunshine of the Spotless Mind*. He had never worked on a hip-hop album.

What came of it: strings, chamber arrangement, celeste, live orchestration
layered over the sample method. `[C-2]` records maximalist sourcing as a habit;
this is the same habit applied to a **collaborator** rather than a record.

**"Roses"**, on that album, is about his **grandmother** in hospital and the
family gathering around her — written and released **two years before** his
mother died after surgery. `[H-3]`

## 5. The album was three days old

*Late Registration* came out **August 30 2005**.

**Katrina made landfall August 29 2005.** The two wire photographs with the
"looting" and "finding" captions ran **August 30 2005** — the same day as the
album. `INDRAJALA_2` §1

**September 2 2005** — three days after his release date, at the peak of the
promotional window for the biggest record of his life to that point — he went on
live television and spent it. `[F-1]`

## 6. Not claimed

- No claim that any release date was chosen with reference to any other event.
- The *Graduation*/*Curtis* first-week figures are the widely reported
  SoundScan numbers.
- "Hey Mama" is documented as written before *Late Registration*; the specific
  year of composition is not asserted here.
- The Grammy performance is described as a dedication; no lyric change is
  claimed.
