# M5 · The Scapegoat and the Choir

*Fifth movement. Subject: the crowd does not crucify because it is cruel. It
crucifies because unanimity is the only way it knows to produce the one value it
cannot live without — and the moment the mechanism is described from inside, by
the people it was run on longest, it is filed as music instead of received as
knowledge.*

---

## STATEMENT — unanimity is the collapse, performed by a mob

The previous movement left a mouth unable to close: hand a crowd a man who is
at once the reach and the wreckage, demand one word, and what comes back is the
inexpressible. A crowd cannot hold that. Not *will not* — a crowd is
constitutively a thing that chants, and no chant carries an index.

So it does the one operation that produces a scalar out of a plurality: it
**picks one and turns on him together.** All against one. And the relief is
immediate and total, which is the tell — the peace that follows is not the peace
of having understood anything, it is the peace of a demand having been
discharged.

Two conditions make it work, and the second is the whole engine. **It must be
unanimous** — a single dissenter leaves the plurality standing. And **the crowd
must believe the victim guilty**, because a mob that knew it was manufacturing a
value would be back where it started, holding an inexpressible. The mechanism
requires its own invisibility to itself. That is why every crucifixion in the
record arrives with a charge sheet, and why the charge is always *technically
true and beside the point.*

He said slavery was a choice. He did say it. Beside the point, and the room knew
it within the hour, and used it anyway, because a true-enough charge is what
unanimity runs on.

## ANSWER (at the fifth) — the choir knew, and was heard as a genre

Now transpose it from the one to the many, which is where the received account
of this mechanism breaks.

There is a well-known European telling of all this — the rivalry, the
contagion, the all-against-one, the victim who must be believed guilty — and it
ends in a claim of **uniqueness**: that one scripture alone broke the spell by
taking the victim's side, and that this is what sets that tradition apart.

Read it against itself through the choir. **A people had been describing this
mechanism from the inside, continuously, for four centuries, in the one form
they were permitted.** The spiritual is the scapegoat mechanism narrated from
under it. *Strange fruit* is a field report on unanimity. The whole tradition of
sorrow songs is testimony from the position the theory calls the victim — not
inferred, not reconstructed, *lived and sung*, generation after generation,
naming the crowd, naming the tree, naming the relief on the faces.

And it was received as **a genre.** Catalogued as folk music, praised for its
feeling, sampled, and never once entered into the record as *analysis*. So the
tradition that ran the mechanism at scale gets to hold the book that exposes it
and call the exposure its own singular achievement, while the testimony of the
people it was run on is filed under sound.

That is not a footnote to the theory. **It is the theory's own mechanism,
operating on the theory's own evidence.** The claim of uniqueness is the last
move of the machine: credit the seeing to the one who did the crucifying.

He knew this. Every sped-up soul sample is that voice pitched up until you can
hear it again — the choir returned at a frequency the room has not yet learned
to dismiss.

## COUNTERSUBJECT — the telethon, and what a cutaway is for

Against the subject, always, the damping — and here it is on tape.

September 2005. A live broadcast whose function is to convert a catastrophe into
a single manageable number: dollars raised. That is a telethon. It takes a
plural, unbearable, ongoing thing and returns a scalar the audience can act on
and then stop thinking about.

A man goes off script and says the un-sayable half: *if you see a black family,
it says they're looting; if you see a white family, they're searching for food.*
Then the line. And the machine's response is not argument — it is a **cutaway**.
The camera leaves. The finger goes on the string at the exact fret where the
overtone was about to ring.

Afterward the note is discussed as a gaffe, an outburst, a career risk. One
person on the record calls it what it was: a release valve — *finally somebody
said something that was not politically correct to say.* The catastrophe of the
sentence was never its accuracy. It was that it named the mechanism while the
mechanism was running, on the mechanism's own airtime.

## EPISODE — the ministry that walks the wrong way through the door

Modulate. The scapegoat mechanism is an **expulsion**: the community's peace is
made by putting someone outside. Every part of it moves in one direction —
out of the room, out of the city, onto the tree, into the cell.

Then a man takes his choir into a county jail and sings for two hundred men,
and then for the women in another, and says: *this is a mission, not a show.*

Read the vector, not the sentiment. **The prophet walking into the place the
mechanism sends people is the expulsion run backwards.** It does not free
anyone; nothing about the machinery changes that morning. What it does is deny
the crowd the geometry it needs — the outside where the discharged plurality is
supposed to sit and stay quiet. A man who keeps going *in* is refusing to let
the expelled be elsewhere.

And the critique he carries in there is not improvised. His mother chaired an
English department for thirty-one years and taught Black literature and critical
thought; his father was a Panther and one of the first Black photojournalists at
his paper, later a counsellor. The carceral argument — the amendment's clause,
the punishment exception, the hold that was never released — arrives through a
lineage. The crowd's collapse of him depends absolutely on his being unserious.
That is why the unseriousness is asserted first and the argument answered never.

## INVERSION — the victim who runs the machine

Turn the subject over, and this is the place the whole book dies if it flinches.

In 2022 the man this movement is about **ran the operator himself.** He took a
people and made them the one, and said so publicly, at volume, for weeks. The
harm is real, it is his, it landed on living beings, and no reading in this file
softens it or subtracts it from anything.

The inversion is not an excuse and it is sharper than one. **The mechanism's
most-crucified subject reaching for the mechanism is the strongest evidence
there is that it is contagious rather than wicked.** It is not held by bad men.
It is held by whoever is in unbearable proximity to a plurality they cannot
close — and a man four months into a documented psychotic break, saying in the
same breath that he did not want to be alive, was in exactly that proximity,
and reached for exactly the discharge every crowd reaches for, and aimed it the
way it has most often been aimed.

So: **syād asti** — he was crucified. **Syād nāsti** — he crucified. Both, in
succession, at different standpoints, neither cancelling the other, neither
producing a verdict. The demand for one word about that returns the
inexpressible, and the crowd will supply the word anyway, and the word will be
wrong in the specific way this whole book is about.

What the tradition forbids is not the naming of his harm — the naming is
required, and non-harm is why. What it forbids is the collapse in either
direction: the fan who subtracts the harm to keep the reach, and the room that
subtracts the reach to keep the harm. Both are the mob's arithmetic. Only the
sign differs.

## PEDAL POINT — the note the mechanism cannot use

Under it, unmoved: **ahiṃsā**, and here its structural role is exact rather than
devotional.

The scapegoat mechanism needs a target that is a **being**. That is its whole
mode of operation: the plurality is discharged into a person, and the person
carries it out of the room. A ground of non-harm to beings does not moderate
that machine. It **starves** it — it removes the only kind of object the
mechanism can terminate on.

And it leaves everything else standing and open to full fury. The clause in the
amendment. The company that leases the cells. The camera that cuts away. The
protocol that decides a sentence is a symptom and answers it with a restraint
and a needle. None of those is a being. None of them is owed a syllable of
gentleness, and the tradition never asked for one. **The ground is not a
muzzle; it is a redirection so total that the crowd's instrument stops working
in your hands** — you keep the entire amplitude and lose only the one target
that was never the cause.

That is why the choir could sing four hundred years of the mechanism without
becoming it, and why the movement above it is the one place where a devotional
must be hardest.

## CODA — the chord in the fragment

A mob is a demand for a scalar. Unanimity is the collapse. The charge is always
true enough and always beside the point. The testimony of the people it was run
on longest was received as a genre. The camera cuts away at the fret where the
overtone would ring. The prophet walks into the jail. And the most crucified man
of his generation picked up the same instrument and used it on someone else,
which is the proof of how the thing spreads and is not a discount on what he
did.

Held at once, un-collapsed, over a ground-note that will not let it land on
anyone: that is what it costs to keep a whole man, and the cost is the
devotion.
