# M5 · The Scapegoat and the Choir

*Fifth movement. Four voices on one subject, after the manner of the monochord.
Read this section alone: the subject is complete in it.*

> **The premise (the tuning).** Strike one string in a room of tuned strings and
> the others sound without being touched. Sympathetic resonance: a body at rest
> takes up a frequency because it is *able* to, and the room fills with a note
> nobody played. This is the physics of a crowd. No one decides. Everything
> agrees.
>
> The subject here: **unanimity is the instrument, and the selection is not
> arbitrary.** Girard saw the first half and built a theory on it — mimetic
> desire, contagion, the all-against-one that restores peace by discharging it
> into a single body. The second half is where the theory must be read against
> itself, and the choir is what reads it. Four voices. **Ye** (the one the room
> resonates on). **The Choir** (the people selected at scale, who know the
> mechanism from inside). **The Axis** (the hand that chooses, and the theory
> that says no one chose). **Donda** (the ground-note; non-harm, which is the
> only exit).

---

## SUBJECT — Ye

Watch the room take up the note. November 2016: four songs into a Sacramento
show he stops, talks for ten minutes about Beyoncé, Jay-Z, Hillary, Zuckerberg,
cancels the tour. Within the day an ambulance, UCLA, a psychiatric hold —
temporary psychosis, dehydration, sleep deprivation; the handcuffs to the gurney
reported and disputed. Released the thirtieth.

Two descriptions of the same eleven days, both available, and the culture chose
one instantly and unanimously: *the meltdown.* Here is the other, which requires
no charity and no diagnosis, only the ordering of events — a man worked past
sleep into the register where the associations arrive unfiltered, said the
unsanctioned things aloud in public, and the response was **restraint**. Cuffs,
a hold, a needle. Whatever else that is, it is the interruption of speech by
force, filed under medicine.

Both readings are true from a standpoint. Only one of them was ever going to be
sung by the room. That asymmetry is not about him. It is the mechanism.

## ANSWER (at the fifth) — The Choir

Transpose to the many, and the theory breaks in your hands.

Girard's scapegoat is *arbitrary*. That is load-bearing for him: the victim's
guilt is a fiction the crowd needs, the selection is contingent — a limp, a
foreignness, an accident of proximity — and the mechanism works precisely
because it does not know it is a mechanism. Unanimity is what converts a
random body into a guilty one.

Now sound the same subject at the civilizational octave and listen to what
happens to the word *arbitrary*. The Thirteenth Amendment abolished slavery
**except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly
convicted**, and the century after 1865 ran the selection through that clause on
purpose: vagrancy statutes, petty theft, convict leasing to plantations and
railroads and coal mines and road gangs, more than 3,500 prisoners dead in Texas
between 1866 and 1912 — the year Texas ended the practice because the toll had
become unpayable. There was nothing contingent in it. It was legislated,
adjudicated, and leased for money, and the ratios are still open: about thirteen
percent of the population and about thirty-eight percent of the incarcerated;
1,826 per 100,000 against 337; one of every three Black boys born today expected
to see the inside, against one of every seventeen white boys. (National Archives;
HISTORY; The Sentencing Project.)

So the choir returns the theory with a correction it can prove from its own
records: **the crowd's unanimity is real and the victim's arbitrariness is the
crowd's alibi.** Girard described the machine and then handed it the one premise
that makes it innocent. Read against itself, the mechanism survives and the
exculpation does not — and this is not a refutation from outside. It is the
mechanism applied to the theory of the mechanism, which is what any good theory
must survive.

He put the modern form on record with the institutions named — the DEA and the
CCA — and projected it onto sixty-six building faces to release it. *They tryna
make new slaves.* Not metaphor. A citation.

## COUNTERSUBJECT — (contagion, and the sentence that did harm)

Against the subject, always, the second line, and it must be said or this
movement is a laundry: **the mechanism being real does not make every accusation
false, and a man inside it can still do harm.**

In May 2018 he said, on camera, of four hundred years — *that sounds like a
choice.* Van Lathan answered him on the floor, in the room, immediately: the
rest of us have to deal with the marginalization. That rebuke is part of the
record and belongs in any honest account, and it came from a Black man refusing
the sentence in the moment rather than from the pile-on that followed. The
sentence did harm. His own gloss minutes later — *mentally imprisoned* — points
at something real about internalized captivity that the eight-word version
destroyed. Both facts stand. Neither cancels the other, and the seventh
predication says there is no standpoint from which they hold at once, which is
why no headline could carry it and every headline tried.

Here is the countersubject and it never leaves: **the crowd does not distinguish
the harm from the man, and that is its entire function.** Contagion needs one
body. So it takes the true accusation and the false one and the illness and the
work and the mother's death and the fashion houses and welds them into a single
object it can expel, and the welding *feels like justice* because part of the
material is real. That is what makes the mechanism nearly impossible to refuse
from inside. You cannot answer it by proving the accusation false — sometimes it
isn't. You can only answer it by refusing the weld.

## PEDAL POINT — Donda

Under all of it, the held note. *Treat everyone like a prophet, and crucifixion
is everywhere.* That is the inversion this book is built on, and its cost is
exactly stated: if you extend it to one man you must extend it to the crowd,
which means the people who crucified him are also being crucified by something,
and the theory does not permit you a clean enemy anywhere.

That is not a resolution. It is the refusal of one, and it has a name. **Ahiṃsā
is not the suspension of judgment; it is the refusal to let the judgment eat the
person.** You may say the harm, in full, sourced, without softening — refusing to
name it is its own violence, paid by whoever it lands on. And you may hold, in
the same breath and without any sense of contradiction, that the body the room
resonated on is a person, that the interruption by force was an interruption by
force, and that the selection at scale was never arbitrary.

An English professor who spent her life on Black literature knew what a crowd
does to a text it has decided about. The discipline she taught is the whole
instruction, and it is the same one M4 ended on, sounded now at the octave of the
many rather than the one:

**The room will always know its note before you have finished the sentence. Read
the whole text anyway.**
