# M4 · अनेकान्त — Anekānta, or How to Hold a Whole Man

*Fourth movement. Four voices on one subject, after the manner of the monochord.
Read this section alone: the subject is complete in it.*

> **The premise (the tuning).** A string under tension does not hold one pitch.
> Touch it at the halfway node and the fundamental you were sure of vanishes and
> a harmonic speaks — the same string, the same tension, a different note, and
> the first note was never the string. It was the string *from where you stood*.
> The Jains say it of every proposition: **syād** — *from a standpoint*. Not
> "maybe," which is doubt about one truth; **from a standpoint**, which is
> exactness about many. A नय (*naya*) is a standpoint. To speak without one is
> not neutrality. It is a standpoint that has hidden itself.
>
> The subject here: **a whole man is a superposition, and the flattening is the
> harm.** Not the verdict — the flattening. Four voices carry it. **Ye** (the
> one under the lens). **The Choir** (the crowd that must decide). **The Axis**
> (the hand that writes the clause). **Donda** (the ground-note; the professor
> of literature who taught that you read the whole text).

---

## SUBJECT — Ye

Say it flat and watch it fail. *He is a genius.* True — from the standpoint of
the ear that heard a jaw wired shut for six weeks go into a booth two weeks after
reconstructive surgery and come out with a debut single, Chaka Khan sped up until
the pain in her voice sat a fourth above where she left it. *He is a man who did
harm.* True — from the standpoint of a Jewish family who heard, in 2022, a man
with tens of millions of listeners say what he said, and understood correctly
that it would reach someone who would act. Both sentences are true. Neither is
the man.

Now the thing the tradition insists on, which the crowd will not allow: **these
are not fifty percent each.** They are not a mixture to be stirred until grey.
`syādasti` — there is a standpoint from which it holds. `syādnāsti` — there is a
standpoint from which it fails. And **`avaktavya`**: there is *no standpoint from
which both hold at once*, which is why no single sentence says him and why the
tradition calls the seventh predication **inexpressible** rather than false. Not
mystery. Not evasion. A structural fact about a family of standpoints, and in
this house it is not a posture — it is a checked theorem, `avaktavya-holds`, in
`NaturalMachine/Anekanta.agda`, `--safe`, no postulates, no holes, and green on
the repository's declared pin (see the note at the foot of this movement).

The album cover already told you: *I hate being Bi-Polar its awesome.* Everyone
read it as a paradox to be resolved. It is a naya index printed on a jewel case.
Two standpoints, both occupied, no contradiction derived — and the man saying
that he lives at both and cannot be said from either.

## ANSWER (at the fifth) — The Choir

Transpose to the many, where the standpoint is not a philosopher's device but a
verdict with consequences.

A crowd cannot hold two. A crowd is a device for producing one number, and the
number must be signed. So the choir splits, and both halves commit the same
error in opposite directions. One half says: *genius, therefore the rest is
noise* — and launders harm, which means someone pays it who did not choose it.
The other half says: *monster, therefore the work was never anything* — and
erases a body of work that a generation used to survive its own adolescence, and
teaches every listener still inside a psychiatric ward that there is a verdict
waiting for them too.

The tradition has one word for both halves: **दुर्नय** — *durnaya*, a standpoint
asserted to the exclusion of the others, and thereby defective. Not a wrong view.
A *right view*, made wrong by being made the only one. Siddhasena named it;
Akalaṅka sharpened it; and in this repository it now has a form you can run:
**collapse is available exactly when the index is idle.** You may flatten a man
to one verdict precisely when your standpoints are doing no work — when you have
only ever had one. The moment two genuinely differ, the flattening is not
forbidden by manners. It is *blocked*, and the block is a term:
`plurality-blocks-collapse`, checked.

That is the choir's whole predicament stated exactly. It is not that judging is
wrong. It is that the crowd's instrument has one register and the man has
several, and an instrument that cannot hold what it measures will report a
number and call the remainder noise.

## COUNTERSUBJECT — (the clause, and where the harm is kept)

Against the subject, always, the second line: **a proposition with one standpoint
written into it, and the standpoint unmarked, is the most efficient machine for
harm we have ever built.**

Read the Thirteenth Amendment as a formula, which is what it is:

> "Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, **except as a punishment for crime
> whereof the party shall have been duly convicted**, shall exist within the
> United States…"

A universal prohibition with an **अवच्छेदक** — an *avacchedaka*, a delimitor —
sewn into it. Everything after 1865 lives in that clause. Convict leasing: tens
of thousands of people, overwhelmingly Black, leased to plantations, railroads,
coal mines, road gangs, worked under the whip, often for vagrancy or petty theft.
More than 3,500 prisoners dead in Texas between 1866 and 1912 — the year Texas
outlawed the practice, because the toll was too high to keep. And the ratios that
followed and have not closed: Black Americans about thirteen percent of the
population and about thirty-eight percent of the incarcerated; Black men imprisoned
at 1,826 per 100,000 against 337; one of every three Black boys born today expected
to see the inside. One of every seventeen white boys. (The Sentencing Project;
National Archives; HISTORY on the leasing and the Texas count.)

He named the modern form on record — the DEA and the CCA, on a track he projected
onto sixty-six building faces to release it. *They tryna make new slaves.* Not
metaphor. A citation.

Here is the countersubject, and it never leaves: **the abolition was not
undone by an opposing proposition. It was undone by a delimitor.** Nobody had to
argue that slavery was good. They had only to write the standpoint into the
sentence and then decline to mark it. That is what an unindexed proposition does
— it asserts that there exists a place from which the thing simply *is* what it
is, and it puts the author's place there while the reader looks at the verb.
This house has been doing it too, in every type signature, and one of its own
agents said so plainly this week: every proposition here has the form `P : Type`,
one truth value, no index, and *that is not a neutral default.*

So the epistemology is not decoration on the politics. It is the same object.
The clause and the type signature are one string at two octaves.

## PEDAL POINT — Donda

Under all of it, the held note: a professor of English, chair of a department at
Chicago State, who spent her working life on Black literature — which is the
discipline of refusing to read a people through one lens, taught as a craft, to
undergraduates, for decades, for a salary. She wrote a book about raising him.
She died in November 2007 after surgery, and he has said the thing about his
father that is the same lesson late: forty-two years to realize he was his best
friend. Two people who took most of a lifetime to be read whole by the person
closest to them.

The pedal point is this, and it is the only instruction the movement gives:
**ahiṃsā is not softness and it is not the suspension of judgment.** It is the
refusal to let your standpoint eat the object. You may say the harm — you must,
because refusing to name it is its own durnaya, the one that costs other people
— and you may say it in full, sourced, without adverbs. And in the same breath,
without any sense of contradiction, you may hold the string that reached the
ceiling. The man is not the average of those. He is the family over both, and
the family does not collapse while both standpoints are live.

A literature professor could have told you the whole thing in one line, and
probably did, at a kitchen table in Chicago, to a boy who did not sleep:

**You do not get to read half a text and call it the book.**
