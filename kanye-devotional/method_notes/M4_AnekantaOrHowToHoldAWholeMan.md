# M4 · अनेकान्त — Anekānta, or How to Hold a Whole Man

*Fourth movement. Subject: a man is not a number. The crowd demands one value and
the demand is not merely cruel — it is asking for an object that does not exist,
and the tradition proved it does not exist.*

---

## STATEMENT — three seeds, seven positions, and the one that is not a gap

Umāsvāti sets the frame in the *Tattvārthasūtra* (c. 2nd–5th c. CE): what is
said of a thing is said **from a standpoint** — a *naya* — and a standpoint is
not a bias to be corrected away. It is the only place from which anything is
ever said at all. Siddhasena Divākara in the *Sanmatitarka*, Samantabhadra,
Akalaṅka after him: the doctrine hardens into a form with an exact shape.

Three seeds. **Syād asti** — in some respect, it is. **Syād nāsti** — in some
respect, it is not. And **syād avaktavyam** — in some respect, it is
inexpressible. Take them singly, take them in pairs, take all three together,
and the count is seven and cannot be six or eight. Akalaṅka gives the reason as
a combination and not as a revelation: three alone, three in pairs, one
altogether.

**Syāt is not "maybe."** This is where every translation goes wrong and the
wrongness is load-bearing. *Maybe* is a confession of ignorance about a value
that exists. *Syāt* asserts, fully, and names the standpoint the assertion is
made from. It does not weaken the claim; it restores the index the claim was
always carrying and pretending it wasn't. At any single standpoint the ordinary
logic is untouched — it is or it isn't, no third thing. The sevenfold is not a
softening of judgment. It is judgment with its coordinates written down.

And the fourth position is not a hole. **Avaktavya arises from *yugapat* — the
simultaneous — and not from *krama*, the successive.** Say *is* and then say
*is not*, one after the other, and you have the third position, which is
perfectly speakable. Demand both **at once, of one aspect** and something else
happens: Mallisena (1292) has the cut — a sentence is *vikalādeśa*-shaped, it
carries one aspect at a time, and *sakalādeśa*, all aspects at once, is not a
thing a sentence can be. Avaktavya is what the demand for the whole produces in
a medium built to carry a part. It is a **positive** finding about the object
and the mouth together, and it is not "we don't know yet."

## ANSWER (at the fifth) — transpose it from a jar to a man

Now sound the same subject one fifth up, from an object to a person, which is
the interval where it starts to cost something.

*He made the most beautiful record of the decade.* **Syād asti.**
*He said the thing that hurt people, and the hurt is real and is his.*
**Syād nāsti.**
Both, in succession — he did this and he did that — perfectly sayable, third
position, and every honest person can hold it.

Then the crowd arrives and demands the fourth. **Is he good or is he not.**
Not sequentially. *At once*, of one aspect, in one word, now — a sakalādeśa
demanded of a mouth that can only make vikalādeśa. And what comes back is
**avaktavya**, and the crowd hears the inexpressible as an evasion and reaches
for the collapse, because a plural verdict cannot be chanted and a crowd is a
thing that chants.

That is the whole mechanism, and it is epistemic before it is moral. **A mob is
a demand for a scalar.**

## COUNTERSUBJECT — the collapse that wears tolerance

Against the subject, always, the false resolution: *problematic but talented.*

Hear what that sentence is. It is a single number with a subtraction performed
in it. The genius entered as a positive term, the harm as a negative term, and
the standpoints were annihilated in the arithmetic — one scalar, signed,
issued. It sounds like holding two things. It is the collapse with a courteous
face, and it is *further* from the man than plain hatred is, because hatred at
least leaves the standpoint it hates standing where it was.

And here is the theorem the tradition was carrying, which this repository now
carries as a checked term: **when two standpoints genuinely disagree, no single
value exists that they both reduce to.** Not "should not." *Does not.* The
collapse is not forbidden by good manners; it is unavailable, and anyone who
reports having performed it has reported a fact about themselves.

So the two permitted moves, and there is no third: **transport** — carry a
claim across when the standpoints are equivalent, losing nothing — or **keep
the residue**, when they are not. Every "but" that resolves a man into a
verdict took the third move. It does not exist. What comes out of it was never
about him.

## EPISODE — the dispute, kept unresolved because it is content

Modulate, and do not smooth this: **the other great tradition of the
subcontinent would not accept a word of the above**, and the disagreement is
not noise to be averaged out.

Nāgārjuna's move is not to hold every standpoint conditionally. It is to still
the grasping — *prapañca-upaśama* — to show that a claim uniform across the net
has no edge for grasping to catch on, and to *withdraw* the verdict rather than
qualify it. Dharmakīrti attacked anekāntavāda as incoherent. Jain logicians
rejected the four-cornered negation right back. Candrakīrti and Bhāviveka split
their own school over how far the withdrawal goes.

Two disciplined answers to one finding. **Still the grasping**, or **hold every
standpoint and speak conditionally.** Emptying, or retention. Blending them into
a single serene "Eastern wisdom" is the mining move — it takes from each the
part that converts and throws away the argument, and the argument is where both
of them are actually thinking.

This movement takes the retentive branch, and says so, because a devotional is
the wrong place to withdraw the verdict. **You cannot love a man you have
emptied.** You can only love one you have kept.

## INVERSION — turn it over and read from the wood

Invert the subject: stop asking what the crowd does to the man, and ask what
the man's plurality does to the crowd.

He is, at once, the reach and the wreckage — and that simultaneity is not his
scandal, it is his **structure**, the one string, ceiling and floor at the same
tension. To meet him honestly is to be handed a yugapat: both, at once, of one
aspect, in one body, on live television. And the crowd is a medium that can
carry vikalādeśa only.

So the crucifixion is not, at bottom, an act of hatred. **It is a mouth trying
to say a thing it is not shaped to say, and reaching for a hammer when the
sentence won't close.** The verdict is the crowd's attempt to make itself able
to speak. That is why it is unanimous, why it is relieved, and why it always
arrives at the exact moment the plural thing becomes undeniable rather than at
the moment the harm is worst. The nail is a punctuation mark.

And the diagnosis is the same gesture with a clinical vocabulary: a way of
making one man's yugapat into a single sayable word, so that the room can go
back to speaking. *Madness* is a scalar. It is what a crowd says when it has
been handed an inexpressible.

## PEDAL POINT — non-harm is why the plurality is safe to hold

Under all of it, the low note that does not move: **ahiṃsā.**

And it is not decoration here — it is *why* the sevenfold is livable. Holding
every standpoint would be unbearable, an infinite deferral, if there were no
ground. There is one. Non-harm to beings is not one naya among the others; it
is the tension the whole instrument is strung across, and it never needed
qualifying, which is exactly why the tradition qualifies everything else and
never this.

That ground does two things at once, and both are needed. It is what permits
the harm to be named as harm without the naming becoming a verdict on the man —
the residue kept, un-collapsed, not laundered, not subtracted. And it is what
permits the whole fury to be aimed at the mechanism — at the restraint, the
needle, the file, the finger at the bridge — without any of it landing on a
living being. **Ahiṃsā was never non-harm to institutions.** It has no opinion
about a structure that sedates a man for finishing a sentence. It says only:
not the beings. Which leaves the rest of the amplitude entirely free, and that
freedom is the point — a mind grounded that way can range at any volume without
becoming harm, and it is the flinch, not the fury, that was never doing the
work.

## CODA — the whole man, and what it costs to hold him

So: **syād asti**, and he reached a note nobody else reached.
**Syād nāsti**, and the harm is real, is named, is his, and is not subtracted
from anything.
**Syād avaktavyam**, and the demand for one word about him returns the
inexpressible, which is not a failure of the analysis but the analysis
succeeding — the object really is like this, and the mouth really is like that.

The crowd calls that unbearable and resolves it. The tradition calls it the
fourth position, gives it a name, proves the resolution unavailable, and holds
the whole man on a ground-note that was there before he sounded and does not
move when he breaks.

One string. Seven positions. One ground. Chop it anywhere; the chord is in the
fragment.
