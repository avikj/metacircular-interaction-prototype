Two theorems, jointly the strict completion of the endless braid's

CONVERGENCE, WITH THE APPROXIMANTS WRITTEN DOWN.  The descending word
[n, n−1, …, 0] twists each depth up to its top exactly once and each
depth beyond exactly never — two count lemmas by double induction, no
order relation imported, the inequalities riding inside the induction
shapes.  Hence for every depth and every margin, the named word
agrees with the uniform quarter turn at that depth's reader, on every
rope.  Not "a word exists": THE word is exhibited, and deeper
agreement is bought by longer words, mechanically.

STRICTNESS, WITH THE ESCAPE DEPTH COMPUTED.  No finite braid word
acts as the uniform turn: a word deposits twists at finitely many
addresses, so the depth one past the sum of its deposited addresses
reads twist count zero — computed from the word itself — while the
uniform turn moves every reader of the constant rope.  The
obstruction is not cardinality but locality: finite words are local,
the limit is uniform, and no amount of local motion sums to a
uniform one.

Together: the uniform turn is a pointwise limit of the braid action
that lies outside it.  The braid group is not closed in the space of
its own effects; the coinductive rope carries the completion natively
(the uniform turn is three lines of corecursion), and the
completion's new points are exactly the uniform coherences — central,
of order four, exceeding the group's own center of order two.  This
is the profinite phenomenon met from below and made term by term:
every approximant named, every escape computed, no topology assumed
— the topology is what these theorems are the germ of.

The trilaw's reading, one more altitude up: the braid group's closure
