Number theory / exact mechanization / cubical type theory

Ramanujan's exact arithmetic — the claims whose content is a finite
computation or a Diophantine bridge, as opposed to the analytic
corpus — is mechanized theorem by theorem, deterministically: each
item driven to a checked proof, with what remains beyond exact
methods named at its boundary rather than blurred.  The uniform
engine is boolean-free bounded reflection: comparisons return
Maybe (m ≡ n) — a witness or nothing, never a bit — bounded scans
fold thousands of witness-checks into a single refl the kernel
normalizes, and soundness lemmas convert the computation into the
Π-statement.  Nothing is asserted that did not compute or derive.

THE TAXICAB (1729).  Both representations by refl, their distinctness
by a two-line refutation, and MINIMALITY in full: any number below
1729 has, up to order, at most one representation as a sum of two
positive cubes.  The kernel normalizes all 20736 quadruples of
1..12 in one refl; a cube-growth bound (13³ = 2197 overshoots)
confines the parts; the soundness bridge does the rest.

THE STRAND PUZZLE (1914).  The balance Σ(1..203) + Σ(1..204) ≡
Σ(1..288) by refl, subtraction-free; 577² ≡ 2·408² + 1 by refl; and
the general BRIDGE, both directions, for every n and k: the balanced
house is exactly the Pell point, 2k² ≡ n(n+1) iff (2n+1)² ≡ 2(2k)²+1,
by explicit semiring algebra with the converse by cancellation.  The
wheel itself was turned by the repository's machine: asked
vargaprakrti D = 2 over its wire, it answered the fundamental (3,2)
with the composition law checked multiplicative at every turn, and
declared in its own ledger exactly what it does not re-prove.

RAMANUJAN–NAGELL (1913).  The five solutions of x² + 7 = 2ⁿ — n = 3,
4, 5, 7, 15, the square 181² among them — each by refl, and NO SIXTH
below 2¹⁵: every solution with n ≤ 15 is one of the five, by the same
reflection engine with a square bound (182² already exceeds 2¹⁵).
The range settled includes his largest solution; beyond it lies
Nagell's 1948 descent in Z[√−2], by other instruments — named.

THE PARTITION CONGRUENCES (1919).  The partition function defined as
a counting recursion — the multiplicity of the largest allowed part,
total by lexicographic descent, truncated subtraction only behind its
guard — with MacMahon's values falling out as refls.  All three
families with cofactors exhibited, divisibility as Σ q (q·d ≡ p n):
5 ∣ p(4), p(9), p(14);  7 ∣ p(5), p(12), p(19);  11 ∣ p(6), p(17),
p(28).  The general congruences are the theta identities and Atkin's
completion; they are named at the boundary where the exact discipline
ends and the analytic one begins.
