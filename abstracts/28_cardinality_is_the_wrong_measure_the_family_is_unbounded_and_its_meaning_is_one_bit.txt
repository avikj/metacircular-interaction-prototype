CARDINALITY IS THE WRONG MEASURE: THE DERIVATIONS BETWEEN TWO CONNECTED
TERMS EXCEED EVERY BOUND, EVERY ONE HAS A DEFINITE LENGTH, AND ALL OF THEM
MEAN ONE THING
The size of the space of histories is unbounded; the count on any single
history is definite; the meaning of the whole family is a single bit --
three different orders on one object, and confusing them is the error

Foundations / what to measure when a space of proofs is large

The question "how many ways connect two points" has, in this calculus,
three answers that are not the same number, and the development separates
them so that none stands in for another.

The family is unbounded.  From any single derivation between two terms, and
with no hypothesis on the terms, `inflate` builds for every k another
derivation with the SAME endpoints, by going up along `reverse (add-zero
a)` and back down -- two steps that change nothing.  `inflate-len` computes
its length exactly as (k + k) + len d.  The padding is uniform: it needs no
fact about the endpoints beyond one derivation existing at all.

The index is real, not a repetition.  `double-inj` and `inflate-inj` prove
k is recoverable from the padded derivation, so ℕ injects into the
derivation space whenever it is inhabited.  `ananta` states this in the
form the counting apparatus demands: no bound is assignable to the
standpoints between two connected terms.  This is the order the Jaina
scheme calls ananta -- unbounded, exceeding every definite magnitude --
and it is proved, not asserted.

Every single history is definite.  Each derivation nonetheless has a
`len`, a definite natural number; and the kernel's parallel offering is a
`List`, whose count is its `length`, conserved exactly by
`advance-preserves-branch-count`.  So "how long is this one" and "how many
are on offer right now" are both definite, while "how many exist in total"
is not.  Three questions, three orders of answer.

The whole family means one bit.  `the-whole-family-means-one-thing` proves
that the soundness of every padded derivation equals the soundness of the
original, at every environment -- because soundness lands in a proposition
(ℕ is a set), and a map into a proposition carries no bits.  So the
unbounded, injectively-indexed family of histories collapses, downstairs,
to a single meaning.  The order of the OBJECT (unbounded) and the order of
its MEANING (one bit) are not the same order.

The consequence for measurement.  Cardinality is the wrong invariant here,
and the module says exactly why: the space of histories is unbounded, so
its size distinguishes nothing; the definite invariants are the length of
a given history and the count of a presented offering; and the meaning is a
single bit that the entire unbounded family shares.  To ask "is set
cardinality the right measure" is to ask a question this module answers no
to, by exhibiting the same object at three different orders and keeping
them apart.

WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED.  `inflate` exhibits an injection ℕ ↪ Derivation a b,
not a bijection; nothing is claimed about the h-level of that type or that
the padding enumerates it.  Nothing is claimed about `Step a b` for a fixed
pair, which is a different question -- the unboundedness here is in the
length of a walk.  The one-bit collapse is a fact about the ℕ-valued
soundness semantics; it is not a claim that no finer semantics
distinguishes the histories -- a univalent semantics does, and is treated
elsewhere.
