A GENERATING FUNCTION DOES NOT DETERMINE A BIJECTION, AND THE DIFFERENCE
IS A GROUP ACTION
Counting is decategorification; the discarded datum is computed, not
gestured at

Enumerative and bijective combinatorics

The demand for bijective proofs of identities already established
algebraically is usually motivated by taste or by the hope of insight.  We
give it a precise justification.  The map sending a bijection to the
numerical identity it witnesses lands in a discrete type, hence in a
subsingleton, and therefore factors through the propositional truncation of
the bijection type: the preimage of an identity is the entire set of
bijections witnessing it.  No function of the identity, and no manipulation
of the generating function, selects a bijection.  The passage from
bijection to count is decategorification, and the datum it discards is a
group action on the fibers — which we exhibit as a computed object rather
than as a heuristic remark.  A generating-function proof and a bijective
proof are therefore not two routes to the same statement; the second
strictly dominates, and the excess is measurable.

We work in a setting where the categorified statements are the primary
ones.  Configurations are elements of a closed inductive type with six
independent registers, a unary and a binary constructor, and no binding
structure.  Rather than assert a recurrence and verify it numerically, we
construct the splitting: the family of configurations of a given weight is
exhibited in bijection with the disjoint union of two families of smaller
weight by an explicit head decomposition, and the additive recurrence for
the counts is then a corollary of that bijection rather than its
justification.  A second family, indexed by weight together with a
secondary statistic, splits at the head into the two adjacent cells of a
triangular array, giving the array itself as a family of bijections; the
binomial identity is read off, not proved separately.  A duration-preserving
reversal acts on each fixed-weight family as an involution, so that family
carries an order-two action whose orbit structure is invisible to the
count, and we compute the fixed points.  The disjoint-union principle is
proved once in the general form — the fiber of a two-valued map is the
two-valued family of its fibers, hence the counts add — and every
additivity in the development is an instance rather than a repetition.

Two structural facts explain why the excess cannot be recovered later.
First, the identifications between families are *constructed*, not asserted
up to cardinality, and transporting a statement across one is a computation
that runs: an addition defined on one presentation, moved across an
identification with another, evaluates to the standard positional
algorithm on the second, digit by digit.  An identification here is a
program, not a label.  Second, identifications compose strictly and invert
only weakly — composition of the constructed equivalences is associative
and unital on the nose as data, while double inversion of a constructed
equivalence is a syntactically distinct object — and that asymmetry is
exactly the content a cardinality statement discards.

The consequence for practice is a prohibition and a licence.  The
prohibition: identifying two configurations because they have equal weight
is a quotient that destroys the action, and any enumeration that
canonicalises, deduplicates, or normalises its search frontier collapses a
fiber whose size, in the degenerate case where a pattern contains no free
register, is an entire copy of the configuration algebra rather than a
finite ambiguity.  The licence: a family of bijections established once
transports every statement of every family identified with it, in both
directions, at no cost, so a bijective proof is reusable in a way a
generating-function manipulation is not — and the reuse is mechanical
rather than analogical.

All constructions are machine-checked with no postulates and no admitted
goals; the counts are corollaries of the constructions and are never
independently asserted.
