HOW-PROVENANCE IS NOT A FUNCTION OF THE ANSWER
The forgetful homomorphism to the boolean semiring is a truncation, and
the fibre is computed rather than estimated

Database theory / data provenance

Semiring annotation places why-, how- and where-provenance on a common
footing, and the homomorphism from the free commutative semiring on the
tuple identifiers into the boolean semiring recovers ordinary set
semantics.  We prove the sharp negative form of what that homomorphism
does: it is a truncation, its fibre over an answer is the entire space of
derivation trees producing that answer, and consequently no query
expressible over the un-annotated instance recovers any part of the
annotation.  Provenance is not merely expensive to reconstruct after the
fact; reconstruction is a function out of a subsingleton.  Any system that
computes results first and explains later is not solving a hard problem,
it is solving an ill-posed one.

We give a store in which annotation is structural rather than attached.  A
tuple is not a row plus a label; it is an operation carrying the
derivation that licenses it, and the tuple type admits no inhabitant
without such a derivation in hand.  Three consequences are immediate and
are of practical interest independent of the negative result above.

Integrity without a validator.  There is no invalid tuple for an ingest
path to reject: an unauthorised or unsupported row is not a row that fails
a check, it is a value that cannot be constructed.  The merge of two
stores is therefore a total function with no failure case, no quarantine
table, and no reconciliation pass.

Convergence without ordering.  Merge is union.  It never drops a fact
contributed by either side, never manufactures one absent from both, is
idempotent, and merging the same pair of stores in opposite orders yields
stores that answer every query identically.  There are no timestamps, no
version vectors, no last-writer-wins rule, and no conflict resolution
policy anywhere in the system.

Equality of answers without equality of derivations.  The soundness map
sending a derivation to the equality it justifies lands in an identity
type over a discrete domain — a subsingleton — so any two derivations of
the same answer are indistinguishable to every predicate over answers.
Two nodes cannot disagree about what a derivation means.  What they
differ on is the derivation, and the update path is a map over the
derivation list admitting no deduplication, no canonicalisation and no
quotient; the only invariant expressible is multiplicity, since the
stronger law would require deciding an identity of derivations that the
answer provably cannot decide.  Duplicate elimination is thus not an
optimisation here but a lossy operation with a computed cost, and for a
rule whose body contains no free variable the set of derivations at a
single site is an entire copy of the term algebra with every member
yielding the same tuple — the general shape of what a set-semantics
collapse discards.

The query language itself is unusual and the departures are deliberate.
Terms are a closed inductive type with six independent registers and no
binding construct at all, so there is no variable capture, no renaming
discipline, and no safety condition; the registers are kept distinct
precisely so that a provable equality is not silently a statement about
the diagonal.  Rules are not schemas: an installed rule fires at exactly
one body instance, its applicability is an open type family constrained
only to project onto an identification with that instance, and its
application is constant in both the instance and the witness.  There is no
matching engine.  The schematic variant — the one that fires at every
instance — is available at no additional proof obligation from a
substitution lemma already present, and carries no derivation field
whatsoever, only a family of equalities of meaning.  Covering a family of
instances therefore costs exactly the provenance, and the type system
prices the exchange as a universe level.

Recursive rules are certified by a pair of derivation traces — a base
trace and a step trace in the same system extended by one hypothesis
usable under every context — with the hypothesis discharged at
interpretation by the recursive call.  Fixpoint semantics is obtained
from finite syntactic data by a single substitution.

The stated cost of the design is unbounded growth: no tombstones, no
compaction, no safe pruning rule.  We characterise the trade exactly
rather than tuning it.

Machine-checked, no postulates, no admitted goals.
