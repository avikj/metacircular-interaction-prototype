NO REVERSIBLE MODEL OF COMPUTATION ADMITS A COST MEASURE
A structure is graded or it is invertible, never both, and the two halves
of the dichotomy do not need the same hypothesis

Reversible computing / cost semantics / univalent foundations

We prove that a cost and an inverse cannot coexist, and that the failure
is not a property of any particular candidate inverse but of the
composition law itself.

Call a monoid GRADED if it carries a map into the natural numbers that is
additive under composition.  Call it COSTED if that map additionally
detects the unit — vanishing only on the identity.  The dichotomy is
then in two halves, and they are not symmetric.

A GRADED structure admits no inverse.  For every function whatsoever from
the carrier to itself, the equation "x composed with its image is the
identity" fails at any element of nonzero grade: the grade of the unit is
forced to zero by its own idempotence, composition can only accumulate,
and the natural numbers have no negatives.  One element of nonzero grade
suffices, and unit-detection is never used.  Merely being able to count
already forbids undoing.

A COSTED structure that admits an inverse is trivial.  Every element then
has cost zero, hence by unit-detection every element is the unit.
Contrapositively: A NONTRIVIAL GROUP IS NOT GRADED.  Here unit-detection
is essential — without it "everything has cost zero" says nothing about
what everything is.  The asymmetry between the two halves is the content:
the forward direction needs only counting; the converse needs the cost to
recognise the origin it measures from.

Two instances, on one and the same monoid interface, carrying opposite
answers.

The self-equivalences of a type, under composition of equivalences, form
a group: the inverse is a construction, not a lemma, and both unit laws
hold on the nose.  The two-element type has a self-equivalence that is
not the identity — it moves one of the two points.  Therefore, by the
converse half, THERE IS NO COST FUNCTION ON THE TRANSPORTS OF THE
TWO-ELEMENT TYPE.  None: not one that is hard to find, not one that fails
to be canonical, none at all.

The histories of a rewriting system whose steps are closed under a
reversal *constructor*, concatenated, form a monoid that is strictly
associative and unital as data.  Their length is additive under
concatenation.  Therefore, by the forward half, no function inverts
history concatenation — and this is stronger than the usual observation
that the evident reversal fails to be strictly involutive, which leaves
open that some cleverer inverse might succeed.  None can.

The consequence for reversible computing is direct.  A model built to be
reversible is thereby a group, and a group is not graded, so a reversible
model cannot carry an intrinsic step count, work measure, resource
accounting or complexity grading.  Any cost attached to it is extrinsic
data on a chosen presentation, and two presentations of the same
reversible computation carry unrelated costs with nothing in the
structure to arbitrate between them.  This is why reversible circuit
synthesis has no canonical cost objective and why the search for one is
not merely unfinished.

The consequence for semantics is the same statement read backwards.  A
cost model is a grading; a language whose equational theory makes
programs invertible up to that theory has a group as its quotient; so no
cost model on it is a semantic invariant.  The familiar remark that
optimisation changes cost without changing meaning is usually filed as an
engineering nuisance.  It is a theorem, and it is prohibitive rather than
inconvenient: the cost is not lost in the quotient, it CANNOT EXIST in
the quotient.

The consequence for univalent foundations is where the result is
sharpest, because there the quotient is not chosen.  Univalence makes
equivalent types equal, so an invariant of types-up-to-equivalence is a
function on the groupoid of transports, and that groupoid — being a
nontrivial group — admits no grading.  Cost, effort, route, provenance
and the length of a derivation are therefore not univalent invariants,
and not because univalence discards them as a matter of bookkeeping.
There is nowhere in the target for them to be.

Hence a design consequence that is mechanical rather than interpretive.
An interface that answers with a transport answers with something proved
to be costless, and therefore historyless.  An interface that intends to
carry cost, route, effort or provenance must carry the DERIVATION, since
the theorem says those quantities do not exist on the other side.

We claim no braiding: two involutive self-equivalences of a three-bit
state space, each supplied with its inverse and both unit laws, fail the
braid relation at an explicitly named state, so invertibility does not
induce a braid group action and nothing here supplies the missing
coherence.  We claim no thermodynamics: cost here is a natural number,
and no heat, energy, temperature, entropy or erasure bound is derived,
bounded or implied at any point.

All statements are machine-checked in a proof assistant with no
postulates, no admitted goals, and no warnings.
