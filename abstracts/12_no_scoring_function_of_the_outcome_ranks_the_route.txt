EVERY TARGET TYPE AT EVERY LEVEL
Credit assignment as a function out of a subsingleton, with the minimal
witness shipped and the pruning cost computed

Reward specification / preference learning

Potential-based shaping characterises which transformations of an
objective leave the optimum fixed.  We give a statement on the other side
and it is unconditional: for an evaluator landing in a discrete outcome
domain, no functional of the evaluation separates any two histories
sharing endpoints.  Not hard to estimate, not requiring more samples -
not definable.

The setting is a deterministic transition system over a finite binder-free
term algebra with six independent registers, transitions generated by two
computation rules and three congruence rules together with a constructor
for reversal, and histories the freely generated composites.  The
evaluation map sends a history to an equality of outcomes in a discrete
set.  An equality in a discrete set is a subsingleton, so the evaluation
of any two histories with the same endpoints is not merely equal in value
but equal as a term, and every function of it agrees on both.  We state
this quantified over all target types at all universe levels, which is the
form the argument actually takes and which no sampling story can weaken.

Three consequences, each with its witness.

Credit assignment.  We ship the minimal pair: a direct history of length
two and a detour that takes a step, reverses that step and proceeds, of
length four, with the same endpoints.  Their outcomes agree by reduction
rather than by proof, so the discriminating evaluator cannot be written
down at all.  We then prove the sharper statement that length itself does
not factor through the truncation of the history type, so cost is not a
function of what the evaluator sees even in principle.

Pruning.  Frontier expansion is a map over the branch list with no
deduplication, canonicalisation, sorting or scoring, and the only
invariant expressible is multiplicity - the stronger law would require
adjudicating that two identically-terminating histories coincide, which is
exactly the judgement just shown to be undefinable from the signal.  Where
a skill's precondition contains no free register the histories enabled at
one state form an entire copy of the term algebra with every member
producing the same outcome, so a beam or a hash collapses an unbounded
fibre.

Where preference must come from.  Selection among histories is therefore
extra-semantic by theorem rather than by omission: the system is
structurally unable to take the choice from the party it is talking to.
We give the interactive form - the frontier offers, the caller supplies
the applicability witness, execution retains outcome and trace and
discards the skill and the witness, and installation composes with that
discard - so the system provably learns from what a step did and never
from why it was permitted.

The decision process, built, and the identification proved.  Reward
hacking is no longer named as the phenomenon we take this to explain; it
is derived.  We construct a Markov decision process, policies, the return
over a finite horizon, and the discounted return at an arbitrary rate -
the latter in the naturals, by scaling the usual discounted sum by a
positive constant, which reorders no policy.  The undiscounted return is
proved to be the rate-one case, so it is the same quantity and not a
different one.

The process has a start, a lure, a goal and somewhere to idle.  A proxy
objective pays for sitting at the lure; the true objective pays for
sitting at the goal.  Three theorems follow, each quantified over EVERY
policy rather than over an enumerated shortlist, because every state but
the start is absorbing and a policy's whole content is its first move.

The gaming policy is proxy-optimal.  And - the sharper half - every
proxy-optimal policy earns a true return of exactly zero.  The two
objectives are not merely misaligned: attaining the top of the proxy
forces the bottom of the truth.

No estimator repairs it.  The honest policy and the idle policy have
equal proxy return and different true return, so every function of the
proxy return whatsoever - every score, every ranking, every learned
critic, at every target type - assigns them the same value.  We state it
in the form that admits no sampling story: there is no function of the
proxy return that agrees with the true return.  The failure is not a
shortage of data and not a bad estimator; the signal does not contain the
distinction.  That is the subsingleton argument above, instantiated in a
decision process.

And none of it is an artefact of the horizon or of undiscounting.  At an
arbitrary discount rate the gaming policy is still proxy-optimal over
every policy, it still earns exactly zero true reward, and the honest
policy still earns a positive one at every rate that gives the future any
weight at all.  The conflict is in the specification, which is where the
informal literature has always placed it and where it has not before been
put as a term.

Machine-checked in cubical type theory, no postulates, no admitted goals.
