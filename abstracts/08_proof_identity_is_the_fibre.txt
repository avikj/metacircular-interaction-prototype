IDENTITY OF PROOFS IS NOT A FUNCTION OF THE PROPOSITION
Generality is propositional truncation, and normalisation criteria have no
referent in a groupoid of derivations

Proof theory / categorical proof theory

The question of when two derivations of the same sequent are the same
proof has two standard answers.  The normalisation criterion identifies
derivations with a common normal form.  The generality criterion
identifies derivations that behave uniformly in their atoms — the same
natural transformation, the same free construction.  We prove that in a
calculus where reversal is a rule *constructor* rather than an admissible
transformation, the first criterion has no referent, and that the second
is exactly propositional truncation of the derivation type.  Identity of
proofs is then not a criterion to be chosen but a fibre, and we compute it.

The calculus.  Formulas are terms of a closed inductive type with six
independent atoms and no binder — no quantifier, no eigenvariable
condition, no capture discipline, and the atoms are held distinct
precisely so that a derivable equality is not silently an instance of
reflexivity on the diagonal.  Rules are two computational rules, three
congruence rules closing under context, and a constructor for symmetry.
Derivations are freely generated composites.  Because symmetry is
generative rather than derived, derivations are a groupoid and not a
rewriting order: there is no normal form, no cut-elimination question, no
strong normalisation theorem to prove, and no critical pair.  The
normalisation criterion for proof identity is therefore not refuted here,
it is inapplicable.

Composition of derivations is associative and unital *on the nose*, as
data, so formulas and derivations form a strict category.  Inversion must
be assembled from composition and double inversion is a syntactically
distinct derivation, so the groupoid is weak.  Strictly a category, weakly
a groupoid — and the entire content that survives the semantics lives in
that gap.  Specifically: in the interpreting theory, inversion of an
equality is involutive definitionally, while the syntactic symmetry
constructor is not, so the whole semantically inaccessible part of a
derivation is one order-two redundancy the interpretation quotients and the
syntax does not.

Soundness sends a derivation to an equality in a discrete domain, hence
into a subsingleton, and therefore factors through the propositional
truncation of the derivation type.  The preimage of a proved proposition
is not a subclass of the derivations, it is the whole derivation type.  We
draw two consequences.  First, no function of the proposition — no
extraction, no shortest-proof heuristic, no proof-mining functional — is
definable that selects a derivation, and this is a typing fact rather than
a complexity claim.  Second, we exhibit two derivations of one equation
whose endpoints agree by reduction rather than by proof: a direct
derivation, and one that applies a rule, applies its reverse, and
proceeds.  No context distinguishes them.

Generality is where the result bites.  The calculus admits two units of
acquired inference and they are exact duals.  The first inhabits the higher
universe, carries its full derivation as a field, has an *open*
applicability family constrained only to project onto an identification
with its single conclusion, and applies constantly in both the conclusion
and the witness — so it licenses exactly one instance and no more.  The
second inhabits the lower universe, its applicability is a substitution
witness rather than a declaration, it applies at every instance of its
schema, and it carries no derivation whatsoever — only a family of
equalities indexed by valuation.  That family is precisely the image of
the soundness map.  Hence: **a rule is general exactly to the extent that
its derivation has been truncated away.**  Instantiation is sound with no
new proof obligation, drawn from a substitution lemma already present, so
generality costs nothing to obtain and the derivation is its entire price;
the universe level is the ledger of the exchange.

Induction is a certificate consisting of a base derivation and a step
derivation in the same calculus extended by a single conclusion-indexed
hypothesis usable under every congruence context.  Interpretation
instantiates the hypothesis constructor with the recursive call: a free
proof variable discharged at the semantic boundary, converting two finite
derivations into a statement over an infinite domain by one substitution.
Accepted derivations are promoted to rules with no serialisation and no
independent certificate checker — rule, applicability, conclusion and
derivation remain a single typed object, so the trusted boundary is a
constructor.
