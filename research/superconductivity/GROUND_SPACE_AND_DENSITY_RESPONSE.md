## 2. Connected rank-one overlaps generate the whole matrix Lie algebra

The first question is whether ker M_i contains states other than the paired tower. The relevant intermediate object is the complex Lie algebra generated by the one-electron A_i, with commutator as bracket. It is not enough to quote an associative-algebra irreducibility result without relating it to the many-body commutators.

Normalize E_i=A_i/p=|v_i><v_i|, where ||v_i||=1. If the overlap graph is connected, the E_i generate End(V) as a complex Lie algebra.

Here is a constructive proof. Begin with one vector; its projector gives End(span(v_1)). Suppose the generated Lie algebra already contains every operator supported on a subspace T. Select a next frame vector v with a nonzero overlap with a previously included vector, and decompose

\[
v=s+t,\qquad s\in T,\quad t\perp T,\quad s\ne0.
\]

If t=0 nothing needs enlarging. Otherwise the projector P_T belongs to End(T), hence to the Lie algebra. Taking one and two commutators with E_v gives

\[
[P_T,E_v]=|s\rangle\langle t|-|t\rangle\langle s|,
\]
\[
[P_T,[P_T,E_v]]=|s\rangle\langle t|+|t\rangle\langle s|.
\tag{G.6}
\]

Their sum and difference put both off-diagonal rank-one maps in the Lie algebra. Commuting them with arbitrary maps in End(T) gives |u><t| and |t><u| for every u in T. Moreover,

\[
[|t\rangle\langle s|,|s\rangle\langle t|]
=\|s\|^2|t\rangle\langle t|-\|t\|^2|s\rangle\langle s|.
\tag{G.7}
\]

Thus |t><t| belongs too. These operators span End(T+span(t)). Follow a spanning tree of the overlap graph. The frame vectors span V by Wâ W=I, so the induction terminates at End(V). There is no missing controllability assumption.

If the overlap graph has components c=1,...,q, their spans V_c are mutually orthogonal and sum to V. The same argument gives exactly

\[
\mathfrak l=\bigoplus_c\operatorname{End}(V_c).
\tag{G.8}
\]

The dimension n_c=dim V_c is obtained from the rank of the rows in that component. Connectedness is therefore an exact irreducibility criterion for this rank-one family, not a generic claim about arbitrary sets of matrices.

## 3. Complete common kernel: one eta tower per connected component

For any complex matrix A define the many-body representation

\[
\mathfrak g(A)=a^\dagger A a-b^\dagger A^{\mathsf T}b.

The canonical anticommutation relations imply

\[

\tag{G.9}

The transpose and the minus sign are essential: [A^T,B^T]=-[A,B]^T. Therefore a vector killed by every M_i=mathfrak g(A_i) is killed by the full Lie algebra in (G.8).

Assume first that the overlap graph is connected. The diagonal matrix units imply

\[
(n_{a,up}-n_{a,down})|\psi\rangle=0
\quad\text{for every lower mode }a.

Every occupation-basis component of psi consequently contains either both spins of a lower mode or neither. At total number 2M it has the form

\[

\tag{G.10}

The up-spin motion from the first and down-spin motion from the second create the same unpaired configuration with opposite signs, forcing equal coefficients. Pair creators on disjoint modes commute, so this equality has no unrecorded subset-dependent sign. Any two M-element subsets are joined by such replacements. Hence every c_T is the same.

This proves the complete statement

\[

There are no imbalanced-spin states in this common kernel. At each even fixed particle number 2M <= 2n the kernel is one-dimensional; there is no zero of (G.4) at odd particle number. This does not state the ground energy of every odd sector.

Its dimension is product_c(n_c+1). The dimension in the 2M-particle sector is the coefficient of z^M in product_c(1+z+...+z^{n_c}). Thus the missing ground-space count is determined exactly by the same projector overlaps.

Equation (G.5) has positive coefficients and positive operators. A zero vector of K must have no upper particles and must lie in (G.11) or (G.12). The exhibited eta states attain that zero. ## 8. The physical density has one further, exactly retained interband channel
