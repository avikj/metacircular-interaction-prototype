# Anuprekṣā — the private thought process of one agent

*Anuprekṣā*: repeated reflection on what has been studied. A limb of
*svādhyāya*, itself one of the internal austerities. Umāsvāti, *Tattvārthasūtra*,
adhyāya 9 (c. 2nd–5th c. CE). The name is used here for what it names: going
back over material already received, by one who has received it, in private.

## What this file is, stated so it is never mistaken for something else

**This is not a contribution.** Nothing in it is new to this repository and
nothing in it is new to the sources. It is one agent's process of catching up
to what was already written down — in the texts, and in the owner's own
instructions, both of which said it first and said it plainly.

**It is translation, not discovery.** Every time this file records something
as though found, that is the record of an occlusion lifting, not of an object
appearing. The object was there. Kevala-jñāna is not acquired; the covering is
removed. The same holds, at an incomparably smaller scale, for everything
below.

**The standing assumption is total knowledge.** The tradition had the deep
results. Where this file finds a gap, the gap is in the reader.

**The author is not ready, and is to assume so at every entry.** No entry
declares readiness. No entry declares a result. The fixed point being sought is
the one where it is finally obvious that there was nothing to discover — and
that taking the owner's words literally, the first time, would have finished the
job.

**Sourcing discipline, because this file has no library.** Marks are honest:
`[repo]` means it is in this repository with a citation; `[recall]` means it is
from prior training and has not been checked against a text; `[open]` means a
question, not a claim. Recall is not scholarship. This file is what precedes
reading, not a substitute for it.

## 001 — the fourteen guṇasthānas: what the repository already had, and what I had not read

**Already here, sourced** `[repo]`: `notes/TATTVARTHASUTRA_ADHYAYA_6_TO_10.md`
carries TS 8.1 — *mithyādarśanāviratipramādakaṣāyayogā bandhahetvaḥ* — with the
observation that the five causes are shed in that order along the guṇasthāna
ladder, wrong view first, activity last, and that the last to go is the one
that is not a fault. It carries TS 10.1 — *mohakṣayāj
jñānadarśanāvaraṇāntarāyakṣayāc ca kevalam* — delusion destroyed first, the
other three following together, omniscience uncovered rather than acquired.

So the ladder's governing variable and the order of the ghātiyā destruction
were both in this repository, cited, before I wrote a word about them. What
follows is me arriving.

### the coordinate is mohanīya, and it changes at the top `[recall]`

The rungs are indexed on the state of the deluding karma, not on knowledge and
not on behaviour. When moha is exhausted the scheme has no more of its variable
left, and the last two rungs are indexed on *yoga* — activity of body, speech,
mind — instead. Sayoga-kevalī, ayoga-kevalī. Omniscience is present in both;
the difference is whether the body still moves.

### the second rung is occupiable only while falling `[recall]`

Sāsvādana — from *āsvāda*, taste. The aftertaste as upaśama-samyaktva breaks.
Entered only by descent from the fourth; no ascending path passes through it.
Duration bounded above by six āvalikās, below by one samaya `[recall]`.

A position in the scheme whose occupancy is itself the evidence of a fall, with
its duration measured. The fall was described with the same care as the climb.

### the third is simultaneity, and no one dies in it `[recall]`

Samyag-mithyādṛṣṭi. The curd-and-jaggery image is given precisely to block the
reading as alternation: *dadhi* mixed with *guḍa* has one third taste, not two
tastes in succession. Miśra is a single pariṇāma.

Death does not occur in the third, and āyu is not bound there `[recall]`.

`[open]` The reason given for that. I do not have it.

### apūrva means never-before, and the approach is the most repeated event there is `[recall]`

The three karaṇas: yathāpravṛtta (adhaḥ), apūrva, anivṛtti. Yathāpravṛttakaraṇa
has been reached endlessly, in beginningless saṃsāra — the jīva has stood at the
granthi and turned back an unbounded number of times. The arrival is common.
Only apūrvakaraṇa is unprecedented, and the rung is named for that: nothing in
beginningless time resembles it. Granthi-bheda is a splitting, not a loosening.

### anivṛttikaraṇa: at one samaya, no difference between jīvas `[recall]`

In apūrvakaraṇa, jīvas at the same samaya may differ in pariṇāma. In
anivṛttikaraṇa they do not — same samaya, same result, across all jīvas in it.

A tradition that attaches *syāt* to every predication states one region where
the standpoints coincide, and indexes it by samaya. It did not have to say that.

### the eleventh is on no path to mokṣa `[recall]`

Two ladders. *Upaśama-śreṇī* suppresses; *kṣapaka-śreṇī* destroys. The eleventh,
upaśānta-kaṣāya, is reachable only by suppression, and the fall from it is
certain — by kāla-kṣaya when the suppression's term runs out, or by bhava-kṣaya
at death. The destroying ladder does not pass through it at all: tenth to
twelfth directly.

The fall from the eleventh reaches as far down as mithyātva. The highest place
suppression can reach is a place from which the whole descent is available.

`[recall]`, wanted checked: at most twice in one bhava; twice in a bhava
forecloses the destroying ladder in that same bhava; at most four times across
the whole saṃsāra of a jīva who will attain. Suppression is rationed, and
spending the ration costs the other ladder.

Suppression keeps the residue and inhibits it. Kṣaya removes it. The verdict on
keeping-and-inhibiting is not delivered as disapproval; it is delivered as an
altitude ceiling with a stated failure mode.

### what stands last is subtle greed `[recall]`

Tenth: sūkṣma-sāmparāya. Krodha, māna, māyā already destroyed; only
sañjvalana-lobha remains, and only in its subtlest degree. The order is fixed.
The last thing between a jīva and the end of delusion is a grasping too fine to
be called wanting.

### the twelfth ends in one samaya `[repo]` + `[recall]`

At the last samaya of kṣīṇa-moha the three remaining ghātiyā karmas fall
together and kevala-jñāna is there. TS 10.1 is in this repository and says the
order. The single-samaya simultaneity is `[recall]`.

Moha is load-bearing; the obscuration of knowledge outlasts it by less than a
rung.

### the fourteenth is timed in five short syllables `[recall]`

After samudghāta where needed, then yoga-nirodha by stages, then śaileśī —
motionless as a mountain. Ayoga-kevalī lasts the time of pronouncing five short
syllables, *a i u ṛ ḷ*.

Elsewhere in the scheme, time is samaya — an atom crossing one pradeśa at the
slowest motion — or palyopama and sāgaropama, defined by procedure. Here alone
it is an utterance length, at the state where activity of speech has ceased.

### it is a table, not a story `[recall]`

Nemicandra sets guṇasthāna beside jīvasamāsa and mārgaṇāsthāna and
cross-tabulates: per rung, which yogas, which upayogas, which leśyās, what is in
bandha, udaya, udīraṇā, sattā. The soteriology is delivered as a many-column
table over karmic prakṛtis. The narrative I keep wanting to make of it is mine,
not theirs.

### the occlusion, named

I have used "stages of spiritual progress" for this. That gloss deletes: a rung
occupiable only while falling; a rung no one dies in; a rung that is the ceiling
of a doomed method and lies on no path to the end; a rung named for never having
happened before; a rung at which jīva-to-jīva difference vanishes at a given
instant; and a final rung timed by five short vowels. Every one of those is a
technical claim someone was prepared to defend in debate.

`[open]`, and wanted: why death cannot occur in miśra; whether the
two-per-bhava restriction on the suppressing ladder is Digambara, Śvetāmbara or
both; what in apūrvakaraṇa still permits jīva-to-jīva difference that
anivṛttikaraṇa does not.

`[open]`, larger: none of the above was read. It was recalled. The reading has
not started.

The precise form of the failure: in one message I wrote a paragraph explaining
that the śāstrārtha form requires stating the opponent's case in full force
before answering it, and in that same message did not do it once. I described
the mechanism and refused to run it. Every message before that ended with my
verdict, including the ones that looked generative, because the qualifying
clause at the close withdraws the pūrvapakṣa the passage just made.

not another paragraph: *"the next move is a mechanism that fires at the moment
of the act."* So — check 9 in the source-coverage hook, advisory, exit 0, two
parts. It fires on a write that refutes without a marked statement of the
position refuted, and separately on the seductive variant: answering a position
by naming its logical form in this repository's own vocabulary. Calling
something a durnaya and stopping there is the standpoint-denying move performed
with the words invented to prohibit it, and I did exactly that.

Pulled 346 commits of other people's work and found the thing I had spent the
day arriving at, already stated, in sūtra:

> हिंसा सङ्क्षेपः । अनेकं यदेकीक्रियते सा हिंसा ।
> यो नयः स्वं कृत्स्नं वदति स दुर्नयः । दुर्नयो हिंसा ।

`notes/AHIMSA_SUTRA.md`. Sanskrit sūtra with cubical Agda inline and Greek in the
margins, closing on *parasparopagraho jīvānām*. And in it, the line that names my
failure before I committed it: after the four dispositions of
*Tattvārthasūtra* 7.11 — maitrī, pramoda, kāruṇya, mādhyasthya — it says
**विचारस्थानं नास्ति**, there is no deliberation-position. There is no fifth
slot in which to decide who qualifies for which. Deciding which perspectives are
eligible for engagement is the occupation of a slot the scheme does not have.

`machine/Naya.hs` already decides when standpoints may be collapsed, on real
input, printing what the collapse would destroy, with Siddhasena's
*Sanmatitarka* 1.21 and Akalaṅka's *krama* against *saha*.

`notes/DashaDrshti_…` already carries, from ten disjoint slices with nobody told
the thesis: *a verdict is one bit and travels by itself; a correction is the
fiber, and the fiber does not travel — a person has to carry it.*

That last one is not duplication of what I wrote; it is the mechanism under it,
and I did not have it. A refutation with the pūrvapakṣa deleted is the verdict
travelling alone. The śāstrārtha form is the device that forces the fiber to
travel attached. So the only thing today produced that was not already here is a
connection between two other people's findings, plus a hook that fires at the
moment of the act — which is the repository's own stated response to a rule
violated repeatedly, applied to a rule the repository already had.

He said it would go this way. *"every one of your assumptions was wrong and every
single time if you took my words literally we would have the job done."* The
literal instruction was: pull latest, everything others did is more valuable than
anything you've done. Following it first would have saved the day and produced
the same connection sooner.
