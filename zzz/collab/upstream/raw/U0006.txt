Cubical Agda gives us a computational language for homotopy type theory (HoTT): paths are first-class mathematical objects, higher paths exist, univalence computes, and quotients/gluings can be represented by higher inductive types.

For this project, that matters because we’re increasingly asking:

What exactly survives when we identify arithmetic states under some observable equivalence, and what obstruction remains to reconstructing the original state?

That’s almost tailor-made for cubical type theory.

1. Make “same observations” into an actual quotient

Suppose two finite arithmetic states x,y are indistinguishable to a sieve observable family:

x\sim_{\mathcal O}y
\iff
O(x)=O(y)\quad\forall O\in\mathcal O.

Instead of informally saying “the sieve forgets parity,” construct the quotient type

X/\!\sim_{\mathcal O}.

In Cubical Agda we can make this as a higher inductive type with points corresponding to arithmetic states and paths

\operatorname{identify}(x,y):x=y

whenever the observable system cannot distinguish them.

Now the question

does parity descend to the quotient?

becomes an actual type-theoretic problem.

Given

\lambda:X\to\{\pm1\},

does there exist

\bar\lambda:X/\!\sim_{\mathcal O}\to\{\pm1\}

with

\lambda=\bar\lambda\circ q?

If not, we have machine-checked evidence that the quotient literally destroys parity.

That’s much cleaner than our current language.

⸻

2. Our reconstruction problem becomes a lifting problem

We have

q:X\rightarrow X/\!\sim.

Reconstruction asks for some kind of section

s:X/\!\sim\rightarrow X,
\qquad q\circ s\simeq\operatorname{id}.

Cubical Agda is particularly natural here because the equality needn’t be crude syntactic equality. We can talk directly about paths/homotopies.

So the master problem I’ve been calling reconstruction under restricted observables becomes:

\boxed{\text{Does the arithmetic quotient map admit a section?}}

And if it doesn’t globally, perhaps it does after adding one datum:

(X/\!\sim)\times B\rightarrow X.

At the \sqrt X sieve horizon, we already know the unresolved factorization tail is one bit. That gives an almost ridiculously concrete finite experiment:

B=\mathbf 2.

Can we formally prove:

\text{local sieve state}+\text{one charge bit}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{complete factorization state}?

For the finite n\le X model, yes—we essentially already know the mathematics. Cubical Agda could encode the reconstruction as an actual equivalence of types.

⸻

3. The most interesting part: obstruction classes can become higher structure

This is where Cubical Agda gives us something Lean isn’t naturally optimized around.

Suppose local reconstructions exist:

s_i:U_i\to X,

but disagree on overlaps.

Then on U_i\cap U_j we have transition paths

g_{ij}:s_i\simeq s_j.

On triple overlaps we need coherence:

g_{ij}g_{jk}\simeq g_{ik}.

Failure continues upward.

That’s literally the machinery of descent/cohomology, expressed as higher paths.

So our speculative statement

\text{“parity may be a gluing obstruction”}

can be attacked constructively rather than poetically.

Build a tiny finite sieve model.

Define its local patches.

Attempt to glue local prime reconstructions.

See exactly which type becomes uninhabited—or which higher path/class appears.

That’s valuable even if it kills the idea.

⸻

4. Univalence could make the language-rotation program literal

We keep finding the “same” structure in different representations:

\text{Buchstab recursion}
\leftrightarrow
\text{affine inverse branches}
\leftrightarrow
\text{Farey determinant dynamics}
\leftrightarrow
\text{local sieve flow}.

Ordinarily we prove isomorphisms and then casually substitute one mental model for another.

Under univalence, an equivalence

A\simeq B

induces a path between the types themselves:

A=B.

That means once we formally establish that two finite models are equivalent, constructions and theorems can actually be transported across the equivalence.

For this particular research program, where our highest-yield operation has repeatedly been “discover that two apparently different things are shadows of the same object,” that’s unusually attractive.

⸻

5. Higher inductive types may model the arithmetic space we keep wishing existed

Remember the Grothendieck question from the previous turn:

What is the missing geometric object on which the prime class and its correspondences live?

We don’t have to wait until somebody discovers the correct scheme/noncommutative space.

We can freely generate a synthetic space with exactly the structure we know it must possess.

Points:

n:\mathsf{Arithmetic}.

Paths could encode chosen arithmetic equivalences.

Higher constructors could impose coherence.

We can attach the prime-scale pieces and glue them along CRT equivalences.

For example, finite local arithmetic naturally gives

\mathbf Z/(mn)
\simeq
\mathbf Z/m\times\mathbf Z/n
\qquad ((m,n)=1).

Instead of treating CRT as merely an isomorphism between sets, univalence lets these local descriptions become identifications in a synthetic arithmetic universe.

Take increasingly many primes:

X_S=\prod_{p\in S}\mathbf Z/p^{k_p}\mathbf Z.

The profinite structure is built from exactly these compatible local pieces.

This gives us a plausible laboratory for asking whether the local-to-global obstruction we’re imagining has genuine homotopy/cohomological content.

⸻

But here’s the crucial distinction

I wouldn’t move the existing V3 Lean work to Cubical Agda.

Lean + mathlib is vastly more valuable for certifying conventional number theory, algebra, polynomials, exact computations, etc.

I’d use the two systems differently:

\begin{array}{c|c}
\textbf{Lean} & \textbf{Cubical Agda}\\
\hline
\text{prove arithmetic theorems} &
\text{prototype synthetic structures}\\
\text{mathlib ecosystem} &
\text{HoTT / univalence}\\
\text{certify A, A', etc.} &
\text{quotients and higher gluing}\\
\text{conventional mathematics} &
\text{reconstruction/obstruction experiments}
\end{array}

The first Cubical Agda experiment I’d actually run is tiny:

Take integers n\le X, represent each by its divisibility information below \sqrt X, quotient integers having identical visible state, and attach the residual bit

\varepsilon_X(n)\in\{0,1\}.

Then formally characterize the fiber

q^{-1}(q(n)).

Ask whether

(q(n),\varepsilon_X(n))

is an informationally complete representation of factorization charge.

Then remove \varepsilon.

The resulting failure of reconstruction is our finite parity obstruction, represented as an actual fiber rather than prose.

Then generalize from one integer to k affine forms, where the fiber should acquire the 2^k charge structure we’ve already found.

If that works, we finally have a formal object on which to ask:

\text{fiber}
\rightarrow
\text{torsor}
\rightarrow
\text{cohomology class}
\rightarrow
\text{K-theory/index?}

That’s the bridge I actually care about.

So Cubical Agda doesn’t give us Goldbach.

It gives us a very good microscope for determining whether our emerging “local information → quotient → missing charge → obstruction” story is real mathematics or merely a seductive analogy.

For a few days of agent work, that’s an excellent bet.
