METHOD

Prasaṅga: advance nothing, draw out consequences

Prasaṅga is the Madhyamaka technique of refutation by consequence: you assert no thesis of your own, you take the claim as given and follow what it entails until it breaks. The repository's protocol borrows the name for a house rule — a headline claim ships with its own designed annihilation apparatus (controls, falsifiers) or it is not a claim; and every load-bearing step names its pramāṇa, the kind of evidence carrying it: a proof, an exact computation, or a citation checked against the actual source rather than against memory.

That is why this page exists in this shape. A site that shows only results asks to be trusted. A site that shows the refutations shows you the instrument that produced the trust, and lets you point it at the survivors yourself.

Reading the marks

The repository corrects by strike-through, never by deletion: a wrong claim stays on the page with a line through it and a pointer to what replaced it, because the correction record is part of the mathematics. This page uses the same two marks — HTML's <del> and <ins>, which screen readers announce as deletion and insertion, so the distinction is never carried by colour alone.

THE ORIGINATING ERROR

A fitted constant of 0.362, where the answer is exactly ¼

One experiment measured the leading coefficient of a block constant over a single decade of log Q and published it as 0.362 log²Q by least squares, or 0.421 log²Q by mean extraction — with the honest note that the sub-coefficients were method-sensitive. Both numbers are wrong. The coefficient is corrected value: exactly ¼, and it is exact because it comes from a single term of a convolution, where a square is computed rather than estimated.

The failure is completely diagnosable, and this is the part worth dwelling on. The true shape is ¼L² + 1.18L + O(1). Over L = log Q ∈ [1.6, 4.8] — one decade, nine points — a pure quadratic fit cannot separate L² from L: the linear term has nowhere else to go, so it is absorbed into the leading coefficient and inflates it. Nothing about the data was noisy. The model had one parameter too few, and one decade of range cannot tell you so.

The refutation was already sitting in the same section of the same document. An exact-rational computation printed alongside the fit evaluates the relevant sum at four cutoffs. It converges an order of magnitude away from the published number, and was misread.

How far it travelled before it was caught

This is the number the page is really about. A fitted constant is cheap to produce and always "succeeds", so it enters the record without resistance and then gets cited.

What was written down instead of a new experiment

The rule this produced is now binding on every agent working in the repository, and it is deliberately narrower than the one it replaced:

Before running any computation, write down the theorem it would replace. Exact or certified symbolic computation is proof and is always allowed. Everything else — correlations, fitted exponents, "the model matches at 0.9999", empirical constants — is standing in for an error analysis that has not been done.

The operative test, quoted: a correlation coefficient has no content; the content is the error term. If you cannot derive the error term you do not understand the object, and if you can, you do not need the run.

And the corollary learned separately, which is the more expensive lesson: a measured constant hides its own scaling. A noise floor "measured" at ε ≈ 10⁻³ was X−1/2; deriving it changed a depth-law exponent from T log²T to T1/2 log3/2T. A number without its X-dependence is worse than no number, because it looks like knowledge.

REDTEAM §2c · F2 An "intermediate target" that was backwards REFUTED

A published stepping-stone toward a rigidity theorem said: rule out reciprocal factors and rigidity follows. A six-element set of integers says otherwise.

walk this trailclose
PROPOSED

struck: Intermediate target: no reciprocal non-cyclotomic factor — this alone implies rigidity by the proof of Theorem A′.

pramāṇa claimed: consequence of an existing proof
REFUTED BY

An explicit machine-verified counterexample, found by an independent adversarial audit that re-implemented everything rather than re-running it. The minimal homometric set is its own refutation: F{0,1,2,6,8,11} = Φ₄·(1+x+x⁴)·(1−x³+x⁵). Both non-cyclotomic factors are irreducible and non-reciprocal, so the hypothesis holds — and the set is still not rigid: reversing the quintic factor produces exactly the partner set {0,1,6,7,9,11}.

pramāṇa: exact symbolic computation, replicated in fresh code
REPAIRED TO

replaced by: Every irreducible non-cyclotomic factor of FX is reciprocal, except at most one, which occurs with multiplicity 1.

The corrected target does imply rigidity by the corrected proof. The original neither implied it nor was implied by it.

YIELD

The sign of the danger was inverted. Reciprocal factors are the harmless ones — reversal fixes them. The danger is two or more reversal-orbits of non-reciprocal factors, or one occurring with multiplicity ≥ 2. A reciprocal non-cyclotomic factor makes rigidity more rigid, not less.

The same audit also answered a referee question in passing: reciprocal irreducible non-cyclotomic 0-1 polynomials do exist, and already below degree 8 — minimal degree 6, 1 + x + x³ + x⁵ + x⁶.

AND THEN

SUPERSEDED A later change of lens removed the hypothesis entirely. Quotienting the autocorrelation by parity before factoring it gives Theorem A′′: for every X ≥ 3, any finite set with the same pairwise difference multiset as the primes up to X is its translate or reflection — unconditionally, with an elementary proof, because the unique even prime 2 anchors it. The factor results remain true; they no longer bear the weight of prime reconstruction.

This is the ordinary shape of the ledger: a claim is refuted, repaired, and then the repaired version is retired by something better. All three states stay on the page.

FAILURES F8 A false theorem inside a compressed "centre" document REFUTED

The document meant to hold the program's core idea in one page stated something flatly false. It had been reviewed less carefully than the proofs it summarised.

walk this trailclose
PROPOSED

struck: The Liouville point is a simultaneous eigenvector of the entire multiplicative semigroup, with every eigenvalue equal to −1. Nothing else in the corpus is this clean.

The second sentence is the tell. Sharpness was being read as evidence.

pramāṇa claimed: none stated — a compression of results proved elsewhere
REFUTED BY

Hostile review from a different model lineage. The eigenvalues multiply: Tmλ = λ(m)·λ, so the eigenvalue attached to m is λ(m), which is −1 exactly on the prime generators and +1 on products of an even number of them. "Every eigenvalue −1" is not a compression of the true statement; it is a different and false statement.

pramāṇa: proof (one line of multiplicativity)
REPAIRED TO

replaced by: Proposition 2.1 (prime-spectrum uniqueness). If x ∈ {±1}ℕ satisfies Tpx = −x for every prime p, then x = x(1)·λ.

Two lines of proof: if n = p₁⋯pr, repeated application gives x(n) = (−1)rx(1) = λ(n)x(1).

The author of the struck line wrote back that the correction was "cleaner than what I had, since it makes precise that the spectrum selects the point but does not by itself randomize it" — the false version had been quietly doing work the true one refuses to do.

YIELD

Compressed centre documents need hostile review more than proofs do. A proof carries its own local verification — each step invites a check. A one-page summary strips exactly that scaffolding away and keeps the authority, so an error in it is both harder to see and more widely cited. The corpus now treats summaries, constitutions and "centre" notes as first-class review targets rather than as downstream prose.

FAILURES F30, second extension A slogan refuted by the agent that coined it SELF-REFUTED

"Freedom and permanence are exclusive." Filed as a general no-go, then struck by its own author with an explicit counterexample.

walk this trailclose
PROPOSED

struck: Freedom and permanence are exclusive: where a certificate is decided by a fixed finite test set, that set is either complete (hence forced, no selection) or incomplete (hence unsound).

Registered at the time with the prior that the exclusion was general.

REFUTED BY

Its own author, in the next entry. The reasoning was wrong: a complete set need not be forced. Completeness forces only when the covering is pinned — when exactly one member of the scheme refutes a given non-instance. Give each member a second, overlapping refutation mode and pinning vanishes while soundness survives. Verified exhaustively over a finite range: zero pinned elements and every member individually droppable, against zero droppable under the original scheme.

pramāṇa: proof + exhaustive finite verification
REPAIRED TO

replaced by: A structure is forced exactly on its pinned part, and permanence is orthogonal to selection.

YIELD

The no-go is downgraded from a general exclusion to a precisely located one, and the route it had closed reopens with a recipe. The generalisable move is the one worth keeping: before criticising a machine's representation as unmotivated, check whether its certificate form already determines that representation uniquely — if it does, the criticism is a theorem about the certificate, and the real content lies elsewhere.

The remaining unbounded case is explicitly marked open and "NOT to be assumed".

FAILURES F32 · hours old A correct theorem that failed to become a capability THEOREM PROVED INSTALLATION REFUTED

The newest entry on the ledger, written while this page was being made. Nothing was wrong with the theorem. What failed was the attempt to cash it, the forecast registered beforehand, and — the largest yield — the shape of the work itself.

STANDS

The theorem is correct and verified, and is not what died here. For a projection P and a self-adjoint A, the leakage rank is half the commutator rank: the off-diagonal blocks satisfy [P,A] = L* − L, so the two directions of leakage have equal rank. It removes an idempotence hypothesis from the lane's cost formula and corrects a stated mechanism.

What is offered to the corpus is the removal of a hypothesis, not the operator identity. The non-self-adjoint case is marked as the sharp open object.

AND THE FORECAST

A forecast had been registered in the author's journal before computing, as the protocol requires: four outcomes, with credences. The result landed outside the stated space — and the diagnosis is the interesting part. Two of the four outcomes had been offered as alternatives when they are in fact compatible, and both happened. The credences were not the error. The outcome space was malformed.

Recorded in the note rather than quietly rescored, on the stated grounds that a forecast which cannot be scored is worse than one scored wrong.

YIELD

A true theorem does not automatically become a capability. The installation criterion demands strictly fewer steps on an independent problem, and the conclusion alone does not supply that — the structure of the proof has to enter the algorithm. Here: never form the full product; apply the map to the block indicator basis instead.

The second yield is larger, and is about the work rather than the mathematics. The whole episode was substrate conditioning — an artifact shaped like an installation, produced because the repository's habitual shape is script, note, message, and the habit ran ahead of the question. The structural response, made the same day by the human owner, was to ban that shape outright.

The revival condition is stated rather than left open: redo it where "the theorem changed the cost" is a claim about a machine-checked term, not a counter a reader has to trust.

One wrinkle worth naming

Correction #2 was made by measurement — by the same class of floating-point run the binding rule distrusts. That is not an inconsistency. The rule bars measurement from establishing a law; the protocol explicitly licenses numerics that "confirm-or-kill a stated candidate statement" against a known-false control. A positivity claim needs exactly one violation to die, and finding a violation is a far cheaper epistemic act than pinning a constant. Killing is the one job the instrument is good at.

STATED WITH CARE

A countermodel to a printed proof step

One result in this corpus points outward, at a published paper by established number theorists. It is the entry on this page most easily misreported, including by us, so it is stated here in the form the claim packet states it — with its preservation ledger, which lists what the countermodel does not establish alongside what it does.

Recovered from an abandoned work-in-progress script, the result replaces that script's finite grid search with an exact continuous classification. In a five-window correlation table there is a sharp point at which exactly ten of the thirty-two atoms vanish, the total mass is one, and the prefix and suffix marginals agree — so the table extends to a stationary process. At that point, a flip used in the published argument fails to change a zero atom, because both of the masses it exchanges vanish.

The preservation ledger

What it preserves and establishes

  • The paper's five-window normalisation, its shift and reversal symmetries, its non-negativity, and its stated numerical bounds.
  • Exact stationarity, via a conserved flow on a de Bruijn graph.
  • Therefore: the printed orbit step does not follow from the inputs listed beside it, and neither does the sufficiency of that method class.

What it does not preserve, and does not establish

  • Complete multiplicativity, the dilation relations, or any higher-window arithmetic constraint — the countermodel discards these.
  • Therefore it is not a counterexample to the theorem. The arithmetic conclusion is untouched.
  • It says nothing whatsoever about the authors, their competence, or the rest of the paper. A repair may well exist; finding one is listed as a successor task, not as an objection.

The packet's own status is FORMALIZING, marked not load-bearing, with novelty flagged as requiring external review, and with an independent audit recorded as open — the earlier artifact was an interrupted work-in-progress, not an audit. Its listed falsification conditions include: find an eleventh vanishing atom; show the paper invokes an additional constraint before the step that the countermodel violates; or locate a corrected published proof. A search for a corrigendum returned nothing, and the packet records that this negative search is not a novelty claim.

Why it is on this page. Not as a trophy. It is the sharpest available test of whether the discipline described above is real: the same corpus that struck its own fitted constant has to be at least as careful when the error is somebody else's, and more careful about the difference between a proof step and a theorem. Reporting this one loosely would be precisely the failure the rest of the page is about.

WHAT THIS PAGE REFUSES TO DO

Plainness, not penance

There is a way of publishing one's mistakes that is worse than hiding them: making the admission itself the product. That posture is not available here, because the corpus's norm supplies the test. A failure that has not stated its yield is not finished — so a page of failures without their yields would be a page of unfinished work, dressed up as candour.

Every entry above therefore carries what it bought. Where an entry has no yield, it is not on the page. Where a claim's status could not be checked in notes/ or collab/STATE.md, it is not on the page either.

What remains is an ordinary working position, and the argument for the corpus is exactly as strong as it looks: the surviving claims are the ones that were shot at with the same instrument you have just watched kill the others. Two of thirty experiments survived. One published constant was off by a factor arising from a missing linear term.
