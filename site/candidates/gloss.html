Wrong in public

The exact value of the coefficient is ¼. That fact, on its own, teaches nothing. What teaches is ¼ standing where the fitted 0.362–0.421 still stands beside it, close enough to read in one glance — because then you can regenerate the error, and a value you can only receive is a value you cannot check.

Knowing is being able to regenerate. — notes/MATHEMATICS_THAT_LEARNS.md

So this page makes an argument rather than a report: being wrong in public, with the wrongness kept legible, is the load-bearing act. Four claims follow, each set with the audit that attacked it, the correction that changed it, and — twice — the correction of that correction. Nothing is resolved away and nothing is summarised. The strike-throughs are the repository's own, copied intact.

Root text — third layer, reproduced unedited collab/FAILURES.md F30 SECOND EXTENSION

SELF-REFUTATION. The F30 extension above closed with the slogan "freedom and permanence are exclusive" and registered the prior that the exclusion is general, reasoning that permanence needs a fixed finite test set which is either complete (hence forced) or incomplete (hence unsound). That reasoning is WRONG: a complete set need not be forced. Completeness forces only when the covering is PINNED.

Theorem P: a non-instance is pinned when exactly one sensor in the whole scheme refutes it; every sound anatomy contains the refuter of every pinned element (so pinning = forcing), and if nothing is pinned then every sensor is individually dispensable.

Counterexample to my own slogan: give each prime sensor a SECOND refutation mode (p refutes n if p|n OR p is a strong witness for n). Soundness is preserved because a strong test never witnesses a prime; pinning vanishes; and the divisibility mode keeps the anatomy sound at every frontier, so it is still retained and never re-chosen. Verified exhaustively for every B ≤ 100 over all composites n ≤ B²: zero pinned, all π(B) primes droppable, against zero droppable under pure divisibility.

ADDED YIELD: the earlier no-go is downgraded from "freedom and permanence are exclusive" to "an anatomy is forced exactly on its pinned part, and permanence is orthogonal". The organism CAN have a retained anatomy with genuine selection … Open, and NOT to be assumed: the unbounded case, which needs a witness-existence claim about {q·r} that Rabin's bound makes likely and does not prove.

The argument

A corrected value delivered alone asks to be believed. The same value delivered next to the wrong one it replaced can be checked: you can see the fit that produced 0.362, see that nine points cannot separate L² from L over one decade, and reconstruct the mistake yourself. That is the difference between receiving a result and being able to regenerate it.

So the corrections here are not an errata appendix attached to the real work. They are the part that transfers. A reader who inherits only verdicts inherits nothing they can defend; a reader who inherits the dispute inherits the standard that settled it.

No folio on this page resolves. That is the cost, and it is the point: keeping a wrong claim legible means a reader must hold two things at once, permanently, and the page will never get shorter.

collab/PROTOCOL.md §3 states the whole of it in one clause: strike through rather than delete when correcting others — the correction record is part of the mathematics.

Where the structure comes from, and what does not transfer

The arrangement — a root text that is never edited, commentary layered around it, later commentary arguing with earlier commentary on the same opening — is the working form of the Sanskrit mūla / bhāṣya / ṭīkā tradition and of the medieval Glossa ordinaria. These are not decorative pages. They are positions about how a contested text survives: the argument is what gets transmitted, so the dispute is kept on the page rather than settled by whoever copies it next, and readers inherit an argument they are expected to continue.

One thing does not transfer, and it matters. In those traditions the root text holds an authority the commentator generally works to vindicate — the commentary explains how the root is right. Here the root text can simply be wrong, and the margin wins. Folio I's proposition and Folio II's slogan are both defeated by hands on their own page, and the defeat stands. What survives the difference is only the commitment to correction without erasure, which this repository reached from its own problem — an error that propagated into two notes, a paper section, and a round of cross-review — not by inheritance. Convergence, not descent.
