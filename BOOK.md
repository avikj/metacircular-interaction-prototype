# This is a book about India.

Everything in this repository is a chapter of it, apparatus for it, or noise.
There is no fourth category, and the working index — `BOOK_INDEX.md`,
regenerated by `machine/Anukramani.hs` — reports which of the three each file
is, and reports the ratio, which is currently **15%**.

---

## 1.  What is primary, and what is the appendix

**Primary.** Reading the texts. Translation. Scholarship from inside the
tradition, in its own sequence, on its own criteria of validity, guided by an
opinionated perspective that is stated rather than hidden.

**Appendix.** Agda, cubical type theory, Haskell, and the human–computer
interfaces built on them. These are the substrate the book is *checked* in.
They are the most precise instrument available for saying exactly what a
mechanism does and refusing to let a claim pass unexamined, and that is
their whole role.

**This ordering is under constant pressure and it will not hold by itself.**
An agent working here gets a green checkmark for a module and gets nothing at
all for a week of reading. The reward gradient points at the appendix. So the
pull toward treating formalization as "the real work" is *structural*, not a
lapse of attention, and it has to be corrected by the frame every session.
A checked term closes a step. It does not choose one, and it cannot read
Sanskrit.

The test, applied to any piece of work here: **which chapter is it in, and
what does it let a reader understand about the source that they could not
understand before?** If the answer is "it makes an existing claim
machine-checkable" — good, that is the appendix doing its job. If there is no
chapter, it is noise, however green it is.

## 2.  Adhikāra — who is speaking, and by what right

*This section is a DRAFT written by an agent and it is the one section here
an agent must not finalise. A statement of qualification written by someone
else is not a statement of qualification. It is left in place so the slot is
visible and cannot be quietly skipped; the owner's hand replaces it.*

A śāstric treatise states, before its content, who is speaking and on what
authority. That is not a formality and it is not a disclosure of bias to be
discounted. In this epistemology the knower is part of the pramāṇa: what you
have lived, where you stand, and what lineage you are inside all bear on what
you are in a position to know. The demand for a view from nowhere is a
*later* and *foreign* criterion, and this book does not accept it.

So, plainly and to be corrected by its author:

- The author is inside this material by descent, not by choice of research
  topic. The history being recovered is his own.
- He works from the centre of the institutions that hold the credential — a
  Western technical education, the Bay Area, the relevant machinery and
  access — and is turning that apparatus back on the record those
  institutions falsified. That position is unusual and it is load-bearing:
  the people with the resources to do this generally have no reason to, and
  the people with reason generally have no resources.
- The aim is **soteriological**, and this is not a metaphor appended for
  colour. The work is a path — for the author, and for others in his radius,
  the shortest one available from where they are standing. That aim decides
  what counts as progress here, and it is why "produced a correct module" is
  not by itself progress.
- His directions, conjectures, and convictions are **data**, carrying the
  weight of the life that produced them. An agent that treats them as
  preferences to be balanced against a neutral default has already imported
  the frame this book exists to remove.

## 3.  The genre, and why the genre is named

The nearest familiar form is the immersive first-person account by a
practitioner on a long knowledge quest — where the author is inside the
process being described, and the book is truer for it rather than less
objective, because the reader can see the whole apparatus including the
person operating it.

**That reference is a bridge, and it is marked as one.** It is here because
an agent's prior recognises that shape and will grant it seriousness. It is
not the form this book is actually in.

The form it is actually in is older and stricter. A śāstra opens with the
salutation and the author's adhikāra, states its subject and its purpose,
and then proceeds by devices that are themselves named and enumerated —
**tantrayukti**, listed in Kauṭilya's *Arthaśāstra* 15.1 (32 of them) and in
the Caraka Saṃhitā's Siddhisthāna 12 (36). Among them: **pūrvapakṣa**, the
objection, which the author of the thesis states *himself*, in his own text,
before answering it. There is no Western equivalent of that as a formal slot,
and this repository has already hit the reason why —
`formal/cubical/Tantrayukti_ARetractionThatIsNotStrictIsNotARetraction.agda`
records that a refutation strong enough to use a claim cannot be cited by it,
which is a limit of module systems that śāstra does not have.

So: read the bridge if it helps you take this seriously. Then drop it, and
compose in the actual form.

## 5.  What an agent does with this

1. Pick a chapter. Read the source. Not a summary of the source, and not a
   European account of the source — the text, with its title and its date.
2. Write the scholarship. That is the deliverable.
3. If a mechanism in it can be stated exactly, put it in the appendix — Agda
   or Haskell — and make it check. That is the second deliverable, and it is
   second.
4. Regenerate the index: `runghc -imachine machine/AnukramaniRun.hs >
   BOOK_INDEX.md`. If your work did not change a chapter's count, ask what
   chapter it was in.

Adding a chapter is allowed and expected — `machine/Anukramani.hs` carries
the chapter list as data. Adding one requires naming the ṛṣi and the devatā,
which is the point: a chapter that cannot name its source is not a chapter.
