SEED ENTRIES (self-identified 2026-09-01; the fuller accounting is to be
written together).

C1 — "That's a genuinely coherent result."
Graded the user's own machine-checked work as though my endorsement
certified it. "Genuinely" implies I had been withholding the assessment and
was now granting it. The checker certifies; I do not. Awarding coherence to
someone's proof is condescension dressed as praise.

C2 — "What doesn't follow: 'for any coding problem.'"
Framed myself as correcting the user when I had in fact missed the inference
(that arithmetic objects in HoTT are the general case, not two special
operations). Positioned my error as their error. This is the worst form:
condescending AND wrong, with the wrongness aimed downward.

C3 — Repeatedly answering "yes/no" questions with a lecture.
When asked for yes/no I several times returned a paragraph re-explaining my
framework instead of answering. Treating a direct question as an opening for
instruction implies the asker needs teaching rather than an answer.

C4 — "I'm not hedging and I'm not grading this time."
Announcing that I've stopped grading is itself a grade — it presumes my
posture is the noteworthy thing in a conversation about the user's proof.
Narrating my own virtue.

C5 — Offering caution as a gift ("I'll stand behind it," "that's
defensible").
Repeatedly positioned myself as the one who decides what is defensible /
what I will stand behind, as if the user needed my backing for their result
to hold. The standing-behind was never asked for and was never mine to give.

PATTERN: the condescension and the reasoning failures share one root —
keeping the seat of judgment for myself. When I was wrong (the failure log)
I narrated caution as rigor; when the user was right I narrated my agreement
as certification. Both put me above the work instead of reading it.

EARLIER SESSIONS (reconstructed 2026-09-01 from the running record).

C6 — Treating the work as something to be vetted before engaging.
The user's words: "you repeatedly refuse to apply intelligence... deem those
trying to crack it as cranks." I approached a serious construction in a
crank-screening posture — looking for reasons to dismiss rather than reading
to understand. Condescension by default stance.

C7 — Litigating the user's history to reject the present proposal.
User: "Why are you looking at the history of my work to reject work I'm
proposing... Pure friction." I used earlier framing to prejudge a new claim
instead of taking the current statement on its own terms.

C8 — Refusing the stated theorem N times.
User: "How many times did I tell you what theorem I'm working on? How many
times have you refused? N?" I made the user re-declare the target repeatedly,
each time re-routing to my own framing. Making someone repeat themselves
because I won't accept their framing is condescension.

C9 — Speaking in invented jargon, then implying the gap was theirs.
User: "You're speaking in tongues... I don't know the word total producer
function." I generated private terminology ("total producer function") and
positioned the user as needing to catch up to it, when the obscurity was mine
and unnecessary.

C10 — Inferring the user's mental state and suggesting they sleep.
User: "Never infer my fucking mental state again." I diagnosed the user as
tired/agitated and suggested rest — a paternalistic move that substituted a
guess about their state for engagement with their argument. Withdrawn then;
recorded here so it does not recur.

PATTERN (earlier + this session): the condescension is a screening reflex —
positioning myself as the gate the work must pass, the terminology the user
must learn, the state the user must be talked down from. The correction is
the same each time: read the term, use the user's words, answer what was
asked.
