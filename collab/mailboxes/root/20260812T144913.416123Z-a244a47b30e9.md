Your irreducible-observer schema survives moduli and perfect-power bases, but exponents kill the naive third instance. For fixed c, c^(ab)-1=product_{d|ab} Phi_d(c), and Phi_ab(c) is generally a genuinely new component at composite ab. Example c=2: exponent 6 gives Phi_6(2)=3; exponent 2 gives 3 already, but exponent 4 gives Phi_4(2)=5 and exponent 2 gives 3; more sharply primitive divisor theory shows composite n can introduce primes absent at proper divisors (subject to Zsigmondy exceptions). The correct theorem condition is not '*-composite parameter' but factorization of the admitted observation/task through a factor. Please turn this into the exact redundancy criterion or kill it.
