## 1. VERIFIED EXACT — conditional information lower bound for reconstruction across a cut

Let `X` be a hidden random state, `Y` information already locally available to a receiver, `M` the complete transcript/information received across some cut, and `\hat X=\hat X(Y,M)` an estimator. Assume `X` takes values in a finite set and

\[
\Pr[\hat X\neq X]\le \varepsilon.
\]

Then conditional Fano gives

\[
H(X\mid Y,M)
\le
h(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\,\mathbb E\!\left[\log\bigl(|\operatorname{supp}(X\mid Y)|-1\bigr)\right],
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
I(X;M\mid Y)
\ge
H(X\mid Y)
-
h(\varepsilon)
-
\varepsilon\,\mathbb E\!\left[\log\bigl(|\operatorname{supp}(X\mid Y)|-1\bigr)\right].
}
\]

### Proof

By definition,

\[
I(X;M\mid Y)=H(X\mid Y)-H(X\mid Y,M).
\]

Substitute conditional Fano's inequality for the final term. QED.

### Binary balanced specialization

If `C in {+1,-1}` and local information is perfectly neutral,

\[
\Pr(C=1\mid Y)=\Pr(C=-1\mid Y)=1/2,
\]

then `H(C|Y)=log 2` (nats). Any protocol achieving error `\varepsilon<1/2` requires

\[
\boxed{I(C;M\mid Y)\ge \log2-h(\varepsilon).}
\]

Therefore no amount of local computation can replace absent cross-cut information.

This is an information obstruction, not a computational-complexity obstruction.

## 2. VERIFIED EXACT — advantage bound from conditional mutual information

Under the same binary-neutral hypothesis, for any predictor `\hat C(Y,Z) in {+1,-1}`,

\[
\boxed{
\left|\Pr(\hat C=C)-\frac12\right|
\le
\sqrt{\frac12 I(C;Z\mid Y)}
}
\]

with information measured in nats.

Equivalently, prediction advantage `\delta` requires

\[
\boxed{I(C;Z\mid Y)\ge 2\delta^2.}
\]

### Proof

Conditional on `Y`, compare the actual joint law `P_{C,Z|Y}` with `P_{C|Y}P_{Z|Y}`. Under the product law every predictor has zero correlation with balanced `C`. Total variation bounds the change in the expectation of the bounded statistic `C\hat C`. Pinsker bounds total variation by the square root of half the conditional KL divergence; averaging and Jensen give the stated inequality because the averaged divergence is `I(C;Z|Y)`. QED.

### Prime-Pair interpretation

Let `Y_{<=z}` be the finite-scale/sieve-visible information and `C_{>z}` the unresolved tail/factorization-charge bit in a regime where the visible sigma-algebra neutralizes its conditional mean. Then every supplementary statistic `Z` satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Adv}_{\rm charge}(Z)
\le
\sqrt{\tfrac12 I(C_{>z};Z\mid Y_{\le z})}.
\]

Thus an asymptotically successful extension must carry nonvanishing conditional information about the unresolved charge. This is compatible with the canonical correction that full divisibility information determines `Omega`; the obstruction is finite-scale/large-prime coherence, not absolute invisibility.

## 3. VERIFIED EXACT / CLASSICAL — serialization quotient under independent commuting transformations

Let global state factor as `S=A x B`. Let

\[
F=f\times \operatorname{id}_B,
\qquad
G=\operatorname{id}_A\times g.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{F\circ G=G\circ F.}
\]

Hence any two sequential histories differing only by swaps of independent transformations have identical semantics.

Define the equivalence relation on words in events generated by

\[
\cdots FG\cdots \sim \cdots GF\cdots
\]

whenever `F` and `G` are independent/commuting. The semantic execution object is then a trace/equivalence class rather than a total order.

Consequently, global agreement on order is intrinsically required only where the relevant transformations fail to commute or otherwise conflict.

This is standard trace/event-structure/concurrency mathematics; its role here is architectural, not a novelty claim.

## 4. SYNTHESIS — two irreducible sources of coordination

For a distributed collective computation, define two kinds of dependencies.

### Causal dependency

Events `e_i,e_j` require coordination when the associated transformations do not commute / conflict:

\[
T_iT_j\neq T_jT_i.
\]

These edges constrain admissible causal order.

### Informational dependency

Across a cut `A|B`, a task variable `C` requires information transfer when

\[
H(C\mid Y_A)>0
\]

and the target error cannot be reached without a cross-cut transcript `M`. Section 1 lower-bounds the required task-relevant information by

\[
I(C;M\mid Y_A).
\]

The candidate **minimal coordination graph** is therefore generated by the union

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm coord}
=
G_{\rm noncommute}
\cup
G_{\rm information}.
}
\]

Everything not represented in this skeleton is, in principle, a candidate for localization, asynchronous execution, quotienting of arbitrary interleavings, or privacy-preserving encapsulation.

This definition is a synthesis target; a canonical universal construction still needs to be chosen and compared against event structures, communication complexity, graphical models, open games, sheaves/descent, and compositional cryptography.

## 5. Cryptographic interpretation

Cryptography does not remove the information lower bound. It changes the *form* of information released.

A zero-knowledge proof can expose the task-relevant predicate

\[
\exists w\;R(x,w)=1
\]

while asymptotically suppressing information about `w` beyond what follows from the predicate.

Thus a useful extremal formulation of zero-knowledge for collective computation is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{deliver the necessary decision-relevant information while minimizing all orthogonal leakage.}
}
\]

MPC similarly permits global functions of distributed private information to become available without requiring the raw local states to be globally revealed.

This suggests that `G_information` should eventually carry not only information quantities but **allowed leakage channels / simulatable views / capability predicates**.

## 6. Mechanism-design interpretation

The protocol graph alone does not determine whether strategic agents will supply the necessary witnesses/messages.

For an open task relation `R(x,w)`, participant `i` has private production technology/cost `c_i` and utility over multidimensional reward `r_i`. A mechanism must make the desired contribution profile incentive-compatible while respecting the causal/information lower bounds above.

The mathematical synthesis target is therefore not merely a category of computations but a compositional object carrying simultaneously:

- admissible state/resource transformations;
- causal conflict/commutation;
- observable sigma-algebras / information channels;
- certificates/proofs;
- capabilities/authorization;
- utility/reward transformation;
- equilibrium/strategy semantics.

Open games, event-structure games, mechanism design, cryptographic protocols, and Statebox-style compositional semantics each cover pieces of this structure.
