# PRIME-PAIR RESEARCH DELTA 3 — quotient dynamics and localization of coupling

## VERIFIED EXACT — translation quotient collapses primitive determinant-h states
For gcd(A,B)=1, the Diophantine equation
\[
Bs-At=h
\]
has solutions, and any two solutions differ by
\[
t\mapsto t+Bk,\qquad s\mapsto s+Ak.
\]
This is exactly right multiplication by the unipotent translation matrix [[1,k],[0,1]]. Therefore modulo integer translation, for fixed h there is exactly one intercept class for each ordered coprime pair (A,B).

Consequently the apparent SL2/Farey/Hecke state space largely ABELIANIZES in the generic coprime branch: the quotient state is just (A,B), with h fixed externally. Peeling a good prime from leg 1 multiplies A by p; peeling from leg 2 multiplies B by p (up to naming convention). The Farey relation is exact but may be decorative rather than the source of the hard spectral structure.

## VERIFIED EXACT — coupled/non-coprime branching is supported only at primes dividing h
For the original pair n,n+h, if p does not divide h then p cannot divide both legs simultaneously. Hence throughout the peeled-divisor description, a good prime p∤h can be assigned to at most one of A,B. Only collision primes p|h can occur on both legs and generate shared p-adic depth / non-coprime slope states.

Together with v_p(root separation)=v_p(h), this implies:
- every good prime p∤h is locally an independent one-leg peeling event;
- all genuinely coupled finite-place branching is supported on the finite set p|h;
- those collision corrections are exactly the same finite places responsible for the Hardy–Littlewood singular-series enhancement.

## MAJOR CONSEQUENCE / REFOCUSED LIVE FRONTIER
After factoring the exact local singular series / charge-deformed collision factors, the remaining connected boundary interaction Gamma_h CANNOT be generated by generic finite-prime coupling: good primes are leg-disjoint and locally free. The hard connected remainder must come from one or more of:
1. archimedean ordering/stopping constraints in Buchstab peeling (least-prime order, finite X, positive cone);
2. coherent global additive sampling / rational-frequency effects across many otherwise independent good primes;
3. global L-function spectral corrections;
4. the finite collision primes p|h, but these should already be removable by the exact local factor.

This substantially downgrades the idea that a nontrivial Hecke spectrum of the fixed-determinant matrix space by itself will solve the problem. The matrix representation remains useful bookkeeping and exposes determinant locality, but after translation quotient the generic dynamics is close to a two-color multiplicative prime-assignment process.
