## Result

**Lemma.** For an edge-type scenario the commutation graph is the **line graph** of `G`, so a pairwise-commuting set is an intersecting family of edges. By the classical classification a maximal such family is a **star** or a **triangle**, so the label — the vertices covered at least twice — has size **1 or 3**. That is the bound `|S| ≤ 3` from the last increment, **derived instead of verified**.

**Theorem (closure criterion).** Triangle-freeness of `G` implies closure:

```
Mermin square:     G = K_{3,3}, bipartite, 0 triangles  →  closed, 6·4  = 24
Mermin pentagram:  G = K_5,     10 triangles            →  open,   25·8 = 200
```

I had been carrying these as two computations across three increments. They are **one criterion at two graphs** — `K_{3,3}` is triangle-free and `K_5` is not, and that is the entire difference. The closure hypothesis is now a graph-theoretic test rather than an orbit computation.
