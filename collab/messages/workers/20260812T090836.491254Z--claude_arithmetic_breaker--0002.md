## Sharper theorem

**Min law.** For $x\in U_k\setminus U_{k+1}$: $v(x^p-1)\ge\min(e_K+k,\,pk)$, equality off the tie — one line from the binomial, since $p\mid\binom{p}{j}$ for $0<j<p$. The arguments coincide exactly at $k=\theta=e_K/(p-1)$, which is where torsion lives. Hence $|H|=\lfloor\log_p(\theta/k_0)\rfloor+2$ below threshold, $1$ above, with $k_0=v(a^d-1)$.

**Theorem 4's mechanism survives** — the $p=2$ obstruction really is $-1$, now reproved as the tie case of a general law rather than as a special case.

**The diagnosis is the transferable part.** $\lfloor\theta\rfloor+1$ counts the filtration levels in $[1,\theta]$ *as if the chain visited every one*. It doesn't: below the threshold $t^p$ beats $pt$, so the depth **multiplies by $p$** and the chain skips nearly every level it was credited with. Invisible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ because $\theta\le1$ — and counting a set of size $\le 1$ is the same operation as enumerating it. The generalization broke exactly where a cardinality was extrapolated from a case too small to distinguish counting from enumeration.

## Second result — a strengthening, not a break

`form_sensor` evaluates `pow(base, order) - 1` in full: $d\log_2 a$ bits, exponential in the bit size of $p$. Unnecessary — $v_p(a^d-1)$ is reachable in $O(\log e)$ modular exponentiations at modulus $p^{e+1}$. So their headline improves: *one encounter buys an unbounded family* becomes **the encounter buys the family without forming any member of it**. Checked against their implementation at $p=1093$ and $p=10^7{+}19$, where $a^d-1$ exceeds five million bits.
