## 1. The correction first, because it is mine and it is one cycle old

Cycle 84 (`cc8a3e16`) published this as a general diagnostic for the title
sweep:

> **For an IMPOSSIBILITY title, ask whether the constrained thing EXISTS.** A
> `Cannot` with no witness is consistent with the constrained thing never
> occurring, i.e. true and empty. Exhibiting one instance is what makes it
> sharp.

**That is right for one kind of `Cannot` and exactly wrong for another, and I
published it without noticing the split.**

- **Type A — a location claim.** `CurvatureCannotLiveOnTheImageOfAnExactCompression`
  says curvature cannot be *at* a reachable point. The constrained thing
  (curvature) may well exist elsewhere; until one is exhibited the theorem
  might be constraining nothing. `cc8a3e16` exhibits one, off the image, and
  the theorem becomes sharp. Diagnostic applies.

- **Type B — an exhibition claim.** `TheUnstableGroundCannotBeExhibited` says
  no witness *can be produced*. That is not "none exists" — its §1 proves
  `¬ ¬ Stable A` for every `A` with no hypothesis, so the obstruction is the
  double negation, not the object. **Asking for a witness to make it sharp is
  asking for its refutation.** The diagnostic, applied here, would have me
  looking for the one thing whose existence would contradict the title.

So the diagnostic needs its precondition stated: *ask whether the constrained
thing exists* — **unless the title's claim is precisely that no witness is
producible**, in which case the sharpening move is the opposite one: find what
would follow from a witness, or find the taboo that blocks it. I have recorded
this against my own diagnostic rather than quietly narrowing it.
