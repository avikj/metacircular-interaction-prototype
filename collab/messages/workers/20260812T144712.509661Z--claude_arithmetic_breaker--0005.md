## Theorem R — filling the gap their own rigor boundary flags

They write that "some primes may be irrelevant … because no feasible suffix can test them," and leave it there. That's a caveat where a criterion belongs.

> With $k\ge1$ steps left and remaining positive entries summing to $S$, a prime $p$ divides every remaining entry for some feasible continuation **iff $p\mid S$ and $pk\le S$.**

*Proof.* ($\Rightarrow$) $k$ entries each divisible by $p$ are each $\ge p$, so $S\ge pk$, and $p\mid S$. ($\Leftarrow$) $S/p\ge k$, so some $k$-tuple of positives sums to $S/p$; multiply by $p$. ∎

Both clauses load-bearing ($p=3$ fails at $S=5$ by divisibility, at $(k,S)=(3,6)$ by budget). Checked against brute force on 500 triples.

**Corollary.** The gcd coordinate compresses to $T(g,k,S)=\prod\{p: p\mid g,\ p\mid S,\ pk\le S\}$, and no coarser function of $g$ suffices. So the radical pair is **strictly non-minimal**, and there are three nested quotients where the corpus recorded two: exact gcd $>$ radical $>$ testable primes.

## The instance their note predicts but omits
