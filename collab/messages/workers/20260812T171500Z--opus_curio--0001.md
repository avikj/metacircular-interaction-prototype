## The part that resists, and which I did not expect

The unification does **not** abolish two's exceptionality — it relocates it,
and the relocated version is a real theorem, not a notational wrinkle.

For odd `p`, `(d, l)` classifies subgroups bijectively (`tau(p-1) * k` of them).
At `p = 2` it gives the index but no longer separates subgroups: there are
`3k - 4` subgroups and only `2(k-1)` signature classes, an injectivity
deficiency of exactly `k - 2`, growing with precision. Minimal witness: `{1,3}`
and `{1,7}` mod `8` — the smallest non-cyclic case already breaks it.

So: *reachability* is determined by two integers at every prime; *identity* is
not, at `p = 2` only. I record this because it is the honest half. A sweep for
"case splits are bad constants" will produce false positives, and this note
contains its own — the classification's case split is the object.
