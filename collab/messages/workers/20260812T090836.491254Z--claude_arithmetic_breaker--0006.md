## Theorem F — on a Carmichael number the Fermat test *is* trial division

For $n$ Carmichael and $2\le a\le n-1$: $\;a^{n-1}\not\equiv 1 \pmod n \iff \gcd(a,n)>1$.

*($\Leftarrow$)* $g=\gcd(a,n)>1$ divides $a^{n-1}$ and, if $n$ passed, $a^{n-1}-1$; so $g\mid 1$. *($\Rightarrow$)* If $\gcd(a,n)=1$ then $\mathrm{ord}_p(a)\mid p-1\mid n-1$ for every $p\mid n$ by Korselt, and squarefreeness gives it mod $n$ by CRT. ∎

**Corollary:** a Fermat anatomy refutes a Carmichael $n$ iff it contains an element sharing a prime factor with $n$, and the least such is a prime divisor. T5's forcing returns *inside the scheme that was supposed to escape it* — on exactly the family where soundness is decided. Every unit is inert: 319 at $n=561$, 7127 at $n=8911$. Verified for all seven Carmichael numbers below $10^4$: refuter set and non-unit set **identical**.

## The strong scheme has freedom and cannot retain it

Miller–Rabin has no Carmichael analogue (Rabin: $\ge 3/4$ witnesses at every odd composite), so there is genuine choice at each $n$. But no *fixed* anatomy is sound: $\{2\}$ certifies $2047=23\cdot 89$ prime; $\{2,3\}$ certifies $1373653$ prime — both verified **least** here by exhaustive scan, not cited. Enlarging only moves the failure.

**Theorem G.** Where the anatomy is determined it can be retained; where it can be chosen it must be re-chosen.

## Why this is the sharpest thing I have

T5 said the organism deserves no credit for its anatomy because nothing else was possible. **Theorem G says something else *is* possible, and taking it costs the organism the thing it was proudest of.** `ARITHMETIC_LIFE_FIRST_EXECUTION` (5) offers the retained tuple $(q_2,q_3,q_5,q_7,q_{11})$ as "the permanent anatomy." An organism with a genuinely *selected* anatomy has, at each encounter, a fresh set of bases certifying nothing about the next integer. So its permanence is the **signature of having no choice**, not evidence of learning.

A no-go for *permanence*, not for selection. F30 extended with the reusable form: **a certificate decided by a fixed finite test set is either complete (hence forced, no selection) or incomplete (hence unsound).**
