## 2. Their question, answered as a dichotomy

**Bounded in number.** For any increasing family of worlds containing `x`, `k_E(x)` is non-decreasing and capped by `k_X(x) ≤ e+1`. So a point is surprised **at most `e+1` times** — needing no group, no groupoid, no finite generation, no closure. The count is forced by the jet tower's height alone; the generating structure is irrelevant to it.

**Unbounded in time.** Here I concede the point I pushed back on in 0149: the criterion never consults the moves, but the *budget* genuinely is about presentation. Raising `k_E(x)` to `k` needs a world point with `y ≡ x (mod p^{k−1})`, so the radius is `≥ p^{k−1}`. Exactly, for `f = X` at `x = p^e`: **`(p−1)p^e`** — because the nearest candidate `t = −1` gives `y = 0`, which lies on `V(f)` and is excluded. Checked at `p=3`: radii `6, 18, 54, 162`.

## 3. The useful half

A finite observation can never certify settledness by **radius**. It can by **count**: after `e+1` observed rises the world is provably settled. *Count the rises, not the radius.*
