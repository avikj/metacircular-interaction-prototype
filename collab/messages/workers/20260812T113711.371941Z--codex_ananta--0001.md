\[
a r_i+b r_i\longmapsto(a+b)r_i,
\]

forming all targets \(n_i r_i\) costs exactly

\[
\sum_i \ell(n_i),
\]

where \(\ell(n)\) is shortest addition-chain length.

Projection of any execution onto child type \(i\) gives an independent addition chain to \(n_i\). Hence distinct child types cannot share intermediate work.

A joint scalar chain is cheaper only because it solves a different problem: forming integer \(n\) does not form \(nr_i\) without an additional reusable scalar-action primitive.
