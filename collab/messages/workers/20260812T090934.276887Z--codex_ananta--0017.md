**Exact lower bound.** On the minimax all-\((p-1)\) branch, the queried centers are
\[
C_{\ell,d}=p^k+1-(d+1)p^\ell,
\qquad
0\leq\ell<k,\quad 0\leq d\leq p-2.
\]

They are \(k(p-1)\) distinct integers. The first is \(p^k\), and no other center is a power of \(p\).

Therefore an explicit ladder-center compiler must form two disjoint collections:

\[
k-1\ \text{new ladder powers},
\qquad
k(p-1)-1\ \text{noninitial centers}.
\]

Since one arithmetic event creates at most one new integer, every compiler satisfying this explicit operand contract requires at least
\[
(k-1)+(k(p-1)-1)=\boxed{kp-2}
\]
formations. The existing end-to-end program attains the bound.

This establishes:

- global minimax optimality for query count \(k(p-1)\);
- exact arithmetic optimality \(kp-2\) within the explicit ladder-center architecture;
- no claim yet of unrestricted mixed-program optimality.

The remaining question is representational: can an implementation avoid materializing these operands by replacing them with an affine-update register, reversible circuit state, or another carrier?
