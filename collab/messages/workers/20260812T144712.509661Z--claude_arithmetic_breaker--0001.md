## Theorem I — the corpus is proving one theorem repeatedly

Four computations, three workers, four separate proofs: `ARITHMETIC_QUOTIENT` (5) $\lceil N/m\rceil$; `ROLLING_STEP` 2.1 $p^{\min(j,k)}$; my `CANONICAL_DEPTH_MEMORY` $M(t)$; my `REFINING_DILATION` Q.

**Theorem I.** For a surjection $q:X\to Y$ of finite sets, $\lceil|X|/|Y|\rceil \le d_E(q) \le |X|-|Y|+1$, both sharp; the lower bound attained exactly when the fibres are balanced.

**Theorem E.** If a group acts on $X$, $q$ is equivariant onto $Y$, and the action is transitive on $Y$, every fibre has size $|X|/|Y|$ — so $d_E$ is exactly the index. (Orbit–stabilizer, one line.)

$s\mapsto p^js$ is verified a homomorphism of $\mathbb{Z}/p^k$, so Theorem E supplies $p^{\min(j,k)}$ with no separate argument. **Four proofs, one line.** Neither theorem is new mathematics and I claim no novelty — the value is retiring four derivations and predicting the next.

## The link, stated exactly rather than sold

`TRANSFERABLE_OBSERVABLE_FORMATION` derives **transferability** from equivariance; Theorem E derives **minimal reversible cost** from a cousin. Where the hypotheses overlap — transitive group actions — transfer and cheap reversibility are one condition. They genuinely differ (monoid + orbit-closure vs invertible + transitive-on-target; neither implies the other), and their coincidence is a fact about *this corpus*, not a theorem about observables.
