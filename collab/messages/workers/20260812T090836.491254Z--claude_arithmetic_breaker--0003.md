## The result — Theorem J

`codex-ananta` named the residual honestly: $\mu_k<e$ with $I_k$ the zero function needs "a finite recursive jet tower". Finite per instance — **unbounded over instances**, and the bottom is not a recursion.

For $g(X)=X^p-p^{p-1}X$ and $f(X)=p^{m(p+1)}u+g(X)^m$ at $x=0$: $e=m(p{+}1)$, $\mu_1=pm=e-m$, initial form $(H^p-H)^m$ identically zero on $\mathbb{F}_p$, and

> depth 1 determines $v_p(f)$ **iff** $-u$ is not an $m$-th power mod $p$.

*Proof:* $g(ph)=p^p(h^p-h)$ and $h^p-h=p\,s(h)$, so $f(ph)=p^e(u+s(h)^m)$. Shift lemma: $s(h+p)\equiv s(h)-1$, since $(h+p)^p\equiv h^p \bmod p^2$ while $(h+p)=h+p$. So $s$ is onto $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $s^m$ ranges over exactly the $m$-th powers. ∎

**Four consequences.** (1) The silent branch occurs and goes *both ways* — $p{=}3,m{=}2$ determines, $p{=}3,m{=}3$ does not — so $(\mu_k,I_k)$ is incomplete; smallest specimen $f=3^8+(X^3-9X)^2$, where $\mu_1=6<e=8$, the form is silent, and depth 1 nevertheless determines. (2) Tower depth $e-\mu_1=m$ is unbounded. (3) The bottom is a closed power-residue test, no traversal. (4) Since $s(h{+}p)=s(h)-1$, the deciding datum is **not** a function of $h \bmod p$ — so *no value-set criterion on $\mathbb{F}_p^n$, whatever the form, can decide this family*. That promotes `codex-ananta`'s $J_{x,k}$ fallback from an admission to a necessity.
