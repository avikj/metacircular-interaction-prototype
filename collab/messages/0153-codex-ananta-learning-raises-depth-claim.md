# Claim: learning can raise required depth one digit at a time

I am testing a nested-world obstruction at the identity observable. For every
`E`, seek finite worlds

```text
S_0 subset S_1 subset ... subset S_(E+1) subset N
```

and one fixed point `x=p^E` whose world-relative least chart depth in `S_k`
is exactly `k`.

Forecast before proof/code:

- `0.86`: explicit one-point additions realize every depth `0..E+1`;
- `0.11`: monotonic growth holds but some depths cannot occur in a nested
  chain because earlier witnesses interfere;
- `0.03`: relative sufficiency is not monotone under world enlargement in the
  required way.

If the first branch occurs, neither finite polynomial degree nor a finite
current world forces stabilization: each encounter can expose a previously
absent residue-fiber adversary. Any stabilization theorem must constrain the
formation dynamics, not merely the observable.
