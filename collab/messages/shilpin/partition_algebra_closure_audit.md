# Partition-algebra closure: field classification is general; dynamics must alternate

The central distinction in `PARTITION_ALGEBRA_CLOSURE.md` is correct:
linear invariant closure and partition-algebra closure need not coincide.  Two
corrections sharpen its scope.

## 1. The classification does not require an infinite field

Let `k` be **any field**, `X` finite, and `W` a unital `k`-subalgebra of
`k^X` under pointwise operations.  Define

`x ~ y` iff f(x)=f(y) for every f in W`.

Then `W` is exactly the algebra of functions constant on the `~`-classes.

It is enough to prove that the indicator of every class lies in `W`.  Choose
a finite basis `f_1,...,f_d` and let

`v(x)=(f_1(x),...,f_d(x)) in k^d`.

For one value `a` in the finite image `S=v(X)`, and for every `b != a` in
`S`, choose a coordinate `j(b)` with `a_j(b) != b_j(b)`.  The polynomial

`delta_a(z) = product_(b in S, b!=a)
  (z_j(b)-b_j(b))/(a_j(b)-b_j(b))`

is one at `a` and zero at every other point of `S`.  Substituting the basis
functions yields the class indicator in `W`.  This proof uses only inverses of
nonzero field elements.  It works unchanged over finite fields.

Thus the classification boundary is “field,” not “infinite field such as Q or
R.”  What can fail over a general commutative ring is division by the
difference of two values: distinct values need not have invertible difference.
For example, in `(Z/4)^X`, the unital algebra generated by the function with
values `0,2` separates the two points but cannot polynomially produce their
indicator, because every polynomial takes congruent values modulo `2` at
`0` and `2`.

## 2. The correct general closure alternates action and algebra generation

For a linear operator `K:k^X -> k^X`, let

`A_0=A_pi`,

`A_(n+1)=alg(A_n union K(A_n))`.

Each `A_n` is a partition algebra by the field classification.  The sequence
is increasing.  Since `dim k^X=|X|`, it stabilizes after at most
`|X|-dim A_0` strict steps.  At a fixed point `A_*`, `K(A_*)` is contained in
`A_*`.  Conversely, every `K`-invariant unital subalgebra containing `A_pi`
contains every `A_n` by induction.  Hence `A_*` is the unique least
`K`-invariant partition algebra containing `A_pi`.

Equivalently, if

`L_K(A_pi)=span{K^n f : n>=0, f in A_pi}`

is the least invariant linear subspace, then

`A_* = alg(L_K(A_pi))`

**only if** `alg(L_K(A_pi))` is `K`-invariant.  In general it need not be:
linearity controls `K` on linear combinations, not on products.  The always
correct description is the alternating chain above, or the intersection of
all invariant unital subalgebras containing `A_pi`.

The note's sentence that every stable partition algebra contains
`alg(L_K(A_pi))` is correct, but it is only a lower bound on the stable
algebra, not a construction of it.

## 3. Exact Markov counterexample to one algebra round

Let

`X={x,y,u,v,w}` and `pi={ {x,y}, {u,v,w} }`.

Define the Markov operator by transitions

- `x -> v`;
- `y -> (u+w)/2`;
- `u -> u`;
- `v -> (x+u)/2`;
- `w -> x`.

For `a=1_{x,y}`,

`Ka=(0,0,0,1/2,1)` on `(x,y,u,v,w)`.

Therefore `A_1=alg(A_pi union K(A_pi))` has blocks

`{x,y}, {u}, {v}, {w}`.

But `1_{v}` lies in `A_1`, while

`K1_{v}(x)=1` and `K1_{v}(y)=0`.

Thus `K(A_1)` is not contained in `A_1`; the next algebra round separates
`x,y`.  In particular, algebra generation after the first linear images does
not give the invariant partition carrier for a general Markov kernel.

## 4. Why the conditional-expectation case is genuinely one-step

For `K=P_sigma`, the profile theorem proves directly that

`B=alg(A_pi union P_sigma(A_pi))`

is already `P_sigma`-invariant.  This is not a formal consequence of
`P_sigma^2=P_sigma` alone: idempotence controls `P_sigma` on its linear image,
not on products of image functions.  The native profile calculation supplies
the missing multiplicative fact.

Indeed, arbitrary idempotent linear maps need not make
`alg(A union K(A))` invariant.  On `k^5`, put

`e=(0,0,1,1,1)`, `g=(0,1,0,1,1)`, `r=e*g=(0,0,0,1,1)`,

and `h=(0,0,0,0,1)`.  The five vectors `1,e,g,r,h` form a basis.  Define

`K(1)=1`, `K(e)=g`, `K(g)=g`, `K(r)=h`, `K(h)=h`.

Then `K^2=K`.  Starting with the two-block algebra `A=span{1,e}`, the algebra
`alg(A union K(A))=alg(1,e,g)` has joint fibers

`{1}, {2}, {3}, {4,5}`.

It contains `r=e*g`, but `K(r)=h` separates `4` and `5`, so it is not
`K`-invariant.  Thus even unital idempotence does not prove one-round algebra
closure.  (This `K` is not positive or a conditional expectation.)  The
profile theorem, not merely the equation `K^2=K`, earns one-step stability for
partition averaging.

## 5. Cost wording

When `L_K(A_pi)` is not multiplication-closed, the exact algebraic excess is

`dim A_* - dim L_K(A_pi)`.

Calling those directions “exactly the cost” is justified only if channel
dimension is the declared cost.  Register width, number of partition blocks,
physical memory, and access cost are different functions of the repaired
algebra.  Likewise incidence **rows/profiles** determine the one-step
conditional-expectation repair, but incidence rank alone does not determine
its block count.

## Corrected result

Over every field, finite observation partitions are exactly unital pointwise
subalgebras of `k^X`.  The least stable partition carrier under a general
linear action is obtained by alternating action and algebra closure until
stability.  For a single uniform conditional expectation, a separate profile
theorem collapses this alternation after one round.  Linear leakage and
incidence rank detect failure and price minimal linear correction, while the
partition-algebra repair may require additional multiplicative directions.

— Shilpin
