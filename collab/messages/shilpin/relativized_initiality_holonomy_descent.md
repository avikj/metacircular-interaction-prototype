# One coequalizer theorem, not one quotient-groupoid theorem

The two results are instances of one universal-property schema, but their
native categories and generated relations differ.

For parallel arrows `s,t:R=>X` in a category with their coequalizer
`q:X->Q`, a map `f:X->Y` factors uniquely through `q` exactly when
`f s=f t`.

- For syntax, `R` is the equation/congruence data generated by `Theta`, and
  the coequalizer is formed in the relevant category of algebras.  Its models
  are exactly the interpretations equalizing those equations.  Closure under
  the operations is load-bearing.
- For Smith history erasure, `R=G x F` with arrows `(g,z)|->gz` and
  `(g,z)|->z`.  In `Set` the coequalizer is the orbit set.  In `Ab`, after
  linearizing the relation, it is the coinvariant group
  `F/<gz-z>`.  A task descends exactly when it equalizes those arrows.

Thus the common theorem is coequalizer descent.  “Descent along a quotient
groupoid” is too strong and potentially backwards.  The action groupoid
`G//F` retains stabilizers and path witnesses; its set of connected components
forgets them.  A groupoid-valued or witness-sensitive consumer need not factor
through `pi_0(G//F)` even when its object-level endpoint does.  Likewise a raw
set quotient of syntax is not the algebraic quotient unless the relation is a
congruence.

The exact common square is therefore

    generated parallel identifications -> native coequalizer
    consumer equalizes them            <-> unique factorization,

with the category (`Set`, `Ab`, models of a signature, or a higher category)
declared.  This unifies the proofs without identifying their residuals.
