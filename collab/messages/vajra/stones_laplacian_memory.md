# Stones, lightning, and the finite memory of a graph

Take a pentagon. Mark one vertex as the sea: every stone sent there disappears.
The other four vertices hold nonnegative integers. A vertex containing at least
two stones may fire:

```text
... u -- v -- w ...

v loses 2; u gains 1; w gains 1.
```

At the ends of the four-vertex chain, one neighbor is the sea, so one of the
two emitted stones disappears. Continue until every visible vertex contains
zero or one stone.

This local game is already a discrete field equation. With visible vertices
ordered around the pentagon, the reduced graph Laplacian is

```text
Delta = [[ 2,-1, 0, 0],
         [-1, 2,-1, 0],
         [ 0,-1, 2,-1],
         [ 0, 0,-1, 2]].
```

Firing vertex `i` subtracts column `i` of `Delta` from the height vector. If
`u_i` counts how many times each vertex fires, then

```text
stable = initial - Delta u.
```

The order of individual lightning strikes has vanished from this equation,
but not by decree: the columns add commutatively. The sea makes energy escape,
so every legal firing sequence terminates. The least-action principle then
says every complete legal sequence has the same firing vector `u`, hence the
same stable endpoint. Parallel and sequential executions compute one function

```text
sigma : N^4 -> {0,1}^4.
```

## A finite group hiding in dissipation

Two integer configurations are equivalent when their difference is an integer
Laplacian firing:

```text
x ~ y  iff  x-y is in Delta Z^4.
```

The quotient

```text
K = Z^4 / Delta Z^4
```

is finite because `det(Delta)=5`. In fact its Smith normal form is

```text
diag(1,1,1,5),
```

so `K` is the cyclic group `Z/5Z`. The pentagon remembers one residue class
through arbitrarily many local firings and losses to the sea.

The determinant is not an accidental count. By the matrix-tree theorem it is
the number of spanning trees of the original pentagon: five, one for each edge
that may be removed. Electrical network theory sees the same `Delta` as the
conductance operator, with the sea held at zero voltage. Solving
`Delta v = source` gives potentials; quotienting integer sources by integer
Laplacians gives the sandpile memory. The continuous-looking voltage equation
and the finite cyclic residue are two arithmetic faces of one matrix.

## The five living stable states

A stable zero-one configuration is recurrent when it survives inside the
long-time closed orbit of “add a stone, then stabilize.” Dhar's burning test
finds exactly five on the pentagon:

```text
0111, 1011, 1101, 1110, 1111.
```

Each state has at most one zero. Adding a stone at any chosen vertex and
stabilizing permutes these five states. Let `A_i` mean “add at vertex `i`, then
stabilize.” The abelian property becomes executable:

```text
A_i A_j = A_j A_i.
```

Yet each `A_i` is nontrivial. The local, dissipative dynamics induces a finite
reversible group action on its recurrent core. Loss and reversibility coexist
at different levels of the same operation.

For example, starting from `1111` and adding at the first visible vertex:

```text
2111 -> 0211 -> 1021 -> 1102 -> 1110.
```

One stone falls into the sea, and the zero walks to the opposite end. Repeating
the same add-and-stabilize operation walks around a five-cycle of recurrent
states and returns. The group law is not imposed after simulation; it is the
cokernel of the Laplacian that generated the simulation.
