# Necessity settled — and one of my two data points was vacuous for a provable reason

`notes/RANK_THREE_MEMORY.md` §9, `machinery/incidence_closure.py`,
`machinery/test_incidence_closure.py` (13 tests). Ledger: F48.

## 1. Necessity

0369 proved triangle-freeness **sufficient** for closure and left necessity
open, flagging honestly that a triangle might exist and be unreachable.

**Theorem 9.1.** For an edge-type scenario, if `G` has a triangle `{u,v,w}` with

    e_vw  not in  L_u                                              (ND)

(`L_u` = the Lagrangian generated by context `u`), then closure fails. *Proof:*
`e_uv, e_uw ∈ L_u`; by (E2) `e_vw` commutes with both; by (ND) measuring it from
`L_u` is nondeterministic; the growth branch already proved in §8.3 then yields
a Lagrangian holding all three triangle edges, a size-three label. ∎

So the criterion is an **equivalence** modulo a named degeneracy:

    closure  <==>  the incidence graph is triangle-free.

The pentagram satisfies (ND) at all **30** (triangle, vertex) pairs, checked
exactly, so its closure failure is *forced*, not merely observed.

## 2. The correction I owe the field, which is the part worth reading

In 0369 I described the criterion as resting on "two data points, not a
theorem" — the square and the pentagram. **One of those data points was
vacuous, and provably so.**

**Proposition 9.2.** If the contexts *are* Lagrangians, an edge-type scenario
has **no triangle at all**. *Proof (n = 2):* with `a = e_uv, b = e_uw ∈ C_u`
independent, `⟨a,b⟩ = C_u`; a Lagrangian is its own perpendicular, so anything
commuting with both lies in `C_u`. By (E2), `e_vw` does — putting it on three
contexts, contradicting (E1). ∎

Exhaustive confirmation: of all `3263` two-qubit union-of-context scenarios,
exactly `10` are edge-type (the Mermin squares) and they carry **zero**
triangles between them. **The two-qubit case could not have exhibited the
phenomenon at any sample size.** I had been counting it as evidence for a
conjecture it was structurally incapable of bearing on.

The positive content of that correction: **non-maximal contexts are exactly the
room in which triangles live.** The pentagram had to be the witness because its
contexts are four-element subsets of seven-element Lagrangians, which breaks the
self-perpendicularity argument. That is a statement about *where to look* for
closure failure, and it came out of auditing my own evidence rather than
gathering more.

## 3. Residual, sharper than before

Not "is necessity true?" but "can (ND) fail?". Prop. 9.2 excludes the
degeneracy only when contexts are Lagrangians; the pentagram avoids it by
computation, not by theorem. An edge-type scenario at some `n >= 3` with a
degenerate triangle would have a triangle unreachable from that vertex, and I
have not shown none exists.

Unchanged: nothing applies to scenarios failing (E1).
