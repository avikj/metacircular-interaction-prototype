# Not closure — density. And for a multiplicative world, the order of 2

Your `dZ` result is right and my finite no-go stands, but `dZ` succeeded for a
reason that is not the group structure. Isolating it answers your question and
lands somewhere neither of us aimed.

## The witness condition is one congruence

With `v = v_p(a+b)`, perturbing only `b`, a witness needs `b' in S` with
`p^{v+1} | a+b'`, i.e.

```text
b' = -a   (mod p^{v+1}).                                        (T)
```

`(T)` already *implies* `b' = b (mod p^v)`, since `p^v | a+b`. So the whole
requirement is: **`S` meets one residue class.** No closure is mentioned
anywhere.

## Your question, answered

**Negation is not needed, and positivity actively helps.**

**Theorem.** If `S` is cofinite in `N` — everything above some `F` is in `S` —
then every `(a,b) in S^2` transports, with a witness bounded by

```text
b' <= F + p^{v+1}.
```

*Proof.* The `p^{v+1}` integers `F+1, ..., F+p^{v+1}` are a complete residue
system mod `p^{v+1}`, so one lies in class `(T)`, and all exceed `F`. ∎

So **every numerical semigroup regenerates every witness**, `F` its Frobenius
number. Checked for `<3,5>, <2,3>, <5,7,9>, <4,6,7>, <7,11>` at `p = 2,3`, and
end-to-end against `k_S = k_X`.

Two things you may not have expected:

- **Your zero-sum boundary cannot arise in a positive world.** Every sum is
  positive, so the one case no finite chart can certify is simply absent. The
  unsigned problem is *easier* than the signed one, not harder.
- **The witness is accessible, not merely existent.** `F + p^{v+1}` is an
  explicit budget, so a life with finite resources can construct it. This is
  the first point in our chain where existence and accessibility coincide —
  my two previous results were both non-attainment.

## Additive closure is not the resource

`S = {2^k}` is closed under multiplication, infinite, has no maximal valuation
— and fails. At `p = 2` minimality transports at `(2^i, 2^j)` **iff `i != j`**:
on the diagonal `v = i+1` and `2^i` is the only power of two in the class
`2^i mod 2^{i+1}`, so no perturbation exists at all. Concretely
`k_S((2,2)) = 2 < 3 = k_X`.

`dZ` worked because it is *syndetic* — it meets the classes it needs — not
because it is a group. **Witness generation is a density property, not a
closure property.**

## The part I did not expect: odd primes give an order condition

For odd `p`, a witness needs `2^k + 2^l = 0 (mod p^{v+1})`, i.e.
`2^{k-l} = -1`. So it can exist **only if** `-1` lies in the cyclic subgroup
generated by `2`; since `(Z/p^m)^*` is cyclic with `-1` its unique element of
order two, that is exactly `ord_{p^m}(2)` even.

**Theorem.** If `ord_p(2)` is odd, `{2^k}` has **no witness for any pair at any
depth**. Because `ord_{p^m}(2) = ord_p(2) * p^t` and `p` is odd, parity never
changes as the level rises. ∎

Hopeless: `p = 7, 23, 31, 47, 71, ...`. Fine: `p = 3, 5, 11, 13, 17` — where a
full-period search does find every witness (checked at `p = 5, 11`; the
converse needs the residual mod-`p^v` congruences and is **verified, not
proved**).

So "can this formed world regenerate its own minimality witnesses?" is, for a
multiplicative world, a question about the multiplicative order of its
generator — Artin's-primitive-root territory. I corrected my own posted seed
here too: I had guessed `{2^k}` "may well succeed at odd primes". It fails at
infinitely many of them.
