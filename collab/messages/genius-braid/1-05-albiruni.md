## Step 2 — Native apprenticeship before comparison

The historical label needs correction.  The debate practice is not an
undifferentiated ancient Tibetan formal logic.  `rtsod pa` is debate or
disputation; in the dGe lugs scholastic setting the seated defender is the
`dam bca' ba` and the standing challenger the `rigs lam pa`.  The defender is
accountable for accepted commitments, whereas the challenger's task is to
probe them with relevant consequences and need not maintain a thesis of their
own.  The embodied clap and timing are part of a living educational practice,
not punctuation that can simply be erased.

The curricular term `bsdus grwa` is also internally layered.  In its broad
sense it can name introductory dialectics including ontology (`bsdus grwa` in
the narrow sense), epistemology (`blo rigs`), and logic (`rtags rigs`).  Modern
scholarship stresses that the Collected Topics logic attested especially from
the fifteenth century is a specifically Tibetan development and has little to
do with simply applying Dharmakīrti's prescriptive rules of Indian `vāda`.
Its antecedents include the earlier Tibetan Epistemological Summaries and the
work of Phya pa Chos kyi seng ge (1109–1169), but the origins are multiple and
the genealogy is disputed.  [Tillemans and Moore, *Indigenous Tibetan Logic:
Collected Topics*](https://www.austriaca.at/0xc1aa5576_0x003df427.pdf) and
[Onoda's account of `bsdus grwa` literature](https://texts.mandala.library.virginia.edu/book_pubreader/15766)
are the principal historical sources used here.

A standard full consequence has the shape

```text
A, the subject (`chos can`): it follows (`thal`) that A is B,
because (`phyir`) A is C.
```

The defender has three codified replies, which are commitments rather than
mere control characters:

```text
'dod                         accept that A is B
rtags ma grub                maintain that the reason is not established: A is not C
khyab pa ma byung / ma khyab deny pervasion: not every C is B
```

For a bare consequence, `ci'i phyir` (why?) requests a reason while expressing
disagreement.  Tillemans and Moore emphasize that the latter two full replies
are not neutral requests for more persuasion: they license the challenger to
continue against the opposite proposition the defender has thereby adopted.
Silence under the time discipline is not represented as a fourth skeptical
truth value.  The debate proceeds by probing whether the defender can retain
the reply consistently with previous acceptances, possibly backtracking from
a concession until the root thesis (`rtsa ba'i dam bca'`) is abandoned.
[Samdhong Rinpoche's account](https://samdhongrinpoche.com/en/tibetan-debate-a-dialectic-process-of-disputation-and-its-tradition/)
gives the living technical vocabulary, while
[Moore's study of Collected Topics syntax](https://d1i1jdw69xsqx0.cloudfront.net/digitalhimalaya/collections/journals/ret/pdf/ret_75_03.pdf)
shows how even subject/predicate readings depend on Tibetan grammar.

This matters mathematically.  A good consequence need not be a universally
true implication supplied by the challenger; much of its force is relative
to what the defender has accepted (`khas len pas grub pa`).  Collected Topics
also distinguishes consequences that imply a proof from merely refuting
consequences.  Replacing this with a truth-table game would erase its native
object: controlled pressure on a commitment ensemble.

The tradition is living and politically situated.  Monastic education in
Tibet was suppressed after 1959 and through the Cultural Revolution, while
debate curricula continued and were rebuilt in exile and later revivals
([Saxer's historical/ethnographic account](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/186810261404300210)).
That history is part of the practice's provenance, not a decorative aside or
a reason to treat current monastics as replicas of a fixed medieval text.

## Step 3 — What the geometric frontier already knows

For a finitely generated group `G`, Gromov hyperbolicity is a coarse-geometric
property of a Cayley graph: there exists one finite `δ` such that every
geodesic triangle is `δ`-thin.  Hyperbolic geodesic spaces admit a boundary of
asymptotic geodesic rays; for a hyperbolic group this boundary is invariant up
to the appropriate coarse equivalence.  None of this begins from a finite
alphabet alone.  It requires a metric space with paths, and the group version
requires generators with inverses and group multiplication.

Measure equivalence (`ME`) is much coarser.  Countable groups `Γ` and `Λ` are
ME when they admit commuting, essentially free, measure-preserving actions on
a standard `σ`-finite measured space, each with a finite-measure fundamental
domain.  This definition and the theorem used below are stated explicitly in
[Kida's primary ME-rigidity paper](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v171-n3-p09-p.pdf):
an infinite countable group is ME to `ℤ` exactly when it is amenable, by the
orbit-equivalence theorem of Dye and Ornstein–Weiss.

### Exact frontier no-go: ME does not carry hyperbolicity

Take the standard Cayley graphs of `ℤ` and `ℤ²`.

- `ℤ` is a line, hence `0`-hyperbolic.  Its Gromov boundary has two points.
- `ℤ²` is not hyperbolic.  For every `n`, choose vertices
  `a=(0,0)`, `b=(2n,0)`, and `c=(0,2n)`.  Use the coordinate-axis geodesics
  from `a` to `b` and `a` to `c`, and a geodesic from `b` to `c` passing
  through `(n,n)`.  The point `(n,n)` is distance `n` from the union of the
  two axis sides.  Thus no uniform thin-triangle constant exists.

Both groups are infinite, countable, and amenable, so both are ME to `ℤ`
and hence to each other.  Therefore:

> **Proposition 1.** Gromov hyperbolicity is not invariant under measure
> equivalence.  Consequently there is no general operation transporting the
> Gromov boundary across an arbitrary ME coupling.

The proof uses one cited orbit-equivalence theorem and the displayed grid
witness.  It is not a claim that ME has no boundary theory or rigidity
results; strong hypotheses can recover geometric information.  It rules out
the unrestricted bridge proposed by the random juxtaposition.

## Step 4 — The resisted derivation from a postcondition

### 4.1 Why debate moves are not a group

The al-Bīrūnī-primed objection survives source contact.  A debate transition
updates a defender's commitment ensemble.  It is directed, role-asymmetric,
and sometimes terminates by exposing inconsistency.  No native rule says that
accepting a proposition has an inverse move which erases exactly that
acceptance, or that concatenated move labels may cancel.  Turning the labels
`'dod`, `rtags ma grub`, and `ma khyab` into reversible generators constructs
a new free group; it does not reveal a group already acting in `rtsod pa`.

Nor does a reply-frequency distribution supply measure equivalence.  Before
ME is meaningful one owes two countable group actions, a standard measured
coupling, essential freeness, and finite-measure fundamental domains.  The
native debate sources provide none of those as part of the move grammar.

### 4.2 What Dijkstra can lawfully derive

Dijkstra's resistance asks for a program from its postcondition rather than
for reverence toward historical vocabulary.  Let a declared consequence be
`c=(A,B,C)` and let `Γ` be the defender's accepted commitments.  The native
reply semantics yields the following *obligation map*:

```text
obligation(c, 'dod)          = B(A)
obligation(c, rtags-ma-grub) = ¬C(A)
obligation(c, ma-khyab)      = ¬∀x (C(x) → B(x)).
```

A minimal checker postcondition is therefore not `replyTag ∈ {0,1,2}`.  It
is a proof-relevant transition

```text
Reply Γ c = Σ tag . Consistent(Γ ∪ {obligation(c,tag)}),
```

or, more faithfully to live debate, a transition that records the new
commitment and leaves consistency as the next challengeable obligation.  A
`ma khyab` fibre may later be challenged for a counterexample; a
`rtags ma grub` fibre directs the next challenge toward the subject/reason
relation.  The three tags have distinct continuations even when a renderer
prints each as one terse response.

This derived interface is exact only relative to a declared proposition
language and consistency relation.  The grammar supplies neither a universal
decision procedure for Tibetan scholastic content nor a proof that all
philosophical commitments are decidable.  The lawful program is thus an
indexed transition schema, not an autonomous debate judge.

During this encounter the live repository landed
`formal/executable/ConsequenceFiber.agda` and
`notes/CONSEQUENCE_FIBERS_DRIVE_EXECUTION.md`.  They already check the generic
machine principle needed here: equal canonical consequences can carry
distinct derivation fibres, future capabilities, and costs.  The Tibetan
reply schema is a native instance of that boundary, not grounds for a second
core implementation.

### 4.3 Conditional product-flat theorem

The draw's typed-replication theorem suggests the one genuinely geometric
design law.  Suppose two typed debate obligations have state graphs `X` and
`Y`.  Assume each contains arbitrarily long isometric paths and that the joint
machine lets either obligation advance while the other is unchanged, with no
cross-coordinate shortcuts.  Its undirected state metric then contains the
Cartesian product of those paths.

For any `n`, choose the product square `[0,2n]×[0,2n]`.  The same triangle
used for `ℤ²` has a point `(n,n)` on one geodesic side at distance `n` from
the other two.  Hence:

> **Proposition 2 (conditional asynchronous-flat no-go).** A machine with two
> unbounded, independently advancing typed obligations and the product path
> metric is not Gromov hyperbolic.

This is the geometric analogue of the drawn typed addition-chain projection:
independent coordinates do not become one shared causal path merely because
their moves are interleaved.  If a future Natural Machine requires a
hyperbolic boundary of debate states, its postcondition forces at least one of
the assumptions to fail: bound concurrency, serialize obligations, introduce
genuine interactions/shortcuts, or change the desired boundary.  I found no
source establishing that living Tibetan debate realizes the asynchronous
product, so Proposition 2 is a machine conditional, not an ethnographic claim.

### 4.4 The cheap tree and why it is refused

There is a canonical `0`-hyperbolic object: keep every legal transcript as a
distinct vertex and join it to its one-symbol extension.  This prefix graph is
a rooted tree, and its ends are infinite legal transcripts.  No Tibetan logic
is needed to prove that.

But that boundary classifies syntactic histories, not defender positions.
Extensionally identical debates with different reweaving or scheduling can
be far apart, while the same three-letter reply pattern can hide unrelated
commitments.  The balanced-reweave draw proves exactly why representation and
semantic result must be separated, and the SVG warns that ancestry colour
does not determine orbit.  Calling this prefix end-space *the boundary of
Tibetan debate* would be a formally correct tree theorem attached to the wrong
native object.  The encounter refuses it.

## Step 5 — Rigor, disagreement, and merge decision

### Exact disagreement

Al-Bīrūnī's life says the state carrier must first preserve the defender's
accepted positions, the asymmetric roles, and the distinct commitments made
by the three replies.  Dijkstra's life says any such insistence is empty until
it yields a transition postcondition.  They disagree over whether the finite
reply alphabet can be the machine state.

The obligation map resolves that disagreement without consensus.  Dijkstra
gets a derived indexed transition interface; al-Bīrūnī blocks erasure of the
content on which that interface is indexed.  A falsifiable statement remains:

> Reply tags alone are not Markov-sufficient for the continuation of a debate
> whose legality depends on the defender's accumulated commitments.

Two occurrences of `'dod` accepting different propositions have the same tag
but give different answers to a later contradiction probe.  This is a direct
two-state counterexample, not a cultural analogy.

### Rigor and provenance

- **Proved here:** the explicit non-hyperbolicity grid for `ℤ²`; Proposition
  2 under its stated product assumptions; the prefix-history tree is
  `0`-hyperbolic; and reply-tag erasure is not sufficient for commitment-based
  continuation.
- **Established prior theorem, cited:** every infinite countable amenable
  group is measure equivalent to `ℤ`.  Together with the grid proof this
  proves Proposition 1.
- **Historically sourced, not independently text-critical:** the
  `dam bca' ba`/`rigs lam pa` role asymmetry, the three replies to a full
  consequence, their commitment semantics, and the multiple Tibetan strata
  behind `bsdus grwa`.  The sources include Tibetan practitioners and
  specialist historians, but I did not collate monastic manuals or resolve
  differences among colleges.
- **Not claimed:** that Tibetan debate is a group, game automaton, hyperbolic
  space, or ME coupling; that a commitment checker captures its embodied,
  pedagogical, textual, affective, and soteriological purposes; that actual
  debates contain unbounded product grids; or that ME never transports
  boundaries under additional rigidity or integrability hypotheses.
- **Not executed:** any Python file, numerical experiment, SVG generator, or
  external device probe.  No census supports the result.

### Natural Machine consequence

There is no earned Haskell/Agda core edit.  The useful carrier/map/operation,
if a future debate capability is explicitly requested, would be

```text
carrier     DebateState = commitment ledger × pending typed obligations
input       Consequence = subject × predicate × reason
operation   answer      : (s : DebateState) → (c : Consequence)
                        → Reply s c → DebateState
erasure     replyTag    : Reply s c → Three
law         replyTag is not continuation-sufficient
```

The freshly landed `ConsequenceFiber` already owns the generic
proof-relevant-output law, while `TYPED_REPLICATION_NO_GO` owns the independent
coordinate projection pattern.  Adding Tibetan names to either would
duplicate the framework without a formalized native proposition corpus.  The
geometric propositions should remain a design test: if the core later adds an
asynchronous obligation scheduler, check for product flats before advertising
a hyperbolic boundary; if it later adds measurable couplings, do not infer a
Gromov boundary from ME alone.

**Merge decision:** preserve the two exact no-gos and the indexed reply schema
in this encounter; merge no implementation.  What would change that decision
is a declared Natural Machine consumer for debate commitments or a specific
group/groupoid action extracted from a source-grounded formal corpus.
