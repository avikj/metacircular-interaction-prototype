## Step 2 — Learn the instrument traditions before optimizing them

The three named traditions do not present one generic “ancient analog
computer.” Their native objects and uses differ.

### Planispheric astrolabe

An astrolabe composes an alidade measurement with a rotating `rete` carrying
an ecliptic and star pointers over a `tympanum` or climate engraved for a local
latitude. The plate therefore is not dispensable context: altitude/azimuth
circles encode a particular local horizon. Oxford's instrument guide gives
this component-level account and lists time, solar, trigonometric, prayer, and
qibla uses on different scales ([History of Science Museum, parts of the
astrolabe](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/students/03to04/Astrolabes/Starholder_parts.html)).
Its spherical astrolabe carries 19 bright-star pointers and solves astronomy
and time-telling problems by rotating the rete ([Oxford spherical
astrolabe](https://www.hsm.ox.ac.uk/spherical-astrolabe)); another Jaipur
instrument has 35 named stars but a projection fixed to latitude 27 degrees
([Oxford Jaipur astrolabe](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/astrolabe/exhibition/largest.html)).

Abu Rayhan al-Biruni's two astrolabe treatises survive in multiple Arabic
manuscripts. The Utrecht work provides a critical Arabic edition and English
translation rather than relying on a retrospective European paraphrase
([Rezvani, *Two Treatises on the Astrolabe by Abu Rayhan Muhammad ibn Ahmad
Biruni*](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/381551)). Its titles already
preserve the operational aim: “Opening the Way towards Using the Different
Kinds of Astrolabes” and “Bringing what is in the Potentiality of the
Astrolabe into Actuality” ([critical-edition review](https://journals.openedition.org/abstractairanica/55257?lang=en)).

Chaucer's later English treatise is useful evidence of transmitted operation,
not a substitute for the Arabic tradition. Its editor notes that not all star
names fit on the rete and directs the user to choose a represented star for a
lunar comparison ([Skeat commentary](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Complete_Works_of_Geoffrey_Chaucer/Volume_3/Treatise_on_the_Astrolabe_Commentary)).
This is evidence of a material/graphic limit and task dependence. It is not a
documented pairwise collision graph, a star-coverage table, or an objective
optimized by a maker. The forecast's proposed conflict graph therefore fails
its own provenance test.

### Sanskrit `gola` / `gola-yantra`

Bhaskara I's commentary on the `Gola` chapter of the *Aryabhatiya* gives a
construction because the device helps explain Aryabhata's spherics. The
Sanskrit `gola` can name spherics, a celestial sphere, or an armillary sphere;
those meanings should not be collapsed. The described Indian instrument is
substantially different from the Greek form and is set according to latitude
([Peng Lu, “Bhaskara I on the Construction of the Armillary
Sphere”](https://www.wisdomlib.org/uploads/journals/hssa/3_1_1.pdf)). Its
rings materialize a coordinate geometry and teaching/observational practice.
They are not a menu of independent binary sensors. Replacing a ring can alter
the coordinate interface itself, which is a stronger dependency than ordinary
set selection.

### `Zhou bi` gnomon computation

The *Zhou bi suan jing* uses a vertical gnomon and its shadow in numerical
cosmography, orientation, and seasonal observation. Its eight-`chi` gnomon,
solstitial shadows, shadow-distance rule, and similar-triangle calculation
belong to a declared geometric model, not to Boolean membership tests. Cullen's
critical study and translation explicitly includes the translation, a sun-
height appendix, and the old shadow table ([Cambridge edition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/astronomy-and-mathematics-in-ancient-china/FA899B1F1C95108D3C914E7048698A0C)).
The base text describes erecting the gnomon at sunrise and sunset so the two
shadow endpoints determine east-west and their bisection determines
north-south ([Chinese Text Project, volume 2](https://ctext.org/zhou-bi-suan-jing/juan-xia)).

A shadow length is a ratio-bearing measurement with time, location,
orientation, calibration, and cosmological assumptions. Thresholding it into
an invented yes/no probe changes both the observation map and its error model.
No selected source specifies a finite library of shadow tests, a monetary or
material cost, or a global cover objective. Thus the gnomon route supplies a
model-sensitive observation interface but no historical test-cover instance.
