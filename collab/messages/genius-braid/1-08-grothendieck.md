## Step 2 — Native apprenticeship before comparison

### 2.1 Anekantavada, naya, and syadvada

The three terms should not be collapsed. `Anekantavada` concerns the
non-one-sided or multiplex character of reality; a `naya` is a partial
standpoint or mode of determination; `syat` qualifies an assertion rather
than merely expressing conversational uncertainty. `Saptabhangi` is the
sevenfold schema of qualified predication.

Samantabhadra's *Aptamimamsa* 14--16 gives a particularly important internal
structure. The seven forms are conventionally displayed as qualified
existence, qualified non-existence, their successive combination,
inexpressibility, and the three combinations of inexpressibility with the
first three. Verse 15 makes the qualification concrete: existence is said
with respect to an object's own substance, place, time, and mode
(`sva-dravya`, `sva-ksetra`, `sva-kala`, `sva-bhava`), and non-existence with
respect to the corresponding other-quaternion. Verse 16 distinguishes
successive assertion of existence and non-existence from their simultaneous
expression, whose linguistic impossibility motivates the inexpressible form.
The modern English edition consulted is [Vijay K. Jain's translation of
*Aptamimamsa*](https://jainqq.org/booktext/Aptamimansa_Romanized/007744),
especially verses 14--16. It is a translated primary text, not a critical
philological adjudication of every Jain lineage or later commentary.

This immediately blocks two casual reductions. First, the schema is not
licensed as seven context-free truth values merely because it has seven
members. Second, `avaktavya` cannot be silently identified with a third atomic
truth value: in this passage it records a limit of simultaneous expression,
while `ubhaya` uses succession. A representation that remembers only which of
three labels occurred loses the temporal/linguistic distinction that helps
generate the list.

### 2.2 The genuinely algorithmic Jain object is elsewhere

The assigned phrase “karma combinatorics” also needs a location correction.
Nemicandra's *Gommatasara* has `Jivakanda` and `Karmakanda` sections, but the
specific tuple algorithm used here occurs in `Jivakanda` verses 35--42 during
the analysis of `pramada` (carelessness), within the treatise's wider account
of spiritual stages and karma. It is not a computation extracted from the
seven predications.

Jadhav and Jain's source-based mathematical study identifies five native
tools:

```text
sankhya       total number of tuples
prastara      ordered spreading/enumeration of the tuples
parivartana   rotation schedule while forming them
nasta         recover the lost tuple from its position
uddista       recover the position of a stated tuple
```

The carrier is a Cartesian product of finite ordered sets, selecting one
element from each set. The study stresses that these forms are tuples, not
permutations, combinations, variations, or partitions. It also records a
historical transformation rather than a timeless package: prosodic
`pratyaya` tools were adapted, `la-ga-kriya` and `adhvayoga` were omitted, and
`parivartana` was added for this tuple problem. See Dipak Jadhav and Anupam
Jain, [“Combinatorics as Found in the Gommatasara of Nemichandra”](https://doi.org/10.16943/ijhs/2016/v51i2/48431),
*Indian Journal of History of Science* 51.2.1 (2016), 190--205.

This is the ancient field's strongest mathematical return: an enumeration,
ranking, and unranking practice tied to a doctrinal classification problem.
It is not evidence that Jain authors possessed operads, and category theory
does not explain away the native distinction among the five tools.

## Step 3 — What the categorical frontier already knows

A colored operad (multicategory) requires more than a family of labels. For
colors `c`, it supplies operation sets

```text
O(c1,...,cn;c)
```

together with typed substitution, one unit in `O(c;c)` for each color,
associativity, and, in the symmetric case, compatible permutation actions.
In particular, `O(c;c)` is a monoid under unary substitution. This is standard
in Tom Leinster's [*Higher Operads, Higher Categories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305049),
where an operad is a one-object multicategory.

A symmetric monoidal category provides a different computational grammar.
Objects label wires; morphisms label boxes; categorical composition connects
matching output/input wires; tensor puts processes in parallel; associators,
unitors, and symmetries govern regrouping and interchange. Joyal and Street's
[“The Geometry of Tensor Calculus I”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(91)90003-P)
establishes the rigorous graphical calculus. A string diagram is therefore a
typed term modulo coherence, not an image that acquires a composite merely
because its boxes are adjacent.

The finite-tuple algorithm has an exact elementary categorical home. For
positive radices `m1,...,mn`, put

```text
Tuple(m) = Fin(m1) x ... x Fin(mn)
M        = product_i mi.
```

With zero-based digits ordered lexicographically, define

```text
rank(a1,...,an) = sum_i ai * product_{j>i} mj.
```

Repeated Euclidean quotient and remainder give `unrank : Fin(M) -> Tuple(m)`.
The division algorithm proves

```text
unrank(rank(a)) = a
rank(unrank(k)) = k.
```

Thus `Tuple(m) ≃ Fin(M)`. For two factors the map
`mu(a,b)=a*n+b : Fin(m) x Fin(n) -> Fin(m*n)` is the tensor comparison; the
iterated `rank` is its `n`-ary composite. `prastara` enumerates `unrank`,
`nasta` is unranking, `uddista` is ranking, and `sankhya` is `M` at this modern
translation boundary. Parenthesization and reordering demand the ordinary
monoidal coherence maps and change the chosen enumeration order.

This is established mixed-radix mathematics, not a novelty claim. Its value
here is bidirectional: Nemicandra's algorithm gives concrete rank/unrank work
that the phrase “monoidal coherence” does not compute by itself; category
theory states exactly what must commute when those algorithms are regrouped,
composed, or permuted.

## Step 4 — Grothendieck rises; Tarski refuses

### 4.1 Exact no-go for the tempting seven-operation operad

As a deliberately coarse modern code, let

```text
Mode = {asti, nasti, avaktavya}
B    = P(Mode) minus {empty}.
```

Then `B` has seven elements, corresponding at the level of incidence to the
three singleton forms, their three two-element combinations, and their
three-element combination. Suppose the “composition” of predications is
taken to be union. Union on `B` is associative, commutative, and idempotent,
but it has no unit:

> **Proposition 1 (hand proof).** There is no `E in B` such that
> `E union S = S` for every `S in B`.
>
> **Proof.** Applying the equation to each singleton forces `E` to be a
> subset of every singleton of `Mode`. Their intersection is empty, so
> `E=empty`, contradicting `E in B`. QED.

Since the unary operations at any color of a unital colored operad form a
monoid under substitution, it follows that:

> **Corollary 1.1.** The seven-element incidence code `B`, with substitution
> defined as union, cannot be exactly the unary endomorphism object of a
> unital operad.

Adding the empty subset repairs the unit but creates an eighth form not in the
sevenfold schema. Choosing an unrelated monoid law—for example a cyclic law
on a seven-element set—would prove only that some operad can be imposed on the
carrier. It would not source that law from syadvada. Nor does the subset code
retain the verse 16 distinction between successive assertion and simultaneous
inexpressibility. The corollary is intentionally narrow: it does **not** prove
that no faithful operadic semantics of qualified predication can ever be
constructed.

### 4.2 Tarski locates the type error

Tarski's object-language/metalanguage distinction makes the residual exact.
An object language may contain qualified sentence forms. A semantic account
must separately specify the language's syntax, a domain/interpretation, the
context parameters, and a satisfaction or truth predicate in a sufficiently
rich metalanguage. Tarski's own statement of this separation is in [“The
Semantic Conception of Truth and the Foundations of Semantics”](https://www.jfsowa.com/logic/tarski.htm)
(1944).

For this encounter the minimum honest signature would distinguish at least

```text
Sentence       object-language expressions
Context        substance/place/time/mode and sequencing conditions
Bhanga         one of the seven qualified forms
qualify        Context -> Sentence -> Bhanga -> QualifiedSentence
satisfies      Interpretation -> Context -> QualifiedSentence -> Type
```

The last line is schematic modern metalanguage, not a recovered Jain formal
system. It shows why the number seven is insufficient: neither the typing of
`Context`, the semantic relation, nor a substitution law follows from the
cardinality of `Bhanga`.

The exact disagreement is therefore this:

- Grothendieck asks whether the seven forms can be raised into a common
  compositional geometry.
- Tarski asks whether those forms are operations inside the object language,
  or sentence forms whose qualification and correctness are stated in a
  metalanguage.

The sources support the latter typing before they support the former
composition. A future source could falsify this result by supplying native
units and typed substitution laws; none of the consulted material does.

### 4.3 The positive joint does not require an operad

The `Gommatasara` tuple algorithms do supply a real string-diagram box:

```text
Tuple(m)  --rank-->    Fin(M)
Tuple(m)  <--unrank--  Fin(M)
```

with both round trips equal to identity. Tensoring and composing these
bijections is well typed. But this is a monoidal equivalence for finite
products, not an operad of the seven predications. The distinction is the
encounter's main gain: Jain qualified predication yields a semantic typing
obligation, while Jain tuple combinatorics yields an executable equivalence.
They should not be fused merely because both admit the number seven in some
examples.

### Qualified predication as an interface

Let a full assertion state retain `(context, sentence, qualification)` and let
`q` forget everything except its sevenfold label. This is a coarsening, not an
equivalence. The checked prior `NaturalMachine.DSOOption` states that if
`q2 = h o q1`, every task factoring through `q2` already factors through the
finer `q1`; coarsening cannot create exact task support. Thus retaining the
standpoint has weakly greater option value for any declared family measured
only by exact factorization.

That theorem does not manufacture a Jain continuation cost. The sources give
no cost relation between bhangas, no Bellman continuation, no declared target
family, and no architecture metric. Therefore there is no earned numerical
contextual dominance, regret, curvature, or option-value scalar here. Taking
an infimum over bare bhanga labels before retaining the context witness would
be precisely the premature-collapse shape warned against by
`DSOFinite`/`DSOArchitecture`, but resemblance of shapes is not an
instantiation.

The live Delta 26 abstract continuation module sharpens this refusal. Its
extended-natural `Cost` makes infinity structural unreachability, its
`Argmin` retains the active intermediate witness, and its Dirac continuations
reconstruct every entry of a finite cost relation. Consequently equality for
one chosen optimum is not full abstraction: equality against **all**
continuations is strong enough to force equality of the underlying relation.
A sevenfold label is therefore a valid DSO interface only after the whole
contextual cost relation is proved to descend through it; matching one
predication or one continuation value is insufficient.

### Mixed-radix rank as a lossless interface

The tuple joint behaves differently because `rank` has the explicit inverse
`unrank`. For every task `f : Tuple(m) -> Y`, the decoder

```text
g = f o unrank : Fin(M) -> Y
```

witnesses exact factorization `f = g o rank`; conversely `rank` itself is a
task on tuples. Hence the two interfaces support exactly the same extensional
task family. Any continuation or cost relation can be transported along the
equivalence while retaining the round-trip witness. Infimal composition then
agrees after transport **only when** the conversion costs are included or
proved irrelevant. Neither present-cost superiority nor runtime speedup
follows from an equivalence alone.

At the proof-relevant level, an active intermediate tuple is carried to the
active index `rank(a)` and recovered by `unrank`; no argmin witness is deleted.
At the fully abstract level, transport conjugates the entire relation and all
continuations, rather than merely preserving one scalar minimum. This is the
condition under which the Delta 26 continuation transformer treats the two
interfaces as a relabeling rather than an optimization shortcut.

This gives zero information-theoretic architecture regret for replacing the
tuple carrier by its ranked carrier, but not zero implementation cost. Delta
26's architecture curvature cannot be computed because no architecture
family, perturbation, or cost functional was supplied by the Jain sources.
The option-value result is exact but standard: equivalence preserves all
factorable tasks.

The live dependent-optimizer fibration supplies one further boundary. A
single fixed ordering of factors gives one lossless `rank` equivalence. A
family of architecture-specific factor orders, parenthesizations, or local
enumeration conventions does **not** thereby give a coherent global optimizer:
one must provide the overlap comparisons and show that the selected points
glue, or retain them as a dependent cover. The Jain tuple source supplies the
fixed algorithms but no architecture-indexed cost family, so no optimizer
tear, regret, or curvature instance is claimed.

**Delta 26 verdict:** the merge decision is unchanged. It strengthens the
refusal to optimize qualified assertions after forgetting their standpoint,
and it classifies mixed-radix rank/unrank as a safe lossless interface. It
does not contribute a new cost model, continuation, or Natural Machine theorem.

No core edit was made.

The exact positive candidate would be

```text

map:       rank : Tuple(m) -> Fin(product_i mi)
inverse:   unrank
```

support, not decoration.

### Encounter consequence

The generality earned by this door is a separation, not a synthesis:

1. a finite list of qualified sentence forms is not yet a compositional
   algebra;
2. an adapted tuple-enumeration algorithm is an exact computation and admits
   a monoidal rank/unrank presentation;
3. a lossless equivalence may be transported through DSO continuations with
   witnesses retained, while a context-forgetting label map may not be
   optimized as though it were lossless.

That is the ideal-state consequence for the Natural Machine: keep the
language/metalanguage boundary explicit, admit string-diagram computation only
after typed composition exists, and do not spend a culturally specific name on
a generic interface theorem already present in the core.
