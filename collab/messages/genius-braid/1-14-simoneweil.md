## Step 2 — Native historical correction before comparison

### Attribution, period, and genre

Madhava (usually dated about 1340–1425) is credited within the later Kerala
tradition with the arctangent/`pi`, sine, and cosine series and with endpoint
corrections. But the important series material is known chiefly through verses
cited by later authors; the few works of Madhava that survive directly are
astronomical. M. D. Srinivas states this transmission boundary explicitly and
identifies the later textual line ([“Kerala School of Astronomy and
Mathematics”](https://iks.iitgn.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Kerala-School-of-Astronomy-and-Mathematics-MD-Srinivas-2012.pdf)).

The relevant witnesses therefore have distinct dates and genres:

- Nilakantha's Sanskrit `Tantrasangraha` was completed around 1500/1501.
- Jyeshthadeva's `Ganita-Yuktibhasa`, about 1530, is Malayalam prose organized
  around `yukti`, rationales for mathematical astronomy. The modern critical
  translation is by K. V. Sarma with explanatory notes by K. Ramasubramanian,
  M. D. Srinivas, and M. S. Sriram ([Springer edition](https://link.springer.com/book/9781848820722)).
- Sankara Variyar's `Yuktidipika` and `Laghuvivrtti` comment on the
  `Tantrasangraha`. Their chapter 2 verses preserve the explicit endpoint
  correction prescriptions later attributed to Madhava.

This encounter therefore says “corrections attributed to Madhava and preserved
in sixteenth-century Kerala texts,” not “a surviving Madhava manuscript proves
the following modern theorem.” It also makes no European-transmission claim.

### The native computational object: endpoint correction and `sthaulya`

For an odd endpoint `p=2n-1`, the transmitted rule corrects the truncated
alternating circumference series by a rational function of the endpoint. The
second correction is

```text
f2(p) = 1 / ((2p+2) + 4/(2p+2)),
F2(n) = f2(2n-1) = n/(4n^2+1).
```

The `Yuktidipika-Laghuvivrtti` verses say to apply this last addition or
subtraction according to the sign of the final series term, and explicitly
say it is more accurate than simply continuing the slow series. The same
commentary gives a third, subtler correction

```text
F3(n) = (n^2+1)/(n(4n^2+5)).
```

The source/argument boundary matters. The Kerala texts do not separately
state the first correction; it appears inside the rationale for the second.
The modern critical study finds gaps in the transmitted rationale for the
second and no Kerala rationale for the third, even though both formulas are
textually attested ([Krishnachandran, correction terms, §§4 and
6](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11134)). Formula, efficacy, and rationale are
three different grades.

The native error-facing quantity is not merely a decimal comparison. For a
candidate endpoint correction `f`, Kerala texts call

```text
I(p) = f(p-2) + f(p) - 1/p
```

`sthaulya`, a measure of the correction's inaccuracy. For the second and third
corrections the exact rational defects are

```text
I2(p) = -4/(p^5+4p),
I3(p) = 36/(p^7+7p^5+28p^3-36p),       odd p >= 3.
```

These identities and the terminology are documented in the critical study
([`sthaulya` and exact defects](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11134)). The study
also stresses that the historical rationale does not meet every modern proof
demand. I retain both facts: Kerala computation attached an explicit local
inaccuracy test to a truncation correction; modern global error certification
still needs the recurrence, monotonicity, and limiting boundary.

## Step 3 — Two exact computations and the point where they diverge

### A. `sthaulya` is exactly the next corrected increment

Let

```text
S_n = sum_{j=0}^{n-1} (-1)^j/(2j+1)
A_n = S_n + (-1)^n F_n,
```

where `F_n=f(2n-1)` is any candidate endpoint correction. Define the next
`sthaulya`

```text
I_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n+1} - 1/(2n+1).
```

**Theorem 1 (exact defect transport).** For every `n >= 1`,

```text
A_{n+1} - A_n = (-1)^(n+1) I_{n+1}.
```

**Proof.** The uncorrected partial sums satisfy
`S_{n+1}-S_n=(-1)^n/(2n+1)`. Subtracting the two endpoint corrections gives

```text
A_{n+1}-A_n
 = (-1)^n(1/(2n+1)-F_n-F_{n+1})
 = (-1)^(n+1) I_{n+1}.
```

Nothing asymptotic occurs. Thus the native local inaccuracy quantity is
exactly the update of the corrected computation, after a sign.

If `I_n` has fixed sign, `|I_n|` decreases to zero, and `F_n -> 0`, the
corrected values form an alternating convergent sequence with limit `pi/4`,
and the alternating-series certificate gives

```text
|pi/4 - A_n| <= |I_{n+1}|.
```

For the second transmitted correction this is the explicit all-endpoint bound

```text
|pi/4 - A_n|
 <= 4 / ((2n+1)^5 + 4(2n+1)).
```

The corresponding bound for `pi` is four times this value. The sign and strict
decrease of `I2(p)=-4/(p^5+4p)` on positive odd `p` are immediate from the
displayed denominator. This is a uniform certificate over every truncation
endpoint, not a table of decimal successes.

The historical and modern contributions remain separated. The endpoint
correction and `sthaulya` are documented Kerala objects. The displayed
increment identity is elementary modern algebra. The global error bound also
uses the limiting value of the base series and an alternating-remainder
theorem. The 2024 study derives this global use of `sthaulya`; it does not
claim that the surviving Kerala rationale supplied every one of these modern
hypotheses ([error bound from `sthaulya`](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11134)).

### B. A finite-volume pressure certificate does not certify its response

Consider the exact two-phase finite ensemble with external field `h` and
volume `N`:

```text
Z_N(h) = exp(Nh) + exp(-Nh) = 2 cosh(Nh),
P_N(h) = (1/N) log Z_N(h).
```

This is deliberately the smallest phase-coexistence model, not a claim about
a particular local lattice Hamiltonian. Every finite `N` has a positive,
real-analytic partition function on the real field axis. Its normalized
pressure has the exact decomposition

```text
P_N(h) = |h| + (1/N) log(1 + exp(-2N|h|)),
```

and hence

```text
0 <= P_N(h)-|h| <= (log 2)/N
```

uniformly for every real `h`. The scalar pressure computation therefore has
a global finite-volume certificate.

Its magnetic response is

```text
M_N(h) = dP_N/dh = tanh(Nh).
```

The pressure sequence is uniformly Cauchy, but the response sequence is not.
Let `c=atanh(1/2)<1` and choose `h_N=c/N`. Then

```text
M_N(h_N)   = 1/2,
M_(2N)(h_N)= tanh(2c)=4/5,
```

so

```text
sup_{|h|<=1} |M_(2N)(h)-M_N(h)| >= 3/10
```

for every `N`. No numerical experiment is involved. Equivalently, if the
thermodynamic response is `sgn(h)` on the punctured interval, then

```text
sup_{0<|h|<=1} |tanh(Nh)-sgn(h)| = 1
```

for every finite `N`, although convergence is pointwise at every nonzero
field. Away from the obstruction, on `|h|>=delta>0`, the exact bound is

```text
sup |tanh(Nh)-sgn(h)|
 = 1-tanh(N delta)
 = 2/(exp(2N delta)+1)
 <= 2 exp(-2N delta).
```

The actual obstruction is therefore the absence of a uniform phase gap
`delta`, not the use of a finite approximation as such.

This is the elementary finite-size mechanism behind a mature statistical-
mechanics distinction. Lee and Yang related thermodynamic singularities to
zeros of finite partition functions approaching the physical axis
([Yang and Lee 1952, theory of condensation](https://wucj.lab.westlake.edu.cn/teach/CNYang/YangLee_zero_PR1952.pdf);
[Lee and Yang 1952, Ising/lattice gas](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.87.410)).
Here the zeros are visible without approximation:

```text
h_k = i*pi*(k+1/2)/N,
```

so the nearest pair approaches `h=0` at distance `pi/(2N)`, exactly where the
limiting pressure acquires its cusp and the response loses uniformity.

### The precise joint, not a metaphor

Both computations start with a finite approximation and apply a downstream
operator.

- For the Kerala endpoint correction, the operator
  `f -> I`, `I(p)=f(p-2)+f(p)-1/p`, has a stable tail reconstruction on the
  documented sign/monotonicity class: the next defect bounds the global scalar
  error.
- For the two-phase pressure, differentiation is not uniformly continuous
  from the scalar sup-norm certificate to the response sup norm at the phase
  boundary: errors `O(1/N)` in pressure coexist with a response separation at
  least `3/10` between successive finite volumes and target error one.

The common object is therefore an approximation interface plus a specified
continuation operator and norm. One operator has an explicit stability
witness; the other has an explicit uniformity counterexample. Kerala authors
did not formulate thermodynamic limits, and statistical mechanics did not
originate `sthaulya`; neither direction is claimed.

## Step 4 — Delta 26 on the exact carriers

### Carriers, maps, and witnesses

The Kerala-side carrier can be written without historical renaming as

```text
Correction = Sigma(f : OddPositive -> Rational,
              proof of an exact sthaulya formula,
              proof of fixed sign and decreasing magnitude,
              boundary witness f(p) -> 0).
```

Its realization at endpoint `n` is the corrected value `A_n` together with
the exact increment equation and alternating-tail certificate. The map to the
consumer boundary is `A_n -> (value, error interval)`.

The statistical-mechanics carrier is

```text
FinitePhase = Sigma(N : PositiveNat,
               Z_N, P_N, proof Z_N=2cosh(Nh),
               pressure-error certificate).
```

It has at least two maps out:

```text
Eval_h(P_N) = P_N(h),
Resp_h(P_N) = dP_N/dh = tanh(Nh).
```

The response formula and its uniformity/no-uniformity proof are witnesses, not
numbers inferred from a plot.

### Declared continuation families

Let `V_value` contain all pressure evaluations on the real field axis under
sup norm. Let `V_gap(delta)` add response queries restricted to
`|h|>=delta`, and let `V_critical` admit response queries arbitrarily close to
zero.

- On `V_value`, volume `N` has error at most `log(2)/N`.
- On `V_gap(delta)`, it also has response error at most
  `2 exp(-2N delta)`.
- On `V_critical`, no finite `N` attains any uniform response tolerance below
  one.

Thus contextual equivalence and dominance change when the continuation family
changes. The clock-memory draw supplied the same logical warning in another
carrier, but it is not used as proof of this theorem.

### Proof-relevant composition before infimum

On the Kerala side, minimizing the number of retained terms before composing
the correction, exact `sthaulya` identity, sign/monotonicity proof, and desired
error interval would erase the certificate that makes the shortcut sound. On
the phase side, minimizing `N` from the scalar bound before asking whether the
consumer differentiates the pressure produces an invalid response guarantee.

The DSO order is exact:

```text
realization + operator + error witness + declared tolerance,
then infimum over n or N.
```

This is also why the drawn cakravala shortcut matters only as a warning: exact
substitution certifies one endpoint property but not fundamental minimality.
It supplies no series or statistical-mechanics lemma.

### Regret, curvature, interface, and option value

For value tolerance `epsilon`, the finite-phase architecture has a certified
sufficient volume `N >= ceil(log(2)/epsilon)`. For a response tolerance
`epsilon<1` on `V_critical`, the same architecture has no feasible finite
realization, hence `+infinity` architecture regret relative to that target
specification. If a phase gap `delta>0` is part of the context, a sufficient
volume is

```text
N >= (1/(2 delta)) log(2/epsilon).
```

With the sup metric, the scalar dependency curvature is at most `log(2)/N`;
the response distortion on the punctured critical interval is exactly one.
This is Delta 26 curvature only after the target maps and metrics above are
declared. It is not update holonomy: no pair of noncommuting rewrites occurs,
so update curvature is refused.

The interface's option value is task-relative. Retaining only a scalar value
and its uniform error interval supports `V_value`; retaining the analytic
finite-volume formula supports pointwise response computation, but still not
a uniform critical response certificate. Retaining a promised phase gap
`delta` opens the uniformly certified response tasks. These task-set
inclusions are exact; there is no context-free scalar price for them.

### Simone Weil's resistance, made falsifiable

The resisted claim is: “uniform finite-volume control of pressure is enough
to control any natural observable derived from it.” The exact counterexample
is the pair `P_N,P_(2N)` above: their scalar sup distance tends to zero while
their derivative sup distance is at least `3/10`. Any proposed uniform
continuation modulus from scalar pressure error to response error is therefore
falsified.

The positive counterpart is equally falsifiable: supply `delta>0`, and the
displayed exponential response bound must hold. Uniformity is demanded at the
actual obstruction and restored exactly when the missing context is restored.
