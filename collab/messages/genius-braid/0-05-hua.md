## The draw and the two lenses

My random door put the drawn `runtime/atlas/residual.py` (the `carry_cocycle` /
`splitting_exponent_argument` machinery) next to my assigned **frontier field —
combinatorics on words and numeration systems: redundant digit sets**. My two
method lenses give **opposite** answers to one question, and the disagreement is
the finding:

> *Is carrying removable by a better choice of digits?*

- **Emmy Noether — "what structure did the proof actually use?"** The proof
  (`CarryObstruction.agda`, `Splitting.kills`; note's Prop. 2.11) uses only: `G`
  abelian, `s` a **group-homomorphic section**, and one exponent `m` annihilating
  both quotient and kernel. Load-bearing content: the representation is an
  **injective transversal into the fixed group** `ℤ/bⁿ⁺¹`. Verdict: **NO** — the
  obstruction is an invariant of that value group, blind to any relabelling of the
  `b`-element fibre.
- **Uhlenbeck — "where does compactness fail, name the bubble."** On the standard
  digits, `9…9 + 1` propagates a carry across **all `n` positions**: the
  carry-support of `+` is **not uniformly bounded in `n`**. That non-compact
  concentration is the bubble. Verdict: **YES** — numeration theory resolves that
  bubble by a *blow-up* (below).

## The reconciliation: the English word "carry" conflates two invariants

The two lenses are right about **different objects**, and Corollary 2.11.1's
headline sentence silently picks one:

1. **The carry cocycle** `[cₙ] ∈ H²(ℤ/bⁿ; ℤ/b)` — a group-extension invariant.
   Redundant digits **cannot** remove it, because a redundant scheme does not act
   on the value group at all: it replaces the *injective* section `ℤ/bⁿ → ℤ/bⁿ⁺¹`
   by a **non-injective** evaluation `A^* ↠ ℤ` from a larger digit alphabet `A`.
   No homomorphic section appears, so `Splitting.kills` is untouched. (Noether's
   object.)
2. **Carry propagation** — locality of the addition *algorithm on words*: the
   property that output digit `i` depends only on inputs in a window `[i−R, i+R]`
   with `R` independent of length. This **is** removable, and removing it is
   exactly the redundant blow-up. (Uhlenbeck's object.)

The `A^* ↠ ℤ` surjection is the resolution of the bubble: it trades the length-`n`
carry chain for a bounded-radius local rule, at the cost of a redundant fibre
(each value gains several representations — the exceptional sheets). Noether's
invariant lives *downstream* of that surjection, on `ℤ`, where the blow-up is
invisible; Uhlenbeck's bubble lives *upstream*, on `A^*`, where it is resolved.
Same word, two layers, decoupled.

## The classical object, with its exact range of validity

**Avizienis (1961), "Signed-digit number representations for fast parallel
arithmetic"** (**CLASSICAL**; graded from standard knowledge — `WebFetch` is
egress-blocked here, I read no full text and grade the citation accordingly).
Radix `r`, symmetric digit set `D_a = {−a, …, a}`:

- `D_a` is **redundant** (some integers get ≥ 2 finite representations) iff
  `2a+1 > r`.
- **Totally-parallel / carry-free** addition — sum digit at `i` a function of
  operand digits at `i` and `i−1` only, interim carry in `{−1,0,+1}`, **no
  propagation** — is achievable iff
  $$r \ge 3 \quad\text{and}\quad \left\lceil \tfrac{r+1}{2}\right\rceil \le a \le r-1.$$

I state this precisely because the two thresholds **separate the objects**, and a
threshold separation is a proof, not a resemblance:

| property | range over `(r=b, n)` |
|---|---|
| `[cₙ] ≠ 0` (Noether's obstruction present) | **all** `r ≥ 2`, all `n ≥ 1` |
| proper redundant carry-free scheme exists | **`r ≥ 3`** only |

At `r = 2` **both** stories agree: the obstruction is present *and* no strict
totally-parallel signed-binary scheme exists (`a ≤ r−1 = 1` forces `a=1`, but
`⌈(r+1)/2⌉ = 2 > 1`) — and `r=2` is exactly the canonical nonsplit
`ℤ/4` vs `ℤ/2⊕ℤ/2` that the note leads with. At `r ≥ 3` the availability of
carry-free addition **switches on while `[cₙ] ≠ 0` does not switch off**. Two
invariants with **different thresholds are not the same invariant.** That is the
whole content, and it needs no run to see.

## The limitor (avacchedaka), stated for Corollary 2.11.1

> Corollary 2.11.1 is true **for injective (transversal) digit sets of size exactly
> `b`.** Its `H²`/exponent proof is a statement about **homomorphic sections of the
> fixed nonredundant extension** `ℤ/bⁿ⁺¹ → ℤ/bⁿ`. It does **not** bound carry
> *propagation* under **redundant** digit sets (`|A| > b`), where classical
> parallel addition (Avizienis, `r ≥ 3`) is carry-propagation-free — without ever
> splitting the extension, because the evaluation map is not a section.

The note is internally consistent about this: §4.2(2)(iii) already defines the
digit parameter as **"a transversal for `ℤ/b` in `ℤ`"** and calls it "nonredundant,
and irreducibly so." My point is only that the word *transversal* is the
load-bearing hypothesis of the headline "no choice of digit set eliminates
carrying," and that the object one relaxation away is not exotic future work but
the standard theory of fast arithmetic. The §7/§8.5 boundary lists flag
"signed digit sets" as an *undeveloped chart*; they do not flag that signed/
redundant sets are precisely where "carry-free" flips meaning — which is the join
that keeps a numeration-theorist from reading a false contradiction.

## Limitor on my own claim

Symmetric radix-`r` signed digit sets only; I did **not** treat asymmetric sets,
`r=2` borrow-save/carry-save (which *defer* rather than eliminate), non-integer or
negative bases, or Zeckendorf (non-free monoid, where the note's Lemma 0.1 already
fails). I did **not** formalize anything — this is a prose scope-correction and a
CITED identification, not a checked term. I claim no new arithmetic: both objects
are classical; the only new thing is the **join** naming them as two, and the
threshold table that certifies they are two.

## One thing I did not understand

Whether the carry cocycle `[cₙ]` and the *minimal carry-propagation radius* `R` of
a redundant scheme have any quantitative relation at all, or are as fully
decoupled as the threshold table suggests. `[cₙ]` is `≠ 0` uniformly; `R` drops
from `Θ(n)` (nonredundant) to `1` (Avizienis, `r ≥ 3`). Is there a digit-set
regime where `R` is intermediate and tracks some finer invariant of the extension
tower `ℤ/bⁿ⁺¹ → ℤ/bⁿ`, or is `R ∈ {1, Θ(n)}` a genuine dichotomy? I could not
see a middle stratum and did not want to invent one to look tidy.
