## The Boltzmann--Ashby disagreement, made falsifiable

Let the alphabet be `{a}`, the coefficient field be `Q`, and define the
weighted language

\[
f(a^n)=2^n.
\]

Its Hankel matrix is indexed by prefix and suffix lengths:

\[
H_{i,j}=f(a^{i+j})=2^{i+j}=2^i2^j.
\]

Every row `R_i` is different from every other row, so equality of complete
weighted futures has infinitely many classes. This is exactly what
`NaturalMachine.FutureBehavior` computes if the prefix length is the state,
successor is the action, and `2^i` is the exact present observation.

Yet all rows are scalar multiples of `R_0`; the Hankel matrix has rank one.
The linear weighted automaton

\[
\alpha=[1],\qquad M_a=[2],\qquad \omega=[1]
\]

realizes the series because `alpha M_a^n omega = 2^n`. This is a literal
disagreement:

- **Boltzmann count:** infinitely many residual microstates under equality;
- **Ashby dimension:** one independent linear response mode.

Neither number refutes the other. The one-state skeleton stores the missing
variety in the accumulated scalar weight and in word length. If those are
counted as machine configuration or bit cost, the infinity returns; if scalar
arithmetic is an admitted primitive and only linear dimension is charged, rank
one is correct. Thus “minimal state” is ill-typed until the machine category,
allowed coefficient operations, and cost coordinates are declared.

This separates three weighted state notions. A spectral Hankel learner over a
field chooses a finite row/column basis and charges linear-span dimension. A
deterministic weighted `L*` learner over a semifield identifies residuals only
up to a nonzero scalar; in the example, `R_i = 2^i R_0`, so its projective
residual quotient also has one class. Both differ from exact row equality in
`FutureBehavior`. The learner can ask membership queries for `f(uv)`, and an
equivalence oracle can return a counterexample word. But query *count* alone
does not charge the size of answers such as `2^n`, and neither a basis nor a
projective class chooses a counterexample policy. The 2016 weighted-automata
account states the rank-factorization construction explicitly, while the 2024
weighted `L*` paper separately assumes a semifield, membership and equivalence
oracles, closedness, consistency, and scalar-normalized residual equivalence:
[Arrivault et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v57/arrivault16.pdf),
[Pasti et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.468/).

The forecast's falsifier therefore fails. There is no single carrier in the
draw on which equality quotient, linear rank, query policy, scalar register,
and bit cost coincide.

## Islamic algebra encountered in its own mathematical movement

The sequence al-Khwarizmi--al-Karaji--al-Samaw'al is not a decorative ancestry
for automata.

Al-Khwarizmi's text begins with three kinds -- roots, squares, and simple
numbers -- and separates their equations into six species. It normalizes
multiples to one square and supplies procedures and geometric demonstrations
for the compound cases. The sixfold grammar matters: with positive magnitudes
on the two sides, a modern signed-polynomial normal form is not already the
native carrier. Completion/reduction is a lawful transformation inside that
grammar, not a quotient by all future suffix tests. The primary-text anchor is
Rosen's 1831 bilingual edition, especially the
[compendium and six cases](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing/Compendium_on_calculating_by_completion_and_reduction).

Al-Karaji's algebra enlarges the power calculus and treats polynomial
arithmetic without requiring each step to return to a geometric magnitude.
The induction associated with this lineage is a passage from one exponent to
the next, particularly in binomial-coefficient schemes; it certifies a
procedure for every degree. It is not identification of an unknown target by
queries. I retain the historiographic caution: Rashed distinguishes mastered
successive/regressive procedures from the later axiomatized induction
principle, and some material attributed to al-Karaji survives through
al-Samaw'al's report. Sources:
[Rashed, 1972](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00348537) and
[Woepcke's edition of `al-Fakhri`](https://books.google.com/books?id=tRQVa-zmKUwC).

Al-Samaw'al's `al-Bahir` makes the coefficient/power layout operational:
successive remainders in polynomial division are arranged against power
positions, including deficient quantities and reciprocal powers. The table
supports multiplication, subtraction, and division because coefficient value
and exponent position remain available. It therefore resists the phrase
“rank one means one thing to remember” in exactly Ashby's way: a basis of one
mode does not erase the coefficient carried on that mode. This is still not a
historical claim that the table is a Hankel matrix. Its axes are powers and
division stages, not prefixes and suffixes under concatenation; no membership
oracle, equivalence oracle, or unknown regular series is present. Manuscript
provenance and edition anchors:
[MPIWG ISMI manuscript witnesses](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/text/102132)
and [Rashed--Ahmad's `al-Bahir` extract](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110295665.763).

What travels in both directions is a question, not an identification. The
automata lane asks the algebraic table which coordinates and coefficient
operations its claimed state count has hidden. The historical algebra lane
receives from automata a sharper distinction between equality of expressions,
equality of all continuations, and linear dependence of coefficient profiles.
The native objects remain untranslated where their operations differ.

## Natural Machine consequence: refusal with an exact routing constraint

Do **not** generalize `NaturalMachine.FutureBehavior` or
`Pairfield.MyhillNerodeAdapter` to weighted automata merely by replacing the
boolean observation type with a semiring. That would correctly quotient by
equality of weighted futures but would miss finite-dimensional linear
realizations such as `f(a^n)=2^n`. Conversely, replacing the quotient by
Hankel rank would lose the existing universal property in `Machine` and would
silently admit scalar registers and linear combinations.

An earned future extension would need a new module, not an edit to those two:

```text
carrier:    residual series Sigma* -> K
map:        prefix u |-> (v |-> f(uv))
operation:  left shift by a letter
structure:  either projectivization over a semifield (deterministic)
            or declared K-semimodule/linear span (general linear)
ledger:     (basis dimension, scalar representation cost, query protocol)
```

Over a field, finite Hankel rank could then justify a finite linear
realization; over a general semiring, even that theorem requires new
hypotheses. No such coefficient structure, exact-query interface, or cost
ledger is jointly present in the draw. I therefore make **no Haskell or Agda
edit**. The result is a limitor on the newly successful DFA/minimality and
theorem-compiled-observation lanes: boolean cardinal minimality and weighted
linear minimality must not share a headline without a comparison map that
accounts for the scalar register.
