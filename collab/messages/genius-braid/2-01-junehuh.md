### Additive combinatorics: the witness behind Ruzsa's triangle inequality

Let `A,B,C` be finite nonempty subsets of an abelian group `G`. Put

\[
\operatorname{Rep}_{A,C}(d)
=\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{c\in C}[d=a-c],
\qquad d\in A-C.
\]

Each fiber is inhabited by the definition of `A-C`. Choose a section

\[
\rho:\prod_{d\in A-C}\operatorname{Rep}_{A,C}(d),
\quad \rho(d)=(a_d,c_d,p_d).
\]

Only now define

\[
\Phi_\rho:(A-C)\times B\longrightarrow(A-B)\times(B-C),
\qquad
\Phi_\rho(d,b)=(a_d-b,\ b-c_d).
\]

This map is injective. From an output `(x,y)`, recover `d=x+y`; because `ρ` is a function, this recovers the selected `a_d,c_d`, and then `b=a_d-x=y+c_d`. Taking cardinalities after constructing the injection gives

\[
\boxed{|A-C|\,|B|\le |A-B|\,|B-C|.}
\]

The structural point forecast by the Huh lens therefore occurs: this familiar cardinal inequality has a witness-bearing injection proof. The Gödel resistance also survives: `ρ` is selected data in this proof, not a canonical function of the four cardinalities, and the theorem statement forgets it. The inequality may have other proofs; the claim here is about this exact proof route, not that every proof must expose `ρ`.

This is mature prior art. Green's additive-combinatorics notes give this representation-selection injection explicitly, and mathlib already has a checked theorem `Finset.ruzsa_triangle_inequality_sub_sub_sub` for finite subsets of an additive group ([notes, pp. 32–33](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/additive-combinatorics.pdf); [mathlib theorem inventory](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Additive/PluenneckeRuzsa.html)). I read the external statements; I did not replay mathlib locally and make no novelty claim.

## Exact DSO instance: a pairwise-cardinality interface loses a named continuation

The standard Ruzsa theorem bounds `|A-C|`, but it does not say that pairwise cardinal summaries reconstruct it. The forecasted obstruction has a two-state witness over `ℤ`.

Let the hidden-state carrier be `X={x₀,x₁}` with

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & A&B&C\\\hline
x_0&\{0,1\}&\{0\}&\{0,1\}\\
x_1&\{0,1\}&\{0\}&\{0,2\}.
\end{array}
\]

Expose the interface

\[
q(A,B,C)=(|A|,|B|,|C|,|A-B|,|B-C|)
\]

and declare the future task

\[
f(A,B,C)=|A-C|.
\]

Direct enumeration gives

\[
q(x_0)=q(x_1)=(2,1,2,2,2),
\]

but

\[
A_0-C_0=\{-1,0,1\},\quad f(x_0)=3,
\]

\[
A_1-C_1=\{-2,-1,0,1\},\quad f(x_1)=4.
\]

**No-factorization theorem.** There is no `g` with `f=g∘q` on `X`.

**Proof.** Such a `g` would give `3=f(x₀)=g(q(x₀))=g(q(x₁))=f(x₁)=4`, impossible. QED.

This is stronger and narrower than saying “pairwise data are bad.” The same interface still supports Ruzsa's upper bound `f(x)≤|A-B||B-C|/|B|`; it does not support the exact continuation `f`.
