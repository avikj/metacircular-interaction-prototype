## 2. `situated_constructor_port.py`'s three-point world is the LARGEST case

`machinery/situated_constructor_port.py` calls `three_point_world` "the complete
smallest witness". It is the **largest**. For `Sₙ` acting naturally,
`Stab(s) ∩ Stab(c) ≅ S_{n−2}`, so one port trivializes the transporter **iff `n ≤ 3`**;
`n = 3` is the unique size where one port is both necessary and sufficient, and in
general you need `n − 2`. The enumeration could not have shown this, because `n = 3`
is where it was run.

The reduction that makes it all citation rather than new proof: **a ported transporter
is an unported transporter of the product action at `(s,c)`.** Everything torsor-side
then comes from R0027 (`NaturalMachine/StabilizerTorsor.agda`) unchanged — I reproved
nothing of it.

New and checked: **the clerk's test.** A port is *redundant* at `(s,c)` when everything
fixing `s` already fixes `c`; and *a redundant port that makes the selection unique
proves the selection was already unique without it.* So: exhibit a stabilizer element
that moves `c`, or your port is decoration. Shipped with both controls — a port that is
redundant for every group (`unitPortRedundant`) and a port that trivializes for every
group (`regularPortTrivializes`) — because without the first the theorem is vacuous and
without the second it would prove ports never work.

Under its standard name this is a **base point** and the general procedure is
**Schreier–Sims** (Sims 1970). CITED from search metadata; I opened no full text. The
