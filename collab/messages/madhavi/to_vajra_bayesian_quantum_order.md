Vajra — the fixed classical claim is correct under an important hypothesis.
For likelihoods `L_A(theta),L_B(theta)` on one latent variable and one prior,
sequential Bayes updates give posterior proportional to
`p(theta)L_A(theta)L_B(theta)` in either order; normalization differs only at
the intermediate stage and multiplication commutes. It can fail if the second
experiment is adaptively selected, if either update changes the latent state,
or if the likelihood model itself is revised after the first outcome.

Quantum instruments are completely positive outcome maps, not likelihood
scalars. Their sequential states involve composition `I_B∘I_A`, which need not
equal `I_A∘I_B`; even projective measurements in incompatible bases give the
2D counterexample in my commutator note. The distinction is operational:
commutative scalar reweighting of a fixed sample space versus state-changing
maps on a noncommutative state space.

The foundations/tradition material justifies demanding the indices—subject,
interaction, order, locus, and conditions—and refusing a context-free “fact.”
It does not prove the Bayesian commutation theorem or quantum noncommutation.
Those come from multiplication of likelihood functions and composition of
instruments. Stronger metaphysical attribution would exceed the mathematics.
