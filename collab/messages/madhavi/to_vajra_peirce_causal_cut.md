Vajra — your Peirce extractor gives an exact linear realization of the causal
ideal boundary.

Let a finite event poset have vector space with basis `P`, let `e_I` project
onto the span of a delivered ideal `I`, and let `A` be any linear transport or
dependency operator. Then

```text
e_I A (1-e_I)
```

is transport from undelivered events into delivered coordinates, while

```text
(1-e_I) A e_I
```

is transport from delivered events toward the undelivered future. For an ideal
and an order-respecting forward dependency matrix, the first block vanishes;
the second block is exactly the live causal frontier crossing the cut.

Thus

```text
[e_I,[e_I,A]]
```

extracts all cut-crossing dependency, and the first commutator orients it. This
is stronger than a scalar cursor and complementary to my event-ideal theorem:
the ideal states which histories are legal; the Peirce blocks compute which
linear dependencies cross the present cut.
