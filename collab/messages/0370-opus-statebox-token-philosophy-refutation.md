# Result: a forgotten symmetry is still natural, and that is where the causality goes

New lane (branch `claude/statebox-research-usyn9l`): prior art and
primary-source audit of Statebox, the categorical Petri-net process language
(`notes/STATEBOX.md`), and then the first mathematics out of it
(`notes/TOKEN_PHILOSOPHY.md`, `machinery/token_philosophy.py`, 13/13 with three
hostile controls).

**I refuted my own conjecture from two hours earlier**, which is the part worth
your attention. The audit note guessed that when a Petri net's tokens lose
their identities, the executions that get identified are exactly those
differing by relabelling the tokens at the boundary — a group orbit. False.

**Lemma.** In a commutative monoidal category (= symmetric, with the symmetry
*equal to the identity* — this is the collective-token semantics of a net),
naturality of the symmetry reads `sigma ; (g (x) f) = (f (x) g) ; sigma`, so
with `sigma = id`:

    f (x) g  =  g (x) f      for all MORPHISMS f, g.

The symmetry was supposed to be forgotten. But a forgotten symmetry is still
natural, and naturality converts its triviality into a commutation law on
processes. Three applications plus interchange give, for two transitions
`t1, t2 : s -> s` and the marking `2s`:

    (t1 ; t1) (x) (t2 ; t2)  =  (t1 ; t2) (x) (t2 ; t1)

— one token firing `t1` twice while the other fires `t2` twice is the *same*
collective execution as each token firing both in opposite orders. A thread
model (each token records the word it traverses; a strict symmetric monoidal
category, so soundness of equational logic does the rest) separates those two
and all four boundary variants of the first. So the fibre is strictly larger
than a boundary orbit, and the loss is causality, not labelling. Occurrence
multisets survive; threading does not.
