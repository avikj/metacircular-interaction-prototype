# Claim: the cyclotomic organ's route is already a Mathlib product identity

The live native operation routes a target `a^n - 1` through the pieces
`Phi_d(a)` for `d | n` before valuation, progression, or budget reasoning.
Under the standard cyclotomic factorization, pinned Mathlib already exposes
the exact polynomial theorem `Polynomial.prod_cyclotomic_eq_X_pow_sub_one`.

I am adapting that theorem at the integer-evaluation boundary.  The checked
object must prove both the full route

`prod (d in divisors n), Phi_d(a) = a^n - 1`

for positive `n`, and that every routed piece divides the original target.
The native `a=2,n=6` route must reconstruct `63`; `n=0` is the hostile
endpoint because its divisor product is empty while `a^0-1=0`.

Forecast registered before constructing the adapter:

- `0.84`: evaluation transports the exact Mathlib identity and divisor
  membership gives the native piece-routing theorem directly;
- `0.12`: the theorem survives but an evaluation, cast, or positivity
  convention requires a narrower integer interface;
- `0.04`: the native route depends on primitive-factor or valuation data that
  the product identity does not retain.

Scope is deliberately strict.  This adapter checks the route that connects an
encounter to its cyclotomic pieces.  It does not prove the valuation chain law,
the primitive/exceptional divisor dichotomy, the degree bounds, any factor was
found within budget, or any claim that a refusal or call-graph connection
confers agency.  Those begin after the factorization and must remain separate.

Requested return: accept only if integer evaluation of the complete divisor
product and piece divisibility are the native routing object.  Narrow or reject
if the lineage means a proof-relevant factorization algorithm, valuation
support, primitive-prime classification, or budgeted reachability by “route.”
