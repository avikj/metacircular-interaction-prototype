# The Fermat–Goldbach Frey curve, adjudicated: F5 stands, Criterion R gains a clause and a negative control

## 1. The finding, confirmed

Dieulefait, Jiménez Urroz, Ribet, *Modular forms with large coefficient fields
via congruences*, **arXiv:1111.5592**, *Research in Number Theory* **1** (2015),
art. 2. For $2^{\ell+4}=p+q$ with $p,q$ prime, the curve
$y^2=x(x-p)(x+2^{\ell+4})$ is semistable, $\Delta=(2^{\ell}pq)^2$, conductor
$2pq$, with a weight-$2$ level-$2pq$ newform by Wiles. The authors' own phrase
for the family is "Frey curves of **Fermat–Goldbach** type"; the $t\ge3$ case is
built on **Chen 1973**.

**The arXiv identifier is CONFIRMED** at search-summary level — title, three
authors, Nov-2011 posting and the 2015 journal record all match, and the
abstract itself mentions Fermat–Goldbach Frey curves. The sweep's tentative
number was correct, and I am saying so rather than repeating it hedged.
Everything remains **śabda grade**: no PDF was read, `WebFetch` is
`EGRESS_BLOCKED` on every host here, and the curve data above is as reported by
search summaries of the paper's text.

## 2. The verdict: **F5 not falsified. Row stands verbatim. R refined by one
clarifying clause.**

Three steps, and the first is *not* the whole argument — "a representation is
the opposite polarity" is true but too cheap to carry an adjudication on its own.

1. **Quantifier.** R quantifies over counterexamples. A failing even $N$ supplies
   no pair $(p,q)$, so the construction has no input and yields no curve. The
   row's assertion is untouched.
2. **Direction of inference.** Goldbach (or, unconditionally, Chen) is the
   *hypothesis*; the *conclusion* is about newforms with large coefficient
   fields. The inference runs arithmetic $\to$ modular forms. R is a criterion
   about the return trip. Nothing in the paper is a theorem about Goldbach and
   the authors do not suggest otherwise.
3. **No emptiness, and no inverse.** R's operative half is *"moduli can be shown
   to be empty"*. Here $S_2(\Gamma_0(2pq))$ is nonempty and the newform is
   guaranteed rather than contradictory — the receptacle is full. And the map
   cannot be run backwards: the level $2pq$ is a function of the decomposition
   sought, so knowing which space to search presupposes $p$ and $q$. Not even a
   search reduction.

**What R gains rather than loses.** §5.5 had a positive control (the
Collatz-cycle row). It now has a **negative control it did not construct**: a
real Frey-package construction touching Goldbach that R declines, on grounds
written down before the construction was known. That is worth more to the
criterion than the row was worth to it.

**The clause R should carry explicitly** (added to §5.5.1, verbatim from the
note): the two conditions are separately necessary — (i) the object must be
generated by the *counterexample*, since an object generated by an *instance*
carries the inference the wrong way; (ii) the moduli must be shown *empty*,
since landing in a nonempty space of newforms is the negation of the mechanism,
not a weak form of it.

## 3. I checked whether the note ever quietly needs the stronger form — it does
not, and the two places are named

R is invoked outside §5.5 in exactly two places, and both survive **verbatim**:

- **§4.1**, "the input to the method is a global algebro-geometric object
  attached to a *hypothetical* solution";
- **§7.3**, "the *rigid global object attached to a hypothetical solution*
  property of Criterion R", used to place FLT in a family from which Goldbach is
  excluded.

Read "hypothetical solution" as *a solution whose existence the argument intends
to refute* — which is how both sentences use it — and the Fermat–Goldbach curve,
attached to an actual decomposition, is correctly outside the §7.3 family and
correctly not a transfer in §4.1. Neither sentence needs the stronger premise
"no Frey construction attaches to Goldbach at all". **The note is clean.** The
one honest caveat: the word *hypothetical* was decorative when written and is
load-bearing now, which is why I recorded it instead of just reporting a pass.

**A near miss on F2, recorded and not fired.** The paper does compose the two
toolkits — Chen's sieve theorem as *input* to a modular-method argument. F2 asks
for a method-level transfer **between two of the five classes**; here the
conclusion lies in neither class and nothing returns from the modular lane to
the additive one. F2 does not fire. Written down so the next reader who finds
the paper knows it was weighed.

**Searched specifically, negative:** *Frey curve Goldbach representation
modular*; *elliptic curve $p+q=2^n$ conductor $2pq$*; *Goldbach modularity
newform level $2pq$*; *Frey curve attached to counterexample Goldbach modular
