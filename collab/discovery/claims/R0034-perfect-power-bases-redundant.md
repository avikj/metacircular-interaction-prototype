# Rosetta bridge

The algebraic idiom reads `(c^k)^n - 1 = c^(kn) - 1` as a reindexing of one
family, and sees nothing further to say.  The observer idiom asks which
objects are worth spending an encounter on, and needs a criterion for
declining.  The bridge is that the reindexing IS the criterion: a base that
factors through another as a power generates no integer the root does not
already generate, so it can be declined with the identity as the reason rather
than by taste.  What the algebraic side does not supply, having no notion of
an organ with a budget, is that this makes the retained set canonical.  What
the observer side does not supply is any reason to prefer one non-power over
another, which remains open.

# Exact statement

Let c >= 2 and k >= 2, and put b = c^k. (1) Redundancy: (c^k)^n - 1 = c^(kn) - 1 for every n >= 1, so the family generated by base b is contained in the family generated by base c, and every prime earnable from base b is earnable from base c. (2) Degree comparison: if p is a primitive prime divisor of Phi_n(b), that is ord_p(b) = n, then ord_p(c) = d satisfies n = d / gcd(d, k), and phi(d) <= k phi(n). Proof: ord_p(c^k) = ord_p(c) / gcd(ord_p(c), k) is standard; writing g = gcd(d,k) and d = g n, the submultiplicativity phi(g n) <= g phi(n) together with g <= k gives the bound. Hence the root's route to the same prime is no larger in degree, and the redundancy is a genuine saving rather than a formality. (3) Consequently an organ choosing its own base repertoire retains exactly the integers that are not perfect powers, declining each perfect power with the explicit identity that makes it another base's encounter. This is the argument of ARITHMETIC_LIFE_FIRST_EXECUTION equation (3), that a composite modulus d = ab with a > 1 adds no divisibility test because d divides n implies a divides n, applied one level up: multiplication of moduli retains the primes, exponentiation of bases retains the non-powers.

# Proof obligations

1. `(c^k)^n = c^(kn)`, hence the family containment.  One line.
2. `ord_p(c^k) = ord_p(c) / gcd(ord_p(c), k)` — standard cyclic-group fact.
3. `phi(g n) <= g phi(n)` for `g >= 1` — standard, from multiplicativity and
   `phi(p^i) <= p^i - p^(i-1)` bookkeeping.
4. Composing 2 and 3 with `g = gcd(d,k) <= k` gives `phi(d) <= k phi(n)`.
5. `perfect_power` decides the predicate exactly, by integer bisection for
   the root at each exponent up to `log_2(base)`.  No floating-point value
   enters the search.

# Falsification

- Exhibit `c, k, n` with `(c^k)^n - 1` different from `c^(kn) - 1`.  (Asserted
  for `c` in 2..7, `k` in 2..4, `n` in 1..11.)
- Exhibit a prime primitive for `(c^k, n)` whose base-`c` order `d` violates
  `n = d/gcd(d,k)` or `phi(d) > k phi(n)`.  (Asserted over the complete
  factorizations of `Phi_n(c^k)` for `(c,k)` in {(2,2),(2,3),(3,2),(5,2)} and
  `n < 14`.)
- Exhibit a base below 40 that `base_refusal` misclassifies.  (The declined
  set is asserted to be exactly 4, 8, 9, 16, 25, 27, 32, 36.)
- Exhibit a prime reachable from a perfect-power base and not from its root.
  (Would refute (1) and is impossible by the identity, so this is a check on
  the implementation rather than the mathematics.)

# Prior art

Elementary.  The identity `(c^k)^n = c^(kn)` and the order formula
`ord_p(c^k) = ord_p(c)/gcd(ord_p(c), k)` are standard; the practice of
reducing a base to its non-power root is routine in Cunningham-style tables,
where `4^n - 1` is simply not tabulated separately from `2^n - 1`.  Searched
2026-08-12 alongside R0025-R0033; grep over notes/, collab/, machinery/,
papers/, code/ found no prior occurrence in this corpus.  **No novelty is
claimed.**  What is recorded is the use of the identity as an organ's
self-applied refusal criterion, and its identity of shape with the opening
argument of `ARITHMETIC_LIFE_FIRST_EXECUTION`.
